Absolutely. **Conjunctions are one of the most important grammar topics for Verbal Ability**, because they appear in **error spotting, sentence correction, fill-in-the-blanks, sentence improvement, and para jumbles**.

Let's learn them from the very beginning and then go into the tricky rules.

# CONJUNCTIONS — Complete Concept

## 1. What is a Conjunction?

A **conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, clauses, or sentences.**

Think of a conjunction as a **bridge**.

For example:

> Rahul **and** Aman are friends.

`and` connects two words:

> Rahul + Aman

Another:

> I was tired, **but** I continued studying.

`but` connects two clauses:

> I was tired
> I continued studying

So the basic idea is:

> **Conjunction = connecting word**

---

# 2. What exactly can a conjunction connect?

A conjunction can connect:

### A. Word + word

> Rahul **and** Aman

### B. Phrase + phrase

> in the morning **and** in the evening

### C. Clause + clause

> I wanted to go, **but** I was busy.

### D. Sentence/idea + sentence/idea

> Work hard, **and** you will succeed.

---

# 3. What is a Clause?

Before going further, you need to understand **clause**, because conjunctions frequently connect clauses.

A **clause** is a group of words containing a **subject + verb**.

Example:

> **I study.**

Subject = I
Verb = study

So this is a clause.

Another:

> **She is sleeping.**

Subject = she
Verb = is sleeping

---

### Example with conjunction

> I study **and** my brother plays.

There are two clauses:

> **I study**
> **my brother plays**

`and` connects them.

---

# 4. Three Main Types of Conjunctions

For Verbal Ability, you should know these three:

1. **Coordinating Conjunctions**
2. **Subordinating Conjunctions**
3. **Correlative Conjunctions**

Let's understand each.

---

# 5. Coordinating Conjunctions

These connect **equal grammatical elements**.

For example:

> Rahul **and** Aman

Both are nouns.

Or:

> I studied **and** I passed.

Both are independent clauses.

The most important coordinating conjunctions can be remembered using:

# FANBOYS

> **F** — For
> **A** — And
> **N** — Nor
> **B** — But
> **O** — Or
> **Y** — Yet
> **S** — So

This is extremely useful for exams.

---

# 6. AND

`And` generally expresses **addition**.

> Rahul **and** Aman came.

Meaning:

> Rahul + Aman

Another:

> She bought a pen **and** a notebook.

---

### AND can connect clauses

> I studied hard **and** I passed the exam.

Two ideas are being added.

---

# 7. BUT

`But` expresses **contrast**.

> He is poor, **but** he is honest.

The ideas contrast:

> poor ↔ honest

Another:

> I was tired, **but** I continued studying.

---

# 8. YET

`Yet` also expresses **contrast**, similar to `but`.

> He is poor, **yet** he is happy.

Meaning:

> Despite being poor, he is happy.

A useful distinction:

> **but** = contrast

> **yet** = contrast, often with a sense of something surprising/unexpected

---

# 9. OR

`Or` expresses **choice/alternative**.

> Would you like tea **or** coffee?

> You can walk **or** take the bus.

---

### OR can also mean otherwise

> Hurry up, **or** you will miss the train.

Meaning:

> If you don't hurry, you will miss the train.

---

# 10. NOR

`Nor` expresses a **negative alternative**.

> He doesn't smoke, **nor** does he drink.

Notice something important:

> **nor does he drink**

not:

> ❌ nor he drinks

We'll come back to this because it is an important exam rule.

---

# 11. SO

`So` expresses **result/consequence**.

> It was raining, **so** we stayed home.

Cause:

> It was raining.

Result:

> We stayed home.

Another:

> He worked hard, **so** he passed.

---

# 12. FOR

`For` can mean **because**, especially in formal/literary English.

> I stayed home, **for** I was sick.

Meaning:

> I stayed home because I was sick.

Don't confuse this with the preposition `for`.

Compare:

> I bought a gift **for** my friend.

Here `for` is a **preposition**.

But:

> I stayed home, **for** I was sick.

Here `for` is a **conjunction**.

---

# 13. FANBOYS Summary

| Conjunction | Main idea            |
| ----------- | -------------------- |
| **For**     | reason               |
| **And**     | addition             |
| **Nor**     | negative alternative |
| **But**     | contrast             |
| **Or**      | alternative          |
| **Yet**     | contrast             |
| **So**      | result               |

---

# 14. Subordinating Conjunctions

Now we get to a much bigger category.

A **subordinating conjunction** connects:

> **Dependent clause + Independent clause**

It makes one clause dependent on another.

For example:

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

`I stayed home` can stand alone.

But:

> because I was sick

doesn't normally express a complete thought by itself.

So:

> **because I was sick**

is a dependent/subordinate clause.

---

# 15. Independent vs Dependent Clause

### Independent clause

Can stand alone:

> I was tired.

Complete meaning.

### Dependent clause

Cannot normally stand alone:

> because I was tired

It feels incomplete.

Put them together:

> I went to bed **because I was tired**.

---

# 16. Important Subordinating Conjunctions

You should learn them by **meaning**, not as a random list.

---

## A. Cause / Reason

Common ones:

> because
> since
> as
> as
> for

Examples:

> I stayed home **because** I was sick.

> **Since** it was raining, we stayed home.

> **As** I was tired, I went to bed.

---

# 17. BECAUSE vs SO

This is a very important exam rule.

`Because` introduces a **reason**.

`So` introduces a **result**.

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

Reason → I was sick.

Or:

> I was sick, **so I stayed home**.

Result → I stayed home.

### Don't normally use both together:

> ❌ Because I was sick, **so** I stayed home.

Use one structure:

> ✅ Because I was sick, I stayed home.

OR:

> ✅ I was sick, so I stayed home.

This is a very common error.

---

# 18. Condition

Important conjunctions:

> if
> unless
> provided that
> provided
> as long as
> in case

Examples:

> **If** you work hard, you will succeed.

> **Unless** you study, you will fail.

---

# 19. UNLESS

`Unless` means approximately:

> **if not**

Example:

> Unless you study, you will fail.

Means:

> If you do not study, you will fail.

---

### Important:

Don't normally use another negative with `unless`.

> ❌ Unless you **don't** study, you will fail.

> ✅ Unless you study, you will fail.

Because `unless` already contains the idea of **if not**.

---

# 20. Time

Important conjunctions:

> when
> while
> before
> after
> until
> till
> since
> as soon as
> whenever

Examples:

> Call me **when** you arrive.

> Wash your hands **before** you eat.

> I will wait **until** you return.

> **After** he finished his work, he went home.

---

# 21. UNTIL

`Until` means:

> up to the time when something happens.

> I waited **until** he came.

Meaning:

> I waited up to the time he came.

---

# 22. WHILE

`While` often expresses two things happening at the same time.

> I studied **while** my brother watched TV.

It can also show contrast:

> **While** Rahul is intelligent, he is careless.

Here `while` means something like **although/whereas**.

---

# 23. Contrast / Concession

Important conjunctions:

> although
> though
> even though
> even if
> whereas
> while

Examples:

> **Although** he is poor, he is honest.

> **Though** he worked hard, he failed.

> **Even though** it was raining, we went outside.

---

# 24. Although vs But

This is one of the **most important conjunction errors**.

Don't normally use:

> ❌ Although he is poor, **but** he is honest.

Use:

> ✅ Although he is poor, he is honest.

OR:

> ✅ He is poor, but he is honest.

### Rule:

**Although/Though + no but**

Similarly:

> Because + no so

These are called **correlative/paired structure errors** in many exam questions.

---

# 25. Purpose

Common conjunctions/structures:

> so that
> in order that

Example:

> I studied hard **so that** I could pass.

Meaning:

> I studied hard for the purpose of passing.

---

# 26. Result

Important structure:

> so...that

Example:

> He was **so tired that** he fell asleep.

Another:

> It was **so hot that** we stayed inside.

---

# 27. Such...that

Another result structure:

> **such + noun + that**

Example:

> It was **such a beautiful day that** we went outside.

Compare:

> **so + adjective/adverb + that**

> He was **so tired that** he slept.

But:

> **such + noun phrase + that**

> It was **such a difficult exam that** many students failed.

---

# 28. Correlative Conjunctions

These are **pairs of conjunctions that work together**.

The important ones are:

> **either...or**

> **neither...nor**

> **both...and**

> **not only...but also**

> **whether...or**

> **rather...than**

> **no sooner...than**

> **hardly...when**

> **scarcely...when**

These are extremely important for Verbal Ability.

---

# 29. EITHER...OR

Used to express a choice between two alternatives.

> **Either** Rahul **or** Aman will come.

Meaning:

> One of them will come.

Another:

> You can **either** study **or** sleep.

---

# 30. NEITHER...NOR

Used for two negative alternatives.

> **Neither** Rahul **nor** Aman came.

Meaning:

> Rahul didn't come + Aman didn't come.

---

# 31. BOTH...AND

Used to join two positive elements.

> **Both** Rahul **and** Aman are students.

> She is **both** intelligent **and** hardworking.

---

# 32. NOT ONLY...BUT ALSO

Adds emphasis to two things.

> He is **not only** intelligent **but also** hardworking.

Another:

> She **not only** sings **but also** dances.

---

# 33. WHETHER...OR

Used when there are alternatives/possibilities.

> I don't know **whether** he will come **or** not.

> **Whether** you win **or** lose, you should try your best.

---

# 34. RATHER...THAN

Shows preference.

> I would **rather** stay home **than** go outside.

Important:

> rather + V1 + than + V1

> I would rather **stay** than **go**.

---

# 35. NO SOONER...THAN

This is a **very important exam structure**.

Meaning:

> One thing happened immediately after another.

Example:

> **No sooner did he arrive than** it started raining.

Notice:

> **No sooner ... than**

NOT:

> ❌ No sooner ... when

---

# 36. HARDLY...WHEN

Same general idea.

> **Hardly had I reached home when** it started raining.

Structure:

> **Hardly + had + subject + V3 + when**

---

# 37. SCARCELY...WHEN

Similar:

> **Scarcely had he arrived when** the meeting began.

Remember:

> **No sooner → than**

> **Hardly → when**

> **Scarcely → when**

This is a classic competitive-exam question.

---

# 38. Subject-Verb Agreement with Either...Or / Neither...Nor

This is extremely important.

When two subjects are connected by:

> either...or

or

> neither...nor

the verb generally agrees with the **nearer subject**.

### Example:

> Either Rahul or his friends **are** coming.

Nearest subject:

> friends → plural

Therefore:

> **are**

---

Reverse it:

> Either his friends or Rahul **is** coming.

Nearest subject:

> Rahul → singular

Therefore:

> **is**

---

### Same with neither...nor:

> Neither the teacher nor the students **are** ready.

Nearest subject = students → plural.

> Neither the students nor the teacher **is** ready.

Nearest subject = teacher → singular.

This is a **very important Verbal Ability rule**.

---

# 39. Parallelism with Conjunctions

This is another major topic.

When conjunctions connect things, the connected elements should generally have the **same grammatical form**.

### Correct:

> She likes **singing and dancing**.

Both are `-ing` forms.

### Incorrect:

> ❌ She likes **singing and to dance**.

Better:

> ✅ She likes **singing and dancing**.

---

Another:

> He wants **to study and to work**.

or:

> He wants **to study and work**.

Both are parallel.

---

### Another example:

> She is **intelligent and hardworking**.

Adjective + adjective.

Not:

> ❌ She is intelligent and works hard.

That can be grammatically possible in some contexts, but when the structure is intended to coordinate two descriptions, parallel construction is preferred.

---

# 40. AND vs AS WELL AS

These are not exactly interchangeable in every grammar question.

### AND

> Rahul **and** Aman are friends.

The subjects joined by `and` normally form a plural subject.

> Rahul and Aman **are** friends.

### AS WELL AS

> Rahul, **as well as** Aman, is coming.

The main subject is:

> Rahul

So the verb agrees with Rahul:

> Rahul ... **is**

This is an important subject-verb agreement trap.

---

# 41. AND Can Change Meaning

Normally:

> Bread **and** butter **are** on the table.

Two separate things.

But if two things are considered **one combined item/idea**, a singular verb can sometimes be used.

Example:

> Bread and butter **is** my usual breakfast.

The phrase is being treated as one meal.

This connects with the **subject-verb agreement** topic you studied earlier.

---

# 42. Conjunction vs Preposition

Sometimes the same word can have different grammatical roles.

For example:

### Before as preposition

> I arrived **before lunch**.

`lunch` is a noun.

### Before as conjunction

> I arrived **before he came**.

`he came` is a clause.

So:

> **Preposition + noun/pronoun/gerund**

> **Conjunction + clause**

This distinction can help in grammar questions.

---

# 43. Conjunction vs Adverb

Some words such as `however`, `therefore`, `moreover`, etc. can function as **conjunctive adverbs**, not ordinary coordinating conjunctions.

For example:

> He was tired; **however**, he continued working.

Don't automatically treat every word that connects ideas as a conjunction.

For your current level, the most important thing is to recognize the three main conjunction categories.

# 45. Common Conjunction Errors for Verbal Ability

These are worth memorizing.

### ❌ Although...but

> ❌ Although he is poor, but he is honest.

> ✅ Although he is poor, he is honest.

---

### ❌ Because...so

> ❌ Because he was sick, so he stayed home.

> ✅ Because he was sick, he stayed home.

OR:

> ✅ He was sick, so he stayed home.

---

### ❌ Unless...not

> ❌ Unless you don't study, you will fail.

> ✅ Unless you study, you will fail.

---

### ❌ No sooner...when

> ❌ No sooner did he arrive when it started raining.

> ✅ No sooner did he arrive **than** it started raining.

---

### ❌ Hardly...than

> ❌ Hardly had he arrived than it started raining.

> ✅ Hardly had he arrived **when** it started raining.

---

### ❌ Either...nor

> ❌ Either Rahul nor Aman will come.

> ✅ Either Rahul **or** Aman will come.

---

### ❌ Neither...or

> ❌ Neither Rahul or Aman came.

> ✅ Neither Rahul **nor** Aman came.

---

Memorize these as **fixed pairs**:

| First     | Second   |
| --------- | -------- |
| either    | or       |
| neither   | nor      |
| both      | and      |
| not only  | but also |
| whether   | or       |

| hardly    | when     |
| scarcely  | when     |
| rather    | than     |
| so        | that     |
| such      | that     |

If an exam gives you:

> Neither Rahul ___ Aman came.

Your brain should immediately say:

> **nor**

# 47. How to Solve Conjunction Questions

When you see a blank:

> He was tired ___ he continued working.

Don't immediately search your memory for a conjunction.

First identify the **relationship**.

Tired + continued working = **contrast**

Therefore:

> **but/yet**

---

Another:

> He was tired ___ he went to bed.

Relationship:

> tired → went to bed

This is **result**:

> **so**

---

Another:

> He went to bed ___ he was tired.

Now it's:

> reason

Therefore:

> **because**

This way, you can solve questions through **logic**, not memorization alone.

---

# 48. A Simple Mental Model

Whenever you see a conjunction, ask:

> **"What relationship exists between these two ideas?"**

Is it:

**Addition?**

> and

**Contrast?**

> but / although / yet

**Choice?**

> or

**Reason?**

> because

**Result?**

> so

**Condition?**

> if / unless

**Time?**

> when / before / after

**Purpose?**

> so that

**Two-part relationship?**

> either...or
> neither...nor
> both...and
> not only...but also

This makes conjunction questions much easier.

### The most important thing

Don't try to memorize conjunctions as a giant list.

For Verbal Ability, learn them in **three layers**:

**Layer 1:** What a conjunction does → **connects things**

**Layer 2:** What relationship it expresses → **addition / contrast / reason / result / condition / time / choice**

**Layer 3:** Exam rules → **although-but, because-so, unless-not, either-or, neither-nor, no sooner-than, hardly-when, parallelism, subject-verb agreement**

Once these three layers are clear, conjunction questions become much more logical instead of something you have to blindly memorize.
