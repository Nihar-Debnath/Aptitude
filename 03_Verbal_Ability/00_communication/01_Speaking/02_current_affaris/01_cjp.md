The **Cockroach Janta Party (CJP)** is a **recent youth-led protest movement in India**, not an established registered political party. It emerged after a Supreme Court judge **Surya Kant** reportedly referred to unemployed young people as "cockroaches" while discussing repeated exam-related litigation. Many students adopted the term ironically, turning it into a symbol of resistance. ([AP News][1])

(Surya Kant is an Indian jurist and the 53rd Chief Justice of India.)

### Why are they protesting?

The movement began over anger about:

* Repeated **entrance exam paper leaks** (including NEET and other competitive exams).
* Allegations of corruption and poor accountability in the education system.
* High youth unemployment.
* Demands for reforms in examination and recruitment processes. ([AP News][1])

### What are their main demands?

According to recent reports, protesters are calling for:

* The resignation of Education Minister **Dharmendra Pradhan**.
* Strong action against those responsible for paper leaks.
* Reforms to make exams fair and transparent.
* Compensation for families affected by alleged exam-related tragedies, including student suicides. ([The Guardian][2])

### What happened during the protests?

* Large demonstrations took place in **New Delhi** and several other cities.
* Police used **lathi charges (batons)** and **tear gas** after protesters attempted to march toward Parliament despite restrictions.
* Videos of the police action spread widely online, increasing public attention.
* Some opposition politicians expressed support for the protesters, while CJP leaders have said they want to keep the movement focused on student issues rather than party politics. ([The Times of India][3])

### Government response

Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** has said the government will pursue faster action against officials involved in exam corruption, including proposals for fast-track courts. However, protest leaders have said those announcements do not fully address their demands and have continued their demonstrations. ([The Guardian][2])
