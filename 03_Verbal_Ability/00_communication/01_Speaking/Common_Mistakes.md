This is actually one of the best questions you can ask. If you know **what to look for**, your practice becomes much more effective.

When you finish a speaking session, don't just ask, "Was it good?" Instead, review yourself against a checklist.

---

# 1. Fluency

**Question:** Did I speak continuously?

### Common Mistakes

* Long pauses
* Getting stuck searching for words
* Restarting sentences repeatedly
* Speaking one word at a time

❌ Example

> "I... um... think... communication... is... very... important..."

✅ Better

> "I think communication is important because it helps people understand each other."

---

# 2. Filler Words

Everyone uses fillers, but too many make you sound less confident.

Common fillers:

* Um
* Uh
* Like
* You know
* Actually
* Basically
* Literally
* Kind of
* Sort of

❌

> Actually... actually... basically... um...

Try replacing fillers with a brief pause instead.

---

# 3. Grammar

Notice patterns rather than worrying about every tiny mistake.

Common errors:

### Tense

❌

> Yesterday I go to college.

✅

> Yesterday I went to college.

---

### Subject–Verb Agreement

❌

> He go to school.

✅

> He goes to school.

---

### Articles

❌

> I bought book.

✅

> I bought a book.

---

### Prepositions

❌

> Discuss about

✅

> Discuss

---

# 4. Pronunciation

Watch for:

* Mispronouncing common words
* Stressing the wrong syllable
* Speaking too fast so words blur together

Examples:

* Comfortable
* Vegetable
* Develop
* Opportunity
* February

---

# 5. Vocabulary

### Repeating the same words

❌

> Good... good... good... good...

Instead:

* Excellent
* Beneficial
* Effective
* Valuable
* Useful

---

### Using simple verbs repeatedly

Instead of only:

* Do
* Get
* Make

Use:

* Achieve
* Develop
* Improve
* Create
* Solve

---

# 6. Sentence Variety

Many learners use only short, simple sentences.

Instead of:

> I like coding.
>
> It is fun.
>
> It is useful.

Try:

> I enjoy coding because it allows me to solve problems creatively and build useful applications.

---

# 7. Confidence

Observe:

* Is your voice too quiet?
* Do you sound unsure?
* Do you end every sentence like a question?
* Are you apologizing too much?

---

# 8. Pace

Too fast:

People can't follow.

Too slow:

People lose interest.

Aim for a steady, conversational speed.

---

# 9. Organization

Good speaking usually follows a clear flow.

Instead of random ideas:

> AI... college... friends... coding...

Use a simple structure:

1. Introduce the topic.
2. Explain your main idea.
3. Give an example.
4. Conclude.

---

# 10. Eye Contact (if speaking to people or recording video)

Avoid:

* Looking only at the floor.
* Looking away constantly.

Try to maintain natural eye contact or look at the camera if you're recording.

---

# 11. Body Language

Watch for:

* Slouching
* Folded arms
* Fidgeting
* Excessive hand movement
* No facial expression

---

# 12. Word Repetition

❌

> I think... I think... I think...

or

> So... so... so...

Try to vary your wording.

---

# 13. Clarity

Can someone understand your point after hearing you once?

If not:

* Use shorter sentences.
* Explain one idea at a time.
* Avoid adding unnecessary details.

---

# 14. Connecting Words

Many learners jump from one sentence to another.

Use connectors such as:

* First
* Secondly
* However
* Therefore
* For example
* In addition
* Finally
* Overall

These make your speech sound more organized.

---

# 15. Ending

Don't stop abruptly.

Instead of:

> Yeah... that's it.

Try:

> Overall, I believe communication is an essential skill because it helps us build relationships, express ideas clearly, and succeed both personally and professionally.

---

One more tip: since you're preparing for a **communication assessment**, don't try to fix all 15 areas at once. Pick **one or two focus areas per week**. For example:

* **Week 1:** Fluency + reducing filler words
* **Week 2:** Grammar + sentence structure
* **Week 3:** Vocabulary + connectors
* **Week 4:** Confidence + body language

This focused approach makes improvement much more noticeable than trying to monitor everything in every session.
