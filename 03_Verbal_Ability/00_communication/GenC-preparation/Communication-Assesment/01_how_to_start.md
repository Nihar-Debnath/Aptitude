The Communication Assessment is actually one of the easiest rounds to improve in because it mainly tests **how clearly and naturally you communicate**, not whether you speak like a native English speaker.

For **Cognizant GenC**, the communication assessment usually evaluates:

* Speaking fluency
* Pronunciation
* Grammar
* Vocabulary
* Listening skills
* Reading aloud
* Confidence while speaking

---

# Step 1: Understand the sections

Most communication assessments (Versant, Mercer Mettl, or similar) contain tasks like:

### 1. Read Aloud

A paragraph appears.

You read it aloud.

Example:

> "Technology has transformed the way people communicate around the world."

The system checks:

* Pronunciation
* Pace
* Clarity

---

### 2. Repeat Sentences

You'll hear:

> "The meeting has been postponed until tomorrow morning."

Then you repeat it exactly.

This tests:

* Listening
* Memory
* Pronunciation

---

### 3. Answer Short Questions

Example:

"What do you do in your free time?"

Speak for 20–40 seconds.

---

### 4. Describe an Image

They show a picture.

Example:
A park with children playing.

You might say:

> "I can see a park. There are several children playing football. Some people are walking on the pathway. The weather looks pleasant."

---

### 5. Opinion Questions

Example:

"Should students work while studying?"

You speak for around one minute.

---

### 6. Listen and Answer

You listen to a short conversation and answer questions about it.

---

# Step 2: Build the right skills

Instead of trying to improve "English" as a whole, focus on these five skills.

## Speaking

Every day for 15–20 minutes:

Pick any topic.

Examples:

* My hometown
* My college
* Artificial Intelligence
* Cricket
* Why I chose BCA
* My favorite movie

Speak continuously.

Don't stop.

Record yourself.

---

## Reading

Read English aloud.

Good sources:

* BBC News
* GeeksforGeeks articles
* Wikipedia
* Your DBMS notes
* English novels

Read for 10 minutes daily.

---

## Listening

Watch English videos.

Recommended:

* BBC Learning English
* TED Talks
* Tech conference talks
* Simple podcasts

Try understanding without subtitles first, then check with subtitles if needed.

---

## Grammar

Focus only on the basics:

* Tenses
* Subject-verb agreement
* Articles (a, an, the)
* Prepositions
* Common sentence structures

You don't need advanced grammar for GenC.

---

## Vocabulary

Learn **5–10 new words daily**.

More important than difficult words is knowing how to use common words naturally.

---

# Step 3: Improve pronunciation

Don't try to fake an American or British accent.

Aim for:

* Clear pronunciation
* Moderate speed
* Proper pauses

Good free resources:

* YouGlish
* Cambridge Dictionary pronunciation
* BBC Learning English pronunciation videos

---

# Step 4: Practice exactly like the test

Every day:

* Read one paragraph aloud.
* Answer five random questions.
* Describe one image.
* Speak for one minute on a topic.
* Repeat sentences from YouTube videos.

This closely matches the actual assessment.

# A 30-minute daily routine

* **10 minutes:** Read an English article aloud.
* **10 minutes:** Speak on 2–3 random topics and record yourself.
* **5 minutes:** Listen to English audio and repeat sentences.
* **5 minutes:** Learn a few new words and revise basic grammar.

If you do this consistently for **2–3 weeks**, you'll notice a clear improvement.
