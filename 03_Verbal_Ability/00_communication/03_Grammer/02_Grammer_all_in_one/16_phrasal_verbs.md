A **phrasal verb** is a combination of:

> **Verb + one or more particles** (usually an adverb or preposition)

Together, they form a **new meaning** that is often different from the original verb.

### 1. Basic idea

For example:

**give** = to hand something to someone
**give up** = to quit / stop trying

> I **gave** him the book.
> I **gave up** smoking.

Here, **give + up = give up**, and the meaning changes.

---

## 2. Common structure

| Structure                   | Example             | Meaning       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| Verb + adverb               | **give up**         | quit          |
| Verb + preposition          | **look after**      | take care of  |
| Verb + adverb + preposition | **look forward to** | await eagerly |

### Examples

**look + after → look after**

> She **looks after** her brother.
> = She takes care of her brother.

**turn + off → turn off**

> Please **turn off** the fan.
> = Stop the fan.

**run + out of → run out of**

> We **ran out of** water.
> = We have no water left.

---

# 3. Why are they called "phrasal" verbs?

Because the verb works together with another word to form a **phrase with a particular meaning**.

For example:

> **take** = move/carry something
> **take off** = leave the ground / remove something

So:

> The plane **took off**. ✈️
> **Take off** your shoes.

Same phrasal verb, but different meanings depending on context.

---

# 4. The most important thing: don't translate word-by-word

This is where phrasal verbs can be confusing.

For example:

> **give up**

If you translate literally:

**give = देना**
**up = ऊपर**

❌ "देना ऊपर"

But **give up** means:

> **quit / stop doing something**

Similarly:

**look after** ≠ look + after

It means:

> **take care of**

---

# 5. Phrasal verbs can be transitive or intransitive

### A. Transitive phrasal verb

It needs an object.

> Please **turn off** the light.

**turn off** = phrasal verb
**the light** = object

Another:

> I **picked up** the phone.

**picked up** = answered/lifted
**the phone** = object

---

### B. Intransitive phrasal verb

It doesn't need an object.

> The car **broke down**.

You don't need to say what it broke down.

**break down** = stop working

> My computer **broke down**.

---

# 6. Separable phrasal verbs

Some phrasal verbs can have their object **between** the verb and particle.

Example:

> **Turn off** the light.

You can also say:

> **Turn the light off.** ✅

So:

**turn off + object**

or

**turn + object + off**

### More examples

> Pick up the book.
> Pick the book up.

> Put on your shoes.
> Put your shoes on.

---

## ⚠️ But pronouns are different

With a pronoun, the object usually **must go between** the verb and particle.

> Turn **it** off. ✅
> Turn off **it**. ❌

> Pick **it** up. ✅
> Pick up **it**. ❌

This is an important exam rule.

---

# 7. Inseparable phrasal verbs

Some cannot be separated.

For example:

**look after**

> I **look after my sister**. ✅

You cannot say:

> I **look my sister after**. ❌

Other examples:

* look into = investigate
* look for = search
* look after = take care of
* depend on = rely on
* deal with = handle

---

# 8. Three-word phrasal verbs

Some contain **three words**:

> **look forward to**

Meaning = eagerly wait for something.

> I **look forward to** meeting you.

Another:

> **put up with**

Meaning = tolerate

> I can't **put up with** his behavior.

Another:

> **get rid of**

Meaning = remove/eliminate

> I want to **get rid of** these old clothes.

---

# 9. One verb can have MANY phrasal verbs

This is extremely common.

Take **get**:

| Phrasal verb   | Meaning                       |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| get up         | rise from bed                 |
| get in         | enter                         |
| get out        | leave                         |
| get over       | recover from                  |
| get along with | have a good relationship      |
| get rid of     | remove                        |
| get through    | successfully finish/deal with |
| get back       | return                        |

Example:

> I **get up** at 7 AM.
> I **get along with** my classmates.
> I finally **got over** my fear.

So don't assume that the meaning of the main verb remains the same.

## 11. Phrasal verb vs ordinary verb

Compare:

> **I postponed the meeting.**

and

> **I put off the meeting.**

Both mean approximately the same thing.

**postpone** = ordinary single-word verb
**put off** = phrasal verb

This is why phrasal verbs are so common in **spoken and informal English**.

---

# 12. Very important for exams

When you see something like:

> The manager **called off** the meeting.

Don't interpret **called** separately.

The complete unit is:

> **call off = cancel**

Therefore:

> The manager called off the meeting.
> = The manager **cancelled** the meeting.

### Think of phrasal verbs as vocabulary units:

**give up → quit**
**put off → postpone**
**call off → cancel**
**find out → discover**
**look after → take care of**
**carry on → continue**

That's the easiest way to learn them.

### In one line:

> **Phrasal verb = Verb + particle(s) → a new meaning**

**go + on → continue**
**give + up → quit**
**look + after → take care of**
**put + off → postpone**
