Absolutely. Since you're learning English grammar systematically, let's do **conjunctions from the basics to exam-level rules**, including the types, pairs, rules, common mistakes, and how to identify them.

# CONJUNCTIONS — COMPLETE EXPLANATION

## 1. What is a Conjunction?

A **conjunction** is a word that **connects** words, phrases, or clauses.

Think:

> **Conjunction = Connector**

### Examples

**Connecting words:**

* Ram **and** Shyam
* Tea **or** coffee
* Poor **but** honest

**Connecting phrases:**

* in the morning **and** in the evening

**Connecting clauses:**

* I stayed home **because** it was raining.
* I studied hard **so** I passed.

---

# 2. What exactly can a conjunction connect?

A conjunction can connect:

### A. Word + Word

> Ram **and** Shyam are friends.

`Ram` + `Shyam`

### B. Phrase + Phrase

> He likes **playing cricket** and **watching football**.

### C. Clause + Clause

> I went home **because I was tired**.

Here:

* `I went home` → clause 1
* `I was tired` → clause 2
* `because` → conjunction

---

# 3. First understand: What is a Clause?

This is extremely important for conjunctions.

A **clause** generally contains a **subject + verb**.

### Example

> I studied.

* Subject = I
* Verb = studied

So this is a clause.

> because I studied

This is also a clause because it contains:

* Subject = I
* Verb = studied

---

## Two major types of clauses

### Independent Clause

Can stand alone as a complete sentence.

> I went home.

Complete meaning.

### Dependent Clause

Cannot normally stand alone.

> because I was tired

You would not normally say:

> ❌ Because I was tired.

It leaves us waiting for the main idea.

So:

> I went home **because I was tired**.

The conjunction **because** connects the independent clause with the dependent clause.

---

# 4. Three Main Types of Conjunctions

For exams, remember these three:

| Type              | Main purpose                          | Examples                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Coordinating**  | Joins equal elements                  | and, but, or, so            |
| **Subordinating** | Joins dependent clause to main clause | because, although, if, when |
| **Correlative**   | Works in pairs                        | either...or, neither...nor  |

Let's understand each properly.

---

# 5. Coordinating Conjunctions

These connect **equal grammatical elements**.

The most important coordinating conjunctions are remembered using:

## FANBOYS

| Letter | Conjunction | Meaning/use          |
| ------ | ----------- | -------------------- |
| F      | **For**     | reason               |
| A      | **And**     | addition             |
| N      | **Nor**     | negative alternative |
| B      | **But**     | contrast             |
| O      | **Or**      | choice               |
| Y      | **Yet**     | contrast             |
| S      | **So**      | result               |

### Example

> I was tired, **but** I continued studying.

Both sides are independent clauses:

> I was tired.
> I continued studying.

`but` connects them.

---

# 6. AND

**And = addition**

It adds information.

> Ram **and** Shyam are friends.

> She bought apples **and** oranges.

> He studied hard **and** passed the exam.

### Common mistake

Don't use **and** when the relationship is clearly cause/effect.

❌ He was sick **and** he stayed home.

This isn't necessarily grammatically wrong, but it doesn't clearly express the reason.

Better:

> He was sick, **so** he stayed home.

---

# 7. BUT

**But = contrast**

> He is poor **but** honest.

> She is young **but** intelligent.

> I wanted to go, **but** I was busy.

Think:

> A happened, **BUT** something contrasting happened.

---

# 8. OR

**Or = choice / alternative**

> Do you want tea **or** coffee?

> You can walk **or** take a bus.

It can also indicate a consequence/warning:

> Hurry up, **or** you will miss the train.

Meaning:

> If you don't hurry, you will miss the train.

---

# 9. SO

**So = result**

> It was raining, **so** I stayed home.

Relationship:

**Cause → Result**

> It was raining → I stayed home.

Therefore:

> It was raining, **so** I stayed home.

---

# 10. FOR

`For` can be a coordinating conjunction meaning **because**.

> I stayed home, **for** I was sick.

However, this usage is relatively formal/literary.

In normal modern English:

> I stayed home **because** I was sick.

is much more natural.

### Important

`for` can also be a **preposition**:

> This gift is **for** you.

Here it is NOT a conjunction.

---

# 11. NOR

**Nor** adds another negative alternative.

> He doesn't smoke, **nor** does he drink.

Notice the unusual word order:

> nor **does he drink**

Not:

> ❌ nor he does drink

We'll return to this important rule later.

---

# 12. YET

**Yet = but / nevertheless**

> He is poor, **yet** he is happy.

> She studied hard, **yet** she failed.

It expresses contrast.

Compare:

> He is poor **but** happy.

> He is poor **yet** happy.

Both express contrast.

---

# 13. Coordinating Conjunction Summary

| Conjunction | Main idea            | Example                              |
| ----------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **and**     | addition             | Ram and Sam came.                    |
| **but**     | contrast             | He is poor but honest.               |
| **or**      | choice               | Tea or coffee?                       |
| **nor**     | negative alternative | He doesn't smoke, nor does he drink. |
| **for**     | reason               | I left, for I was tired.             |
| **so**      | result               | It rained, so we stayed home.        |
| **yet**     | contrast             | He is weak, yet brave.               |

# 14. Subordinating Conjunctions

These are extremely important for verbal ability.

A **subordinating conjunction** connects:

> **Independent clause + dependent clause**

Examples:

* because
* although
* though
* if
* unless
* until
* while
* when
* whenever
* before
* after
* since
* as
* whereas
* where
* wherever
* whether

---

# 15. BECAUSE

**Because = reason/cause**

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

Structure:

> Result + because + Cause

### Example

> He failed **because he didn't study**.

Cause:

> he didn't study

Result:

> he failed

---

# 16. SO vs BECAUSE

This is very important.

### Because → gives the cause

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

### So → gives the result

> I was sick, **so** I stayed home.

Think:

**BECAUSE → WHY?**

**SO → WHAT HAPPENED AS A RESULT?**

---

# 17. Although / Though

Both show **contrast**.

> **Although** he was tired, he continued working.

> He continued working **although** he was tired.

### Important exam rule

Don't normally use **although + but** together.

❌ Although he was tired, **but** he continued working.

Correct:

> Although he was tired, he continued working.

OR:

> He was tired, **but** he continued working.

### Same idea

| Word     | Function |
| -------- | -------- |
| although | contrast |
| though   | contrast |
| but      | contrast |

---

# 18. Though vs Although

They are very similar.

> **Although** he was poor, he was happy.

> **Though** he was poor, he was happy.

`Although` is slightly more formal.

`Though` can also appear at the end of a sentence:

> I didn't like the movie. It was interesting, **though**.

You generally wouldn't use `although` this way.

---

# 19. EVEN THOUGH

**Even though = strong contrast**

> Even though he was extremely tired, he continued working.

Compare:

> Although he was tired...

versus:

> Even though he was extremely tired...

`Even though` emphasizes the unexpected contrast.

---

# 20. IF

**If = condition**

> **If** you study, you will pass.

Condition:

> you study

Result:

> you will pass

Basic structure:

> If + condition → result

---

# 21. UNLESS

**Unless = if not**

This is one of the most important exam concepts.

> You will fail **unless you study**.

Means:

> You will fail **if you do not study**.

So:

> **unless = if...not**

### Example

> Unless you hurry, you will miss the train.

=

> If you don't hurry, you will miss the train.

---

## Don't use extra NOT after unless

❌ Unless you **don't** study, you will fail.

This changes the meaning and is generally incorrect for the intended condition.

Correct:

> Unless you study, you will fail.

---

# 22. WHEN

**When = time**

> Call me **when you arrive**.

> I was sleeping **when he called**.

It tells us **at what time** something happens.

---

# 23. WHILE

**While = during the time that**

> I listened to music **while I was studying**.

It can also show contrast:

> **While** I like tea, my brother likes coffee.

Here `while` means something closer to **whereas**.

So context matters.

---

# 24. BEFORE

Shows an earlier event.

> Wash your hands **before you eat**.

Order:

**Wash hands → eat**

---

# 25. AFTER

Shows a later event.

> We went home **after the movie ended**.

Order:

**Movie ended → went home**

---

# 26. UNTIL / TILL

Shows a time limit.

> Wait here **until I return**.

Meaning:

> Continue waiting up to the time I return.

`Till` and `until` are usually interchangeable.

---

# 27. SINCE

`Since` can indicate **time** or **reason**.

### Time

> I have lived here **since 2020**.

### Reason

> **Since** you are here, let's start.

Here:

> Since = because

This is a common source of confusion.

---

# 28. AS

`As` has several meanings.

### Reason

> **As** it was raining, we stayed home.

= because

### Time

> **As** I was leaving, he arrived.

= while/when

### Manner

> Do it **as** I showed you.

= in the way that

So you must understand the context.

---

# 29. WHERE / WHEREVER

### Where

> Stay **where I told you**.

### Wherever

> I will follow you **wherever you go**.

`wherever` means **any place that / no matter where**.

---

# 30. WHETHER

**Whether = expresses alternatives/uncertainty**

> I don't know **whether he will come**.

Often:

> whether...or

> I don't know **whether he will come or not**.

> Whether you win **or** lose, keep trying.

---

# 31. Correlative Conjunctions

These conjunctions work **in pairs**.

Think:

> **Correlative = couple/pair**

The most important ones:

| Pair                    | Meaning                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **either...or**         | one of two choices        |
| **neither...nor**       | not one and not the other |
| **both...and**          | two things together       |
| **not only...but also** | addition/emphasis         |
| **whether...or**        | alternatives              |
| **as...as**             | equal comparison          |
| **so...that**           | result                    |
| **such...that**         | result                    |
| **no sooner...than**    | immediate sequence        |
| **hardly...when**       | immediate sequence        |
| **scarcely...when**     | immediate sequence        |

---

# 32. EITHER...OR

Means **one or the other**.

> You can **either** study **or** sleep.

> **Either** Ram **or** Shyam will come.

### Important Subject-Verb Agreement Rule

With:

> either A or B

the verb generally agrees with the **nearer subject**.

### Example

> Either Ram or his friends **are** coming.

Nearest subject:

> friends → plural → **are**

But:

> Either his friends or Ram **is** coming.

Nearest subject:

> Ram → singular → **is**

This is a VERY important exam rule.

---

# 33. NEITHER...NOR

Means:

> not A + not B

> **Neither** Ram **nor** Shyam came.

Neither = not one
Nor = not the other

### Subject-Verb Agreement

Again, the verb generally agrees with the **nearer subject**.

> Neither Ram nor his friends **are** coming.

`friends` → plural → **are**

> Neither his friends nor Ram **is** coming.

`Ram` → singular → **is**

---

# 34. BOTH...AND

Means **A + B together**.

> **Both** Ram **and** Shyam are students.

> She is **both** intelligent **and** hardworking.

### Important

Because `both...and` refers to two subjects, use a **plural verb**.

> Both Ram and Shyam **are** students.

Not:

> ❌ Both Ram and Shyam **is** students.

---

# 35. NOT ONLY...BUT ALSO

Adds emphasis.

> He is **not only** intelligent **but also** hardworking.

Meaning:

> He is intelligent + hardworking.

### Important rule: Parallelism

The grammatical structure after both parts should match.

Good:

> She is **not only intelligent but also hardworking**.

Both are adjectives:

* intelligent
* hardworking

Good:

> He **not only studied but also worked**.

Both are verbs:

* studied
* worked

Bad:

> ❌ He not only studied but also was hardworking.

The structures don't match well.

---

# 36. Parallelism

This is extremely important in grammar questions.

When using paired conjunctions, keep the structures parallel.

### Correct

> She likes **singing and dancing**.

Both = `-ing` forms.

### Incorrect

> ❌ She likes **singing and to dance**.

Better:

> She likes **singing and dancing**.

---

### Another example

Correct:

> He wants **to study and to work**.

Also acceptable:

> He wants **to study and work**.

Both structures are parallel.

---

# 37. WHETHER...OR

> **Whether** you stay **or** leave, I will support you.

It presents alternatives.

> I don't know whether he will come or not.

---

# 38. AS...AS

Used for comparison.

> He is **as tall as** his brother.

> She is **as intelligent as** her sister.

Negative:

> He is **not as tall as** his brother.

---

# 39. SO...THAT

Shows result.

> He was **so tired that** he fell asleep.

Structure:

> so + adjective/adverb + that

Examples:

> She was **so beautiful that** everyone noticed her.

> He ran **so quickly that** nobody could catch him.

---

# 40. SUCH...THAT

Also shows result.

> It was **such a difficult problem that** nobody solved it.

Compare:

### SO

> so + adjective + that

> It was **so difficult that** nobody solved it.

### SUCH

> such + noun phrase + that

> It was **such a difficult problem that** nobody solved it.

---

# 41. NO SOONER...THAN

Shows that one event happened immediately after another.

> **No sooner had** I reached home **than** it started raining.

Important pair:

> **No sooner → than**

NOT:

> ❌ No sooner...when

---

# 42. HARDLY...WHEN

> **Hardly had** I reached home **when** it started raining.

Pair:

> **Hardly → when**

---

# 43. SCARCELY...WHEN

> **Scarcely had** I reached home **when** it started raining.

Pair:

> **Scarcely → when**

---

# 45. Conjunctions for Cause and Effect

Since you were also studying cause & effect, connect these concepts.

### Cause conjunctions

| Conjunction | Example                            |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------- |
| because     | I left because I was tired.        |
| since       | Since it was late, we left.        |
| as          | As it was raining, we stayed home. |
| for         | I left, for I was tired.           |

### Result conjunctions

| Conjunction | Example                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| so          | It rained, so we stayed home.         |
| therefore*  | It rained; therefore, we stayed home. |
| hence*      | It rained; hence, we stayed home.     |

`Therefore` and `hence` are generally treated as **conjunctive adverbs/adverbs**, not ordinary coordinating conjunctions.

That's an important distinction.

> **If = condition**

# 49. Common Conjunction Mistakes

These are especially useful for competitive exams.

## Mistake 1: Although + But

❌ Although he was tired, **but** he continued.

✅ Although he was tired, he continued.

OR

✅ He was tired, **but** he continued.

---

## Mistake 2: Because + So

❌ Because he was sick, **so** he stayed home.

Usually incorrect because both are performing the same cause-result connection.

✅ Because he was sick, he stayed home.

OR

✅ He was sick, so he stayed home.

---

# 50. No sooner + when

❌ No sooner had he arrived **when** the meeting started.

✅ No sooner had he arrived **than** the meeting started.

Remember:

> **NO SOONER → THAN**

---

# 51. Hardly + than

❌ Hardly had he arrived **than** the meeting started.

✅ Hardly had he arrived **when** the meeting started.

Remember:

> **HARDLY → WHEN**

---

# 52. Neither + or

❌ Neither Ram **or** Shyam came.

✅ Neither Ram **nor** Shyam came.

Remember:

> **NEITHER → NOR**

---

# 53. Either + nor

❌ Either Ram **nor** Shyam will come.

✅ Either Ram **or** Shyam will come.

Remember:

> **EITHER → OR**

---

# 54. Both + or

❌ Both Ram **or** Shyam came.

✅ Both Ram **and** Shyam came.

Remember:

> **BOTH → AND**

---

# 55. Unless + negative

❌ Unless you don't work hard, you will fail.

For the intended meaning:

✅ Unless you work hard, you will fail.

Because:

> unless = if not

---

# 56. Conjunction vs Preposition

Some words can perform different grammatical jobs.

For example:

### Before as conjunction

> I ate **before I left**.

`before` connects two clauses.

### Before as preposition

> I ate **before dinner**.

`before` is followed by a noun phrase.

---

### After as conjunction

> I went home **after the movie ended**.

### After as preposition

> I went home **after the movie**.

So don't identify a word as a conjunction just because it is commonly used as one.

Look at **what comes after it and what job it performs**.

---

# 57. Conjunction vs Preposition vs Adverb

This is slightly advanced but useful.

Consider **before**:

### Preposition

> I arrived **before lunch**.

`before` + noun

### Conjunction

> I arrived **before the meeting started**.

`before` + clause

### Adverb

> I had seen him **before**.

Here `before` stands by itself.

Same word, different grammatical function.

---

# 58. Coordinating vs Subordinating — Main Difference

This is probably the most important conceptual distinction.

### Coordinating

Connects **equal elements**.

> Ram **and** Shyam

Both are nouns.

> I was tired, **but** I worked.

Both clauses can stand independently.

---

### Subordinating

Makes one clause dependent on another.

> I worked **although I was tired**.

`I worked` → independent

`although I was tired` → dependent

# 60. A Very Important Rule: Commas

When two independent clauses are joined by a coordinating conjunction, a comma is commonly used.

> I was tired, **but** I continued working.

> It was raining, **so** we stayed home.

But when the conjunction connects short elements, you don't necessarily need a comma.

> Ram and Shyam went home.

Not:

> ❌ Ram, and Shyam went home.

---

# 61. Subordinating Conjunction at the Beginning

When the dependent clause comes first, use a comma.

> **Because I was tired,** I went home.

> **Although he was poor,** he was happy.

> **If you study,** you will pass.

When the dependent clause comes second, a comma is often unnecessary.

> I went home **because I was tired**.

> He was happy **although he was poor**.

# 62. Conjunctions and Subject-Verb Agreement

This is an important exam area.

### AND → generally plural

> Ram and Shyam **are** friends.

### OR / NOR → nearer subject usually determines the verb

> Either Ram or his friends **are** coming.

> Either his friends or Ram **is** coming.

### BOTH...AND → plural

> Both Ram and Shyam **are** here.

# 63. Conjunctions and Tenses

Conjunctions can affect tense patterns.

For example, in future conditions:

> **If you study, you will pass.**

Not usually:

> ❌ If you will study, you will pass.

For a normal future condition:

> **if + present simple → will + base verb**

Examples:

> If it rains, we will stay home.

> If he comes, I will talk to him.

---

# 64. Unless + Future

Same idea:

> Unless you study, you will fail.

Not:

> ❌ Unless you will study, you will fail.

---

# 65. Time Conjunctions + Future

This is another common exam rule.

After:

* when
* before
* after
* until
* as soon as
* once

we commonly use the **present tense** for a future meaning.

### Correct

> When he **comes**, I will talk to him.

Not:

> ❌ When he **will come**, I will talk to him.

### Correct

> I will call you when I **reach** home.

Not:

> ❌ when I **will reach** home.

Think:

> **Time conjunction + present → future main clause**

---

# 66. AS SOON AS

Means immediately after.

> **As soon as** he arrives, call me.

Future meaning:

> As soon as he **arrives**, I **will call** you.

Not:

> ❌ As soon as he will arrive...

---

# 67. ONCE

Means after something happens / when something has happened.

> **Once** you finish your work, you can leave.

> Once he arrives, we will start.

# 69. How to Identify a Conjunction in an Exam

Use this simple method.

### Step 1: Find the connecting word

Example:

> I stayed home **because** I was sick.

### Step 2: Look at what it connects

`I stayed home` + `I was sick`

Two clauses.

### Step 3: Ask what relationship it expresses

Why did I stay home?

→ because I was sick.

Therefore:

> `because` = subordinating conjunction expressing cause.
