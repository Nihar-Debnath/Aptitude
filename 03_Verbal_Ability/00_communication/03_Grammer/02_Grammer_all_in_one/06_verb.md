# 🔥 VERB — Complete Concept

## 1. What is a Verb?

The simplest definition:

> **A verb is a word that tells us about an action, state, or occurrence.**

Examples:

* Ram **runs**.
* She **eats** rice.
* I **know** the answer.
* He **is** happy.
* They **have** a car.
* The glass **broke**.

Notice something important:

A verb does **not always mean physical action**.

### Action

> He **runs**.

### Mental action

> I **know** the answer.

### State

> She **is** happy.

### Possession

> He **has** a car.

So don't memorize:

> "Verb = action word"

That's useful for beginners, but **incomplete**.

A better mental model is:

> **Verb = the word that tells us what the subject does, is, has, experiences, or what happens to it.**

---

# 2. Why are Verbs So Important?

A sentence normally needs a **finite verb** to express a complete thought.

Compare:

> Ram to school.

❌ Not a complete sentence.

> Ram **goes** to school.

✅ Complete.

The verb tells us what is happening.

And verbs carry a HUGE amount of grammatical information:

* **time** → tense
* **person** → I/you/he
* **number** → singular/plural
* **voice** → active/passive
* **mood** → statement, command, condition, etc.
* **possibility/necessity** → can, may, must, should
* **completion/continuity** → has eaten, is eating, etc.

That's why verbs are one of the most important parts of English grammar.

---

# 3. Main Types of Verbs

There are several different ways we classify verbs.

Don't mix them together.

A verb can belong to **multiple categories at the same time**.

For example:

> She **is reading** a book.

"reading" is:

* a main verb
* an action verb
* a transitive verb

And "is" is:

* an auxiliary/helping verb

So classifications can overlap.

The major categories you need are:

1. Main verb
2. Helping/Auxiliary verb
3. Linking verb
4. Transitive verb
5. Intransitive verb
6. Regular verb
7. Irregular verb
8. Finite verb
9. Non-finite verb
10. Modal verb
11. Phrasal verb

Let's go one by one.

---

# 4. Main Verb

The **main verb** is the verb that carries the main meaning of the sentence.

Example:

> Rahul **plays** cricket.

Main verb = **plays**

> She **is reading**.

Here:

* is → helping verb
* reading → main verb

> They **have finished** the work.

* have → helping verb
* finished → main verb

So:

> **Main verb = the main action/state/meaning of the verb phrase.**

---

# 5. Helping Verb / Auxiliary Verb

An auxiliary verb helps the main verb.

The three primary auxiliary verbs are:

### BE

* am
* is
* are
* was
* were
* be
* being
* been

### HAVE

* have
* has
* had

### DO

* do
* does
* did

Examples:

> She **is** working.

> They **have** finished.

> He **does** not know.

The helping verb can tell us things such as:

* tense
* continuous action
* completion
* questions
* negation
* emphasis

---

# 6. Modal Auxiliary Verbs

These are a special group of helping verbs.

Important modals:

* can
* could
* may
* might
* shall
* should
* will
* would
* must

They express ideas like:

### Ability

> I **can** swim.

### Permission

> **May** I come in?

### Possibility

> It **might** rain.

### Necessity

> You **must** study.

### Advice

> You **should** exercise.

### Future

> I **will** call you.

Very important rule:

> **Modal + base form of verb**

Correct:

> He can **swim**.

Incorrect:

> He can **swims**. ❌

Correct:

> She should **go**.

Incorrect:

> She should **goes**. ❌

This becomes extremely important in sentence correction.

---

# 7. Linking Verb

This is a very important concept.

A linking verb does **not describe an action**.

Instead, it **connects the subject to information about the subject**.

Example:

> Rahul **is** intelligent.

"Is" connects:

**Rahul → intelligent**

Another:

> She **became** angry.

**She → angry**

Another:

> The food **looks** delicious.

**food → delicious**

Common linking verbs include:

* be
* seem
* become
* appear
* look
* feel
* smell
* taste
* sound
* remain
* stay
* grow
* turn

Example:

> The soup **tastes** good.

Here "tastes" is linking the soup to "good".

But be careful.

The same verb can sometimes be an action verb.

### Linking

> The soup **tastes** delicious.

### Action

> He **tasted** the soup.

In the second sentence, "tasted" means **performed an action**.

---

# 8. Transitive Verb

Now we come to one of the most important concepts for exams.

A **transitive verb** requires/typically takes a **direct object**.

Example:

> She **bought** a book.

Ask:

> Bought what?

Answer:

> a book

Therefore:

**bought = transitive verb**

Another:

> He **kicked** the ball.

Kicked what?

→ the ball.

So:

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

Example:

> Rahul | kicked | the ball.

---

# 9. Intransitive Verb

An intransitive verb does **not take a direct object**.

Example:

> He **slept**.

You cannot say:

> He slept what? ❌

There's no direct object.

Another:

> The baby **cried**.

Another:

> Birds **fly**.

So:

> **Intransitive verb = verb that does not take a direct object.**

---

# 10. The Same Verb Can Be Transitive OR Intransitive

This is a common exam trap.

Example:

> The boy **runs**.

No object → intransitive.

> The boy **runs** a company.

Here "runs" has an object:

**a company**

→ transitive.

Another:

> She **opened** the door.

Transitive.

> The door **opened**.

Intransitive.

So don't memorize:

> "open is transitive"

Instead ask:

> **Is there a direct object in this particular sentence?**

---

# 11. Direct Object

Since transitive verbs depend on objects, understand this properly.

A direct object receives the action of the verb.

> Ram kicked **the ball**.

Who kicked?

→ Ram

Kicked what?

→ the ball

Therefore:

* Ram = subject
* kicked = verb
* ball = direct object

Another:

> She reads **books**.

Reads what?

→ books.

---

# 12. Indirect Object

Sometimes a sentence has two objects.

> I gave **Rahul** a book.

There are two objects:

* Rahul → indirect object
* book → direct object

Think:

> I gave **what?** → a book

> I gave a book **to whom?** → Rahul

So:

**Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object**

---

# 13. Regular and Irregular Verbs

This classification is based on how verbs form their past tense.

## Regular verbs

Usually add **-ed**.

| Base  | Past    |
| ----- | ------- |
| walk  | walked  |
| play  | played  |
| work  | worked  |
| clean | cleaned |

## Irregular verbs

Don't follow the normal -ed pattern.

| Base  | Past  |
| ----- | ----- |
| go    | went  |
| eat   | ate   |
| see   | saw   |
| come  | came  |
| write | wrote |
| take  | took  |

You need to learn irregular verbs gradually because they are extremely common.

---

# 14. The Different Forms of a Verb

This is VERY important.

Take the verb:

> **write**

It has several forms.

| Form    | Name   | Example        |
| ------- | ------ | -------------- |
| write   | V1     | I write        |
| wrote   | V2     | I wrote        |
| written | V3     | I have written |
| writing | V-ing  | I am writing   |
| writes  | V-s/es | He writes      |

You'll often hear:

> V1, V2, V3, V4, V5

These refer to these forms.

### V1

Base form:

> go

### V2

Past:

> went

### V3

Past participle:

> gone

### V4

-ing form:

> going

### V5

-s/es form:

> goes

These labels are extremely common in Indian competitive-exam preparation.

---

# 15. V1, V2, V3 — Understand Their Actual Use

Don't just memorize the table.

### V1

Used in places like:

> I **go**.

> They **go**.

> He can **go**.

### V2

Simple past:

> I **went** yesterday.

### V3

Usually works with auxiliary verbs.

> I have **gone**.

> She has **written**.

> The work was **completed**.

### V-ing

Continuous structures:

> I am **reading**.

> They were **playing**.

### V5

Simple present with third-person singular:

> He **plays**.

> She **writes**.

> Rahul **goes**.

---

# 16. Finite Verb

Now we get into a more advanced but VERY important concept.

A **finite verb** is a verb that is connected to:

* tense
* subject/person
* number

Example:

> He **plays** cricket.

"plays" is finite.

Why?

Because it changes according to the subject:

> He **plays**.

> They **play**.

And it can show tense:

> He **played**.

---

# 17. Non-Finite Verb

A non-finite verb does not itself show tense/person/number.

Three major types:

1. Infinitive
2. Gerund
3. Participle

This is one of the most important areas of verb grammar.

---

# 18. Infinitive

Usually:

> **to + V1**

Examples:

* to eat
* to go
* to study
* to play

Example:

> I want **to study**.

"to study" doesn't itself show tense.

You don't say:

> to studied ❌

or

> to studies ❌

It's:

> to study ✅

### But there is also a bare infinitive

Sometimes we use V1 without "to".

Example:

> I can **swim**.

> Let him **go**.

> Make him **study**.

Here:

**swim, go, study** = bare infinitives.

---

# 19. Gerund

A gerund is an **-ing form functioning as a noun**.

Example:

> **Swimming** is good exercise.

What is the subject?

→ Swimming.

Therefore "swimming" is functioning as a noun.

Another:

> I enjoy **reading**.

"Reading" is the object of "enjoy".

So:

> **Gerund = verb + ing functioning as a noun.**

This distinction is extremely important.

---

# 20. Present Participle

The same **-ing form** can function as a participle.

Example:

> The **crying** baby needs milk.

"Crying" describes the baby.

It's functioning like an adjective.

Therefore:

**crying = present participle**

Compare:

> **Swimming** is fun.

→ noun → gerund

> The **swimming** boy is my brother.

→ describing boy → participle

Same "-ing", different job.

This is why you shouldn't simply say:

> "-ing = gerund"

❌ Wrong.

---

# 21. Past Participle

Usually V3.

Examples:

* eaten
* written
* broken
* gone
* finished
* taken

It can be used in:

### Perfect tense

> I have **finished**.

### Passive voice

> The work was **finished**.

### Adjective-like use

> a **broken** window

---

# 22. Verb Phrase

Sometimes several words work together as the verb.

Example:

> She **is reading**.

The complete verb phrase is:

**is reading**

Another:

> He **has been working**.

Verb phrase:

**has been working**

Break it:

* has → auxiliary
* been → auxiliary
* working → main verb

Another:

> They **will have completed** the work.

All of:

**will have completed**

forms the verb phrase.

---

# 23. Tense and Verbs

Verbs are heavily connected with tense.

There are traditionally **12 tenses**.

### Present

1. Simple Present
2. Present Continuous
3. Present Perfect
4. Present Perfect Continuous

### Past

5. Simple Past
6. Past Continuous
7. Past Perfect
8. Past Perfect Continuous

### Future

9. Simple Future
10. Future Continuous
11. Future Perfect
12. Future Perfect Continuous

For example:

### Simple Present

> I **write**.

### Present Continuous

> I **am writing**.

### Present Perfect

> I **have written**.

### Present Perfect Continuous

> I **have been writing**.

The verb forms and auxiliary verbs change.

---

# 24. Subject-Verb Agreement

This is another huge area.

The verb must agree with the subject in number/person.

### Singular

> He **plays**.

### Plural

> They **play**.

Notice:

> He play ❌

> He plays ✅

But:

> They plays ❌

> They play ✅

This becomes a major topic in **sentence correction**.

---

# 25. Verb + s Is Actually a Common Trap

Many students think:

> Singular → add s
> Plural → don't add s

That's not exactly the correct mental model.

In the simple present:

### Third-person singular

* he
* she
* it
* Rahul
* the boy

→ usually V5

> He **runs**.

### I/you/we/they

→ V1

> They **run**.

So:

> He runs.
> They run.

---

# 26. Do/Does/Did

These are auxiliary verbs, but they can also be main verbs.

### Helping verb

> Do you **know** him?

Here:

* do → auxiliary
* know → main verb

> He **doesn't like** tea.

* does → auxiliary
* like → main verb

Notice:

> He doesn't **likes** tea. ❌

Why?

Because after **does**, we use V1:

> He doesn't **like** tea. ✅

Similarly:

> Did you **go** there?

Not:

> Did you **went** there? ❌

Because:

> did + V1

---

# 27. Have/Has/Had

They can also have two jobs.

### Auxiliary

> I **have finished** my work.

Here "finished" = main verb.

### Main verb

> I **have** a car.

Here "have" itself carries the main meaning.

Same with:

> She **has** a beautiful house.

---

# 28. Be Verb

This is one of the most important verbs in English.

Forms:

**Present**

* am
* is
* are

**Past**

* was
* were

Other forms:

* be
* being
* been

It can act as:

### Linking verb

> He **is** happy.

### Auxiliary

> He **is working**.

### Passive auxiliary

> The work **was completed**.

So don't assume:

> "is = linking verb"

Not always.

---

# 29. Action Verb

An action verb expresses an action.

Examples:

* run
* jump
* eat
* write
* speak
* study
* open
* kick

But remember:

Action doesn't necessarily mean physical movement.

> I **think** about my future.

"think" expresses a mental action.

---

# 30. Stative Verbs

These express a **state rather than an action**.

Examples often include:

* know
* believe
* understand
* love
* hate
* want
* need
* remember
* own
* belong
* seem

For example:

> I **know** the answer.

Usually we don't say:

> I am knowing the answer. ❌

Instead:

> I know the answer. ✅

This becomes important when learning continuous tenses.

---

# 31. Dynamic vs Stative

Compare:

> I **think** he is right.

Here "think" = opinion/state.

But:

> I **am thinking** about the problem.

Here "thinking" = active mental process.

So even stative-looking verbs can sometimes become dynamic depending on meaning.

---

# 32. Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb consists of:

> **Verb + particle**

Examples:

> give **up**

> look **after**

> turn **off**

> find **out**

Their meaning can be different from the original verb.

Example:

> **give up** = quit

> He **gave up** smoking.

"give" alone doesn't mean quit.

Another:

> **look after** = take care of

> She **looks after** her brother.

Phrasal verbs are VERY important for vocabulary and verbal ability.

---

# 33. One Verb Can Have Many Forms and Jobs

Take:

> **break**

Forms:

* break
* broke
* broken
* breaking
* breaks

Examples:

> I **break** things.

> I **broke** the glass.

> I have **broken** the glass.

> I am **breaking** the glass.

> He **breaks** the glass.

Same verb, different grammatical forms.

---

# 34. The Most Important Mental Model

When you see a verb, don't immediately ask:

> "Which type of verb is this?"

Instead ask these questions **in order**:

### Question 1: What is the verb?

> She **writes** a letter.

Verb = writes.

### Question 2: Is it the main verb or helping verb?

Writes = main verb.

### Question 3: Does it have an object?

Writes what?

→ a letter.

So it's transitive.

### Question 4: What form is it?

writes = V5.

### Question 5: Is it finite or non-finite?

"writes" changes according to subject/tense.

→ finite.

### Question 6: What tense?

> She writes.

→ simple present.

This method makes verb questions MUCH easier.

---

# 35. Let's Analyze Some Sentences

### Example 1

> Rahul plays cricket.

**Rahul** = subject
**plays** = main verb
**cricket** = object

"plays":

* action verb
* transitive
* finite
* V5
* simple present

---

### Example 2

> Rahul is playing cricket.

**is** = auxiliary
**playing** = main verb

"playing":

* action verb
* transitive
* V-ing
* non-finite

"is" is finite because it carries tense/agreement.

The entire verb phrase:

> **is playing**

---

### Example 3

> Swimming is good exercise.

"Swimming":

* verb-derived form
* V-ing
* gerund
* functioning as noun

"Swimming" is the subject.

---

### Example 4

> The swimming boy is my brother.

"swimming":

* V-ing
* present participle
* describing "boy"

"is":

* linking verb
* finite

---

### Example 5

> He can solve the problem.

"can":

* modal auxiliary

"solve":

* main verb
* V1
* bare infinitive

"problem":

* object

Important:

> can + V1

---

### Example 6

> She has completed the work.

"has":

* auxiliary

"completed":

* main verb
* V3
* past participle

Entire verb phrase:

> has completed

---

### Example 7

> The door opened.

"opened":

* main verb
* V2
* finite
* intransitive

There is no direct object.

9. Transitive vs intransitive

### Level 4 — Advanced

12. Finite vs non-finite

14. Gerund
15. Participles

### Level 5 — Exam application
