Absolutely. **Verb is probably the most important part of speech after nouns/pronouns**, because verbs are what make a sentence actually *do something* or *describe a state*.

And just like with nouns, don't learn verbs as only a definition. Learn **what they do, where they appear, how they change, and what tricks English has with them.**

# What is a Verb?

The basic definition is:

> **A verb is a word that expresses an action, state, or occurrence.**

Examples:

* run
* eat
* sleep
* think
* know
* be
* have
* become
* happen

But here's a better way to think about it:

> **A verb tells us what the subject does, what the subject is, or what happens.**

Look at these:

> Rahul **runs**.

Action.

> Rahul **is** tired.

State.

> An accident **happened**.

Occurrence.

All three contain verbs.

---

# Why are verbs so important?

Because a normal English sentence needs a **verb**.

Look at:

> Rahul.

Not a complete sentence.

> Rahul happy.

Not standard English.

> Rahul **is** happy.

Now we have a proper sentence.

The verb **is** connects the subject with its state.

Another:

> I **study**.

Now the sentence tells us what I do.

So you can think of a verb as the **engine of a sentence**.

---

# 1. Action Verbs

These show an action.

> I **run** every morning.

> She **opened** the door.

> They **play** football.

> He **eats** rice.

Actions don't necessarily have to be physical.

> I **think** about my future.

> She **knows** the answer.

> They **remember** him.

Thinking, knowing, remembering are also actions/states expressed through verbs.

---

# 2. State Verbs

These describe a condition or state rather than an obvious physical action.

Examples:

* be
* know
* believe
* understand
* like
* love
* hate
* want
* need
* own
* seem
* belong

Examples:

> I **know** the answer.

> She **likes** coffee.

> He **owns** a car.

> They **believe** you.

These become important later because many state verbs normally aren't used with the continuous form.

For example:

> I know him. ✅

Usually not:

> I am knowing him. ❌

We'll study this properly when we get to **tenses**.

---

# 3. Transitive Verbs

A transitive verb usually needs an **object**.

Example:

> I **bought** a phone.

Bought what?

→ a phone.

So:

**I** = subject
**bought** = verb
**a phone** = object

More examples:

> She **opened** the door.

> He **kicked** the ball.

> I **watched** a movie.

The action moves from the subject to an object.

---

# 4. Intransitive Verbs

These don't need an object.

> The baby **slept**.

> He **laughed**.

> They **arrived**.

You don't need to say:

> He laughed something. ❌

"Laughed" can stand by itself.

---

# Compare These

### Transitive

> She **opened the door**.

Opened what?

→ the door.

### Intransitive

> She **laughed**.

Laughed what?

Nothing.

---

Some verbs can be **both** depending on how they're used.

> The door **opened**.

Intransitive.

> She **opened** the door.

Transitive.

This is one reason you shouldn't memorize verbs as permanently "transitive" or "intransitive."

---

# 5. Linking Verbs

These don't show an action toward an object.

Instead, they **connect the subject to information about the subject**.

The most important linking verb is:

**be**

Forms:

* am
* is
* are
* was
* were
* be
* been
* being

Example:

> Rahul **is** intelligent.

"Is" connects Rahul → intelligent.

Another:

> She **became** angry.

> He **seems** tired.

> The food **looks** delicious.

Common linking verbs include:

* be
* become
* seem
* appear
* feel
* look
* sound
* smell
* taste
* remain

---

# 6. Helping Verbs / Auxiliary Verbs

This is **very important**.

Sometimes a verb helps another verb.

Example:

> I **am studying**.

"Studying" carries the main meaning.

"Am" helps create the tense.

So:

**am = helping verb**
**studying = main verb**

Other common helping verbs:

### Be

am, is, are, was, were

> She **is studying**.

### Have

have, has, had

> I **have finished**.

### Do

do, does, did

> Do you understand?

These three are extremely important because they also help form **questions, negatives, and tenses**.

---

# 7. Modal Verbs

These are special helping verbs.

The major ones are:

* can
* could
* may
* might
* will
* would
* shall
* should
* must

They express things like:

**Ability**

> I **can** swim.

**Possibility**

> It **might** rain.

**Permission**

> **May** I come in?

**Future**

> I **will** call you.

**Advice**

> You **should** study.

**Obligation**

> You **must** wear a helmet.

---

# Very Important Rule with Modals

After a modal, use the **base form** of the verb.

> I can **swim**. ✅

Not:

> I can **swims**. ❌

Not:

> I can **swimming**. ❌

Not:

> I can **to swim**. ❌

Same pattern:

> She can **dance**.

> They should **study**.

> He must **leave**.

This rule will become very useful later.

---

# The Forms of a Verb

Here's where verbs become much more complicated than nouns.

Take:

**work**

There are several important forms:

| Form            | Example |
| --------------- | ------- |
| Base            | work    |
| -s form         | works   |
| Past            | worked  |
| Past participle | worked  |
| -ing form       | working |

For a regular verb, past and past participle happen to be the same.

But look at **go**:

| Form            | Verb  |
| --------------- | ----- |
| Base            | go    |
| -s              | goes  |
| Past            | went  |
| Past participle | gone  |
| -ing            | going |

This is why you'll eventually need to learn **verb forms**.

---

# The 5 Forms You Should Know

For example:

### Eat

1. **eat** — base
2. **eats** — -s form
3. **ate** — past
4. **eaten** — past participle
5. **eating** — -ing

### Write

1. write
2. writes
3. wrote
4. written
5. writing

### Go

1. go
2. goes
3. went
4. gone
5. going

You'll see these forms constantly when we study tenses.

---

# Regular vs Irregular Verbs

## Regular verbs

Usually form the past with **-ed**.

> work → worked

> play → played

> clean → cleaned

> open → opened

---

## Irregular verbs

Don't follow the normal pattern.

> go → went

> eat → ate

> see → saw

> take → took

> write → wrote

> buy → bought

> come → came

These have to be learned.

---

# The Verb "BE"

This verb deserves special attention.

**Be** is probably the strangest and most important verb in English.

Its forms include:

> am, is, are, was, were, been, being

Examples:

> I **am** a student.

> He **is** tired.

> They **are** happy.

> She **was** sick.

> We **were** late.

Notice something:

The verb isn't always describing an action.

It can simply describe a **state or identity**.

---

# Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with the subject in certain ways.

This becomes especially visible in the present simple.

> I work.

> You work.

> We work.

> They work.

But:

> He works.

> She works.

> It works.

Notice the **-s**.

This is one of the most important basic English rules:

> **He/She/It + verb-s** in the present simple.

Examples:

> He plays football.

> She likes music.

> It works well.

But:

> I play football.

> They like music.

> We work hard.

---

# The Famous "Does" Trap

This is a very common mistake.

Correct:

> He **likes** coffee.

But when you use **does**, the main verb goes back to the base form:

> He **doesn't like** coffee.

Not:

> He doesn't **likes** coffee. ❌

Why?

Because **does** is already carrying the grammatical marking.

Same with questions:

> Does he **like** coffee? ✅

Not:

> Does he **likes** coffee? ❌

This pattern is extremely important.

---

# Do / Does / Did

These are both normal verbs **and** helping verbs.

### Normal verb

> I **do** my homework.

Here "do" means perform.

### Helping verb

> **Do** you like coffee?

Here "do" doesn't mean "perform." It helps form the question.

Negative:

> I **do not** like coffee.

Past:

> **Did** you see him?

Notice:

> Did you **see** him? ✅

Not:

> Did you **saw** him? ❌

Because after **did**, use the base verb.

---

# Have / Has / Had

These can also be main verbs or helping verbs.

### Main verb

> I **have** a car.

Meaning = possess.

### Helping verb

> I **have finished** my work.

"Have" helps form the tense.

Similarly:

> She **has** a car.

> She **has finished** her work.

> They **had** dinner.

> They **had finished** dinner.

---

# Verb + Object

A very common structure is:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

> I eat rice.

I = subject

eat = verb

rice = object

> She reads books.

She = subject

reads = verb

books = object

> They built a house.

They = subject

built = verb

house = object

---

# Verb + Complement

Some verbs are followed by information that describes the subject rather than an object.

> She is **happy**.

> He became **a doctor**.

> The food looks **delicious**.

Here:

She → happy

He → doctor

Food → delicious

These are called **subject complements**.

Don't worry about memorizing the terminology yet. The important thing is understanding the structure.

---

# Phrasal Verbs

This is one of the **tricky parts of English**.

A verb combines with a particle/preposition and creates a new meaning.

Examples:

**give up** = stop trying

> Don't give up.

**look after** = take care of

> She looks after her brother.

**find out** = discover

> I found out the truth.

**turn on** = activate

> Turn on the light.

**turn off** = deactivate

> Turn off the fan.

The annoying part?

You often **cannot guess the meaning** just from the individual words.

"Give" = provide.

"Up" = higher.

But "give up" = quit.

So phrasal verbs need to be learned as **units**.

---

# Verbs Can Change Meaning

Look at:

> I **have** a car.

Possession.

> I **have** breakfast.

Eat/consume.

> I **have** to study.

Need/must.

Same verb.

Different meaning.

Another:

> He **runs** every morning.

Physical running.

> He **runs** a company.

Manages a company.

So don't assume that one verb has only one meaning.

---

# Verbs and Tense

Verbs change according to **time**.

Present:

> I work.

Past:

> I worked.

Future:

> I will work.

But English has a much larger tense system:

* Present Simple
* Present Continuous
* Present Perfect
* Present Perfect Continuous
* Past Simple
* Past Continuous
* Past Perfect
* Past Perfect Continuous
* Future forms

This is why learning verbs properly **before diving into tenses** is useful.

---

# The -ing Form

The **-ing** form can have different jobs.

### Continuous verb

> I am **studying**.

Studying is part of the verb phrase.

### Noun / Gerund

> **Studying** is important.

Here "studying" functions as a noun.

### Adjective / participle

> The **crying** baby needs milk.

Here "crying" describes the baby.

Same form:

**studying / crying**

But different grammatical jobs.

We'll deal with this properly later.

---

# Infinitive

Another important verb form is:

**to + base verb**

Examples:

> to eat

> to sleep

> to study

> to learn

> to become

Examples:

> I want **to learn** English.

> She needs **to study**.

> He decided **to leave**.

This is called the **to-infinitive**.

But remember: after modal verbs, we don't normally use "to."

> I can swim. ✅

> I can to swim. ❌

---

# Bare Infinitive

Sometimes the base verb appears without "to."

After modal verbs:

> I can **swim**.

After "do":

> Do you **know** him?

After certain other structures:

> Let me **help** you.

> Make him **stop**.

This is called the **bare infinitive**.

---

# A Very Important Difference: Verb vs Verb Phrase

Sometimes the "verb" in a sentence isn't just one word.

> She **is studying**.

The complete verb phrase is:

**is studying**

> They **have finished**.

**have finished**

> He **will come**.

**will come**

So when analyzing sentences, don't always look for only one verb word.

---

# Common Verb Mistakes

### ❌ He go to college.

### ✅ He goes to college.

---

### ❌ She don't like it.

### ✅ She doesn't like it.

---

### ❌ Did you went there?

### ✅ Did you go there?

---

### ❌ He can speaks English.

### ✅ He can speak English.

---

### ❌ I am understand.

### ✅ I understand.

---

### ❌ I am knowing the answer.

### ✅ I know the answer.

---

### ❌ She is having a car.

### ✅ She has a car.

Here **have** means possession, so normally we don't use the continuous form.

---

# How to Identify a Verb

When reading a sentence, ask:

### 1. What is the subject doing?

> Rahul **runs**.

Runs = verb.

### 2. What is happening?

> An accident **happened**.

Happened = verb.

### 3. What state is the subject in?

> She **is** tired.

Is = verb.

### 4. Is there a helping verb?

> He **is studying**.

"is" + "studying" = verb phrase.

---

# Let's Analyze a Sentence

Take:

> **My brother is learning English at college.**

**My** → determiner
**brother** → noun
**is** → helping verb
**learning** → main verb
**English** → noun
**at** → preposition
**college** → noun

The complete verb phrase:

> **is learning**

---

Another:

> **She doesn't understand the question.**

She → pronoun

doesn't → helping verb

understand → main verb

the → determiner

question → noun

Notice:

> doesn't **understand**

NOT

> doesn't **understands**

---

# The Most Important Things to Learn About Verbs

Don't try to memorize everything at once.

Your foundation should be:

### Level 1 — Understand what a verb is

Action, state, occurrence.

### Level 2 — Recognize the major types

* main verbs
* helping verbs
* linking verbs
* modal verbs
* transitive verbs
* intransitive verbs

### Level 3 — Learn verb forms

> go → goes → went → gone → going

### Level 4 — Learn subject-verb agreement

> I work.

> He works.

### Level 5 — Learn how auxiliary verbs work

> do / does / did

> am / is / are / was / were

> have / has / had

### Level 6 — Then learn **tenses**

This is where you'll really start using verbs.

## One thing I want you to remember

Don't think:

> **Verb = action word**

That's useful for a first introduction, but it's **too limited**.

Think:

> **A verb tells us what the subject does, what the subject is, or what happens.**

That's much closer to how verbs actually work.

And now you've got the first three major foundations:

**Noun → what we're talking about**
**Pronoun → a replacement for a noun**
**Verb → what the subject does/is/what happens**

The next part of speech, **adjective**, becomes much easier now because its main job is to **describe nouns and pronouns**.
