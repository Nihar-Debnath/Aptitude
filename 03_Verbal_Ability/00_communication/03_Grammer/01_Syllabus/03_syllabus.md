Yes. If your goal is to become **decent to moderately good at English for real-life communication**, I would not follow a school grammar syllabus exactly. Most school syllabi spend too much time on things like voice change, narration, and obscure grammar rules while not spending enough time on speaking, listening, and natural sentence patterns.

Instead, follow this roadmap:

# Stage 1: Build the Foundation (2–4 weeks)

Learn:

### Parts of Speech

* Nouns
* Pronouns
* Verbs
* Adjectives
* Adverbs
* Prepositions
* Conjunctions

### Basic Sentence Structure

* Subject + Verb + Object
* Subject + Verb + Complement

Examples:

* I play cricket.
* She is happy.
* We study computer science.

### Articles

* a
* an
* the

### Basic Questions

* What?
* Why?
* When?
* Where?
* Who?
* How?

Goal:

* Create simple, correct sentences.

---

# Stage 2: Master Tenses (4–6 weeks)

This is the most important stage.

Learn all 12 tenses, but in this order:

### First

* Present Simple
* Present Continuous
* Past Simple
* Future Simple

### Then

* Present Perfect
* Present Perfect Continuous

### Then

* Past Continuous
* Past Perfect
* Past Perfect Continuous

### Finally

* Future Continuous
* Future Perfect
* Future Perfect Continuous

Focus on:

* When each tense is used
* Positive sentences
* Negative sentences
* Questions

Example:

Present Simple:

> I work.

Negative:

> I don't work.

Question:

> Do you work?

Goal:

* Express time correctly.

---

# Stage 3: Core Communication Grammar (3–4 weeks)

### Subject-Verb Agreement

* He plays
* She works
* They play

### Modals

* can
* could
* should
* would
* may
* might
* must

### Imperatives

* Sit down.
* Open the door.

### Comparisons

* big
* bigger
* biggest

Goal:

* Sound more natural in conversation.

---

# Stage 4: Sentence Expansion (3–4 weeks)

### Conjunctions

* and
* but
* because
* although
* however

### Relative Clauses

* who
* which
* that

Example:

> The man who lives next door is a doctor.

### Conditional Sentences

First:

> If it rains, I will stay home.

Second:

> If I were rich, I would travel.

Third:

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

Goal:

* Express complex ideas.

---

# Stage 5: Real Spoken English (Ongoing)

This is where many learners fail.

Learn:

### Phrasal Verbs

* give up
* look for
* find out
* pick up
* figure out

### Common Expressions

* Sounds good.
* No worries.
* Fair enough.
* That makes sense.

### Contractions

* I'm
* I've
* we'll
* don't
* can't

### Informal Speech

* gonna
* wanna
* gotta
* kinda

Goal:

* Understand movies and native speakers.

---

# Stage 6: Intermediate Grammar

### Reported Speech (Narration)

> He said that he was busy.

### Passive Voice

> The work was completed.

### Gerunds and Infinitives

> I enjoy reading.
>
> I want to read.

### Embedded Questions

> Do you know where he lives?

Goal:

* Speak and write more professionally.

---

# Stage 7: Advanced English

### Advanced Conditionals

### Inversion

> Never have I seen such a thing.

### Subjunctive

> I suggest that he study harder.

### Formal Writing Structures

Goal:

* Academic and professional English.

---

# What You Should Do Every Day

A good daily routine would be:

### 15 Minutes

Grammar study

### 20 Minutes

Read English articles, blogs, Reddit, or books

### 20 Minutes

Watch English content

Examples:

* YouTube
* Movies
* Series
* Interviews

### 10 Minutes

Write something

Examples:

* Journal entry
* Opinion
* Summary of a video

### 10 Minutes

Speak aloud

Talk about:

* Your day
* Gym
* College
* Programming projects

---

# Resources Most Learners Use

### Grammar

* English Grammar in Use

  * One of the most popular self-study grammar books in the world.

### Vocabulary

* Vocabulary.com
* Cambridge Dictionary

### Listening

* BBC Learning English
* VOA Learning English

### Speaking

* Shadowing movie dialogues.
* Talking to yourself in English.
* Language exchange partners.
