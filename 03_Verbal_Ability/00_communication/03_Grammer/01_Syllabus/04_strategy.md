Yes, with one important condition:

**Grammar alone will not make your speaking strong.**

Many students know all 12 tenses, passive voice, narration, conditionals, etc., yet struggle to hold a 5-minute conversation. On the other hand, many people who speak English fluently cannot explain what a "past perfect continuous tense" is.

Think of English like going to the gym:

* Grammar = learning exercise techniques.
* Vocabulary = having the equipment.
* Speaking = actually lifting the weights.

You need all three, but the actual improvement comes from practice.

---

## What Really Builds Strong Speaking?

If I had to rank the factors:

### 1. Listening (30%)

You need thousands of examples of natural English in your brain.

Watch:

* YouTube
* Podcasts
* Movies
* Series
* Interviews

Listen actively:

> "How did they say that?"

not just:

> "What did they say?"

---

### 2. Speaking Practice (30%)

You must speak regularly.

Talk about:

* Your day
* Your college
* Programming
* The gym
* A movie you watched

Even if you're alone.

For example:

> "Today I went to the gym. I trained my back and biceps. The workout was challenging, but I enjoyed it."

Do this daily.

---

### 3. Vocabulary & Phrases (20%)

Instead of learning isolated words:

❌ acquire = obtain

Learn chunks:

✅ "I couldn't figure it out."
✅ "That makes sense."
✅ "I'm looking forward to it."
✅ "It depends on the situation."

Native speakers rely heavily on these chunks.

---

### 4. Grammar (20%)

Grammar helps you avoid mistakes and express ideas clearly.

You need enough grammar to:

* talk about the past
* talk about the future
* ask questions
* explain reasons
* compare things
* express possibilities

That's why the roadmap focuses on practical grammar first.

## A Reality Check

Looking at your messages, your biggest issue is **sentence construction**, not vocabulary.

For example, you wrote:

> "are you sure that following this structure will build my english speaking storng?"

A natural version would be:

> "Are you sure that following this roadmap will improve my English speaking skills?"

or

> "Will following this roadmap make my English speaking stronger?"

Notice:

* Capitalization
* Word choice
* Sentence structure
* Natural phrasing

These improvements come from **reading, listening, and speaking**, with grammar supporting them.

So yes, the roadmap is solid, but if your goal is strong speaking, I'd suggest spending roughly:

* 20% Grammar
* 40% Listening
* 30% Speaking
* 10% Writing

That's much closer to how people actually become confident English speakers.
