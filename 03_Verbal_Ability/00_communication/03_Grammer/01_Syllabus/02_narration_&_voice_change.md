Great question.

**Narration (Reported Speech)** and **Voice Change (Active ↔ Passive Voice)** are important grammar topics, but their importance depends on your goal.

If your goal is:

* Speaking naturally with people
* Understanding movies and series
* Improving conversational English

then these are **not as important as tenses, sentence structure, articles, modal verbs, and phrasal verbs**.

However, if your goal is:

* School/college exams
* Competitive exams
* Formal writing
* Reading newspapers and academic content

then narration and voice change become much more important.

---

# 1. Narration (Reported Speech)

Narration means reporting what someone said without quoting their exact words.

### Direct Speech

> John said, "I am tired."

These are John's exact words.

---

### Indirect (Reported) Speech

> John said that he was tired.

Now you're reporting what John said.

---

## Common Changes

### Pronoun Change

Direct:

> She said, "I love my dog."

Indirect:

> She said that she loved her dog.

---

### Tense Change

Usually when the reporting verb is in the past.

| Direct | Indirect |
| ------ | -------- |
| am/is  | was      |
| are    | were     |
| do     | did      |
| have   | had      |
| will   | would    |
| can    | could    |

Example:

Direct:

> He said, "I am busy."

Indirect:

> He said that he was busy.

---

### Time Word Changes

| Direct    | Indirect         |
| --------- | ---------------- |
| today     | that day         |
| tomorrow  | the next day     |
| yesterday | the previous day |
| now       | then             |
| here      | there            |

Example:

Direct:

> She said, "I will come tomorrow."

Indirect:

> She said that she would come the next day.

---

## Questions in Narration

Direct:

> He asked, "Where are you going?"

Indirect:

> He asked where I was going.

Notice:

❌ He asked where was I going.

✅ He asked where I was going.

No question word order after reporting.

# 2. Voice Change (Active and Passive Voice)

This changes the focus of a sentence.

---

## Active Voice

The doer performs the action.

> Rahul wrote the letter.

Subject = Rahul

Object = letter

---

## Passive Voice

The receiver gets the action.

> The letter was written by Rahul.

Now the focus is on the letter.

---

## Formula

Active:

> Subject + Verb + Object

Passive:

> Object + be verb + Past Participle + by + Subject

---

Example:

Active:

> She cleaned the room.

Passive:

> The room was cleaned by her.

---

# Common Passive Forms

### Present Simple

Active:

> He writes a book.

Passive:

> A book is written by him.

---

### Past Simple

Active:

> He wrote a book.

Passive:

> A book was written by him.

---

### Future

Active:

> He will write a book.

Passive:

> A book will be written by him.

---

# Where Passive Voice Is Used

Very common in:

### News

> Three people were arrested.

> A bridge was constructed.

---

### Science

> The experiment was conducted.

---

### Formal Writing

> The project was completed successfully.

---

# Do Native Speakers Use These Often?

### Narration

Very often.

Examples:

> He told me he was busy.

> She said she would come later.

> My friend told me he got a new job.

You use reported speech almost every day.

---

### Passive Voice

Moderately often.

Examples:

> My phone was stolen.

> The package was delivered.

> The meeting was canceled.

Native speakers use passive voice naturally, but not constantly.
