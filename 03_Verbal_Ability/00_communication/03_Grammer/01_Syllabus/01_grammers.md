# What Grammar Actually Is

Grammar is simply the set of rules that tells us:

* how words are arranged
* how sentences are formed
* how time is expressed
* how ideas are connected

For example:

❌ "Yesterday I go market."

Correct words, wrong grammar.

✅ "Yesterday I went to the market."

---

# The Most Important Grammar Topics

You do **not** need to learn every grammar rule at once.

Focus on these topics in order.

---

# 1. Parts of Speech

Every word belongs to a category.

### Nouns

Names of people, places, things.

* boy
* computer
* city
* happiness

Example:

> The boy uses a computer.

---

### Pronouns

Replace nouns.

* I
* you
* he
* she
* they
* we

Example:

> John is my friend.
>
> He is my friend.

---

### Verbs

Actions or states.

* run
* eat
* think
* sleep
* be

Example:

> I run every morning.

---

### Adjectives

Describe nouns.

* big
* smart
* beautiful

Example:

> She is a smart student.

---

### Adverbs

Describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

* quickly
* slowly
* very

Example:

> He runs quickly.

---

### Prepositions

Show position or relationship.

* in
* on
* at
* under
* beside

Example:

> The book is on the table.

---

### Conjunctions

Join ideas.

* and
* but
* because
* although

Example:

> I stayed home because it was raining.

---

# 2. Sentence Structure

This is the most important topic.

Most English sentences follow:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

Example:

> I eat apples.

Subject = I

Verb = eat

Object = apples

---

More examples:

> She likes coffee.

> They play football.

> We study programming.

---

# 3. Tenses

Tenses tell us **when** something happens.

This is the heart of English grammar.

---

## Present Simple

Habit or fact.

> I study every day.

> He works in Bangalore.

---

## Present Continuous

Happening right now.

> I am studying.

> She is cooking.

---

## Past Simple

Finished action.

> I studied yesterday.

> She went home.

---

## Future Simple

Future action.

> I will study tomorrow.

> They will come later.

---

These four are used constantly.

Master them first.

---

# 4. Subject-Verb Agreement

Verb must match the subject.

### Correct

> He plays football.

> She works hard.

> It looks good.

---

### Incorrect

> He play football.

> She work hard.

---

Remember:

For **he/she/it** in present simple:

Add **s/es**

> play → plays

> work → works

> watch → watches

---

# 5. Articles

Very important.

### a

General singular thing.

> I saw a dog.

---

### an

Before vowel sounds.

> an apple

> an engineer

---

### the

Specific thing.

> The dog is mine.

---

# 6. Questions

### Using Do/Does

> Do you play cricket?

> Does he work here?

---

### Past

> Did you eat?

> Did she call?

---

### With Be

> Are you ready?

> Is he coming?

---

# 7. Negatives

### Present

> I do not like coffee.

> He does not smoke.

---

### Past

> I did not go.

> She did not call.

---

### Short Forms

> don't

> doesn't

> didn't

Native speakers use these constantly.

---

# 8. Modal Verbs

These make English sound natural.

### Can

Ability

> I can swim.

---

### Could

Past ability / polite request

> Could you help me?

---

### Should

Advice

> You should study more.

---

### Must

Necessity

> You must wear a helmet.

---

### Might

Possibility

> It might rain.

---

# 9. Conditionals

Used in daily conversation.

### First Conditional

Real future possibility.

> If it rains, I will stay home.

---

### Second Conditional

Imaginary situation.

> If I were rich, I would travel the world.

---

### Third Conditional

Past regret.

> If I had studied harder, I would have passed.

---

# 10. Passive Voice

Very common in news and formal English.

Active:

> The police arrested him.

Passive:

> He was arrested by the police.

---

# 11. Reported Speech

When telling someone what another person said.

Direct:

> John said, "I am busy."

Reported:

> John said that he was busy.

---

# 12. Relative Clauses

These make sentences more advanced.

Simple:

> I know a man.

Advanced:

> I know a man who works at Google.

Words:

* who
* whom
* whose
* which
* that

---

# 14. Phrasal Verbs

This is where many learners struggle.

Examples:

### Give up

> Don't give up.

### Look after

> She looks after her brother.

### Figure out

> I figured it out.

### Pick up

> I'll pick you up.

Movies are full of phrasal verbs.

---

# 15. The Grammar That Gives the Biggest Improvement

If you learn only these topics well, your English will improve dramatically:

1. Sentence structure (Subject + Verb + Object)
2. Present, Past, Future tenses
3. Subject-verb agreement
4. Articles (a, an, the)
5. Questions and negatives
6. Modal verbs
7. Phrasal verbs
8. Conditionals

These eight topics probably cover **80–90% of everyday English communication**.

# A Practical Study Plan for You

Since you're learning from movies and series:

### Daily (20–30 minutes)

1. Watch 5–10 minutes of English content.
2. Write down:

   * new words
   * interesting phrases
   * unusual grammar patterns
3. Create 3–5 sentences using them.
4. Speak those sentences aloud.

Example:

Movie sentence:

> "I should've called you."

Grammar learned:

> should have → should've

Your sentences:

> I should've studied yesterday.

> I should've gone earlier.

> I should've listened to my teacher.

This method teaches vocabulary and grammar together, which is much more effective than memorizing rules alone.
