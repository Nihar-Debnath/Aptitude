Good observation. The answer is:

> **No. Rule 12 is a separate Subject-Verb Agreement rule.**

However, there is **one connection** between them, which is probably why you got confused.

---

# universal rule

**Universal Facts, Habits, Proverbs, Idioms**

This rule tells you **when to use the Simple Present Tense**.

Examples:

* The Sun **rises** in the east.
* Honesty **is** the best policy.
* He **goes** to school every day.

Focus:

> **Type of sentence** (fact, habit, proverb)

---

# Rule 12

**Amounts of Time, Money, Distance, Weight, Measurement**

This rule tells you **how to treat the subject**.

When the whole amount is considered **one unit**, it is grammatically **singular**.

Examples:

* Five kilometers **is** a long walk.
* Two hours **is** enough.
* Ten thousand rupees **is** sufficient.
* Fifty kilograms **is** too heavy.

Focus:

> **Nature of the subject** (one unit)

---

## Why do they look similar?

Take this sentence:

> Two hours **is** enough.

It is:

* Present tense ✅
* Singular verb ✅

But the reason for **is** is **NOT** because it is a universal fact.

The reason is:

> **Two hours** is treated as **one block of time**.

---

## Compare

### universal rule

> The Earth **revolves** around the Sun.

Why **revolves**?

Because:

* Subject = Earth (singular)
* It is a universal fact.

---

### Rule 12

> Five kilometers **is** a long distance.

Why **is**?

Because:

* Five kilometers = one distance (one unit)

---

### If it is NOT one unit

Then use a plural verb.

Example:

> Five kilometers **separate** the two villages.

Here "five kilometers" refers to the actual distance **stretching between** the villages, not a single amount being evaluated.

Another example:

> Two hours **have** passed.

Here we're talking about **two individual hours passing**, not "a period of two hours" as one unit.

---

# Easy Comparison

| Rule    | Looks at                                | Example                             |
| ------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| universal rule  | Type of sentence (fact, habit, proverb) | The Sun **rises** in the east.      |
| Rule 12 | Subject as one unit                     | Five kilometers **is** a long walk. |

---

# Exam Trick

Whenever you see:

* **hours**
* **days**
* **kilometers**
* **rupees**
* **liters**
* **tons**
* **percent**

Ask yourself:

> **Is this one amount/unit?**

If **YES** → Singular verb

* Two hours **is** enough.
* Five lakh rupees **is** sufficient.

If **NO** (individual items are being emphasized) → Plural verb

* Two hours **have** passed.
* Five kilometers **separate** the villages.

So, **universal rule and Rule 12 are independent rules**. They can appear together in the same sentence (e.g., *Two hours is enough* is in the simple present), but **the singular verb comes from Rule 12 (one unit), not from universal rule (universal facts/habits).**
