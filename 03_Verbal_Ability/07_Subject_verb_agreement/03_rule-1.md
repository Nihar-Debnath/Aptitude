That's completely normal. Subject–Verb Agreement looks easy, but when you see **12 rules at once**, it becomes confusing.

The mistake was trying to learn everything together.

Let's learn it the way you would learn programming: **one concept at a time.**

---

# Step 1: What is a Subject?

The **subject** is **who or what is doing the action**.

Example:

> **Nihar** studies every day.

Ask:
**Who studies?**

Answer:
**Nihar** → Subject

---

Another example:

> **The dog** barks loudly.

Who barks?

**The dog**

Subject = **The dog**

---

# Step 2: What is a Verb?

The **verb** is the **action**.

Examples

* Nihar **studies**.
* The dog **barks**.
* They **play**.
* We **eat**.

---

# Step 3: The Only Rule You Need Right Now

Forget everything else.

Just remember this.

| Subject          | Verb  |
| ---------------- | ----- |
| One person/thing | plays |
| More than one    | play  |

Examples

### One person

He **plays** football.

One boy → **plays**

---

One girl

She **writes** well.

---

One dog

The dog **barks**.

---

### More than one

They **play** football.

---

The boys **play** football.

---

The dogs **bark** loudly.

---

# Here's the Weird Part (That Confuses Everyone)

Normally we think:

> Singular = small word

> Plural = bigger word

But verbs work the opposite way.

| Subject | Correct Verb |
| ------- | ------------ |
| He      | plays        |
| They    | play         |

The **singular verb** has **-s**.

The **plural verb** has **no -s**.

This is what almost everyone gets wrong at first.

---

# Easy Formula

```
One person
↓
Verb + s

More than one
↓
Base verb
```

Examples

One student

→ studies

Three students

→ study

---

One teacher

→ teaches

Many teachers

→ teach

---

One child

→ plays

Many children

→ play

He

B) plays

---

2.

They

A) works

B) work

---

3.

The dog

A) bark

B) barks

---

4.

The students

A) study

B) studies

Don't think about grammar rules yet. Just answer these four. Once you get these right consistently, we'll move to the next rule. This step-by-step approach is much easier than memorizing a dozen rules at once.
