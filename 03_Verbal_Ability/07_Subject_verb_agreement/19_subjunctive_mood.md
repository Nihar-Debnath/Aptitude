Ah! This is **not actually a Subject-Verb Agreement rule**, even though many competitive exam teachers teach it in that chapter.

This is the **Subjunctive Mood**.

---

# What is the Subjunctive Mood?

The **subjunctive mood** is used to express things that are **not real**, such as:

* Wishes
* Imaginations
* Unreal situations
* Hypothetical conditions
* Suggestions or demands

Example:

> I wish I **were** rich.

You are **not** rich.

It is only an **imaginary wish**.

---

# Why "were" and not "was"?

Normally, according to Subject-Verb Agreement:

| Subject | Correct Verb |
| ------- | ------------ |
| I       | **was**      |
| He      | **was**      |
| She     | **was**      |
| They    | **were**     |

Example:

> I **was** happy yesterday. ✅

This is a **real past event**.

---

But when the sentence expresses something **unreal or imaginary**, English uses **were** for **all subjects**.

| Subject | Imaginary |
| ------- | --------- |
| I       | were      |
| He      | were      |
| She     | were      |
| It      | were      |

Examples:

✅ I wish I **were** a king.

✅ If I **were** you, I would study harder.

✅ She behaves as if she **were** the boss.

---

# Notice the Difference

### Real Situation

> I **was** sick yesterday.

This really happened.

Use **was**.

---

### Imaginary Situation

> I wish I **were** a doctor.

You are **not** a doctor.

Use **were**.

# Common Words That Trigger the Subjunctive

When you see these words, think:

> **"This may be imaginary."**

* if *(only in unreal conditions)*
* if only
* wish
* as if
* as though
* suppose
* imagine
* would rather
* it's time
* in case *(not always subjunctive; depends on usage)*

---

## Examples

### Wish

> I wish I **were** taller.

---

### If

> If I **were** a bird, I would fly.

---

### As if

> He talks as if he **were** a king.

---

### Suppose

> Suppose you **were** the manager.

---

### Would rather

> I'd rather you **were** honest.

# Why do we use "were"?

This is an old rule from English grammar.

Long ago, English had a special verb form called the **subjunctive**.

Most of it disappeared over time, but one famous form is still used:

> **were** instead of **was** for imaginary situations.

That's why even today we say:

> If I **were** you...

instead of

> If I **was** you...

---

# Is "If I was..." wrong?

In **formal English** and competitive exams:

❌ If I was you...

✅ If I were you...

In everyday conversation, many native speakers do say **"If I was..."**, but exams almost always expect **"If I were..."**.

### One correction to your teacher's note

The note says:

> **"The verb used is plural."**

That is **a simplified shortcut**, but it's **not technically correct**.

A more accurate rule is:

> **In imaginary or hypothetical situations (Subjunctive Mood), use "were" instead of "was" for all subjects.**

The verb isn't becoming plural. It's using the **subjunctive form** of the verb **be**. That's why:

* **I were** ✅ (not plural)
* **He were** ✅ (not plural)

The word **were** here is **not** indicating plurality—it is indicating an **unreal or hypothetical situation**. This distinction matters if you want to understand English deeply rather than just memorize exam shortcuts.
