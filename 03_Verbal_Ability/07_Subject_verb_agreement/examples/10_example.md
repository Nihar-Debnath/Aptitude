This is **one of the most famous Subject-Verb Agreement tricks** in competitive exams. You got confused because **two rules are colliding**:

1. **"One of" rule**
2. **"Who" (relative pronoun) rule**

Let's break it down slowly.

---

## The sentence

> **Tom is one of the boys who ____ always on time.**

Options:

* A. is
* B. are ✅
* C. was
* D. none

---

# Step 1: Ignore "Tom is"

The blank is inside the clause:

> **who ____ always on time**

Now ask:

**Who refers to whom?**

---

# Step 2: Find the antecedent of "who"

Sentence:

> Tom is one of **the boys** who ____ always on time.

The word **who** refers to **the boys**, **not Tom**.

Think of it as:

```text
Tom
 ↓
one of
 ↓
the boys
 ↓
who
```

The "who" clause describes **the boys**.

So the meaning is:

> There are many boys.
>
> Those boys are always on time.
>
> Tom is one of them.

Since **boys** is plural,

the verb must also be plural.

✅ **are**

---

# Why "is" is wrong?

You thought:

> One of → singular

That rule applies to the **main clause**, not the **relative clause**.

Main clause:

> Tom **is** one of the boys.

Correct.

Relative clause:

> who **are** always on time.

Here "who" refers to **boys**.

---

## Split the sentence

This is the easiest way.

Original:

> Tom is one of the boys who are always on time.

Split it:

**Sentence 1**

> Tom is one of the boys.

✔ "one" is singular → **is**

**Sentence 2**

> The boys are always on time.

✔ Boys is plural → **are**

Join them:

> Tom is one of the boys who are always on time.

---

# Compare with another sentence

### Sentence A

> Tom is one of the boys who **are** honest.

Meaning:

Many boys are honest.

Tom is one of those boys.

---

### Sentence B

> Tom is the only one of the boys who **is** honest.

Now notice the difference.

Here "who" refers to **the only one**.

Meaning:

Among all the boys,

only Tom is honest.

So

**is**

---

# The Trick

### One of the + plural noun + who

```text
one of the boys who
                 ↑
              refers here
```

Verb = plural

> are

---

### The only one of the + plural noun + who

```text
the only one
      ↑
who refers here
```

Verb = singular

> is

---

# Examples

### Example 1

She is one of the students who **work** hard.

Who → students

Students → plural

✔ work

---

### Example 2

He is one of the players who **have** scored centuries.

Who → players

Players → plural

✔ have

---

### Example 3

He is the only one of the players who **has** scored a century.

Who → the only one

Singular

✔ has

---

# Notebook Rule

### Rule: "One of" + Relative Clause

**Pattern 1**

> One of + plural noun + who/that + **plural verb**

Example:

* Tom is one of the boys who **are** honest.
* She is one of the students who **work** hard.

---

**Pattern 2**

> The only one of + plural noun + who/that + **singular verb**

Example:

* Tom is the only one of the boys who **is** honest.
* She is the only one of the students who **works** hard.

---

### Exam Trick

Whenever you see:

> **one of the ... who**

**Don't look at "one".**

Ask this question:

> **Who refers to whom?**

* If **who** refers to the **plural noun** (boys, students, players) → use a **plural verb**.
* If **who** refers to **the only one** → use a **singular verb**.

This is a favorite pattern in SSC, Banking, CAT, UPSC, and other competitive exams because it tests whether you can identify the **antecedent** (the noun that the relative pronoun refers to), not just the nearest singular word.
