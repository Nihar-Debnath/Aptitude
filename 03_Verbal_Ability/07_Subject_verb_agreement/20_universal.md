This is another rule that is **often taught in a simplified way**. Let's learn the actual grammar behind it.

---

# Rule : Universal Facts, Habitual Actions, Idioms & Proverbs

Your teacher wrote:

> **Universal Facts, Idioms and Phrases, and Habitual Actions use Singular Verb because they are in Present Tense.**

This is **partly correct**, but it's not the complete rule.

The real rule is about the **Simple Present Tense**, not about "always singular verbs."

---

# 1. Universal Facts

A universal fact is something that is **always true**.

Examples:

* The Sun **rises** in the east. ✅
* Water **boils** at 100°C. ✅
* Earth **revolves** around the Sun. ✅

Notice:

* The Sun → singular → **rises**
* Water → singular → **boils**
* Earth → singular → **revolves**

These verbs are singular because **their subjects are singular**, **not because they are universal facts**.

---

## What if the subject is plural?

Example:

> Birds **lay** eggs.

Here:

Subject = Birds (plural)

Verb = **lay**, **not** lays.

So universal facts **do not always use singular verbs**.

The verb still agrees with the subject.

---

# 2. Habitual Actions

Habitual actions mean things done regularly.

Examples:

* He **goes** to school every day.
* She **drinks** coffee every morning.
* They **play** football every Sunday.

Notice:

| Subject | Verb   |
| ------- | ------ |
| He      | goes   |
| She     | drinks |
| They    | play   |

Again, the verb depends on the subject.

---

# 3. Proverbs & Idioms

Proverbs express general truths.

Examples:

* Honesty **is** the best policy.
* Practice **makes** a man perfect.
* Time **waits** for no one.

These have singular subjects, so they use singular verbs.

But consider:

> Actions **speak** louder than words.

Subject = Actions (plural)

Verb = **speak**

Not **speaks**.

So even proverbs follow normal subject-verb agreement.

---

# The Real Grammar Rule

Universal facts, habitual actions, and proverbs are usually written in the **Simple Present Tense**.

The verb follows the **normal Subject–Verb Agreement**.

## Simple Present Rule

| Subject       | Verb        |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| He/She/It     | Verb + s/es |
| I/You/We/They | Base verb   |

Examples:

* The Sun **rises**.
* Birds **fly**.
* Water **boils**.
* Stars **shine**.
* Dogs **bark**.

---

# Why do teachers say "Singular Verb"?

Because **most textbook examples** use singular subjects:

* The Sun
* Earth
* Honesty
* Practice
* Time

So students think it's a special rule.

Actually, it's just normal Subject–Verb Agreement.

# Notebook Summary

## Universal Facts, Habitual Actions & Proverbs

These are generally written in the **Simple Present Tense**.

**The verb agrees with the subject.**

* Singular subject → **Verb + s/es**
* Plural subject → **Base verb**

Examples:

* The Sun **rises** in the east. ✅
* Birds **fly** in the sky. ✅
* He **goes** to school every day. ✅
* They **go** to school every day. ✅
* Honesty **is** the best policy. ✅
* Actions **speak** louder than words. ✅

---

### Exam Tip

If an exam asks you:

> **The Sun ____ in the east.**

Answer: **rises**.

But if it asks:

> **Birds ____ in the sky.**

Answer: **fly**, **not flies**.

So don't memorize **"universal facts always take singular verbs."** The correct rule is:

> **Universal facts are expressed in the Simple Present Tense, and the verb must agree with its subject.**
