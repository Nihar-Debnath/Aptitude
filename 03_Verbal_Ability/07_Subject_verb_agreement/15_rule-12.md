Perfect! 🎉 This is the **last major Subject–Verb Agreement rule**. Fortunately, it's much easier than the previous ones.

# ✅ Rule 12: Amounts of Time, Money, Distance, Weight, and Measurement

## The Rule

When an **amount** is considered as **one whole unit**, use a **singular verb**.

---

## Formula

```text
Amount (as one unit)
        ↓
Singular Verb
```

---

# 1. Time

### Examples ✅

* **Two hours is** enough to finish the exam.
* **Ten minutes is** sufficient.
* **Five years is** a long time.

### Why?

Although **hours**, **minutes**, and **years** are plural, they represent **one period of time**.

Think:

> **One duration** = Singular.

---

# 2. Money

### Examples ✅

* **₹10,000 is** enough.
* **One million dollars is** a huge amount.
* **Fifty rupees is** all I have.

### Why?

You're talking about **one amount of money**, not individual rupees.

---

# 3. Distance

### Examples ✅

* **Five kilometers is** too far to walk.
* **Ten miles is** a long distance.
* **200 meters is** enough for the race.

Again,

It's **one distance**.

---

# 4. Weight

### Examples ✅

* **Ten kilograms is** too heavy.
* **Five pounds is** enough.
* **100 grams is** all we need.

---

# 5. Measurement

### Examples ✅

* **Five liters is** enough milk.
* **Three meters is** sufficient.
* **Two acres is** enough land.

---

# Why Is the Verb Singular?

Take this sentence:

> **Two hours is enough.**

Ask:

Are we talking about:

* Two separate hours? ❌

OR

* One total duration of two hours? ✅

It's **one duration**.

Therefore:

**is**

---

# Compare These Two

### One Unit

> **Five kilometers is** a long distance.

Here, "five kilometers" is treated as **one journey**.

---

### Individual Things

> **Five kilometers of road are** under construction.

Now you're talking about **different sections of road**, not one total distance.

So:

**are**

---

# Another Example

### Amount

> **Ten thousand rupees is** enough to buy a laptop.

One amount.

---

### Individual Notes

> **Ten thousand rupee notes are** lying on the table.

Now we're talking about **many notes**.

So:

**are**

# Exception 🚨

If you're talking about **individual items**, use a **plural verb**.

### Examples

* **Five coins are** on the table.
* **Ten books are** missing.
* **Three chairs are** broken.

Here, you're counting **separate objects**, not one amount.

# 🎯 Final Subject–Verb Agreement Cheat Sheet

| Rule   | Key Idea                                                                                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1**  | Singular subject → Singular verb                                                                 |
| **2**  | Plural subject → Plural verb                                                                     |
| **3**  | **I** and **You** use plural-form verbs (`I play`, `You work`)                                   |
| **4**  | Be verbs: `I am`, `He is`, `They are`                                                            |
| **5**  | `A and B` → Plural (unless one person/one idea)                                                  |
| **6**  | `Everyone`, `Each`, `Somebody`, etc. → Singular                                                  |
| **7**  | `Either...or`, `Neither...nor`, `Not only...but also` → Verb agrees with the **nearest subject** |
| **8**  | Collective nouns (`team`, `committee`) → Usually singular                                        |
| **9**  | `One of`, `Each of`, `Neither of`, `Either of` → Singular                                        |
| **10** | `The number of` → Singular; `A number of` → Plural                                               |
| **11** | Uncountable nouns (`information`, `advice`, `news`) → Singular                                   |
| **12** | Time, money, distance, weight, measurement as **one unit** → Singular                            |

Once you can identify the **real subject** and recognize these patterns, you'll solve the vast majority of SVA questions confidently.
