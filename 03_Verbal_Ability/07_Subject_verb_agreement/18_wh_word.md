This is an excellent topic because **WH-words often confuse students in Subject–Verb Agreement**. The biggest mistake is thinking the **WH-word is the subject**. Most of the time, **it is not**.

---

# First Rule (Golden Rule)

> **The verb agrees with the SUBJECT, not with the WH-word.**

WH-words are:

* What
* Who
* Whom
* Which
* Where
* When
* Why
* Whose
* How

These words usually introduce a question, but **they do not always act as the subject**.

---

# Case 1: WH-word is NOT the subject (Most Common)

Example:

> **Where are the students?**

Find the subject:

Where ❌

the students ✅

Since **students** is plural,

→ **are**

---

Example:

> **Why is Ram absent?**

Subject = Ram (singular)

So

→ **is**

---

Example:

> **When do they arrive?**

Subject = they (plural)

So

→ **do**

---

# Case 2: WH-word IS the subject

Sometimes **who** or **what** itself is the subject.

Example:

> **Who is coming?**

We don't know the person yet.

"Who" acts as the subject.

By convention, English uses a **singular verb**.

✅ Who **is** coming?

---

Example:

> **What happened?**

"What" is the subject.

So

✅ happened

(Not "What happen?")

---

Example:

> **Who has taken my pen?**

Correct:

**has**

because "who" is treated as singular until we know otherwise.

---

# Case 3: "Who" followed by a plural noun

Example:

> **Who are those boys?**

Subject = those boys

Plural

So

→ **are**

---

Example:

> **Who is that girl?**

Subject = that girl

Singular

So

→ **is**

---

# Case 4: "Which"

The verb depends on the noun after "which."

Example:

> **Which book is yours?**

Subject = book

Singular

→ is

---

Example:

> **Which books are yours?**

Subject = books

Plural

→ are

---

# Case 5: "What"

Example:

> **What is your name?**

Subject = your name

Singular

→ is

---

Example:

> **What are your plans?**

Subject = your plans

Plural

→ are

---

# Case 6: "How many"

Look at the noun after it.

Example:

> **How many students are present?**

Students = plural

→ are

---

Example:

> **How many books were sold?**

Books = plural

→ were

---

# Case 7: "How much"

Usually uncountable.

Example:

> **How much water is left?**

Water = singular (uncountable)

→ is

# The Exam Trick (SSC, Banking, CAT)

Whenever you see a WH-word, don't immediately choose the verb.

Follow these steps:

### Step 1

Ignore the WH-word for a moment.

### Step 2

Find the real subject.

### Step 3

Choose the verb according to that subject.

Example:

> **Where ____ the children?**

Subject = children (plural)

Answer:

> **Where are the children?** ✅

---

Example:

> **Which of these books ____ yours?**

Subject = **Which** (the head of the phrase "Which of these books"), which refers to **one book** being selected.

Correct answer:

> **Which of these books is yours?** ✅

Many students mistakenly write **are** because they see "books." The phrase **of these books** is just additional information. The word **Which** asks about **one** item from the group.

## Short Note (for your notebook)

### WH-Words & Subject–Verb Agreement

**Golden Rule:** The **verb agrees with the subject, not with the WH-word.**

* **Where are the students?** → Subject = students → **are**
* **Why is Ram late?** → Subject = Ram → **is**
* **Who is coming?** → "Who" is the subject → **is**
* **Which book is yours?** → book → **is**
* **Which books are yours?** → books → **are**
* **How much water is left?** → water → **is**
* **How many students are present?** → students → **are**

This approach will help you solve almost every WH-word question in subject–verb agreement.
