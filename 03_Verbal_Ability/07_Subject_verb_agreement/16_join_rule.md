Yes, this is a **real and correct English grammar rule**. The teacher is teaching **Subject-Verb Agreement**.

Let's understand it from the beginning.

---

# The Rule

If two nouns/pronouns are connected by expressions like:

* as well as
* along with
* together with
* with
* accompanied by
* besides
* in addition to
* except
* rather than
* unlike
* no less than
* nothing but
* and not

**The verb agrees with the FIRST subject only.**

It does **NOT** treat them as two equal subjects.

---

## Example 1

> Ram as well as his parents **is** coming. ✅

Subject = **Ram** (singular)

"as well as his parents" is just **extra information**.

So,

Ram **is** coming.

Correct verb = **is**

---

Incorrect:

> Ram as well as his parents **are** coming. ❌

because "parents" is not the main subject.

---

## Think of it like this

Sentence:

> Ram, as well as his parents, is coming.

Notice the commas.

The part

> as well as his parents

is almost like a side note.

Main sentence:

> Ram is coming.

Extra information:

> his parents are also coming.

---

# Compare with "and"

This is where students get confused.

### Using AND

> Ram and his parents are coming.

Here,

Ram + parents = two subjects

So plural verb.

```
Ram + Parents
      ↓
     are
```

---

### Using AS WELL AS

> Ram as well as his parents is coming.

Here,

```
Ram
 ↓
is

(as well as his parents)
```

The second part doesn't change the verb.

---

# More Examples

### Correct

The teacher along with the students **was** present.

Main subject = teacher

Teacher → singular

---

The captain together with his teammates **has** arrived.

Captain → singular

---

My brother, along with his friends, **is** going.

Brother → singular

---

The president as well as the ministers **has** agreed.

President → singular

---

# If the first subject is plural

People often forget this.

Example:

> The students as well as the teacher **are** ready.

Main subject = students

Students = plural

So

**are**

not

**is**

---

# Why does English do this?

Because these expressions **do not join two subjects equally**.

They simply **add extra information**.

Grammar calls this an **accompanying phrase** or a **parenthetical addition**.

Think of it as:

> Main subject + extra information

instead of

> Subject + Subject

---

# Where did this rule come from?

This rule has existed in English for centuries.

The expressions

* as well as
* together with
* along with
* with

are **prepositions or prepositional phrases**, **not coordinating conjunctions**.

The coordinating conjunction **and** creates a compound subject:

```
Ram and Shyam
```

But

```
Ram as well as Shyam
```

does **not** create a compound subject.

It merely means:

> Ram, in addition to Shyam

or

> Ram, accompanied by Shyam

That's why the verb follows **Ram**.

---

# One thing many teachers don't tell students

In **modern informal English**, native speakers sometimes use a plural verb because they mentally think of both people.

For example:

> Ram as well as his parents are coming.

You may hear this in casual speech.

However, in **standard grammar**, academic writing, school exams, competitive exams, and formal English, the recommended form is:

> Ram as well as his parents **is** coming. ✅

### Rule

If the subjects are connected by:

* as well as
* along with
* together with
* with
* in addition to
* besides
* accompanied by
* rather than
* except
* etc.

➡️ **Ignore the second subject when choosing the verb.**

| First Subject | Second Subject | Correct Verb      |
| ------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Singular      | Singular       | **is/was/has**    |
| Singular      | Plural         | **is/was/has**    |
| Plural        | Singular       | **are/were/have** |
| Plural        | Plural         | **are/were/have** |

### Examples

✅ **Ram** as well as his friends **is** coming.

* First subject = Ram (Singular)
* Verb = **is**

✅ **The students** along with the teacher **are** ready.

* First subject = students (Plural)
* Verb = **are**

✅ **My father** together with my uncles **has** arrived.

* First subject = father (Singular)
* Verb = **has**

✅ **The players** with the coach **have** reached the stadium.

* First subject = players (Plural)
* Verb = **have**

## Memory Trick (very useful for exams)

Whenever you see these words:

> **as well as, along with, together with, with...**

Ask yourself:

> **"What is the first subject?"**

Then choose the verb according to **only that subject**.

For example:

> **Ram** as well as his parents ___ coming.

Ignore "his parents."

Think:

> **Ram is coming.**

So the answer is:

> **Ram as well as his parents is coming.** ✅

This is the trick used in SSC, Banking, UPSC, CAT, and other competitive exams.
