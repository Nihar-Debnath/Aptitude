# ✅ Rule 8: Collective Nouns

## What is a Collective Noun?

A **collective noun** is **one word that refers to a group of people, animals, or things**.

For example:

* Team
* Family
* Committee
* Jury
* Class
* Audience
* Crowd
* Staff
* Army
* Police *(special case—we'll discuss it separately)*

---

## The Basic Rule

When the group is acting **as one unit**, use a **singular verb**.

### Formula

```text
One group acting together
↓
Singular verb
```

---

## Examples ✅

* The team **is** winning.
* The family **has** arrived.
* The committee **has** made a decision.
* The class **is** studying.
* The audience **was** silent.

Why?

Because each sentence talks about **the whole group as one entity**, not the individual members.

---

# Think Like This

Imagine a cricket team.

There are **11 players**.

But when you say:

> **The team is winning.**

You are talking about **one team**, not 11 separate players.

---

# When Does It Become Plural?

When the members of the group are acting **individually**, especially in **British English**, a plural verb is common.

### Examples

* The team **are** arguing among themselves.
* The committee **have** different opinions.

Why?

Because now we're talking about **the members**, not the group as a single unit.

---

# Which One Should You Use in Placement Exams?

👉 **Always choose the singular verb**, unless the sentence clearly emphasizes individual members.

Most placement exams follow **American English**, where collective nouns are generally treated as singular.

# Special Cases 🚨

## 1. Police

**Police** is **always plural**.

✅ The police **are** investigating the case.

❌ The police **is** investigating.

---

## 2. People

**People** is plural.

✅ People **are** friendly.

❌ People **is** friendly.

---

## 3. Cattle

**Cattle** is plural.

✅ The cattle **are** grazing.

---

## 4. Government (Placement Tip)

In American English:

✅ The government **has** announced a new policy.

In British English, you may also hear:

✅ The government **have** announced...

For placement exams, **prefer the singular form**.

# What is a Collective Noun?

A **collective noun** is a word that refers to **a group of people, animals, or things as one unit**.

Examples:

* Team
* Family
* Committee
* Jury
* Class
* Army
* Crowd
* Audience
* Staff
* Group

Although these words refer to **many members**, the word itself is **singular**.

---

# Rule 1 (Most Important)

**If the collective noun acts as one unit, use a singular verb.** ✅

| Subject       | Verb    |
| ------------- | ------- |
| The team      | is      |
| The family    | has     |
| The committee | decides |
| The class     | was     |

Examples:

* The team **is** playing well.
* The committee **has** made its decision.
* The family **lives** in Kolkata.
* The class **was** silent.

Think of it as:

```text
Team = One group
Family = One group
Committee = One group
```

So, **singular verb**.

---

# Rule 2

**If the members of the group are acting individually, a plural verb may be used (especially in British English).**

Examples:

* The team **are** arguing among themselves.
* The family **have** gone to their separate rooms.

Here, we are talking about the **individual members**, not the group as one unit.

---

# American English vs British English

This is where many learners get confused.

### American English 🇺🇸

Collective nouns are **almost always singular**.

* The team **is** winning. ✅
* The committee **has** decided. ✅

---

### British English 🇬🇧

Both are possible.

* The team **is** winning. ✅ (team as one unit)
* The team **are** wearing different shoes. ✅ (players individually)

---

# Competitive Exams (SSC, Banking, CAT, UPSC)

For exams, follow this simple rule:

> **Treat collective nouns as singular unless the question clearly emphasizes the individual members.**

Examples:

✅ The jury **has** reached its verdict.

✅ The committee **was** formed last year.

---

# Examples

### Singular

* The army **is** ready.
* The audience **was** quiet.
* The crowd **is** cheering.
* The staff **has** been informed.

---

### Plural (Members acting separately)

* The jury **are** disagreeing with one another.
* The committee **have** different opinions.
