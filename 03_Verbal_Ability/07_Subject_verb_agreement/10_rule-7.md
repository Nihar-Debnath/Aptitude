Absolutely. **Rule 7** is one of the most confusing SVA rules in placement exams. Don't memorize it—understand the logic.

# ✅ Rule 7: **Either...Or / Neither...Nor**

## The Rule

When two subjects are joined by:

* **Either...Or**
* **Neither...Nor**

👉 The **verb agrees with the subject closest to it** (the one immediately before the verb).

This is called the **Principle of Proximity**.

---

# Why Doesn't "and" Rule Apply?

Remember Rule 5?

> **A and B** = both are included.

Example:

> Rahul **and** Amit are playing.

Both Rahul and Amit are playing.

---

But now look:

> **Either Rahul or Amit** is playing.

Only **one** person is playing.

So this is **not** a plural subject.

---

# Step 1: Find the Subject Closest to the Verb

Look at this sentence.

> **Either the teacher or the students are coming.**

Don't look at "teacher."

Look here:

```text
Either the teacher or the students are coming.

                          ↑
                  Closest subject
```

Closest subject = **students**

Students = plural

Therefore:

✅ **are**

---

## Another Example

> **Either the students or the teacher is coming.**

```text
Either the students or the teacher is coming.

                           ↑
                   Closest subject
```

Closest subject = **teacher**

Teacher = singular

Therefore:

✅ **is**

---

# Formula

```text
Either A or B

↓

Ignore A

↓

Look only at B

↓

Choose the verb
```

The same rule applies to:

```text
Neither A nor B
```

# What About "Have" and "Has"?

Exactly the same rule.

### Example

> Either my brother or my friends **have** arrived.

Closest subject = friends

Plural

✅ **have**

---

### Example

> Either my friends or my brother **has** arrived.

✅ **has**

# What About Normal Verbs?

Same rule.

### Example

> Either Rahul or his friends **play** cricket.

Closest subject = friends

Plural

✅ **play**

---

### Example

> Either Rahul's friends or Rahul **plays** cricket.

Closest subject = Rahul

Singular

✅ **plays**

# Common Placement Trap 🚨

Question:

> Either the manager or the employees ____ responsible.

Options:

A) is

B) are

Many students immediately see **manager** first and choose **is**.

Wrong.

Always check the word nearest the verb.

```text
Either the manager or the employees are responsible.

                           ↑
```

Closest subject = employees

Answer:

✅ **are**

---

# Difference: "And" vs. "Either...Or"

| Sentence                   | Meaning         | Verb            |
| -------------------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Rahul **and** Amit         | Both            | **are**         |
| Either Rahul **or** Amit   | One of them     | Closest subject |
| Neither Rahul **nor** Amit | Neither of them | Closest subject |

---

# Memory Trick

Imagine the verb is **lazy**. 😄

It doesn't look all the way to the beginning of the sentence.

It only looks at the **nearest subject**.

```text
Either Rahul or the boys are coming.

                👀
              Verb looks here
```

---

# Common Mistakes

❌ Either the students or the teacher **are** coming.

Why wrong?

Closest subject = **teacher** (singular)

✅ Either the students or the teacher **is** coming.

---

❌ Neither my brother nor my parents **has** arrived.

Closest subject = **parents** (plural)

✅ Neither my brother nor my parents **have** arrived.

---

# Summary

| Pattern         | Rule                                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Either A or B   | Verb agrees with **B** (closest subject) |
| Neither A nor B | Verb agrees with **B** (closest subject) |

## One important note

This is the **standard rule tested in placement exams**. In modern English, especially in conversation, native speakers sometimes rewrite these sentences (for example, "Either the teacher or the students are coming" may be rephrased to avoid awkward agreement). For aptitude tests, however, **always follow the "closest subject" rule**. It is the expected answer.

Excellent question.

The answer is **a lot**. In placement exams, they don't always use **either...or** and **neither...nor**. There are several connectors that affect Subject–Verb Agreement.

Let's group them.

---

# 1. **Either...Or** ✅

Verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.

Example:

* Either Rahul or his friends **are** coming.
* Either the students or the teacher **is** coming.

---

# 2. **Neither...Nor** ✅

Same rule.

Verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.

Examples:

* Neither Rahul nor Amit **is** here.
* Neither the teacher nor the students **are** ready.

---

# 3. **Not Only...But Also** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Same rule!**

Verb agrees with the **nearest subject**.

### Examples

* Not only Rahul but also his friends **are** coming.
* Not only the students but also the teacher **is** present.

---

# 4. **Not...But**

Again,

Nearest subject decides.

### Examples

* Not Rahul but his friends **are** responsible.
* Not the students but the principal **is** responsible.

---

# All Four Follow the Same Rule

| Pattern             | Verb Agrees With |
| ------------------- | ---------------- |
| Either...Or         | Nearest subject  |
| Neither...Nor       | Nearest subject  |
| Not Only...But Also | Nearest subject  |
| Not...But           | Nearest subject  |

---

# Examples

### Singular Last

> Either the boys or Rahul **plays** football.

Rahul is nearest.

Singular.

---

### Plural Last

> Either Rahul or the boys **play** football.

Boys are nearest.

Plural.

---

Same for every pattern.
