This is actually a **very smart exam strategy**, especially for **MCQ-based Para Jumbles**.

The slide says:

> **"Check options first and sentence later on, and try to eliminate options. Once the appropriate option is found, no need to read other sentences."**

Let's understand what this means.

---

## ❌ What most students do

Suppose you get this question:

**A.** He finally reached home.

**B.** Rahul finished his office work.

**C.** He got on the bus.

**D.** He left the office.

Options:

1. B D C A
2. D B C A
3. C B D A
4. B C D A

Most students do this:

* Read A
* Read B
* Read C
* Read D
* Try to arrange all four
* Compare with all four options

This takes **1–2 minutes**.

---

# ✅ What the teacher suggests

Instead of arranging the whole paragraph yourself,

**look at the options first.**

Notice:

| Option | First sentence |
| ------ | -------------- |
| 1      | B              |
| 2      | D              |
| 3      | C              |
| 4      | B              |

Immediately ask:

> Which sentence can logically start the paragraph?

Sentence C:

> **He got on the bus.**

Who is **He**?

Unknown.

❌ Can't start.

Eliminate Option 3.

---

Now compare B and D.

B:

> Rahul finished his office work.

This introduces Rahul.

D:

> He left the office.

Again "He" is unknown.

So D can't start.

Eliminate Option 2.

Now only Options 1 and 4 remain.

---

Look again.

Option 1:

B → D

Makes sense.

Rahul finishes work.

↓

Leaves office.

Option 4:

B → C

Rahul finishes work.

↓

Gets on the bus.

Something is missing.

How did he get from office to bus?

Option 1 feels more logical.

Now continue.

D → C

Leaves office.

↓

Gets on the bus.

Perfect.

Then

C → A

Gets on the bus.

↓

Finally reaches home.

✅ Correct answer:

**B D C A**

You solved it mostly by **eliminating options**, not by arranging every sentence from scratch.

---

# Another example

Sentences:

A. Therefore, pollution increased.

B. More factories were built.

C. The government introduced new rules.

D. Air quality improved.

Options:

1. B A C D
2. A B C D
3. C D B A
4. B C A D

---

### Step 1

Look at first sentences.

Option 2 starts with

> Therefore...

Can a paragraph begin with **Therefore**?

No.

❌ Eliminate Option 2 immediately.

---

### Step 2

Only Options 1, 3, and 4 remain.

You already know:

Factories built

↓

Pollution increased

This pair **B → A** is fixed.

Now look at the options.

Only Option 1 has **B A** together.

You don't even need to analyze the rest.

Answer found.

---

# Why is this strategy useful?

Imagine there are **5 sentences**.

Arranging them from scratch has **120 possible orders (5!)**.

But if there are only **4 answer options**, you don't need to think of all 120 possibilities.

Instead, you simply ask:

* Which option starts correctly?
* Which option has the right sentence pair?
* Which option has the correct ending?

Very quickly, only one option remains.

---

# But be careful ⚠️

This trick works **only when the exam gives answer options** like:

* A B C D
* B C A D
* D A C B

It **does not work** in exams where you have to type the sequence yourself or arrange the sentences without options.

---

# For TCS Ignite

This is an excellent strategy because TCS verbal questions are usually **MCQ-based**. You can often eliminate wrong options by checking:

1. **The opening sentence** (avoid starting with pronouns or conclusion words like "Therefore", "However", "Thus").
2. **Obvious sentence pairs** (e.g., noun → pronoun, cause → effect).
3. **The closing sentence** (often contains a conclusion or result).

Many TCS candidates solve para jumbles faster using option elimination than by trying to build the entire paragraph from scratch.
