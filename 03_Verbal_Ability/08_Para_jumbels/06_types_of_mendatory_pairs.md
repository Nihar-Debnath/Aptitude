This slide is explaining the **first type of Mandatory Pair** in Para Jumbles:

# Type 1: Names and Pronouns

The rule is:

> **A pronoun (he, she, his, her, it, they, this, etc.) must come after the noun or name it refers to.**

A pronoun **cannot introduce** a person or thing.

---

## Let's look at the example.

### A.

> **Jethalal's personality sets him apart from the rest.**

This sentence introduces **Jethalal**.

---

### B.

> **Nothing is too small for his attention.**

Who is **his**?

It refers to **Jethalal**.

So B **cannot** come before A.

---

### C.

> **He has a fanatical devotion to detail.**

Who is **He**?

Again, it refers to **Jethalal**.

So C also **cannot** come before A.

---

### D.

> **This is what makes him a different guy.**

"This" refers to an idea already mentioned.

"Him" refers to Jethalal.

So D also **cannot** come first.

---

# Step 1: Find the opening sentence

Which sentence introduces the topic?

Only **A**.

So the paragraph **must start with A**.

Immediately eliminate:

❌ C (starts with "He")

❌ D (starts with "This")

Only these remain:

* A. **ACBD**
* B. **ABCD**

---

# Step 2: Find another mandatory pair

Look at B and C.

### C

> He has a fanatical devotion to detail.

This tells us something about Jethalal.

### B

> Nothing is too small for his attention.

This explains **how** he is devoted to detail.

So:

C → B

is more logical.

---

Then D concludes:

> This is what makes him a different guy.

So the final order becomes:

A → C → B → D

✅ **Answer: ACBD**

---

# Why is this called a Mandatory Pair?

Because these sentences **must stay together**.

Examples:

### Example 1

**Rahul bought a bike.**

**He rides it every day.**

Pair:

Rahul → He

---

### Example 2

**India is the seventh-largest country.**

**It has a population of over a billion.**

Pair:

India → It

---

### Example 3

**The company launched a new product.**

**It became an instant success.**

Pair:

Product → It

---

# Important Pronouns to watch for

Whenever you see these words, ask yourself:

> **Who or what is this referring to?**

| Pronoun | Must refer to                      |
| ------- | ---------------------------------- |
| He      | A male person mentioned earlier    |
| She     | A female person mentioned earlier  |
| His     | A male noun mentioned earlier      |
| Her     | A female noun mentioned earlier    |
| It      | A thing or idea mentioned earlier  |
| They    | People or things mentioned earlier |
| Them    | People or things mentioned earlier |
| This    | An idea or event mentioned earlier |
| These   | Multiple things mentioned earlier  |
| Those   | Multiple things mentioned earlier  |
| Such    | An idea already introduced         |

---

# TCS Ignite Shortcut ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

When solving a para jumble:

1. **Circle all the pronouns** (`he`, `she`, `his`, `it`, `this`, `they`, etc.).
2. **Find the noun or name they refer to.**
3. Treat those as a **mandatory pair**.
4. Eliminate any option that breaks that pair.

This single trick can often eliminate **2–3 options in just a few seconds**, making it one of the highest-value strategies for TCS-style MCQ para jumbles.
