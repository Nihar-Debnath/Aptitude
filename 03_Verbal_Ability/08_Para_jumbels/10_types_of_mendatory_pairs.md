This isn't really a **new mandatory pair**. It's teaching you **how to identify the starting sentence** of a paragraph.

In fact, this is one of the **highest-scoring concepts** in Para Jumbles because if you find the first sentence, you can eliminate many options immediately.

---

# Type 4: How to Identify the Starting Sentence

## Rule 1: The first sentence introduces the topic ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The first sentence usually introduces:

* A person
* A place
* An idea
* A concept
* A definition

### Example

A. Artificial Intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence by machines.

B. It is used in healthcare and finance.

Which comes first?

✅ A

Why?

Because A introduces **Artificial Intelligence**.

"It" in B refers to AI.

---

## Rule 2: The first sentence usually has NO pronouns ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

If a sentence starts with:

* He
* She
* It
* They
* This
* These
* Those
* Such

it usually **cannot** be the first sentence.

Example

A. Rahul won the competition.

B. He celebrated with his friends.

Who is **He**?

Rahul.

So

✅ A → B

---

## Rule 3: Definitions usually come first ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

A. Blockchain is a distributed digital ledger.

B. It is used in cryptocurrencies.

Definition first.

Explanation later.

---

## Rule 4: Universal truths often start paragraphs ⭐⭐⭐

Example

> Water boils at 100°C at sea level.

This is a complete statement.

It doesn't depend on any previous sentence.

So it can start.

---

## Rule 5: Facts can be opening sentences ⭐⭐⭐

Example

> India is the seventh-largest country in the world.

This introduces the topic.

Then details follow.

---

## Rule 6: Philosophical statements often begin paragraphs ⭐⭐⭐

Example

> Success comes to those who work consistently.

After this, the author explains the idea.

---

## Rule 7: Avoid sentences with reference words ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Words like:

* this
* that
* these
* those
* such
* former
* latter
* above
* below

refer to something already mentioned.

Example

> This made the company profitable.

"This" refers to an earlier event.

So it **cannot** be the first sentence.

---

## Rule 8: Look for clue words

Words like:

* Firstly
* Initially
* In the beginning
* Once upon a time
* To begin with

often indicate the beginning.

Example

> In the beginning, the company had only five employees.

Likely the first sentence.

---

# Let's apply this to a quick example

### A

It became one of India's largest IT companies.

### B

Infosys was founded in 1981.

### C

The company expanded rapidly.

### D

Today, it operates in many countries.

### Step 1: Find the starting sentence

* A starts with **It** ❌
* D starts with **Today** (sounds like a later stage) ❌
* C says **expanded rapidly** (after it was founded) ❌
* B introduces **Infosys** ✅

So B is first.

Then:

B → C → A → D

# TCS Ignite Shortcut

Whenever you open a para jumble:

### Step 1 (5 seconds)

Look at the options.

Suppose:

```
A. BCAD
B. ACBD
C. DBCA
D. CABD
```

Notice the first letters:

```
B
A
D
C
```

Now ask:

> Which of these four sentences can naturally start a paragraph?

You often eliminate **2–3 options immediately**.

# Your Complete TCS Method (so far)

This is the sequence I'd recommend you follow in every MCQ:

### ✅ Step 1

**Check the options first.**

* Which sentence appears as the first sentence?
* Which pairs repeat?

### ✅ Step 2

**Find the opening sentence.**

* General statement
* Definition
* Topic introduction
* Person introduction
* No unresolved pronouns

### ✅ Step 3

**Find the strongest mandatory pair.**

* Name → Pronoun
* General → Specific
* Assertion → Example
* Cause → Effect
* Definition → Explanation

### ✅ Step 4

**Eliminate options** that violate the opening sentence or mandatory pairs.

### ✅ Step 5

**Read the remaining option once** to make sure the paragraph flows naturally.

This is a fast, exam-oriented approach and works very well for TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Accenture, and similar MCQ-based para jumble questions.
