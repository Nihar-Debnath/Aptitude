# Method 1: Forward Linking (Beginning → Details)

### Rule

> **If the first sentence introduces something, the next sentence usually explains it.**

Example:

> **Yuvraj has won a hackathon.**

Immediately ask:

> **What's the next natural question?**

* What hackathon?
* What did he build?
* What did he do?

Look at the options.

### P

> He had to write a code for adaptive voice recognition.

This answers the question.

So,

```text
Hackathon
      ↓
Hackathon details
```

This is called **Topic → Explanation**.

---

## Another Example

Sentence 1

> India launched Chandrayaan-3.

Next sentence should be

✅ The mission landed near the Moon's south pole.

Not

❌ Therefore, India became famous.

Details come before conclusion.

---

# Method 2: Backward Linking (Ending ← Previous Sentence)

This is exactly what you noticed.

Sentence 2 says

> Alas, he already had an iPhone.

Ask yourself:

> **Where did the iPhone come from?**

Look at the sentences.

Only Q says

> ...he won an iPhone.

Immediately,

```text
Won an iPhone
        ↓
Already had an iPhone
```

These are naturally connected.

So,

Q must come just before sentence 2.

---

This is called

> **Keyword Linking**

or

> **Backward Connection**

because you start from the ending and move backward.

# This is actually how experts solve Para Jumbles.

They solve from **both ends**.

```text

Middle

Ending
```

Eventually everything joins.

## Your two observations are genuinely useful

What you said can be formalized as:

* ✅ **Forward Linking:** *Introduction → Immediate explanation/details.*
* ✅ **Backward Linking:** *Ending → Find the sentence that naturally leads into that ending.*

These aren't separate grammar rules, but they're excellent **reasoning strategies**. Combined with the opening/closing sentence rules and mandatory pairs, they'll help you solve MCQ para jumbles much faster. I actually recommend using both directions whenever the first and last sentences are fixed, just like in this question.
