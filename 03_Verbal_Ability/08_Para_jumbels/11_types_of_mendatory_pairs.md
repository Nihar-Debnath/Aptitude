# Type 5: How to Identify the Closing Sentence

The **closing sentence** is the sentence that **wraps up the paragraph**. It gives the **final thought, conclusion, result, opinion, or recommendation**.

Think of a paragraph like a movie:

* 🎬 **Start** → Introduce the story.
* 🎭 **Middle** → Explain or develop the story.
* 🏁 **End** → Finish the story.

---

# Rule 1: Look for Conclusion Words ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

If a sentence starts with words like:

* Therefore
* Hence
* Thus
* Finally
* Overall
* Consequently
* As a result

it is **usually** the last sentence.

### Example

A. It rained continuously for three days.

B. The river overflowed.

C. Therefore, many villages were flooded.

The order is:

```
A → B → C
```

Because **"Therefore"** introduces the conclusion.

---

# Rule 2: The Last Sentence Gives the Final Result ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example:

A. The company launched a new product.

B. Customers liked it.

C. Sales increased rapidly.

D. The company became the market leader.

The final outcome is:

> **The company became the market leader.**

So D is the closing sentence.

---

# Rule 3: Recommendation or Opinion Usually Comes Last ⭐⭐⭐

Example:

A. Plastic pollution is increasing.

B. Marine life is affected.

C. Therefore, governments should ban single-use plastics.

The recommendation comes at the end.

---

# Rule 4: Future Prediction Often Comes Last ⭐⭐⭐

Example:

A. AI is improving rapidly.

B. Many industries are adopting AI.

C. It is likely to transform the future of work.

Sentence C is the conclusion.

---

# Rule 5: Never Choose a Sentence That Introduces New Information ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The last sentence should **summarize or conclude**, not suddenly introduce a new topic.

❌ Bad ending:

> Microsoft was founded in 1975.

This introduces a completely new fact.

---

# Example

### A

India has made significant progress in digital payments.

### B

UPI transactions have grown every year.

### C

Many small businesses now accept online payments.

### D

Therefore, India has become one of the world's leading digital payment economies.

Which is the last sentence?

Obviously,

**D**

because it summarizes everything.

---

# TCS MCQ Trick

Suppose the options are:

```
A. BCAD
B. ACBD
C. BCDA
D. CABD
```

Look only at the last letters:

```
D
D
A
D
```

Three options end with **D**.

Ask yourself:

> Does D sound like a conclusion?

If yes,

you've already eliminated Option C without reading much.

This is exactly like your **"check the first sentence first"** strategy, but now you're also checking the **last sentence**.

---

# Final Algorithm (This is what I'd follow in TCS)

## Step 1 ✅ Check the options

Notice:

* Which sentence starts most options?
* Which sentence ends most options?
* Which pairs repeat?

---

## Step 2 ✅ Find the opening sentence

Ask:

* Does it introduce the topic?
* Is it a definition?
* Is it a general statement?
* Does it avoid unresolved pronouns?

---

## Step 3 ✅ Find the strongest mandatory pair

Look for:

* Name → Pronoun
* General → Specific
* Assertion → Example
* Cause → Effect
* Definition → Explanation

---

## Step 4 ✅ Find the closing sentence

Look for:

* Therefore
* Hence
* Thus
* Finally
* Overall
* Conclusion
* Final result
* Recommendation
* Prediction

---

## Step 5 ✅ Eliminate options

Don't arrange all the sentences. Remove options that violate the opening, the mandatory pairs, or the ending.

---

## Step 6 ✅ Read the remaining option once

Confirm that the paragraph flows naturally.

---

# My one addition to these slides

The slides teach **mandatory pairs**, which are excellent. But in real exams, don't think:

> "I must always find a mandatory pair first."

Instead, think:

> **"What is the strongest clue in this question?"**

Sometimes it's the opening sentence, sometimes a pronoun, sometimes a connector like **However**, and sometimes the closing sentence. Use the strongest clue first, eliminate options, and then verify the remaining sequence. This flexible approach is more reliable than forcing the same rule on every question.
