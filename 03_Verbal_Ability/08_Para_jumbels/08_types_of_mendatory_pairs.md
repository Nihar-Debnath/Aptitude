# Type 3: General → Specific

## Concept

The first sentence introduces a **broad idea**.

The next sentence(s) provide **specific details, examples, definitions, or explanations**.

Think of it like a camera:

📷 **Zoomed Out (General)** → 📷 **Zoomed In (Specific)**

Example:

> **General:** India is famous for its festivals.
>
> **Specific:** Diwali, Holi, and Durga Puja are celebrated across the country.

You cannot start with the specific details before introducing the general topic.

---

# Let's solve the question

### Sentence B

> **For traditional companies offshore work means work done abroad.**

This is a **general definition**.

It tells us the usual meaning of "offshore."

---

### Sentence C

> **It is just the opposite in the software business.**

Notice the word **"It."**

What is "It"?

It refers to the definition in **B**.

So,

✅ **B → C** is a mandatory pair.

Also, C cannot start the paragraph because "It" has no reference.

---

### Sentence A

> **Work done by Cognizant in the US is called on site.**

Now we're talking specifically about **Cognizant**.

This is an example of how software companies use these terms.

---

### Sentence D

> **And work done in Bangalore is called Offshore.**

This continues the explanation started in A.

Notice the word **"And"**.

A sentence starting with **And** almost never starts a paragraph.

So,

✅ **A → D** is another mandatory pair.

---

# Now connect the two pairs

We have:

```text
B → C
```

and

```text
A → D
```

How do they connect?

Read them:

### B

Traditional companies:

Offshore = Abroad.

↓

### C

Software companies are the opposite.

↓

### A

Example:

US work = Onsite.

↓

### D

Bangalore work = Offshore.

This flows perfectly.

---

# Check the options

### A. BCAD ✅

```text
B → C → A → D
```

Perfect.

---

### B. ADCB ❌

Starts with A.

But C (opposite) has no previous statement.

Wrong.

---

### C. ADBC ❌

Starts with a specific example before explaining the general concept.

Wrong.

---

### D. BADC ❌

B goes directly to A.

Then C says

> "It is just the opposite..."

Too late.

Wrong.

---

# Answer

✅ **Option A (BCAD)**

---

# How to identify a General → Specific pair

Look for these clues.

## General Statement

Usually contains:

* In general...
* Generally
* Usually
* Most
* All
* Many
* A definition
* A concept

Example:

> Solar energy is becoming popular.

---

## Specific Sentence

Contains:

* Example
* Particular company
* Particular person
* Numbers
* Statistics
* Specific place
* Specific event

Example:

> Tata Power installed over 2 GW of solar capacity.

---

# Another Example

### A

> Birds migrate to warmer regions during winter.

### B

> Siberian cranes travel thousands of kilometers every year.

Here,

A is general.

B is specific.

So,

```text
A → B
```

---

# TCS Ignite Shortcut ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Whenever you read a sentence, ask:

> **Is this introducing the topic, or is it giving details?**

If it's introducing the topic:

* It should appear earlier.

If it's giving details:

* It should appear later.

## Summary of Mandatory Pairs so far

| Type                       | Pattern              | Example                                           |
| -------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Name → Pronoun**      | Rahul → He           | Proper noun before pronoun                        |
| **2. Assertion → Example** | Claim → Proof        | "Software stocks are booming." → "ABX rose 150%." |
| **3. General → Specific**  | Broad idea → Details | "Offshore means..." → "Cognizant example..."      |

One extra tip for this question: **don't rely on just one rule**. The correct answer becomes obvious because **multiple clues work together**:

* **B → C** (`It` refers to B).
* **A → D** (`And` continues A).
* General explanation comes before the specific Cognizant example.

When two or three clues point to the same option, you can be much more confident in your answer.
