**Para Jumbles** (also called **Sentence Rearrangement**) is a very common topic in **Verbal Ability**, especially in exams like **TCS Ignite, TCS Smart, Cognizant GenC, Accenture, Capgemini, Wipro**, and many banking exams.

The goal is simple:

> You are given **4–6 sentences in a random order**, and you have to arrange them to form a meaningful paragraph.

---

## Example

Suppose you get these four sentences:

**A.** Finally, she reached the top of the mountain.
**B.** She woke up early in the morning.
**C.** After eating breakfast, she started her trek.
**D.** She enjoyed the beautiful sunrise from the peak.

Which is the correct order?

Let's think like a story.

* Can someone reach the mountain **before waking up?** ❌
* First she **wakes up**.
* Then she **has breakfast and starts trekking**.
* Then she **reaches the top**.
* Finally she **enjoys the sunrise**.

✅ Correct order:

**B → C → A → D**

---

# How to solve Para Jumbles

There are several clues.

## Rule 1: Find the Opening Sentence

The first sentence usually:

* introduces a person
* introduces a place
* introduces an idea
* doesn't refer to something mentioned earlier

Example:

**A.** He then submitted the assignment.

**B.** Rahul completed his project.

Which comes first?

Obviously,

**Rahul completed his project.**

Then,

**He submitted the assignment.**

Notice something?

Sentence A starts with **He**.

Who is "He"?

We don't know until Rahul is introduced.

So,

B → A

---

## Rule 2: Pronouns come later

Words like

* he
* she
* they
* it
* this
* these
* those
* such

usually refer to something already mentioned.

Example

A. They were very expensive.

B. I bought a pair of shoes yesterday.

Correct:

B → A

Because "They" = shoes.

---

## Rule 3: Look for chronological order

Many paragraphs describe events.

Example

* Woke up
* Took bath
* Went to office
* Returned home

Simple timeline.

---

## Rule 4: Cause before Effect

Example

A. Therefore, the match was cancelled.

B. It rained heavily throughout the day.

Rain happened first.

So

B → A

---

## Rule 5: General before Specific

Example

A. Tigers are one of the strongest predators.

B. The Bengal tiger is found mainly in India.

General statement first.

Then specific example.

---

## Rule 6: Definition first

Example

A. Artificial Intelligence is the ability of machines to imitate human intelligence.

B. It is used in self-driving cars.

Definition first.

Then applications.

---

## Rule 7: Connecting words

These words tell you where a sentence belongs.

| Word        | Meaning             |
| ----------- | ------------------- |
| However     | Contrast            |
| Therefore   | Result              |
| Moreover    | Addition            |
| Furthermore | Addition            |
| Hence       | Result              |
| Thus        | Result              |
| Instead     | Alternative         |
| Although    | Contrast            |
| Finally     | Ending              |
| Meanwhile   | Simultaneous action |

Example

A. Therefore, production increased.

B. The company bought new machines.

Correct

B → A

---

## Rule 8: Look for repeated keywords

Example

A. Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity.

B. This technology is becoming cheaper every year.

"This technology" refers to **solar panels**.

So

A → B

# A Fast Method for TCS

When you see a para jumble:

**Step 1:** Find the first sentence.

* It introduces the topic.
* It usually doesn't begin with pronouns like *he, she, it, they, this*.

**Step 2:** Find obvious sentence pairs.

* Pronoun → noun (e.g., *Rahul* → *He*)
* Cause → effect
* Problem → solution
* Question → answer
* General → example

**Step 3:** Arrange the remaining sentences into a logical flow.

**Step 4:** Read the completed paragraph once.

* Does it sound natural?
* Does every sentence connect smoothly to the next?

---

# Difficulty in TCS Ignite

From what you've described about **TCS Ignite**, the para jumbles are usually **basic to moderate**, not CAT-level. They often test whether you can recognize a logical sequence rather than solve highly ambiguous arrangements.

So focus on:

* Pronoun references
* Chronological order
* Cause and effect
* Introduction before details
* Linking words (however, therefore, finally, etc.)

Mastering these patterns will solve most TCS-style para jumble questions quickly and accurately.
