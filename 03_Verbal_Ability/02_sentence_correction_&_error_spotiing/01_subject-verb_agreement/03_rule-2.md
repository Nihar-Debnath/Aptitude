# Rule 2: **The Verb Must Agree with the REAL Subject**

Sometimes, extra words come between the subject and the verb. **Ignore those extra words.**

## Formula

> **Subject + extra words + Verb**

Find the **real subject** first.

---

### Example 1

> **The box of chocolates is** on the table.

Ask:
**What is the subject?**

Many students say **chocolates** ❌

But the real subject is **box** ✅

* **Box** = Singular
* **is** = Correct

---

### Example 2

> **The quality of the products is** excellent.

Real subject = **quality** (not products)

✅ The quality **is** excellent.

---

### Example 3

> **The books on the shelf belong** to me.

Real subject = **books**

* Books = Plural
* belong = Correct

---

## Words to Ignore

If you see these after the subject, **don't let them confuse you**:

* of
* with
* along with
* together with
* as well as
* in
* on
* besides

### Example

> **The teacher, along with the students, is** attending the seminar.

Real subject = **teacher**

✅ **is**

Not **are**.

---

### Another Example

> **The captain, as well as the players, was** excited.

Real subject = **captain**

✅ **was**

---

# Common Placement Trap 🚨

❌ **The list of items are on the table.**

Many people choose **are** because they see **items**.

But:

Subject = **list**

Correct:

✅ **The list of items is on the table.**

---

## Memory Trick

Think of it like this:

> **Find the boss (subject). Ignore the followers (extra words).**

Only the **boss** decides the verb.

---

## Mini Quiz

Choose the correct verb.

1. **The bouquet of flowers ___ beautiful.**

* A) are
* B) **is** ✅

2. **The players on the field ___ ready.**

* A) **are** ✅
* B) is

3. **The manager, along with his employees, ___ attending the meeting.**

* A) **is** ✅
* B) are

---

### So far, you only know **2 rules**:

1. **One subject → Singular verb (`He plays`)**
   **Many subjects → Plural verb (`They play`)**

2. **Always identify the real subject. Ignore extra words like `of`, `with`, `along with`, `as well as`.**

These two rules alone solve a large number of Subject–Verb Agreement questions in placement exams. Once you're comfortable with them, we'll move to **Rule 3: Subjects joined by "and."**
