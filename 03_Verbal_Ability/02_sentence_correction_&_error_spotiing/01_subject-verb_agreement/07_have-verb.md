Just like **be** has its own Subject–Verb Agreement rule, **have** also has one.

---

# Rule: **Have / Has Agreement**

The verb **"have"** changes according to the subject.

| Subject | Verb     |
| ------- | -------- |
| I       | **have** |
| You     | **have** |
| We      | **have** |
| They    | **have** |
| He      | **has**  |
| She     | **has**  |
| It      | **has**  |

---

## Examples ✅

* I **have** a laptop.

* You **have** a bike.

* We **have** a meeting.

* They **have** many friends.

* He **has** a car.

* She **has** long hair.

* It **has** four legs.

---

## Wrong ❌

* He **have** a car.
* She **have** a phone.
* They **has** homework.
* I **has** a book.

---

# Memory Trick

Notice something?

| Subject     | Be Verb | Have Verb |
| ----------- | ------- | --------- |
| I           | am      | have      |
| He/She/It   | is      | has       |
| You/We/They | are     | have      |

See the pattern?

Only **He / She / It** are different.

Everything else uses **have**.

---

# Present Perfect Tense

This is where you'll see **has/have** the most.

### Singular

* He **has finished** the work.
* She **has arrived**.
* It **has stopped** raining.

### Plural

* They **have finished** the work.
* We **have arrived**.
* You **have completed** the task.

---

# Simple Present

"Have" can also mean **possess**.

Examples:

* I **have** a laptop.
* She **has** a laptop.
* They **have** a house.
* He **has** a house.

---

# Easy Formula

```text
I / You / We / They  → have

He / She / It        → has
```

---

# Compare All Three Together

| Subject | Be  | Have | Action Verb |
| ------- | --- | ---- | ----------- |
| I       | am  | have | play        |
| You     | are | have | play        |
| He      | is  | has  | plays       |
| She     | is  | has  | plays       |
| It      | is  | has  | plays       |
| We      | are | have | play        |
| They    | are | have | play        |

This table is one of the most important things to memorize for Subject–Verb Agreement.

---

## I think we should slightly improve our learning plan.

Instead of only learning **12 SVA rules**, let's first learn the **verb families**:

1. ✅ Normal verbs (play/plays, work/works)
2. ✅ **Be** verbs (am/is/are)
3. ✅ **Have** verbs (have/has)
4. Then move to the special SVA rules (`and`, `either...or`, `everyone`, `one of`, etc.).

That way, when we reach examples like **"The owner and manager has arrived"**, you'll already know **why it's "has" instead of "have."**

I think this order is much more logical and much less confusing.
