> Q. (a) Hari is a very bright student (b) and he always pay attention (c) to what his teacher have to teach


Let's solve it exactly like you should in an **error spotting exam**.

### Sentence

> (A) Hari is a very bright student
> (B) and he always **pay** attention
> (C) to what his teacher **have** to teach

---

# Step 1: Find the Subject

### Part A

> **Hari** is a very bright student.

Subject = **Hari** (Singular)

This part is correct.

---

# Step 2: Check the Verb

### Part B

> and he always **pay** attention

Subject = **he** (Singular)

Rule:

| Subject          | Verb |
| ---------------- | ---- |
| I, You, We, They | pay  |
| He, She, It      | pays |

So,

❌ He always **pay**

✅ He always **pays**

There is an error in **Part B**.

---

# Step 3: Check Part C

> to what his teacher **have** to teach

Subject = **teacher** (Singular)

Rule:

| Subject  | Verb |
| -------- | ---- |
| Teacher  | has  |
| Teachers | have |

So,

❌ his teacher **have**

✅ his teacher **has**

There is **another error** in **Part C**.

---

# Correct Sentence

> **Hari is a very bright student, and he always pays attention to what his teacher has to teach.**

---

# But wait... there are TWO errors!

This is important.

A good error-spotting question should contain **only one grammatical error**.

Here we have:

* ❌ pay → **pays**
* ❌ have → **has**

So this question is **not well designed** if the instructions say "Find the error" (singular).

---

# If this appeared in an exam

You should think:

> "The question is flawed because both B and C contain errors."

If the exam allows **multiple errors**, then:

* ✅ **B** is incorrect.
* ✅ **C** is also incorrect.

---

## Grammar Rules Used

### Rule 1: Subject–Verb Agreement

**He/She/It + Verb + s/es**

* He pays attention. ✅
* She studies hard. ✅
* It works well. ✅

---

### Rule 2: "Has" vs "Have"

| Subject | Correct Verb |
| ------- | ------------ |
| I       | have         |
| You     | have         |
| We      | have         |
| They    | have         |
| He      | has          |
| She     | has          |
| It      | has          |
| Hari    | has          |
| Teacher | has          |

---

### Final Answer

* **Part B:** ❌ **pay** → ✅ **pays**
* **Part C:** ❌ **have** → ✅ **has**

So, as written, the sentence contains **two grammatical errors**, making it an invalid single-error spotting question.
