This is exactly the kind of question that CAT, GMAT, GRE, and other competitive exams love to ask.

The trick is **NOT grammar first.**
The trick is:

> **Find what is being compared.**

Let's solve it like an examiner.

---

## Step 1: Read the sentence

> The prosecutor's **argument**, which hinged on a little known provision in a 1972 law, differed considerably from **the plaintiff**, who contended that a landmark 1999 Supreme Court decision supported his argument.

Now ask yourself:

**What differed?**

> **The prosecutor's argument differed from ____**

So we are comparing

* prosecutor's **argument**
* plaintiff ❌

Immediately something feels wrong.

---

## Rule 1: Comparisons must compare similar things.

Never compare

* argument ↔ person ❌
* car ↔ driver ❌
* book ↔ author ❌

Compare

* argument ↔ argument ✅
* car ↔ car ✅
* book ↔ book ✅

Examples

❌ My laptop is faster than John.

Are we comparing a laptop with a human?

Correct:

✅ My laptop is faster than **John's laptop**.

---

## Step 2: See what every option means

### A. the plaintiff

Sentence becomes

> argument differed from the plaintiff

Argument vs person

❌ Wrong comparison

Reject.

---

### B. that of the plaintiff

Sentence becomes

> argument differed from **that of the plaintiff**

Now understand "that".

"that" = argument

So it becomes

> argument differed from **the argument of the plaintiff**

Now comparison is

argument ↔ argument

Perfect.

✅ Correct

---

### C. those from the plaintiff

"those" is plural.

But "argument" is singular.

Also

> those from the plaintiff

doesn't clearly refer to arguments.

❌ Wrong.

---

### D. that espoused by the plaintiff

This means

> the argument espoused (supported/advocated) by the plaintiff

Actually this also compares

argument ↔ argument

Grammatically this is possible.

But notice something.

The original sentence already says

> the plaintiff contended that...

So adding

> espoused by the plaintiff

is unnecessary and awkward.

Sentence correction prefers the **shortest correct expression.**

Since **that of the plaintiff** already expresses the meaning perfectly,

D is not the best answer.

---

### E. that from the plaintiff

"from" suggests

received from

or

came from

We don't normally say

> argument from the plaintiff

We say

> argument **of** the plaintiff

or

> argument **made by** the plaintiff.

So

❌ Wrong preposition.

---

# Therefore

✅ Answer = **B. that of the plaintiff**

---

# But HOW do you solve these questions every time?

Use this checklist.

## STEP 1

Find the comparison word.

Usually

* than
* as
* unlike
* like
* compared with
* compared to
* different from
* rather than

Here

> differed from

---

## STEP 2

Circle both sides.

```
The prosecutor's ARGUMENT
        differed from
the plaintiff
```

---

## STEP 3

Ask

Are they the same type?

Argument vs Person

No.

Need an implied noun.

---

## STEP 4

Look for pronouns

Exams love these.

### that

Used for singular nouns.

```
car → that
argument → that
book → that
```

Example

My car is faster than **that of** my brother.

=

My car is faster than **my brother's car**.

---

### those

Used for plural nouns.

Example

The apples in Kashmir are sweeter than **those** in Delhi.

those = apples

---

### one

Used for one item.

I need a pen.

Do you have one?

---

### ones

Plural.

I like the blue shirts, not the white ones.

---

# Another important rule

## "that of"

means

> the noun belonging to someone

Example

His salary is higher than **that of** his brother.

=

His salary is higher than **his brother's salary.**

Not

salary > brother

---

## More examples

### Example 1

❌ The population of India is larger than China.

Population vs country

Correct

✅ The population of India is larger than **that of China.**

---

### Example 2

❌ My score is higher than Rahul.

Correct

✅ My score is higher than **Rahul's.**

or

✅ My score is higher than **that of Rahul.**

---

### Example 3

❌ The engine of BMW is more powerful than Audi.

Correct

✅ The engine of BMW is more powerful than **that of Audi.**

---

# Golden Rule for Sentence Correction

Whenever you see:

* than
* from
* like
* unlike
* compared with
* compared to

**Stop and ask:**

> **What two things are being compared?**

If the two things are different categories (person vs idea, city vs population, company vs product, etc.), the sentence is likely wrong. The correct answer often introduces **that**, **those**, or a possessive form to make the comparison logical.

---

This is one of the highest-frequency concepts in sentence correction. Once you master **logical comparisons** and the use of **that/those**, you'll be able to solve a large number of similar questions very quickly.
