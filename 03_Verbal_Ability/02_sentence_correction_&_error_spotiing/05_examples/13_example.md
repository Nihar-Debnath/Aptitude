This question tests one of the **most common Sentence Correction rules**:

# 👉 Parallelism

If a sentence contains a **list of actions**, they must all have the **same grammatical form**.

---

## Step 1: Ignore the extra information

The sentence is

> Warning that terrorists remain determined to strike the country, senior government officials urged citizens ______ should terrorists succeed in launching an attack.

Ignore

> Warning that terrorists remain determined to strike the country,

and

> should terrorists succeed in launching an attack.

Now you get the core sentence:

> Senior government officials urged citizens ______.

Now ask:

**Urged citizens to do what?**

There are **three actions**.

1. remain vigilant
2. report suspicious behavior
3. take actions

These three should be parallel.

---

## Step 2: Look at Option A

> to remain vigilant **and** report suspicious behavior, **taking** actions...

Notice the pattern.

```
to remain
report
taking
```

The third action is different.

It should also be "take".

❌ Wrong.

---

## Step 3: Look at Option B

> to remain vigilant,
> reporting suspicious behavior
> and take actions

Pattern becomes

```
to remain
reporting
take
```

Three different forms.

❌ Wrong.

---

## Step 4: Look at Option C

> to be remaining vigilant,
> reporting suspicious behavior,
> and taking actions

Pattern

```
to be remaining
reporting
taking
```

Besides sounding awkward,

"to be remaining" is incorrect here.

We simply say

> remain vigilant

❌ Wrong.

---

## Step 5: Look at Option D

> to remain vigilant,
> report suspicious behavior,
> and take actions

Pattern

```
remain
report
take
```

Perfect!

All are **base verbs** after one "to".

This is called **parallel structure**.

✅ Correct Answer = **D**

---

# The Rule Used

When one **"to"** introduces several verbs,

the "to" applies to all of them.

Example:

✅ I want

* to eat
* drink
* sleep

NOT

❌ to eat, drinking, and sleep

---

Another example

✅ She likes

* singing
* dancing
* reading

All are **-ing** forms.

Not

❌ singing, dance, and reading.

---

Another

✅ The company plans

* to hire
* train
* promote

Not

❌ to hire, training, and promote.

---

# A Shortcut for Exams

Whenever you see

* and
* or
* not only...but also
* either...or
* neither...nor

**Immediately check Parallelism.**

Ask yourself:

> Are all the connected words in the same grammatical form?

---

## Here is the pattern

```
to remain vigilant
report suspicious behavior
take actions
```

One **to** is enough.

English understands

```
to remain
(to) report
(to) take
```

The second and third "to" are omitted because they're understood.

---

# Exam Trick

The examiner intentionally changes **one verb** into another form.

For example,

```
to eat
drink
sleep
```

becomes

```
to eat
drinking
sleep
```

or

```
to eat
drink
sleeping
```

Just spotting that mismatch is often enough to eliminate the wrong options.

---

## Your Mental Algorithm for Such Questions

Whenever you see a sentence correction question:

1. **Find the main verb.**
2. **Find the list connected by** *and/or*.
3. **Write only the verbs.**
4. Check whether all verbs have the **same form**.

For this question:

```
remain
report
take
```

All three match.

✅ Hence **Option D** is correct.

Excellent question.

The problem in **Option C** is actually **two separate grammar problems**.

Let's examine it carefully.

---

## Option C

> **to be remaining vigilant, reporting suspicious behavior, and taking actions necessary...**

Let's split it.

```
to be remaining vigilant
reporting suspicious behavior
taking actions
```

At first glance, you might think:

> All three end in **-ing**, so isn't this parallel?

Not exactly.

---

# Problem 1: "to be remaining" is the wrong verb form

English has two forms here.

### Correct

> remain vigilant

or

> be vigilant

Both are natural.

Examples:

✅ Remain calm.

✅ Please remain seated.

✅ Be careful.

---

### Incorrect

> be remaining vigilant

This is a progressive (continuous) form.

We generally **do not use the continuous form after "urge someone to..."**

For example,

❌ The teacher urged us **to be studying**.

✅ The teacher urged us **to study**.

---

Another example

❌ The doctor advised him **to be eating** healthy food.

✅ The doctor advised him **to eat** healthy food.

---

So

> urged citizens **to be remaining vigilant**

sounds unnatural.

---

# Problem 2: The list is not parallel

Look at the intended actions.

The officials urged citizens

* to remain vigilant
* report suspicious behavior
* take action

These are **instructions**.

Instructions normally use the **base verb**.

Examples

The coach told us to

* run
* jump
* swim

NOT

* be running
* jumping
* swimming

---

# Why not use "-ing"?

Because **reporting** and **taking** become activities in progress, not instructions.

Compare these:

### Correct

The manager asked employees

* to arrive early
* submit reports
* attend meetings

---

### Wrong

The manager asked employees

* to be arriving early
* submitting reports
* attending meetings

It sounds as if the actions are already happening, rather than being requested.

---

# The best form is Option D

```
to remain vigilant
report suspicious behavior
take actions
```

Everything is in the **base verb** form.

---

# A simple trick

Whenever you see

> urge someone to...

the verb after **to** is almost always the **base form**.

Examples

✅ urge people to vote

✅ urge citizens to stay indoors

✅ urge students to study

Not

❌ urge people to be voting

❌ urge students to be studying

unless there is a very special context emphasizing an ongoing action.

---

## One more note

There's also a small style issue in the options:

> **take actions**

In modern English, it's much more common to say:

> **take action**

because **action** is usually an uncountable noun in this expression.

So the most natural version of the sentence would be:

> "...urged citizens **to remain vigilant, report suspicious behavior, and take action** necessary to speed up the recovery..."

That said, among the given choices, **Option D** is still the best because it fixes the major grammar issue: **parallelism** and the correct verb form after **urge someone to...**.
