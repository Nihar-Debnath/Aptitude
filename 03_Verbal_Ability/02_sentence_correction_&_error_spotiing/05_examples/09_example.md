Great! This is a **Sentence Improvement** question, not an Error Spotting question.

The **underlined part** is the part you have to check. Your job is to decide:

* Is the underlined part correct?
* If not, which option improves it?

---

# Question

The sentence is:

> **I have told the architect *to put on* his best in the assignment he was handling.**

The underlined part is:

> **to put on**

The options are:

A. **to put in**
B. **to have put up**
C. **is putting**
D. **to put down**
E. **No correction required**

---

# Step 1: Understand the meaning

The sentence means:

> I told the architect to **give his best effort** in the assignment.

Now ask:

Which phrasal verb means **give effort**?

---

# Step 2: Check each option

## A. to put in ✅

We have an English expression:

> **put in one's best**

Meaning:

> **to give one's best effort**

Examples:

* She put in her best effort.
* Please put in your best work.
* He put in a lot of effort.

So,

> I have told the architect **to put in** his best...

This is correct.

---

## B. to have put up ❌

"Put up" has different meanings.

Examples:

* put up a tent
* put up a poster
* put up with someone (= tolerate)

It does **not** mean "give your best."

Wrong.

---

## C. is putting ❌

Now the sentence becomes:

> I have told the architect **is putting**...

After **told**, we use:

> told + someone + **to + verb**

Examples:

* I told him **to study**.
* I told her **to come**.

Not:

* I told him is studying. ❌

---

## D. to put down ❌

"Put down" means:

* place something on a surface
* criticize someone
* write something down

Examples:

* Put down the book.
* Don't put me down.

Wrong meaning.

---

## E. No correction required ❌

Wrong because "put on" is incorrect here.

---

# Final Answer

✅ **Option A — to put in**

---

# Why is "put on" wrong?

Let's learn the phrasal verbs.

## Put on

Usually means:

### Wear

* Put on your shoes.
* Put on a jacket.

---

### Start

* Put on the TV.
* Put on some music.

---

### Pretend

* He put on a smile.
* She put on an accent.

None of these meanings fit the sentence.

---

## Put in

Means:

### Make an effort

* Put in your best effort.
* Put in hard work.
* Put in long hours.

This exactly matches the sentence.

---

# Why is "to" there?

Look at the sentence:

> I have told the architect **to put in** his best.

Whenever we use **tell** like this, the pattern is:

> tell + person + **to + base verb**

Examples:

* I told him **to come**.
* She told me **to wait**.
* They told us **to leave**.

So:

> told the architect **to put in** ✅

## New Topic: Phrasal Verbs

This question isn't really testing grammar. It's testing **phrasal verbs**.

A **phrasal verb** is:

> **Verb + Preposition/Adverb = A new meaning**

Examples:

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| put on       | wear                           |
| put in       | make an effort                 |
| put off      | postpone                       |
| put up       | erect / tolerate (put up with) |
| put down     | place on a surface / criticize |
| put out      | extinguish                     |
| put away     | store                          |

These are very common in placement tests like TCS NQT.

So this question is testing your knowledge of **phrasal verbs**, not tenses or subject–verb agreement.
