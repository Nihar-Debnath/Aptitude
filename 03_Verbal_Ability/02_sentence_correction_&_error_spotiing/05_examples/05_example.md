> Q. (a) Many big companies like Xiomi, Apple and  (b) Google have been launched many smartphones having latest (c) features like auto zoom, face recognition and much more.



Good question. Let's solve it like an examiner.

---

## Sentence

> **(A)** Many big companies like Xiomi, Apple and
>
> **(B)** Google **have been launched** many smartphones having latest
>
> **(C)** features like auto zoom, face recognition and much more.

---

# Step 1: Find the Subject

What is the subject?

> **Many big companies like Xiaomi, Apple and Google**

This is a **plural subject**.

Now ask:

**What are these companies doing?**

They are **launching smartphones**.

---

# Step 2: Check the Verb

The sentence says:

> **have been launched many smartphones**

Let's understand why this is wrong.

### "Have launched" = Active Voice

The companies perform the action.

> Apple **has launched** a new iPhone. ✅

> Google **has launched** a new Pixel phone. ✅

---

### "Have been launched" = Passive Voice

This means **someone launched the companies**.

Example:

> The company **was launched** in 2015.

Who was launched?

👉 **The company**, not the smartphone.

So,

> Companies **have been launched smartphones** ❌

This literally means:

> "The companies were launched smartphones."

That doesn't make sense.

---

# Step 3: Correct the Verb

It should be:

> **have launched**

So,

❌ have been launched

✅ have launched

---

# Correct Sentence

> **Many big companies like Xiaomi, Apple and Google have launched many smartphones having the latest features like auto zoom, face recognition and much more.**

---

# Is there any other error?

Yes, there is a **small issue**.

The sentence says:

> having **latest** features

In standard English, we usually say:

> **the latest features** ✅

or

> **latest features** (without "having")

For example:

* Smartphones with **the latest features**.
* Smartphones featuring **the latest features**.

However, in most error-spotting exams, the intended error is **Part B** because it is a clear grammar mistake (wrong voice).

---

# Grammar Rule

## Active vs Passive

### Active Voice

Subject performs the action.

* Apple **launched** the iPhone.
* Google **develops** Android.
* Companies **launch** smartphones.

---

### Passive Voice

Subject receives the action.

* The iPhone **was launched by** Apple.
* Android **was developed by** Google.
* The smartphone **was launched by** Xiaomi.

---

## Easy Trick

Ask yourself:

> **Who is doing the action?**

If the subject is doing the action, use **active voice**.

If the subject is receiving the action, use **passive voice**.

Here:

* Companies are **doing** the launching.

So use:

> **have launched** ✅

Not:

> **have been launched** ❌

---

## Final Answer

✅ **Error: Part (B)**

**Correction:**

> **Google have launched many smartphones...**

(Although in natural English, because **Google** is a singular company name, we'd usually say **Google has launched**. Here, however, the subject is the whole plural phrase **"Many big companies like Xiaomi, Apple and Google"**, so **have launched** is correct.)

## Imagine this situation

### Sentence 1

> **Apple has launched a new iPhone.** ✅

Ask yourself:

**Who did the launching?**

👉 Apple.

Apple is **doing** the action.

```
Apple  ------------>  launches  ------------>  iPhone
(doer)                                    (object)
```

So we say:

> Apple **has launched** a new iPhone.

---

## Now look at this

> **Apple has been launched.** ✅

This is a completely different meaning.

Now ask:

**Who was launched?**

👉 Apple.

This means **someone started or founded Apple.**

Like:

> Apple **was launched** in 1976.

Here, Apple is **receiving** the action.

```
Someone ------------> launched ------------> Apple
```

# The easiest trick

Whenever you see **has been** or **have been**, ask this question:

> **Is the subject doing the action, or receiving the action?**

### If the subject is doing the action

Use:

* has launched
* have launched
* has written
* have made

---

### If the subject is receiving the action

Use:

* has been launched
* has been written
* has been made

# Here's a trick that works 95% of the time

Cover the verb and ask:

> **Can the subject perform this action?**

### Example

> Apple ______ a new iPhone.

Can Apple launch a phone?

✅ Yes.

So use:

> Apple **has launched** a new iPhone.

---

### Example

> The iPhone ______ by Apple.

Can the iPhone launch Apple?

❌ No.

The iPhone receives the action.

So use:

> The iPhone **was launched** by Apple.
