# Step 1: Read the whole sentence

> In the noon as Dad returned home, he looked for us in the house but really couldn't as everyone were trying to avoid him.

Don't look for the answer immediately.

Read it once.

I notice **two suspicious parts**:

* **In the noon**
* **everyone were**

Let's check them one by one.

---

# Step 2: Check Part A

> **In the noon**

Ask yourself:

Which preposition is used with **noon**?

We say:

* ✅ **at noon**
* ✅ **in the morning**
* ✅ **in the afternoon**
* ✅ **in the evening**
* ✅ **at night**

But **not**:

❌ in the noon

So it should be

> **At noon**

### Rule

| Time Expression  | Correct Preposition |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| at noon          | ✅                   |
| at midnight      | ✅                   |
| at night         | ✅                   |
| in the morning   | ✅                   |
| in the afternoon | ✅                   |
| in the evening   | ✅                   |

So **Part A contains an error**.

---

# Step 3: Check Part B

> he looked for us in the house but really couldn't as

Everything is grammatically fine.

No problem here.

---

# Step 4: Check Part C

> everyone **were** trying to avoid him

Now ask:

What is the subject?

Subject = **everyone**

Here's an important grammar rule.

Although **everyone** refers to many people, **it is grammatically singular**.

Examples:

* Everyone **is** happy.
* Everyone **has** arrived.
* Everyone **was** waiting.

Never say

* ❌ Everyone are
* ❌ Everyone were
* ❌ Everyone have

So,

> everyone **were** ❌

should be

> everyone **was** ✅

---

There are **two errors**.

### Part A

❌ in the noon

✅ at noon

---

### Part C

❌ everyone were

✅ everyone was

# So what do we do?

This is the same situation as your previous question.

# Why is "everyone" singular?

This confuses many students.

Think of **everyone** as:

> every + one

The important word is **one**.

Since **one** is singular, the verb must also be singular.

Examples:

✅ Everyone is here.

✅ Everyone has finished.

✅ Everyone was sleeping.

Even though it refers to many people, the grammar treats it as **one group**.

---

# Compare

| Subject   | Verb |
| --------- | ---- |
| Everyone  | is   |
| Everybody | is   |
| Someone   | is   |
| Nobody    | is   |
| Anyone    | is   |

Never use **are** or **were** with these words.
