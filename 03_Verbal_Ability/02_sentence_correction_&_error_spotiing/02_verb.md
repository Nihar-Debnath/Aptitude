That's actually the perfect place to start. **Subject-Verb Agreement** is impossible to master unless you first understand **what a verb is**.

Let's build it from scratch.

---

# What is a Verb?

A **verb** is simply a **word that tells us what the subject does, is, or has.**

Think of a sentence as a movie.

* **Subject** = The actor (who?)
* **Verb** = The action or state (what is happening?)

### Examples

* I **eat** rice.
* She **runs** fast.
* They **play** cricket.

Here,

* eat = verb
* runs = verb
* play = verb

These show **actions**.

---

## But verbs don't always show actions.

Sometimes they show a **state**.

Examples:

* I **am** happy.
* He **is** tall.
* They **were** tired.

Nobody is doing an action here.

The verb is just telling the **condition** or **state**.

---

# Main Verb

The verb that carries the actual meaning of the sentence is called the **main verb**.

Examples

I eat food.

Main verb = eat

She sings beautifully.

Main verb = sings

They studied yesterday.

Main verb = studied

Without these words, we don't know what happened.

---

# Then what is a Helping Verb?

Sometimes the main verb cannot express everything by itself.

It needs a **helper**.

That helper is called the **Helping Verb (Auxiliary Verb).**

Think of it like this:

Main verb = Hero

Helping verb = Hero's assistant

The assistant doesn't do the main job but helps explain it better.

---

## Example 1

I **am eating** rice.

Main verb = eating

Helping verb = am

What does **am** do?

It tells us the action is happening **right now**.

---

## Example 2

She **has finished** homework.

Main verb = finished

Helping verb = has

"Has" tells us the work is already completed.

---

## Example 3

They **will come** tomorrow.

Main verb = come

Helping verb = will

"Will" tells us the action is in the future.

---

# Why do we even need Helping Verbs?

Without helping verbs, English cannot express many important meanings.

For example:

### Present

I eat.

Simple present.

---

### Present Continuous

I am eating.

Means the action is happening now.

Without "am", "I eating" is incorrect.

---

### Future

I will eat.

Without "will":

❌ I eat tomorrow.

It sounds incorrect in standard English.

---

### Perfect

I have eaten.

Without "have":

❌ I eaten.

Wrong.

---

# Types of Helping Verbs

## 1. Be Verbs

* am
* is
* are
* was
* were
* be
* been
* being

Used for:

* Continuous tenses
* Passive voice
* States

Example

She **is reading**.

---

## 2. Have Verbs

* have
* has
* had

Used for perfect tenses.

Example

I **have completed** my work.

---

## 3. Do Verbs

* do
* does
* did

Used for:

Questions

Do you know him?

Negative sentences

I do not like tea.

Emphasis

I **do** know him!

---

## 4. Modal Helping Verbs

These show possibility, ability, permission, necessity, advice, etc.

* can
* could
* may
* might
* shall
* should
* will
* would
* must
* ought to

Examples

I **can swim.**

You **should study.**

He **must leave.**

---

# One important rule

A sentence can have:

### Only a Main Verb

She sings.

Main verb = sings

---

### Helping + Main Verb

She is singing.

Helping = is

Main = singing

---

### Multiple Helping Verbs + Main Verb

She **will have been working** here.

Helping verbs

* will
* have
* been

Main verb

* working

---

# Relation between Subject and Verb

Now comes the chapter you're starting.

The **subject decides the form of the verb.**

Example:

He **plays** football.

They **play** football.

The subject changes, so the verb changes too.

This matching is called **Subject-Verb Agreement**.

---

# The easiest way to identify a verb

Whenever you read a sentence, ask these questions:

1. **Who or what is the sentence about?** → Subject
2. **What is that subject doing, being, or having?** → Verb

Example:

The students are studying English.

* Subject → The students
* Helping verb → are
* Main verb → studying

---

## Quick Cheat Sheet

| Term                   | Meaning                                                  | Example                   |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Subject                | Who or what the sentence is about                        | She                       |
| Main Verb              | The main action or state                                 | writes                    |
| Helping Verb           | Helps the main verb show tense, mood, voice, or emphasis | is, has, will, do, can    |
| Subject-Verb Agreement | The verb must match the subject in number and person     | He **runs**, They **run** |

Once you're comfortable with these ideas, **Subject-Verb Agreement becomes much easier**, because almost every question boils down to:

1. Find the **subject**.
2. Find the **verb** (including any helping verb).
3. Check whether the verb correctly matches the subject.

After this, you can start learning the **12–15 most important Subject-Verb Agreement rules** that appear frequently in placement exams like TCS, Cognizant, Accenture, and Capgemini.
