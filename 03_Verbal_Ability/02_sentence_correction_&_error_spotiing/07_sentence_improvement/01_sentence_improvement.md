# What is Sentence Improvement?

You are given a sentence.

One part is **underlined or highlighted**.

Your job is to **replace only that part** with a better one.

Example:

> She **don't likes** coffee.

Options:

A. doesn't like

B. don't like

C. doesn't likes

D. No improvement

Correct:

> She **doesn't like** coffee.

Notice:

You don't rewrite the whole sentence.

You only improve the underlined part.

---

# Difference Between Error Spotting and Sentence Improvement

## Error Spotting

Find the wrong part.

Example

> She don't like coffee.

Answer:

> "don't"

---

## Sentence Improvement

Improve the wrong part.

Example

> She **don't like**

Options

* doesn't like
* don't likes
* didn't likes
* No improvement

Answer

> doesn't like

---

# The Golden Rule

Every sentence improvement question follows one principle:

> **Only one grammar rule is broken.**

Your task is to identify **which grammar rule**.

Think like a detective.

---

# Step-by-Step Method

Whenever you see a question:

## Step 1

Read the whole sentence.

Don't look at the options.

Ask:

> Does it sound natural?

---

## Step 2

Find the highlighted part.

Ask:

> What grammar topic is this?

Could it be:

* Subject-Verb Agreement?
* Tense?
* Articles?
* Prepositions?
* Pronouns?
* Modals?
* Conditionals?
* Parallelism?
* Comparison?

---

## Step 3

Mentally correct it.

Only then check the options.

# The 15 Most Common Rules

Almost every sentence improvement question comes from these.

---

# 1. Subject-Verb Agreement ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> She **go** to school.

Correct

> goes

Rule

Singular subject → singular verb.

---

# 2. Tenses ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> Yesterday I **go** to school.

Correct

> went

---

# 3. Articles ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> He is **a honest** man.

Correct

> an honest

---

# 4. Prepositions ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> Interested **on** music.

Correct

> interested in

---

# 5. Pronouns ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> Everyone should bring **their** book.

Many exams expect

> his or her

(Modern English often accepts "their," but exams may follow traditional grammar.)

---

# 6. Modals ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> He can **plays**

Correct

> play

Rule

Modal + V1

---

# 7. Conditionals ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

> If I know her, I would help.

Correct

> knew

---

# 8. Parallelism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is extremely common.

Wrong

> She likes singing, dancing and **to swim**.

Correct

> singing, dancing and swimming

OR

> to sing, to dance and to swim

Everything in a list should have the same form.

---

# 9. Comparisons ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Wrong

> She is smarter than **me am**.

Correct

> me

---

Wrong

> He is one of the tallest boy.

Correct

> boys

---

# 10. Adjective vs Adverb ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Wrong

> He speaks **fluent**.

Correct

> fluently

---

# 11. Verb Forms ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Wrong

> She enjoys **to dance**.

Correct

> dancing

because

enjoy + gerund

---

# 12. Infinitives & Gerunds

Examples

Want → to + verb

Enjoy → verb + ing

Avoid → verb + ing

Hope → to + verb

Finish → verb + ing

---

# 13. Correlative Conjunctions

Wrong

> Neither Ram or Shyam.

Correct

> Neither Ram nor Shyam.

---

Wrong

> No sooner...when

Correct

> No sooner...than

---

# 14. Redundancy

Wrong

> Repeat again

Correct

> Repeat

because repeat already means again.

---

# 15. Idioms

Wrong

> Good in English

Correct

> Good at English

# Questions You Should Ask Yourself

Whenever you solve a sentence improvement question, mentally go through this checklist:

```text
1. Is Subject-Verb Agreement correct?

2. Is the tense correct?

3. Is the article correct?

4. Is the preposition correct?

5. Is the pronoun correct?

6. Is there a modal verb?

7. Is it a conditional sentence?

8. Is the sentence parallel?

9. Is the comparison correct?

10. Is the adjective/adverb correct?

11. Is the verb form correct?

12. Is it a fixed expression or idiom?
```

If you check these 12 points, you'll solve **about 90%** of placement exam sentence improvement questions.

### Our learning plan

We'll master it by solving **50–100 real exam questions**. For every question, we'll identify:

1. **Which grammar rule is being tested?**
2. **Why is the original sentence wrong?**
3. **Why is the correct option right?**
4. **Why are the other options wrong?**

This approach will make you recognize patterns quickly during placements and aptitude tests.
