Absolutely. If your grammar foundation is weak, **don't memorize rules first**. Understand **why the language works**. Once your roots are strong, topics like Subject-Verb Agreement, Repetition Error, Articles, Tenses, etc., become much easier.

Let's build the concept from the ground up.

---

# First, what does "repetition" mean?

In everyday English, **repetition** means:

> **Saying, writing, or doing the same thing more than once.**

Example:

Imagine I say:

> Hello! Hello! Hello!

I'm repeating the same word.

Or if I clap:

👏 👏 👏

The action is repeated.

So,

**Repeat = Do the same thing again.**

---

# Then what is a "Repetition Error"?

A **repetition error** is **not just repeating words**.

It means:

> **Repeating the same idea unnecessarily.**

Notice the difference.

There are **two types of repetition**.

---

# Type 1: Repeating the same word

Example:

❌ I like like cricket.

The word **like** appears twice.

This is an obvious repetition.

---

# Type 2: Repeating the same meaning

This is what placement exams usually test.

Example:

❌ He returned back home.

Let's understand the meaning.

**Return** means

> "to come back"

Now look at **back**.

Back also means

> "to return"

So the sentence is actually saying:

> He came back back home.

The idea "back" appears twice.

That is repetition.

---

# Think of it like this

Imagine your friend says:

> "Let's go upstairs upward."

What?

"Upstairs" already means upward.

The word **upward** adds nothing.

That's repetition.

---

Another example:

Imagine I tell you:

> Bring me a free gift.

What is a gift?

A gift is something given **without payment**.

So,

Free + Gift

means

Free + Free Thing

The word **free** gives no new information.

---

# The Golden Rule

Whenever you see a sentence, ask yourself:

> **Does this word add any new meaning?**

If the answer is **No**,

then that word is probably a repetition error.

---

# Why is repetition considered an error?

English likes **clarity** and **conciseness**.

Imagine two people.

Person A says:

> I returned home.

Person B says:

> I returned back home.

Both mean the same thing.

The second sentence uses an unnecessary word.

English prefers the shorter, cleaner sentence.

---

# Let's understand through real-life examples

## Example 1

He returned back home.

Meaning:

Return = Come back

Back = Back

So,

Come back + Back

One "back" is unnecessary.

---

## Example 2

Please repeat again.

Repeat means

Do again.

Again means

Again.

So,

Again + Again

Wrong.

---

## Example 3

Merge together

Merge means

Join together.

Together means

Together.

Again,

Together + Together.

---

## Example 4

Advance planning

Planning always happens before something.

That means it is already done in advance.

So,

Advance + Planning

is repeating the same idea.

---

# But be careful!

Not every repeated-looking sentence is wrong.

Example:

The boys shouted again and again.

Here,

Again and again means

Repeatedly.

This is an **expression**, not an error.

---

Example:

Very very good.

In grammar, this is repetition.

But in conversation, people use it for emphasis.

So it isn't considered a sentence correction error.

---

# Another important concept

## Repetition vs Emphasis

Look carefully.

### Repetition Error

The extra word adds **nothing**.

Example:

Return back.

Back adds no new meaning.

---

### Emphasis

The repeated word makes the sentence stronger.

Example:

He is very, very intelligent.

The second "very" increases emphasis.

It is intentional.

---

# How to identify repetition in exams

Whenever you see a sentence, follow these three steps.

### Step 1

Read the sentence normally.

---

### Step 2

Ask:

**What does each word mean?**

Example:

Returned = came back

Back = came back

---

### Step 3

Ask:

**If I remove one word, does the meaning change?**

Example:

He returned back home.

Remove "back":

He returned home.

Meaning?

Exactly the same.

Therefore,

"back" is unnecessary.

---

Now look at another sentence.

He came back yesterday.

Remove "back":

He came yesterday.

These two sentences have different meanings:

* **came** = arrived.
* **came back** = returned to a place he had been before.

Here, **back** changes the meaning, so it is **not** a repetition error.

---

# Think of repetition like carrying two identical bags

Suppose you are travelling.

You carry one backpack.

Everything is inside.

Then someone gives you another empty backpack.

Now you're carrying extra weight without any benefit.

That's exactly what repetition is in grammar.

One word already carries the complete meaning.

The second word carries nothing new.

So English throws it away.

---

# Root Formula (Remember Forever)

Whenever you study repetition errors, ask this one question:

> **"Does this word give new information, or is it only repeating the meaning of another word?"**

* ✅ **New meaning** → Keep it.
* ❌ **Same meaning** → Remove it.
