Perfect. Since you've completed **Sentence Correction**, the next topic is **Error Spotting**.

Here's something important:

> **Sentence Correction and Error Spotting are almost the same.**
>
> The only difference is **how the question is asked**.

In Sentence Correction, you're **given options to replace** a part of the sentence.

In Error Spotting, you're **given a sentence divided into parts**, and you must identify **which part contains the error**.

---

# What is Error Spotting?

Example:

> (A) The teacher / (B) along with the students / (C) are going / (D) to the museum.

Question:
Which part has the error?

Answer:
**(C)**

Because:

> The teacher **along with** the students **is** going...

The rule being tested is **Subject-Verb Agreement**.

---

# The Exam Mindset

Don't read the sentence like a story.

Read it like a detective.

Every sentence has checkpoints.

```
Sentence
   ↓
Find Subject
   ↓
Find Verb

Check Agreement
   ↓
Check Pronouns
   ↓
Check Tense
   ↓
Check Prepositions
   ↓
```

This becomes automatic with practice.

# The 12 Major Error Types

Almost every error spotting question belongs to one of these categories.

---

# 1. Subject–Verb Agreement ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The most frequently tested topic.

Examples

❌ The list of items **are** ready.

Correct:

✅ The list of items **is** ready.

Why?

Subject = list

Not items.

---

Another

❌ Neither Ram nor his friends **is** coming.

Correct:

✅ Neither Ram nor his friends **are** coming.

Nearest subject = friends

---

Watch out for words like

* along with
* together with
* besides
* as well as
* accompanied by

They do **not** change the subject.

---

# 2. Tense ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ He has gone yesterday.

Wrong because

Yesterday = Simple Past

Correct

✅ He went yesterday.

---

Another

❌ I am living here since 2015.

Correct

✅ I have been living here since 2015.

---

# 3. Pronouns ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ Every student should bring **their** notebook.

In formal grammar,

Every = Singular

Correct

✅ Every student should bring **his or her** notebook.

(Many modern style guides accept singular "their," but competitive exams often expect traditional agreement.)

---

Example

❌ Me and Rahul went home.

Correct

✅ Rahul and I went home.

---

# 4. Articles (a, an, the) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ He is **an** university student.

Correct

✅ He is **a** university student.

Why?

University begins with "yu"

"a university"

---

Example

❌ She bought **the** milk yesterday.

Often

✅ She bought milk yesterday.

---

# 5. Prepositions ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Examples

❌ Married **with**

Correct

✅ Married **to**

---

❌ Interested **for**

Correct

✅ Interested **in**

---

❌ Good **in** English

Correct

✅ Good **at** English

---

These are fixed expressions.

---

# 6. Parallelism ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ She likes

* singing
* dancing
* to swim

Wrong

Correct

✅ singing
✅ dancing
✅ swimming

---

# 7. Comparison ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ The population of India is greater than China.

Population vs Country

Wrong comparison.

Correct

✅ greater than **that of China**

---

# 8. Modifiers ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ Walking down the road,

the trees looked beautiful.

Who was walking?

Trees?

Impossible.

Correct

Walking down the road,

**I** saw beautiful trees.

---

# 9. Conjunctions ⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ Although he was poor,

but he was honest.

Use one.

Correct

Although he was poor,

he was honest.

OR

He was poor,

but he was honest.

---

# 10. Adjectives vs Adverbs ⭐⭐⭐

Example

❌ She sings beautiful.

Correct

✅ beautifully

---

Example

❌ He drives careful.

Correct

✅ carefully

---

# 11. Double Negatives ⭐⭐

Example

❌ I don't know nothing.

Correct

✅ I don't know anything.

---

# 12. Word Choice / Idioms ⭐⭐

Example

❌ Discuss about

Correct

✅ Discuss

---

❌ Return back

Correct

✅ Return

---

# Memory Trick (Exam Cheat Sheet)

Whenever you see an error spotting question, mentally ask:

```
✓ Subject?
✓ Verb?
✓ Tense?
✓ Pronoun?
✓ Article?
✓ Preposition?
✓ Parallelism?
✓ Comparison?
✓ Modifier?
✓ Conjunction?
✓ Adjective/Adverb?
✓ Idiom?
```

If you check these 12 areas in order, you'll catch the vast majority of grammar errors asked in competitive exams.

# Your Learning Roadmap

Since you're building your English from the basics, I recommend learning Error Spotting in this order:

1. **Subject–Verb Agreement** (most important)
2. **Tenses**
3. **Pronouns**
4. **Articles**
5. **Prepositions**
6. **Conjunctions**
7. **Modifiers (Dangling & Misplaced)**
8. **Parallelism**
9. **Comparisons**
10. **Adjectives vs Adverbs**
11. **Common Idioms & Fixed Expressions**
12. **Mixed Error Spotting Practice**

This order starts with the highest-frequency exam topics and gradually builds to the more subtle ones. Since you've already studied Subject–Verb Agreement and Sentence Correction, you'll find that many Error Spotting questions are simply asking you to apply the same grammar rules in a different format.
