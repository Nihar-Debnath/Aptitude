# Let's start with Parts of Speech

There are **8 Parts of Speech**.

Think of them as the **jobs** that words perform.

| Part of Speech | Job                                            | Example              |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Noun           | Names a person, place, thing, idea             | Rahul, Kolkata, Book |
| Pronoun        | Replaces a noun                                | He, She, They        |
| Verb           | Action or state                                | eat, run, is         |
| Adjective      | Describes a noun                               | tall, red, beautiful |
| Adverb         | Describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb | quickly, very        |
| Preposition    | Shows relationship                             | in, on, under        |
| Conjunction    | Joins words or sentences                       | and, but, or         |
| Interjection   | Shows emotion                                  | Wow!, Oh!            |

If you master these eight, **90% of grammar becomes much easier.**

---

# First: What is an Adjective?

An **adjective** is simply a word that **describes a noun or pronoun**.

It answers questions like:

* Which one?
* What kind?
* How many?

---

## Example

> Rahul is a **smart** boy.

Question:

Who is smart?

👉 Rahul (boy)

So,

**smart** describes Rahul.

Therefore,

**smart = adjective**

---

Another example

> I bought a **red** car.

Question:

Which car?

👉 Red car.

"Red" describes the car.

So "red" is an adjective.

---

More examples

| Sentence       | Adjective |
| -------------- | --------- |
| Beautiful girl | Beautiful |
| Tall building  | Tall      |
| Big house      | Big       |
| Happy child    | Happy     |

Notice something?

Every adjective is describing a **noun**.

---

# Now what is a Verb?

A **verb** tells us:

* what someone **does**, or
* what someone **is**, or
* what someone **has**.

Examples

Rahul **runs**.

runs = verb

---

She **eats**.

eats = verb

---

He **is** happy.

is = verb

---

# Then what is a Verb Phrase?

A **verb phrase** is simply:

> **A verb along with the words that belong to it.**

Think of it as a **verb family**.

---

Example

> Rahul **plays cricket**.

Verb = plays

Verb phrase = plays cricket

Because "plays cricket" works together to express the action.

---

Another example

> She **is studying English**.

Helping verb = is

Main verb = studying

Object = English

Verb phrase = is studying English

---

Another

> They **have completed the project**.

Verb phrase = have completed the project

---

# Why do we call it a "phrase"?

Because it is **more than one word**, but **not a complete sentence** by itself.

For example:

> playing football

Can this stand alone as a sentence?

❌ No.

It has no subject.

So it is a **phrase**.

---

# Difference between Verb and Verb Phrase

### Verb

One word.

> Rahul **runs**.

Verb = runs

---

### Verb Phrase

More than one word acting together.

> Rahul **is running fast**.

Verb phrase = is running fast

---

# Difference between Adjective and Verb Phrase

Look carefully.

### Adjective

Describes a noun.

> Rahul is **smart**.

Question:

What is Rahul like?

Smart.

---

### Verb Phrase

Shows an action.

> Rahul **plays football**.

Question:

What does Rahul do?

Plays football.

---

# Why Parallelism uses these terms

Now look at this.

> Rahul is **smart, kind and honest.**

| Word   | Type      |
| ------ | --------- |
| smart  | Adjective |
| kind   | Adjective |
| honest | Adjective |

Everything matches.

✅ Correct.

---

Now

> Rahul **plays football, watches TV and studies daily.**

| Phrase         | Type        |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| plays football | Verb phrase |
| watches TV     | Verb phrase |
| studies daily  | Verb phrase |

Everything matches.

✅ Correct.

---

Now

> Rahul is **smart, kind and plays football.**

| Item           | Type        |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| smart          | Adjective   |
| kind           | Adjective   |
| plays football | Verb phrase |

First two describe **what Rahul is**.

The third tells **what Rahul does**.

They're different types, so they don't form a balanced list.

❌ Parallelism error.

---

# The Secret That Will Make Grammar Easy

Don't memorize words like **adjective**, **verb phrase**, or **noun**.

Instead, ask these questions:

| Question           | Answer      | Part of Speech |
| ------------------ | ----------- | -------------- |
| Who or what?       | Rahul, Book | Noun           |
| What does it do?   | Run, Eat    | Verb           |
| What is it like?   | Tall, Red   | Adjective      |
| How does it do it? | Quickly     | Adverb         |

If you can answer these questions, you'll identify the grammar naturally.
