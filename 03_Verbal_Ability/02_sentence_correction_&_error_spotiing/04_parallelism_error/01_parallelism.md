Awesome! **Parallelism** is one of the easiest grammar topics if you understand its core idea. It appears in **Sentence Correction**, **Error Detection**, and sometimes even in **Reading Comprehension**.

Let's build it from the ground up.

---

# Step 1: What does "Parallel" mean?

In everyday English,

> **Parallel = Two or more things that follow the same pattern.**

Imagine railway tracks.

```
==================

==================
```

Both tracks go in the same direction.

They are **parallel**.

Now imagine one track suddenly bends.

```
==================

==========
      \
       \
```

It looks wrong.

The same thing happens in grammar.

---

# Step 2: What is Parallelism?

Parallelism means:

> **When two or more similar ideas are written in the same grammatical form.**

In simple words:

> **If one item follows a pattern, the other items should follow the same pattern.**

Think of it as:

```
Pattern
Pattern
Pattern
```

NOT

```
Pattern
Different Pattern
Another Pattern
```

---

# Step 3: Why do we need Parallelism?

Imagine I say:

> I like **playing cricket**, **reading books**, and **to swim**.

Read it slowly.

* playing ✅
* reading ✅
* to swim ❌

Doesn't sound balanced.

Because two items end in **-ing**, but the third changes to **to + verb**.

Correct sentence:

> I like **playing cricket**, **reading books**, and **swimming**.

Now all three follow the same pattern.

---

# Think of it like school uniforms

Three students are standing in line.

```
👔
👔
👔
```

Everything looks balanced.

Now one student wears something different.

```
👔
👕
👔
```

It looks odd.

That's exactly what happens in grammar.

Parallelism is about **keeping the same "uniform"** for similar parts of a sentence.

---

# Where is Parallelism Used?

It usually appears in lists connected by words like:

* and
* or
* nor
* both...and
* either...or
* neither...nor
* not only...but also

---

## Example 1

❌ I like singing, dancing and to read.

Patterns:

* singing (-ing)
* dancing (-ing)
* to read (to + verb)

Different.

Correct:

✅ I like singing, dancing and reading.

---

## Example 2

❌ She is intelligent, hardworking and works honestly.

Patterns:

* intelligent (adjective)
* hardworking (adjective)
* works honestly (verb phrase)

Not parallel.

Correct:

✅ She is intelligent, hardworking and honest.

or

✅ She works intelligently, works hard and works honestly.

---

## Example 3

❌ He wants to study, to work and travelling.

Patterns:

* to study
* to work
* travelling

Wrong.

Correct:

✅ He wants to study, to work and to travel.

or

✅ He wants to study, work and travel.

---

# The Main Rule

**Items connected together should have the same grammatical form.**

Examples:

### Verb + Verb + Verb

✅ Eat, Sleep, Study

❌ Eat, Sleeping, To Study

---

### Noun + Noun + Noun

✅ Books, Pens and Pencils

❌ Books, Pens and Writing

---

### Adjective + Adjective + Adjective

✅ Honest, Intelligent and Kind

❌ Honest, Intelligence and Kind

---

### -ing + -ing + -ing

✅ Reading, Writing and Listening

❌ Reading, Writing and To Listen

---

# Parallelism in Error Detection

## Example 1

**A.** She likes
**B.** reading novels,
**C.** watching movies and
**D.** to dance.

Patterns:

* reading
* watching
* to dance ❌

Error:

**D**

Correct:

> dancing

---

## Example 2

**A.** Rahul enjoys
**B.** swimming,
**C.** running and
**D.** cycling.

Patterns:

* swimming
* running
* cycling

All same.

✅ No error.

---

## Example 3

**A.** The job requires
**B.** patience,
**C.** honesty and
**D.** to work hard.

Patterns:

* patience (noun)
* honesty (noun)
* to work hard (verb phrase)

Wrong.

Correct:

> hard work

or

> diligence

Error:

**D**

# Common Structures Tested in Placements

## 1. AND

Wrong:

> He likes reading and to write.

Correct:

> He likes reading and writing.

---

## 2. OR

Wrong:

> You can study or watching TV.

Correct:

> You can study or watch TV.

---

## 3. BOTH...AND

Wrong:

> She is both intelligent and works hard.

Correct:

> She is both intelligent and hardworking.

or

> She both studies hard and works hard.

---

## 4. EITHER...OR

Wrong:

> You can either go by train or travelling by bus.

Correct:

> You can either go by train or travel by bus.

---

## 5. NOT ONLY...BUT ALSO

Wrong:

> He not only sings but also a dancer.

Correct:

> He not only sings but also dances.

or

> He is not only a singer but also a dancer.

---

# 5-Second Trick for Placement Exams

Whenever you see words like:

* and
* or
* both...and
* either...or
* neither...nor
* not only...but also

Immediately compare the parts on both sides.

Ask:

> **Are these following the same grammatical pattern?**

If yes → ✅ Correct.

If no → ❌ Parallelism error.

✅ No error

# Cheat Sheet

| Pattern                           | ✅ Correct                   | ❌ Wrong                    |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Verb + Verb + Verb                | eat, sleep, study           | eat, sleeping, to study    |
| Noun + Noun + Noun                | honesty, patience, kindness | honesty, patient, kindness |
| Adjective + Adjective + Adjective | smart, honest, kind         | smart, honesty, kind       |
| -ing + -ing + -ing                | reading, writing, speaking  | reading, writing, to speak |
| to + Verb                         | to read, to write, to learn | to read, writing, to learn |

## The One Sentence to Remember Forever

**Parallelism is about balance.**

Whenever two or more similar ideas are joined together, **they should wear the same grammatical "uniform."**

If one item is a noun, the others should be nouns. If one is an **-ing** verb, the others should also be **-ing** verbs. If one uses **to + verb**, the others should too.

That's the single idea behind almost every parallelism question you'll see in placement exams.
