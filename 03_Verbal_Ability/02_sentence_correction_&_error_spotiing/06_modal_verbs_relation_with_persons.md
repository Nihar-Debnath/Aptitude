Excellent question! This is where many students get confused because **modal verbs** and **first/second/third person** are two different grammar topics that overlap only a little.

Let's build the concept from scratch.

---

# Step 1: What are First, Second, and Third Person?

These tell us **who is speaking**.

| Person            | Pronouns                          |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **First Person**  | I, We                             |
| **Second Person** | You                               |
| **Third Person**  | He, She, It, They, Rahul, The dog |

Examples

**First Person**

> I am a student.

> We are friends.

---

**Second Person**

> You are my teacher.

---

**Third Person**

> He is a doctor.

> She sings well.

> Rahul studies every day.

---

# Step 2: What are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are **helping verbs**.

Examples:

* can
* could
* may
* might
* must
* shall
* should
* will
* would

They express:

* ability
* permission
* possibility
* advice
* obligation
* future

Examples

> I **can** swim.

> She **must** study.

> They **will** come.

---

# Step 3: Do Modal Verbs Change with Person?

## Most modal verbs DO NOT change.

Look at "can".

| Person | Sentence       |
| ------ | -------------- |
| I      | I can swim.    |
| You    | You can swim.  |
| He     | He can swim.   |
| She    | She can swim.  |
| They   | They can swim. |

Notice

**can** never changes.

Not

❌ He cans swim.

---

Same for

* should
* must
* could
* may
* might
* will
* would

Examples

I should go.

You should go.

He should go.

They should go.

The modal stays the same.

---

# Step 4: Then why do we learn First, Second, and Third Person?

Because **ordinary verbs** change.

Example

### Verb = play

| Person | Sentence   |
| ------ | ---------- |
| I      | I play.    |
| You    | You play.  |
| We     | We play.   |
| They   | They play. |
| He     | He plays.  |
| She    | She plays. |

Only **third person singular** gets **-s/-es**.

---

Now compare with a modal.

### Modal = can

| Person | Sentence       |
| ------ | -------------- |
| I      | I can play.    |
| You    | You can play.  |
| He     | He can play.   |
| She    | She can play.  |
| They   | They can play. |

No change.

---

# Step 5: Why?

Because the modal already carries the grammatical function.

After a modal, the main verb stays in its **base form (V1)**.

Pattern

```text
Subject + Modal + V1
```

Examples

I can **go**.

She can **go**.

They can **go**.

He will **eat**.

You should **study**.

We must **leave**.

Notice

The verb **never becomes**

❌ goes

❌ studies

❌ eats

after a modal.

---

# Step 6: What about SHALL and WILL?

This is where history comes in.

### Old Traditional Rule (Very Old English)

| Person             | Future |
| ------------------ | ------ |
| I, We              | shall  |
| He, She, You, They | will   |

Examples

I shall go.

We shall win.

He will come.

They will help.

---

### Modern English

Today almost everyone simply uses **will**.

Examples

I will go.

We will go.

He will go.

They will go.

All are correct.

---

**"Shall"** is now mostly used in:

### 1. Formal legal documents

> The tenant **shall** pay the rent.

---

### 2. Rules

> Students **shall** wear uniforms.

---

### 3. Formal promises

> We shall overcome.

---

### 4. Suggestions

> Shall we dance?

> Shall I open the window?

---

# Step 7: Exam Rule

For competitive exams,

remember this:

After every modal,

```text
Modal + V1
```

| Modal  | Correct       | Wrong           |
| ------ | ------------- | --------------- |
| can    | can go        | can goes        |
| should | should study  | should studied  |
| will   | will eat      | will ate        |
| shall  | shall appoint | shall appointed |
| must   | must obey     | must obeyed     |

## One Small Exception Worth Knowing

The only modal with a historical connection to **person** is **shall**.

* **Traditional rule (older English):**

  * **I/We → shall**
  * **He/She/You/They → will**

* **Modern English:**

  * **Will** is used with **all persons** for ordinary future meaning.
  * **Shall** is now mainly used for formal writing, legal language, offers, and suggestions.

So for today's competitive exams, you should focus on this rule:

> **Every modal verb is followed by the base form (V1) of the main verb.**

This is the rule that appears again and again in **Error Spotting**, **Sentence Correction**, and **Fill in the Blanks**.
