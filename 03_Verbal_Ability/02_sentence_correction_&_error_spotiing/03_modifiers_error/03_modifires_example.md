# Example 1

**A.** Walking along the road,
**B.** the trees looked
**C.** very beautiful.
**D.** No error.

### Step 1: Find the modifier

**Walking along the road**

Now ask:

> **Who is walking?**

According to the sentence:

👉 **the trees**

Can trees walk?

❌ No.

**Answer: Part A** (The introductory modifier is dangling because it incorrectly modifies "the trees.")

**Correct sentence:**

> Walking along the road, **I** found the trees very beautiful.

or

> As I was walking along the road, the trees looked very beautiful.

# The Pattern You'll Notice

Almost every modifier question starts like this:

* Walking...
* Driving...
* Reading...
* Sleeping...
* Wearing...
* Looking...
* Having finished...
* Being...
* Covered with...
* While studying...
* After completing...
* Before leaving...

These are **introductory modifiers**.

The examiner wants to trick you into attaching them to the wrong noun.

---

# Your 5-Second Shortcut

Whenever you see a sentence starting with:

> Walking...
>
> Driving...
>
> Reading...
>
> Having...
>
> Being...
>
> After...
>
> Before...
>
> While...

Immediately do this:

### Step 1

Find the first noun after the comma.

Example:

> Walking to school, **the rain** started.

First noun:

👉 rain

### Step 2

Ask:

> **Can this noun perform the action in the opening phrase?**

* Rain walking? ❌
* Rahul walking? ✅
* Mountains covered with snow? ✅
* Interview wearing a coat? ❌

That's how you solve these questions in **5–10 seconds**.

## Mini Quiz (Try Without Looking at the Answers)

Find the error.

### Q1

**A.** After eating dinner,
**B.** the television
**C.** was switched on.
**D.** No error.

---

### Q2

**A.** Wearing a blue shirt,
**B.** Amit
**C.** entered the office.
**D.** No error.

---

### Q3

**A.** While crossing the road,
**B.** the car
**C.** hit the pedestrian.
**D.** No error.

---

### Answers

* **Q1:** **A** ❌ (The television didn't eat dinner.)
* **Q2:** **D** ✅ (Amit is wearing the blue shirt.)
* **Q3:** **A** ❌ (According to the sentence, the car is crossing the road. The intended meaning is that the pedestrian was crossing the road.)

This is exactly the style of modifier questions you'll encounter in placement aptitude tests. Once you make it a habit to ask **"Who is doing the action in the opening phrase?"**, these questions become among the easiest grammar questions to solve.
