# Rule 1: Use **by** for the doer (the person or thing performing the action)

In passive voice, **by** tells us **who did the action**.

### Formula

> Action + by + doer

### Examples

✅ The book was written **by** J.K. Rowling.

Who wrote it?

→ J.K. Rowling

---

✅ The cake was baked **by** my mother.

Who baked it?

→ My mother

---

✅ The thief was caught **by** the police.

Who caught him?

→ The police

---

✅ The speech was delivered **by** the Prime Minister.

Who delivered it?

→ The Prime Minister

---

Your example:

❌ The phrase was said **through** Mahatma Gandhi.

✅ The phrase was said **by** Mahatma Gandhi.

Because Gandhi is the **speaker (doer)**.

---

# Rule 2: Use **with** for the tool, instrument, or method

**With** answers the question:

> **How?**
>
> **Using what?**

### Examples

✅ He cut the apple **with** a knife.

How?

→ Using a knife.

---

✅ She wrote **with** a pen.

Using what?

→ A pen.

---

✅ He hit the ball **with** a bat.

Using what?

→ A bat.

---

✅ The wall was painted **with** a brush.

Using what?

→ A brush.

---

# Compare them

### Example 1

✅ The letter was written **by** Rahul.

Who wrote it?

→ Rahul

---

✅ The letter was written **with** a pencil.

Using what?

→ Pencil

---

Both can appear together.

✅ The letter was written **by Rahul with a pencil.**

Here,

* Rahul = doer → **by**
* Pencil = tool → **with**

---

### Example 2

✅ The window was broken **by** the boy.

Who broke it?

→ The boy

---

✅ The window was broken **with** a stone.

Using what?

→ A stone

---

Complete sentence:

> The window was broken **by the boy with a stone.**

---

### Example 3

✅ The picture was painted **by** Leonardo da Vinci.

Who painted it?

→ Leonardo

---

✅ The picture was painted **with** oil paints.

Using what?

→ Oil paints

---

# A simple trick

Whenever you see **by**, ask:

> **Who did it?**

If you can answer with a person (or sometimes an animal or organization), **by** is usually correct.

Whenever you see **with**, ask:

> **Using what?**

If you answer with a tool, instrument, or material, **with** is usually correct.

## One more thing about your question

The original sentence in the image has **two** issues:

> ❌ The phrase ... **was said through** Mahatma Gandhi.

A more natural English sentence is:

> ✅ **The phrase "Live as if you were to die tomorrow. Learn as if you were to live forever" was said by Mahatma Gandhi.**

or even better:

> ✅ **The quote is attributed to Mahatma Gandhi.**

So, in placement tests, if you see **"said through someone"**, it is almost always an error. The correct preposition is **by** because you're naming the person who said the words.

Yes, but **"by"**, **"till"**, and **"beside"** have completely different meanings. They are **not interchangeable**.

Let's understand them one by one.

---

# 1. **By** (Most Common Meanings)

## A. The doer (Passive Voice)

> Who did the action?

* The book was written **by** Shakespeare.
* The cake was baked **by** my mother.
* The thief was caught **by** the police.

---

## B. Means/Method

* I travelled **by** bus.
* She came **by** train.
* Send it **by** email.
* Pay **by** card.

Notice that for transport, we usually use **by**, not **with**.

✅ by bus
✅ by train
✅ by car
❌ with bus

---

## C. Time (before or not later than)

* Finish the work **by** 6 PM.
* I will call you **by** tomorrow.
* Be here **by** Monday.

It means **before or at that time**.

---

## D. Near/Beside

Sometimes **by** also means **next to**.

* He sat **by** the window.
* There is a tree **by** the river.
* She stood **by** my side.

---

# 2. **Till / Until**

**Till** means **up to a particular time**.

Examples:

* Wait **till** tomorrow.
* I studied **till** midnight.
* Stay here **until** I come back.

### Compare:

**By 6 PM**

Means **before or at 6 PM**.

```
3 PM -------- 6 PM
          ↑
must finish here
```

Example:

> Finish your homework **by 6 PM.**

---

**Till 6 PM**

Means **continue until 6 PM**.

```
3 PM ================== 6 PM
      continue doing
```

Example:

> I worked **till 6 PM.**

---

# 3. **Beside**

**Beside** means **next to**.

Examples:

* Sit **beside** me.
* The school is **beside** the hospital.
* He stood **beside** his friend.

Here, **by** and **beside** can sometimes mean almost the same thing.

Example:

* She sat **by** me. ✅
* She sat **beside** me. ✅

Both are correct.

---

# Don't confuse **beside** with **besides**

These are different words.

### Beside = next to

* Sit beside me.

### Besides = in addition to

* Besides English, I know Hindi.
* Besides Rahul, everyone came.

---

# Quick Comparison

| Word           | Meaning         | Example                       |
| -------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **by**         | doer            | Written **by** Shakespeare.   |
| **by**         | method          | Travel **by** train.          |
| **by**         | deadline        | Finish **by** 5 PM.           |
| **by**         | near            | Sit **by** me.                |
| **with**       | tool/instrument | Cut **with** a knife.         |
| **till/until** | up to a time    | Wait **till** tomorrow.       |
| **beside**     | next to         | Sit **beside** me.            |
| **besides**    | in addition to  | Besides Rahul, everyone came. |

## A memory trick

Whenever you see these words, ask yourself a question:

* **By** → **Who did it?**, **How was it sent/traveled?**, **What's the deadline?**, or **Near what?**
* **With** → **Using what?**
* **Till/Until** → **Continue until when?**
* **Beside** → **Next to what?**
* **Besides** → **In addition to what?**

This questioning habit is very useful in sentence correction because prepositions often depend on the relationship between the words, not just on memorizing meanings.
