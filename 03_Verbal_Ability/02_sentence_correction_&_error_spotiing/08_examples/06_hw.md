Let's understand **why**.

The sentence is:

> It would be a useful exercise for you to say the **speech loudly several times**.

### The problem is "speech"

We need to understand the difference between **speech** and **speak**.

* **speak** = verb → to talk
* **speech** = noun → something that is spoken, usually a formal talk

The sentence says:

> **say the speech**

That's already correct because **speech** is the thing being said.

Now look at:

> say the speech **loudly**

"Loudly" means **in a loud manner/voice**.

But the intended expression is:

> **say the speech loud several times**

In this question, **loud** is being used as an adverb after **say**.

So:

> It would be a useful exercise for you to say the **speech loud several times**. ✅

### Why not the other options?

**A. speech louder** ❌
"Louder" is comparative. It means **more loudly than something else**. There is no comparison here.

**B. speech loudest** ❌
"Loudest" is superlative. It means **the most loud**. No comparison is being made.

**C. speech loud** ✅
Correct in the intended construction.

**D. speech aloud** ❌
This is tempting!

**aloud** means **in a voice that can be heard**, not silently.

For example:

> She read the letter **aloud**. ✅

But in this sentence, the question is specifically testing the use of **loud** with "say."

### Final answer:

> **C. speech loud several times.** ✅

So the corrected sentence is:

> **It would be a useful exercise for you to say the speech loud several times.**
