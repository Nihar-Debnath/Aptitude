The previous question had a **wrong word**.

This question tests whether the **current word is already the best choice**.

---

# Step 1: Read the sentence

> **Being unable to play with the other kids, the autistic kid became even more depressed.**

Ask yourself:

**Why did the child become depressed?**

Because:

> He couldn't play with the other children.

That makes emotional sense.

---

# Step 2: Understand the highlighted word

### Depressed

Meaning:

* sad
* emotionally down
* unhappy
* mentally low

This perfectly fits the context.

---

# Step 3: Check every option

---

## A. Jolly

Meaning:

Happy.

Sentence:

> became even more jolly

This is the opposite of the meaning.

❌ Reject.

---

## B. Cautious

Meaning:

Careful.

Sentence:

> became even more cautious

Does inability to play make someone more careful?

No.

❌ Reject.

---

## C. Reductive

Meaning:

Reducing something or simplifying.

Example:

> reductive reasoning

It doesn't describe a person's emotional state here.

❌ Reject.

---

## D. Drastic

Meaning:

Extreme or severe.

Can we say:

> became even more drastic

No.

**Drastic** describes actions, changes, or measures, not a person's emotion.

Examples:

* drastic measures ✅
* drastic changes ✅
* drastic decision ✅
* drastic person ❌

Reject.

---

## E. No Improvement

The original sentence already makes perfect sense.

✅ Correct.

---

# Answer

✅ **E. No Improvement**

# Exam Trick (Very Important)

Many students think:

> "There must be an improvement."

So they keep searching for another word.

**Wrong strategy.**

First ask:

> **Is the original sentence already correct?**

If **yes**, don't change anything.

That's exactly why exams include **"No Improvement"** as an option.

---

# The Pattern You Should Notice

There are **two kinds of Sentence Improvement questions**:

### Type 1: Grammar-based

Example:

> She **don't like** coffee.

You apply grammar rules.

---

### Type 2: Vocabulary/Meaning-based

Example:

> became even more **depressed**

You ask:

> **Does this word match the situation?**

If yes,

👉 choose **No Improvement**.

---

## A Quick Tip

When the options are **completely different words** (like *jolly, cautious, reductive, drastic*), it's usually a **vocabulary/context** question rather than a grammar question.

If the original word already fits the context naturally, **"No Improvement"** is often the correct answer.
