Excellent question. **Through** and **Across** are two prepositions that learners often confuse because both involve movement. The key difference is **how the movement happens**.

---

# 1. Through = Enter → Pass inside → Exit

Use **through** when something moves **inside** an area, object, or space.

### Think of it like this:

```
Enter → Inside → Exit
```

### Examples

🚶 He walked **through** the tunnel.

```
Entrance -------- Tunnel -------- Exit
        →→→→→→→→→→→
```

---

🚗 We drove **through** the city.

(We went inside the city and came out the other side.)

---

🚶 She walked **through** the forest.

(Inside the forest.)

---

💧 Water flows **through** the pipe.

(The water is inside the pipe.)

---

☀️ Sunlight came **through** the window.

(The light passed inside the opening.)

---

# 2. Across = From one side to the other

Use **across** when you move **over the surface** or **from one side to the opposite side**.

### Think of it like this:

```
Side A -------- Side B
```

### Examples

🚶 He walked **across** the road.

```
Side A  -------- Road -------- Side B
        →→→→→→→→→
```

---

🏊 She swam **across** the river.

(From one bank to the other.)

---

🚶 They walked **across** the bridge.

(On top of the bridge.)

---

🏃 The dog ran **across** the field.

(Over the surface of the field.)

---

# Compare Them

### Road

✅ He walked **across** the road.

(From one side to the other.)

❌ He walked **through** the road.

(Roads don't have an "inside".)

---

### Tunnel

✅ He walked **through** the tunnel.

(The tunnel has an inside.)

❌ He walked **across** the tunnel.

(Not natural in this meaning.)

---

### River

✅ She swam **across** the river.

(From one bank to the other.)

❌ She swam **through** the river.

(Not natural. A river is an open body of water, not an enclosed space.)

---

### Forest

✅ We walked **through** the forest.

(Inside the forest.)

Sometimes you'll also hear:

✅ We walked **across** the forest.

This means you crossed the forest from one edge to the opposite edge. It focuses on **crossing**, not simply being inside.

---

# Another Meaning of "Through"

**Through** can also mean **by means of** or **because of**.

Examples:

✅ I got this job **through** a friend.
(= because of / with the help of a friend)

---

✅ She learned English **through** online courses.
(= by means of online courses)

---

✅ We communicated **through** email.

---

Notice why your previous question was wrong:

❌ The quote was said **through** Mahatma Gandhi.

Why?

Because Gandhi was **the person who said it**, not a **means** or **channel**.

Correct:

✅ The quote was said **by** Mahatma Gandhi.

But:

✅ I heard the news **through** my friend.

(My friend was the source or channel through which I got the news.)

---

## Why do we use **across**?

A **bridge** is something you **cross from one side to the other**.

```
Side A ======== Bridge ======== Side B
      🚗 -------------------->
```

So we say:

* ✅ We drove **across** the bridge.
* ✅ We walked **across** the bridge.
* ✅ They ran **across** the bridge.

The focus is on **crossing** the bridge.

---

## Can we say "through the bridge"?

❌ **We drove through the bridge.**

This is incorrect because you don't drive **inside** a bridge.

Remember:

* **Through** = inside something
* **Across** = from one side to the other

---

## Compare these

### Bridge

✅ We drove **across** the bridge.

---

### Tunnel

✅ We drove **through** the tunnel.

```
Entrance → 🚗 → Inside tunnel → Exit
```

A tunnel has an **inside**, so we use **through**.

---

### Forest

Both can be correct, but they mean slightly different things.

✅ We drove **through** the forest.

* We were driving **inside** the forest.

✅ We drove **across** the forest.

* We crossed the forest from one edge to the other.

---

## A simple rule

Ask yourself this question:

> **Am I moving inside something?**

* Yes → **through**

  * through a tunnel
  * through a cave
  * through a forest

> **Am I crossing from one side to another?**

* Yes → **across**

  * across a bridge
  * across a road
  * across a river
  * across a field
