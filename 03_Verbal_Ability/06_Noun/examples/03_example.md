Excellent! 🔥 This is one of the **most confusing Subject-Verb Agreement rules**, and it's asked in **TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Capgemini, SSC, Banking**, etc.

The trick is that the **meaning is plural**, but the **grammar is singular**.

Let's understand why.

---

# Question 1

### A1

> **Town after town was devastated.** ✅

### B1

> **Town after town were devastated.** ❌

---

## Step 1: Find the subject

What is the subject?

```
Town after town
```

Now most students think:

> There are many towns.

↓

Plural.

↓

Use **were**.

❌ Wrong.

---

## Step 2: Understand the expression

"Town after town"

does **NOT** mean

```
Town + Town
```

Instead it is an **idiomatic expression**.

It means

> One town after another town.

Like a sequence.

```
Town
↓

Next Town
↓

Next Town
↓

Next Town
```

The sentence focuses on **each town individually**.

Because of that,

English treats the whole expression as **singular**.

---

## Therefore

| Subject         | Verb |
| --------------- | ---- |
| Town after town | was  |

✅ Correct

> Town after town **was** devastated.

---

# Think of it like this

Imagine a war.

```
Town A → destroyed

↓

Town B → destroyed

↓

Town C → destroyed

↓

Town D → destroyed
```

You're not saying

> All towns together.

You're saying

> One town after another.

That's why the verb stays singular.

# Question 2

### A2

> Ship after ship are arriving. ❌

### B2

> Ship after ship is arriving. ✅

---

Again

Subject

```
Ship after ship
```

Meaning

```
One ship

↓

Another ship

↓

Another ship

↓

Another ship
```

Each ship arrives one after another.

So grammar treats it as singular.

| Subject         | Verb |
| --------------- | ---- |
| Ship after ship | is   |

# General Rule

Whenever you see

```
Noun after noun
```

Use a **singular verb**.

---

## Examples

### Day after day

> Day after day **passes** quickly.

Not

> Day after day pass.

---

### Student after student

> Student after student **was** absent.

Not

> Student after student were absent.

---

### Car after car

> Car after car **was** stopped.

---

### Question after question

> Question after question **was** asked.

---

# Why Doesn't English Use "Were"?

Because the expression is **not counting**.

It describes a **repeated sequence**.

Imagine dominoes.

```
Town

↓

Town

↓

Town

↓

Town
```

One after another.

English views this as **a repeated singular event**, so the verb is singular.

---

# Similar Expressions

These all take **singular verbs**.

> Page after page **was** torn.

---

### House after house

> House after house **was** destroyed.

---

### Wave after wave

> Wave after wave **was** hitting the shore.

---

### Visitor after visitor

> Visitor after visitor **was** arriving.

# Tricky Comparison

## One by one

> The students entered one by one.

Here

"one by one" is an adverbial phrase, so you don't decide the verb from it.

---

## One after another

> One student after another **was** called.

Singular.

# ⭐ Now I'll teach you the deeper grammar

The rule behind this is:

Normally,

```
Singular noun

↓

Singular verb
```

```
Town

↓

was
```

But in

```
Town after town
```

you still have a **singular noun repeated**.

Notice carefully.

It is **NOT**

```
Towns after towns
```

It is

```
Town

after

Town
```

Both nouns are singular.

The expression simply emphasizes repetition.

That's why the verb **doesn't become plural**.

---

# Easy Memory Trick

Whenever you see this pattern:

```
Noun + after + noun
```

Read it in your mind as:

> **One ___ after another ___**

Examples:

* Town after town → One town after another town
* Ship after ship → One ship after another ship
* House after house → One house after another house

Since you're talking about **one at a time**, use a **singular verb**:

* **was**
* **is**
* **has**

---

This is a very high-scoring rule because most students think **"many towns = were"**, but the exam tests whether you know the **grammatical structure**, not just the meaning.
