---

# Rule 1

When there is **NO number before million**, we use

```text
millions of
```

Meaning

A very large number.

Example

> Millions of people watched the match.

We don't know exactly how many.

Just a huge number.

---

Example

> He donated millions of rupees.

Meaning

He donated a huge amount.

Not exact.

---

# Rule 2

When there **IS a number**, don't add **S**.

Examples

One million

Two million

Five million

Ten million

Notice

Million never becomes

Millions

---

# Very Important Rule

| Expression     | Correct?                                                                                     |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two million    | ✅                                                                                            |
| Five hundred   | ✅                                                                                            |
| Seven thousand | ✅                                                                                            |
| Millions of    | ✅                                                                                            |
| Hundreds of    | ✅                                                                                            |
| Thousands of   | ✅                                                                                            |
| Two millions   | ❌                                                                                            |
| Five hundreds  | ❌                                                                                            |
| Million of     | ❌ (unless preceded by a number, in which case it becomes "one million", "two million", etc.) |

## One Small Note About the Image

The first sentence is perfectly fine.

The second sentence:

> **The Bus Driver donated millions of rupees.**

is also correct if the meaning is **an unspecified, very large amount**.

If the speaker meant an exact amount, the sentence should include a number, for example:

> **The bus driver donated two million rupees.**

Notice that **million** stays singular after **two**. This distinction between **exact number** and **large, unspecified quantity** is exactly what placement exams often test.
