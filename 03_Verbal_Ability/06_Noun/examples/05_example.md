Excellent observation. 👏

This is exactly the kind of question you should ask instead of memorizing rules.

**Your intuition is actually correct.** In normal English, **all four sentences are grammatically correct.**

The difference is **not grammar**, it's **style and naturalness**.

Let's understand.

---

# Sentence 1

### A1

> **My room's roof is leaking badly.**

Is it grammatically correct?

✅ Yes.

But...

Native speakers don't usually say this.

Why?

Because **a room cannot really "own" a roof**.

The roof belongs to the **house**, not the room.

So it sounds a little unnatural.

---

### B1

> **The roof of my room is leaking badly.**

This sounds much more natural.

Because for **parts of objects**, English often prefers

```text
the + noun + of + object
```

Examples

> the roof of the house

> the door of the room

> the wheels of the car

instead of

> the room's roof

---

## Compare

### Natural

> The roof of my room.

### Less natural

> My room's roof.

---

# Rule

English usually prefers **of** when talking about

* parts of things
* objects
* buildings

Examples

✅ the door of the car

✅ the roof of the house

✅ the pages of the book

Instead of

❌ the car's door (possible, but less common in formal English)

❌ the house's roof (possible, but less common)

---

# Here's the Real Rule

This is one of the most important possessive rules.

## We usually use **'s** for

### People

Rahul's book

Mother's love

Teacher's pen

John's laptop

---

### Animals

Dog's tail

Cat's ears

Horse's legs

---

### Time

Today's news

Tomorrow's meeting

Yesterday's match

---

### Organizations

Google's CEO

India's economy

OpenAI's model

---

# We usually use **of** for

### Things

The roof of the house

The color of the car

The pages of the book

The handle of the door

The top of the mountain

---

# Compare

### Person

Rahul's phone

NOT

The phone of Rahul

(Both are correct, but Rahul's phone is much more natural.)

---

### Object

The engine of the car

Instead of

The car's engine

(Both are grammatically correct, but **the engine of the car** is often preferred in formal English.)

## One Important Note

Your question:

> **"It feels like all 4 sentences are correct."**

**You're right.**

If this were a pure grammar test, I'd agree with you.

The reason the teacher is marking one as better is because they're teaching **preferred standard usage**, not absolute grammatical correctness.

Many placement exams ask for the **best** or **most appropriate** sentence, and in those questions, **natural usage** matters in addition to grammar.

So think of it like this:

| Sentence                            | Grammatically Correct? | Most Natural?  |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------- |
| My room's roof is leaking.          | ✅ Yes                  | ❌ Less natural |
| The roof of my room is leaking.     | ✅ Yes                  | ✅ More natural |
| Jethalal's crush is beautiful.      | ✅ Yes                  | ✅ More natural |
| The crush of Jethalal is beautiful. | ✅ Yes                  | ❌ Less natural |

This distinction between **correct** and **preferred** English is something you'll encounter quite often in sentence improvement questions.
