# Question

### A)

> This is my **Brother's-in-law** house.

### B)

> This is my **Brother-in-law's** house.

---

## Step 1: Find the owner

Who owns the house?

Not

```text
Brother
```

The owner is

```text
Brother-in-law
```

That is the complete relationship.

---

## Step 2: Is "brother-in-law" one noun or three nouns?

Although it has three words,

```text
Brother + in + law
```

English treats it as **one compound noun**.

Just like

* mother-in-law
* father-in-law
* editor-in-chief

They are all **one noun**.

---

## Step 3: Where should we put `'s`?

Rule:

> **Add `'s` to the end of the complete compound noun.**

So

```text
Brother-in-law

↓

Brother-in-law's
```

✅ Correct

---

## Why is A wrong?

A says

```text
Brother's-in-law
```

Let's break it.

```text
Brother's

+

in-law
```

Here you made only **brother** possessive.

But the owner is **not just "brother."**

The owner is

```text
Brother-in-law
```

You broke the noun into two parts.

That is why it is wrong.

---

# Think of it this way

Suppose your friend's name is

```text
Mary Jane
```

Now you want to say

Mary Jane's book.

Would you write

```text
Mary's Jane book
```

😂 No.

Because **Mary Jane** is one complete name.

Similarly,

```text
Brother-in-law
```

is one complete noun.

So

```text
Brother-in-law's house
```

# A Trick for Placement Exams

Whenever you see:

* brother-in-law
* father-in-law
* mother-in-law
* sister-in-law
* son-in-law
* daughter-in-law

Think:

```text
Whole phrase = ONE noun
```

Then

```text
Whole noun + 's
```
