Excellent! This is exactly the type of concept that placement exams test. They want to check whether you know **how to count an uncountable noun**.

Let's solve both questions slowly.

---

# Question 1

### A1)

> **My friend gave me all information.**

### B1)

> **My friend gave me all pieces of information.**

Which is correct?

## First, identify the noun.

The noun is:

```text
Information
```

Now ask:

> Is "information" countable?

**No.**

| Noun        | Countable/Uncountable | Verb |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ---- |
| Information | Uncountable           | is   |

---

### Can we count information?

Can we say

```text
1 information

2 informations

3 informations
```

❌ No.

---

### Then how do we count it?

We count the **unit**, not the information itself.

Examples:

* a piece of information
* two pieces of information
* three pieces of information

Notice:

We are counting **pieces**, not **information**.

---

### Is A1 correct?

> My friend gave me all information.

This is **not natural English**. After **all**, English normally expects **the information** (if specific) or **all the information**.

Correct forms are:

> My friend gave me **all the information**. ✅

or

> My friend gave me **all pieces of information**. (Grammatically possible, but less natural.)

---

### Is B1 correct?

> My friend gave me all pieces of information.

This is grammatically acceptable because you're counting **pieces**.

However, in everyday English, native speakers usually say:

> **My friend gave me all the information.** ✅ (Most natural)

or

> **My friend gave me all the pieces of information.** (If you want to emphasize separate pieces.)

---

## Final answer

Between the two options shown,

✅ **B1 is better** because it avoids treating **information** as directly countable.

But the **most natural sentence** is:

> **My friend gave me all the information.**

---

# Question 2

### A2)

> He ate two bread.

### B2)

> He ate two slices of bread.

---

## Step 1

Find the noun.

```text
Bread
```

Can we count bread?

Can we say

```text
1 bread

2 breads
```

Normally,

❌ No.

Bread is **uncountable**.

| Noun  | Countable/Uncountable | Verb |
| ----- | --------------------- | ---- |
| Bread | Uncountable           | is   |

---

## Then how do we count bread?

We count the **piece**.

Examples:

* a slice of bread
* two slices of bread
* a loaf of bread
* two loaves of bread

We count:

```text
Slices

Loaves

Pieces
```

NOT

Bread.

---

### Is A2 correct?

> He ate two bread.

Wrong.

Because

Bread is uncountable.

---

### Is B2 correct?

> He ate two slices of bread.

Correct.

Here

Subject = He

Verb = ate

Object = two slices of bread

Everything is correct.

---

# The Main Rule

Whenever you have an **uncountable noun**, ask:

> **What unit do I use to count it?**

| Uncountable Noun | Count It With        |
| ---------------- | -------------------- |
| Information      | piece                |
| Advice           | piece                |
| Bread            | slice, loaf          |
| Water            | bottle, glass, litre |
| Rice             | bowl, bag, kg        |
| Sugar            | spoon, kg            |
| Furniture        | piece                |
| Equipment        | piece                |
| Luggage          | piece, bag           |
| Paper (material) | sheet                |

---

# A Small Correction to the Image

The image is trying to teach the right idea, but the first sentence is incomplete.

It says:

> **My friend gave me all information.**

In standard English, it should be:

> ✅ **My friend gave me all the information.**

because **all information** without **the** sounds unnatural in this context.

## 🎯 A Pattern You'll See in Placement Exams

Examiners often ask questions like these:

Which sentence is correct?

A. She gave me **two advices**.

B. She gave me **two pieces of advice**.

✅ Correct answer: **B**

---

A. I bought **three breads**.

B. I bought **three loaves of bread**.

✅ Correct answer: **B**

---

A. We need **many equipments**.

B. We need **a lot of equipment**.

✅ Correct answer: **B**

---

These questions aren't really testing vocabulary—they're testing whether you recognize that some nouns are **uncountable** and therefore must be counted using a **unit** (piece, slice, bottle, loaf, etc.) instead of making the noun plural.
