# What is a Compound Noun?

A **compound noun** is **one noun made by joining two or more words**.

Think of it like LEGO blocks.

```text
Word 1 + Word 2 = One New Noun
```

Example

```text
Foot + ball = Football
```

Now,

Football is **one noun**, not two.

---

Another

```text
Black + board = Blackboard
```

Again,

One noun.

---

# Easy Definition

A compound noun is

> **A noun made by combining two or more words that together have one meaning.**

---

# Types of Compound Nouns

There are **3 types**.

---

# Type 1: Closed Compound Nouns

The words are joined together.

Examples

| Word 1 | Word 2 | New Word  |
| ------ | ------ | --------- |
| Foot   | Ball   | Football  |
| Class  | Room   | Classroom |
| Note   | Book   | Notebook  |
| Sun    | Flower | Sunflower |

These become one word.

---

# Type 2: Hyphenated Compound Nouns ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

These use a hyphen (-).

Examples

* Brother-in-law
* Mother-in-law
* Father-in-law
* Sister-in-law
* Son-in-law
* Daughter-in-law

Also

* Passer-by
* Editor-in-chief
* Commander-in-chief

These are asked in exams.

---

# Type 3: Open Compound Nouns

They are written as separate words but together mean one thing.

Examples

* Bus stop
* Ice cream
* Post office
* High school
* Coffee table

Example

```text
Bus + Stop
```

Together they mean

One place.

---

# How do you know it's ONE noun?

Ask yourself

Does the whole phrase have **one meaning**?

Example

```text
Brother-in-law
```

Does it mean

Brother

*

Law

?

No.

It means

> Your spouse's brother (or a sibling's husband, depending on the relationship).

It has one meaning.

Therefore,

It is one noun.

---

# Another Example

Ice cream

Does it mean

Ice

*

Cream

?

No.

It means a dessert.

One meaning.

One noun.

---

# Why are Compound Nouns Important?

Because many grammar rules depend on them.

---

## Rule 1

Plural Form

Example

Brother-in-law

Plural?

Many students think

Brother-in-laws

❌

Wrong.

Correct

Brothers-in-law

✅

---

Why?

Because

Brother

is the main noun.

---

Another

Passer-by

Plural?

Passers-by

NOT

Passer-bys

---

# Rule 2

Possessive Form

Example

Brother-in-law

Possessive?

Brother-in-law's

NOT

Brother's-in-law

---

# Rule 3

Articles

Example

A bus stop

An ice cream

❌

No.

Because

Ice cream starts with

Ice

↓

A vowel sound

So

An ice cream

would be grammatically possible by sound, but in natural English we usually say:

> I'd like **an ice cream**. ✅

For **bus stop**, we say:

> a bus stop

---

# Compound Adjectives

This is another concept.

Don't confuse it with compound nouns.

Example

Five-star hotel

Is

Five-star

a noun?

No.

It describes

Hotel.

So

It is a

Compound Adjective.

---

Compare

| Compound Noun | Compound Adjective |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| Bus stop      | Five-star          |
| Ice cream     | Ten-rupee          |
| Football      | Two-hour           |
| Classroom     | Three-day          |

One is a noun.

One is an adjective.

---

# One More Thing

I've noticed something about your learning style over the past few days.

You don't like memorizing isolated rules—you always ask **"Why?"** and try to connect topics. That's actually the best way to learn grammar because most placement questions combine several concepts.

So from now on, whenever we learn a new topic, I'll always show you:

```text
Concept
        ↓
Why it exists
        ↓
Grammar rule
        ↓
Placement shortcut
        ↓
Common mistakes
        ↓
Tricky questions
        ↓
Connections to other grammar topics
```

That way you'll build a connected understanding instead of a list of rules, and later topics like **Subject–Verb Agreement**, **Articles**, and **Pronouns** will make much more sense because you'll already know how they fit together.
