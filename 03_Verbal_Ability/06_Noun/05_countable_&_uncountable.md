Excellent question. This is actually the **root of the confusion**. Most grammar books teach **Singular/Plural** and **Countable/Uncountable** as separate topics, but they're actually connected.

Let's connect everything.

---

# The Relationship

Think of nouns like this:

```text
                    NOUN
                      │
         ┌────────────┴────────────┐
         │                         │
   Countable                  Uncountable
         │                         │
   ┌─────┴─────┐                   │
   │           │                   │
Singular    Plural            (Usually Singular)
```

Notice something:

> **Only countable nouns can have both singular and plural forms.**

Uncountable nouns **usually have only one form** and take a **singular verb**.

---

# Countable Nouns

Countable nouns have **two forms**.

Example: Book

### Singular

```text
One book
```

Sentence:

> A book **is** on the table.

| Noun | Verb |
| ---- | ---- |
| book | is   |

---

### Plural

```text
Two books
```

Sentence:

> Two books **are** on the table.

| Noun  | Verb |
| ----- | ---- |
| books | are  |

Notice the verb changed.

---

Another example

### Singular

> The student **is** studying.

| Noun    | Verb |
| ------- | ---- |
| student | is   |

---

### Plural

> The students **are** studying.

| Noun     | Verb |
| -------- | ---- |
| students | are  |

---

# Rule

For countable nouns:

```text
Singular noun
↓

Singular verb
```

```text
Plural noun
↓

Plural verb
```

---

# Uncountable Nouns

Now look at water.

Can we say

> one water

Normally, No.

Can we say

> two waters

Normally, No.

Water doesn't have a plural form in normal usage.

So English treats it like **one thing**.

Sentence:

> Water **is** important.

| Noun  | Verb |
| ----- | ---- |
| water | is   |

---

Another

> Milk **is** healthy.

| Noun | Verb |
| ---- | ---- |
| milk | is   |

---

Another

> Information **is** useful.

| Noun        | Verb |
| ----------- | ---- |
| information | is   |

---

Another

> Advice **is** valuable.

| Noun   | Verb |
| ------ | ---- |
| advice | is   |

Notice

Every uncountable noun takes a **singular verb**.

---

# Why?

Imagine water.

Can you divide it into

```text
1 water

2 waters

3 waters
```

No.

English thinks of it as **one mass**.

That's why

```text
Water

↓

Singular

↓

is
```

# But Wait...

You may ask

> Then how do I count water?

Good question.

We count the **container**.

Example

One bottle of water

Now the subject is

```text
Bottle
```

Bottle is countable.

Sentence

> One bottle of water **is** enough.

| Subject | Verb |
| ------- | ---- |
| bottle  | is   |

---

Now

> Two bottles of water **are** enough.

| Subject | Verb |
| ------- | ---- |
| bottles | are  |

Notice

We are **not counting water**.

We are counting bottles.

---

Another

> Three glasses of milk **are** on the table.

Subject

```text
glasses
```

Plural

↓

are

# Some Words Change Meaning

Now comes the tricky part.

## Hair

General

> Her hair **is** beautiful.

Hair = all the hair on her head (uncountable)

| Noun | Verb |
| ---- | ---- |
| hair | is   |

---

One strand

> There **is** a hair in my soup.

Now

Hair = one strand

Countable

---

Two strands

> There **are** two hairs in my soup.

Now it's plural.

# The Verb Rule

This is the table I always use.

| Type               | Example | Verb              |
| ------------------ | ------- | ----------------- |
| Countable Singular | Book    | is / has / was    |
| Countable Plural   | Books   | are / have / were |
| Uncountable        | Water   | is / has / was    |

Examples:

| Sentence                       | Why?               |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| The book **is** interesting.   | Singular countable |
| The books **are** interesting. | Plural countable   |
| Water **is** essential.        | Uncountable        |
| Information **is** useful.     | Uncountable        |
| Advice **is** valuable.        | Uncountable        |
| Furniture **is** expensive.    | Uncountable        |
| Equipment **is** new.          | Uncountable        |

# A Quick Trick for Exams

Whenever you see a noun, ask these questions **in order**:

### Step 1

Can I count it?

↓

Yes

↓

Countable

↓

Can it be one or many?

↓

Singular → **is / has / was**

Plural → **are / have / were**

---

No

↓

Uncountable

↓

Always use

**is / has / was** (in normal usage)

# From now on...

I think this will make learning much easier for you:

Whenever I introduce a noun, I'll always show it in this format:

| Noun    | Countable/Uncountable | Singular      | Plural       | Verb     |
| ------- | --------------------- | ------------- | ------------ | -------- |
| Book    | Countable             | Book          | Books        | is / are |
| Water   | Uncountable           | Water         | —            | is       |
| Advice  | Uncountable           | Advice        | —            | is       |
| Student | Countable             | Student       | Students     | is / are |
| Hair    | Both                  | Hair / a hair | Hairs (rare) | is / are |

This way you'll learn **the noun, its plural (if any), and the correct verb together**, which is exactly how these concepts are tested in placement exams.
