# Chapter 1: What is a Noun?

A **noun** is the **name of a person, place, thing, animal, or idea**.

Think of it like this:

> **If you can give something a name, it is probably a noun.**

Examples:

| Category     | Noun                     |
| ------------ | ------------------------ |
| Person       | Rahul, Doctor, Teacher   |
| Place        | India, Kolkata, School   |
| Thing        | Book, Laptop, Phone      |
| Animal       | Dog, Tiger, Cow          |
| Idea/Feeling | Love, Honesty, Happiness |

Examples in sentences:

* **Rahul** is my friend.
* I live in **Kolkata**.
* This **book** is interesting.
* The **dog** is sleeping.
* **Honesty** is important.

The bold words are all **nouns**.

---

# How to identify a noun?

Whenever you read a sentence, ask:

> **Who?** or **What?**

Example:

> The boy is playing football.

Who is playing?

→ **boy** (noun)

What is he playing?

→ **football** (noun)

# Types of Nouns

There are **five main types** that placement exams focus on.

---

# 1. Proper Noun

A **proper noun** is the **specific name** of a person, place, or thing.

Examples:

* Rahul
* India
* Google
* Kolkata
* Ganga

Notice something:

They always begin with a **capital letter**.

Correct:

> Rahul lives in India.

Wrong:

> rahul lives in india.

---

# 2. Common Noun

A common noun is a **general name**.

Examples:

* boy
* girl
* city
* river
* company

Compare:

| Common Noun | Proper Noun |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| boy         | Rahul       |
| city        | Kolkata     |
| company     | Google      |
| river       | Ganga       |

Think of it like this:

**Common noun = category**

**Proper noun = actual name**

---

# 3. Collective Noun

A collective noun means

> **one word representing a group.**

Examples:

* team
* family
* class
* crowd
* committee
* army

Example:

> The team won the match.

Many players

↓

One team

---

Another:

> My family lives in Kolkata.

Many people

↓

One family

---

# 4. Material Noun

These are materials from which things are made.

Examples:

* Gold
* Iron
* Wood
* Cotton
* Water
* Plastic

Example:

This ring is made of gold.

The chair is made of wood.

---

# 5. Abstract Noun

Abstract means

> You **cannot see or touch** it.

Examples:

* Love
* Beauty
* Courage
* Honesty
* Intelligence
* Happiness

Can you touch happiness?

❌ No

Can you see honesty?

❌ No

These are ideas or qualities.

# Countable and Uncountable Nouns

This is one of the **most important topics** for placement exams.

---

## Countable Nouns

Things you can count.

Examples:

* one apple
* two apples
* three books

These can use

* a
* an
* many
* few

Examples:

a pen

three pens

many books

---

## Uncountable Nouns

Things you usually cannot count individually.

Examples:

* Water
* Milk
* Sugar
* Rice
* Information
* Advice
* Furniture
* Luggage

Wrong

❌ an advice

❌ two informations

Correct

✅ some advice

✅ much information

---

# Singular and Plural

Singular = one

Plural = more than one

Examples

book → books

pen → pens

girl → girls

---

Some irregular plurals

| Singular | Plural   |
| -------- | -------- |
| Man      | Men      |
| Woman    | Women    |
| Child    | Children |
| Tooth    | Teeth    |
| Foot     | Feet     |
| Mouse    | Mice     |
| Goose    | Geese    |
| Person   | People   |

These are asked very often.

---

# Rules for making plurals

## Rule 1

Add **s**

book → books

cat → cats

pen → pens

---

## Rule 2

If ending with

s

x

z

ch

sh

Add **es**

box → boxes

bus → buses

watch → watches

dish → dishes

---

## Rule 3

Consonant + y

Change y → ies

city → cities

baby → babies

lady → ladies

---

But

Vowel + y

Just add s

boy → boys

toy → toys

key → keys

---

## Rule 4

Ends in f or fe

Sometimes change to ves

knife → knives

wife → wives

wolf → wolves

But

roof → roofs

belief → beliefs

(not every word changes)

---

# Possessive Nouns

Possessive means

**ownership**.

Rahul's book

The girl's bag

India's economy

If plural already ends in s

students'

teachers'

Example

The students' classroom.

# For Placement Exams (Very Important)

The questions on nouns usually test these patterns:

1. **Countable vs. Uncountable nouns** (information, advice, furniture, luggage)
2. **Singular vs. Plural forms** (child → children, man → men)
3. **Subject-Verb Agreement** (The team is..., The players are...)
4. **Possessive nouns** (Rahul's, students')
5. **Faulty noun forms** (informations, furnitures, advices)

These are the patterns you should master first, because they appear much more often than simply identifying the type of noun.
