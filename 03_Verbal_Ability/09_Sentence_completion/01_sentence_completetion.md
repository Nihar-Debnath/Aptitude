Sentence Completion is a **Verbal Ability** topic where you are given an **incomplete sentence** and asked to choose the word(s), phrase(s), or clause(s) that make the sentence **grammatically correct, logically meaningful, and contextually appropriate**.

It tests whether you can understand:

* Grammar
* Vocabulary
* Logic
* Context
* Tone

---

# Types of Sentence Completion

## Type 1: Single Blank

One missing word.

**Example:**

> The manager was ________ with the team's performance.

Options:

* A) delighted ✅
* B) delaying
* C) declining
* D) damaging

**Answer:** A

Reason:
"Delighted with" is the correct collocation and makes logical sense.

---

## Type 2: Double Blank

Two words are missing.

**Example:**

> Although the task looked difficult, she completed it with ________ and ________.

Options:

* A) fear, panic
* B) confidence, ease ✅
* C) confusion, doubt
* D) anger, frustration

Here both blanks must fit together logically.

---

## Type 3: Connector / Transition Word

Choose the word that connects two ideas.

Example:

> He studied hard; ________, he failed the exam.

Options:

* Therefore
* However ✅
* Moreover
* Likewise

Since the second idea contrasts with the first, **However** is correct.

---

## Type 4: Idioms & Phrases

Example:

> Don't judge a book by its ________.

Options:

* pages
* cover ✅
* title
* author

---

## Type 5: Vocabulary Based

Example:

> The scientist made an ________ discovery.

Options:

* ordinary
* insignificant
* extraordinary ✅
* average

---

## Type 6: Context Based

Example:

> The baby stopped crying after her mother ________ her.

Options:

* ignored
* comforted ✅
* punished
* blamed

Grammar alone isn't enough—you need the word that best fits the situation.

# Rules to Solve Sentence Completion

### 1. Read the whole sentence first.

Don't look at the options immediately.

Ask yourself:

> "What kind of word is missing?"

---

### 2. Understand the meaning.

Find the main idea.

Example:

> The movie was so ________ that everyone fell asleep.

You can predict the answer should mean **boring** before checking the options.

---

### 3. Identify clue words.

Certain words tell you how the sentence is structured:

| Word      | Meaning             |
| --------- | ------------------- |
| although  | contrast            |
| however   | contrast            |
| but       | contrast            |
| because   | reason              |
| therefore | result              |
| moreover  | addition            |
| despite   | unexpected contrast |
| since     | reason              |

These clue words help eliminate incorrect options.

---

### 4. Check grammar.

Make sure the option matches the required part of speech.

Example:

> She is a very ________ student.

You need an adjective, not a verb or noun.

---

### 5. Check collocations.

Some words naturally go together.

Examples:

* Make a decision ✅
* Take a risk ✅
* Heavy rain ✅
* Strong coffee ✅
* Pay attention ✅

Incorrect combinations:

* Do a decision ❌
* Powerful rain ❌

---

### 6. Eliminate wrong options.

Usually 2–3 options are clearly wrong because they:

* Don't fit the meaning
* Are grammatically incorrect
* Don't match the tone
* Use an incorrect collocation

---

# Example

> Although Ravi was tired, he continued working because he was very ________.

Options:

* lazy
* determined ✅
* careless
* nervous

### Step 1: Notice the clue word

"Although" signals contrast.

### Step 2: Understand the meaning

He was tired, **but** he kept working.

### Step 3: Eliminate

* Lazy ❌ (wouldn't keep working)
* Careless ❌ (doesn't explain persistence)
* Nervous ❌ (not relevant)
* Determined ✅ (fits perfectly)

---

# Your Shortcut Method

When solving Sentence Completion, follow this order:

1. **Read the complete sentence.**
2. **Identify clue words** (although, but, however, because, therefore, etc.).
3. **Predict the missing word** in your own mind before looking at the options.
4. **Check grammar** (noun, verb, adjective, adverb).
5. **Use elimination** to remove options that don't fit the meaning or collocation.
6. **Choose the option that is grammatically correct, logically consistent, and contextually appropriate.**

Since you're preparing for **TCS Ignite**, this topic is usually moderate in difficulty. The biggest scoring advantage comes from quickly spotting clue words and eliminating obviously incorrect options rather than relying on difficult vocabulary.
