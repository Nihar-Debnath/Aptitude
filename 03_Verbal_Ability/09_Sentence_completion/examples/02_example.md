This is **exactly** how TCS expects you to solve these questions. They aren't testing whether you know Dhoni—they're testing whether you can identify the **required part of speech** from the sentence structure.

Let's solve it like an English grammar expert.

---

## The Question

> **MS Dhoni has gained extraordinary ______ throughout the world.**

Options:

A) Momentous

B) playing

C) recognition ✅

D) aggressively

---

# Step 1: Break the sentence into chunks

```
MS Dhoni | has gained | extraordinary ______ | throughout the world
 Subject      Verb            Object
```

The main structure is:

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

---

# Step 2: Find the main verb

The verb is

> **has gained**

Now ask yourself:

> **Gain WHAT?**

Examples:

* gained experience
* gained knowledge
* gained popularity
* gained respect

The verb **gain** normally takes a **noun** as its object.

So after "gained", we need a **noun**.

---

# Step 3: Look at the word before the blank

The word before the blank is

> **extraordinary**

Ask:

**What part of speech is "extraordinary"?**

It is an **adjective**.

Now remember this important grammar pattern:

> **Adjective + Noun**

Examples:

* beautiful girl
* honest person
* difficult exam
* extraordinary achievement
* extraordinary recognition

So after **extraordinary**, we need a **noun**.

---

# Step 4: Identify the part of speech of each option

### A) Momentous

Meaning: very important

Part of Speech:

✅ Adjective

Example:

> a momentous occasion

Can we say:

> extraordinary momentous

❌ No.

It's adjective + adjective, and there is no noun after them.

---

### B) Playing

Part of Speech:

Usually a **verb** (or gerund depending on context).

Can we say:

> extraordinary playing

Not naturally in this sentence after **has gained**.

Also,

> gained playing

doesn't make sense.

---

### C) Recognition

Part of Speech:

✅ Noun

Examples:

* gain recognition
* gain respect
* gain popularity

Now read it:

> MS Dhoni has gained extraordinary recognition throughout the world.

Perfect.

---

### D) Aggressively

Part of Speech:

Adverb

Adverbs modify verbs.

Examples:

* runs aggressively
* speaks aggressively

Can we say

> extraordinary aggressively

No.

Adjective + Adverb is incorrect here.

---

# The Grammar Formula

Whenever you see

```
Verb + Adjective + ______
```

think

```
Verb + Adjective + Noun
```

Because adjectives describe nouns.

---

# Another Way (Phrase Pattern)

Memorize this pattern:

```
Gain + Noun
```

Examples:

* gain knowledge
* gain confidence
* gain experience
* gain recognition
* gain popularity
* gain respect

Notice that **gain** is almost always followed by a **noun**.

---

# Parts of Speech Shortcut

| If you see...        | The blank is usually... |
| -------------------- | ----------------------- |
| very ______          | Adjective               |
| an ______            | Noun                    |
| a ______             | Noun                    |
| has gained ______    | Noun                    |
| is ______            | Adjective               |
| runs ______          | Adverb                  |
| speaks ______        | Adverb                  |
| to ______            | Verb                    |
| can ______           | Verb                    |
| will ______          | Verb                    |
| extraordinary ______ | Noun                    |
| beautiful ______     | Noun                    |
| honest ______        | Noun                    |

# Your Solving Order for Parts of Speech Questions

Whenever you see a sentence completion question, follow this sequence:

### Step 1

Find the **main verb**.

> Here: **has gained**

---

### Step 2

Ask:

> "What does this verb require?"

Here:

**gain → noun**

---

### Step 3

Look at the word before the blank.

Here:

**extraordinary** → adjective

Adjectives describe nouns, so the blank should be a noun.

---

### Step 4

Check each option's part of speech.

* Momentous → Adjective ❌
* Playing → Verb/Gerund ❌
* Recognition → Noun ✅
* Aggressively → Adverb ❌

Only **recognition** satisfies **both** the grammar and the meaning.

---

This is the exact thought process you should train for TCS:

**Sentence structure → Main verb → Word before the blank → Required part of speech → Eliminate options.**

Once you practice this, you'll often be able to eliminate **3 options without even reading their meanings**. That's one of the fastest ways to solve sentence completion questions in aptitude exams.
