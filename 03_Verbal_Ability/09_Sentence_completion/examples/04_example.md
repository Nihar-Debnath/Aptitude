You're right that some answer keys mark **3) goes**. The reason is that this is using an **informal idiomatic expression**.

> **That girl really goes.**

Here, **goes** does **not** mean "walks" or "travels."

It means:

> **She is very energetic, lively, or impressive.**

Examples:

* *This car really goes.* → The car is very fast.
* *That band really goes.* → The band performs with great energy.
* *That girl really goes.* → The girl is very lively/energetic.

### Why not the others?

* **takes** ❌ Usually needs an object (*takes what?*)
* **talks** ❌ Usually you'd say *talks a lot* or *talks well*.
* **backs** ❌ Usually needs an object (*backs whom?*)
* **goes** ✅ Can stand alone as an idiomatic expression.

### **Answer: ✅ 3) hears about**

The sentence:

> **If he ______ your plans, he will be surprised.**

### Step 1: Identify the grammar rule

This is a **First Conditional** sentence.

Pattern:

> **If + Present Simple, ... will + Verb**

So after **If he**, we need a **present simple verb**.

* hears for ❌
* would hear ❌ (wrong tense)
* hears about ✅ (present simple)
* will hear ❌ ("will" is not used in the *if*-clause)

---

### Step 2: Check the verb + preposition

The correct expression is:

> **hear about something** ✅

Examples:

* I heard about the accident.
* She heard about your success.
* He heard about your plans.

We **do not** say:

* hear for ❌

---

### Final sentence

> **If he hears about your plans, he will be surprised.** ✅

**Meaning:** If he comes to know about your plans, he will be surprised.

### Quick Trick for Exams

Whenever you see:

> **If + ______ , will + verb**

👉 Fill the blank with **Present Simple** (not *will*, not *would*).

So the answer is **✅ 3) hears about**.
