This is a **Vocabulary / Word Usage** question, specifically a **contextual vocabulary (fill-in-the-blank)** question.

The question gives you a sentence with a blank and asks you to choose the word that makes the sentence **both grammatically and contextually correct**.

### Step 1: Understand the sentence

> She arranged them on the big table on the terrace where they looked ______ like clothes set out for a wedding.

The important part is:

> **"looked ______ like clothes set out for a wedding."**

We need an adjective/adverb that describes **how they looked**.

### Step 2: Understand the options

| Word           | Meaning                                       |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Imperfect**  | Having flaws; not perfect                     |
| **Impeccable** | Perfect; without any fault                    |
| **Faultless**  | Without any mistakes or faults                |
| **Criminal**   | Related to crime; someone who commits a crime |

Now ask: **Which one makes sense in context?**

If clothes are arranged on a table and they look beautiful/neat/perfect, we can say:

> They looked **impeccable** like clothes set out for a wedding.

But there's an important grammatical point: **"impeccable" normally means flawless/perfect**, and the sentence's natural construction is actually somewhat awkward.

Among the given options, **B. Impeccable** is the intended answer.

### Why not the others?

**A. Imperfect ❌**

> They looked imperfect like clothes set out for a wedding.

This suggests the clothes had flaws. The wedding comparison doesn't support that meaning.

**C. Faultless ❌**

"Faultless" also means without any fault, so semantically it could describe clothes. However, **"impeccable" is the more appropriate vocabulary choice in this sentence/question.**

**D. Criminal ❌**

> They looked criminal like clothes...

Doesn't make contextual sense.

### 🧠 How to solve these questions in exams

Don't immediately look for grammar rules. Use this **3-step method**:

**1. Identify what the blank is describing.**

Here:

> they looked ______

So the blank needs a word describing their appearance.

**2. Know the meanings of the options.**

* imperfect → flawed
* impeccable → perfect
* faultless → perfect
* criminal → related to crime

**3. Put each option into the sentence and check the meaning.**

The sentence is describing clothes arranged in a way that resembles clothes prepared for a wedding. The intended positive meaning is **impeccable**.
