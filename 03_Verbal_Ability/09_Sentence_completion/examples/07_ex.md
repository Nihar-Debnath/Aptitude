The correct answer is **B. had she** ✅

> **Hardly had she drunk the magic liquid when she began to see everything in strange and vivid colours.**

This is a very important **inversion rule** in English grammar.

### Why is it "had she"?

Normally, we say:

> She had drunk the liquid **when** she began to see colours.

But when a sentence starts with **Hardly**, **Scarcely**, or **Barely**, we use **inversion**.

### The rule

**Hardly + had + subject + V3 + when + past simple**

So:

> **Hardly had she drunk** the liquid **when** she began...

The normal order:

> She **had drunk** the liquid.

After **Hardly**:

> Hardly **had she drunk** the liquid.

Notice that **had comes before the subject "she"**.

### 🔥 Remember these three patterns

| Starting word | Structure                          | Connector |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------- |
| **Hardly**    | Hardly + **had + subject + V3**    | **when**  |
| **Scarcely**  | Scarcely + **had + subject + V3**  | **when**  |
| **No sooner** | No sooner + **had + subject + V3** | **than**  |

Examples:

> **Hardly had I reached** home **when** it started raining.

> **Scarcely had he arrived** **when** the meeting began.

> **No sooner had she entered** **than** everyone became silent.

### Why the other options are wrong?

**A. she had ❌**

> Hardly she had drunk...

No inversion. Incorrect.

**B. had she ✅**

> Hardly had she drunk...

Correct inversion.

**C. has she ❌**

The second clause is in the **past** ("began"), so **had** is appropriate, not **has**.

**D. she had had ❌**

"Had had" is possible in some sentences, but here it would mean **past perfect of "have"**, which doesn't fit because the verb needed is **drunk**.

### ⭐ Exam shortcut

Whenever you see:

**Hardly / Scarcely / Barely / No sooner**

immediately think:

> **INVERSION!**

And remember:

**Hardly → when**
**Scarcely → when**
**Barely → when**
**No sooner → than**

So your answer is:

**✅ B. had she**
