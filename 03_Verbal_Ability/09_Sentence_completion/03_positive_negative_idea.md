This is actually the **core trick** of Sentence Completion. Once you understand it, many questions become much easier.

When I say **+ve** and **-ve**, I'm **not talking about mathematics**. I'm talking about the **emotion, quality, or meaning** of the words.

Let's learn it step by step.

---

# What does +ve mean?

A word with a **positive meaning**.

Examples:

* Honest ✅
* Intelligent ✅
* Brave ✅
* Happy ✅
* Beautiful ✅
* Successful ✅
* Hardworking ✅
* Generous ✅

These all give a **good impression**.

---

# What does -ve mean?

A word with a **negative meaning**.

Examples:

* Lazy ❌
* Dishonest ❌
* Weak ❌
* Poor ❌
* Careless ❌
* Cruel ❌
* Angry ❌
* Corrupt ❌

These give a **bad impression**.

---

# Case 1: Support Signals → Same Direction (+ve → +ve or -ve → -ve)

Support words include:

* and
* also
* moreover
* furthermore
* similarly
* in addition

These words mean:

> "The second idea is **similar** to the first."

### Example 1

> Rahul is intelligent and ______.

"Intelligent" is **positive (+ve)**.

Since **and** supports the first idea,

the blank should also be **positive (+ve)**.

Options:

* hardworking ✅ (+ve)
* lazy ❌ (-ve)
* careless ❌ (-ve)
* dishonest ❌ (-ve)

So:

> Intelligent (+ve) → And → Hardworking (+ve)

**Pattern:**

```
+ve -------- and -------- +ve
```

---

### Example 2

> The manager is rude and ______.

"Rude" is **negative (-ve)**.

Since "and" keeps the same direction,

the second word should also be negative.

Options:

* polite ❌ (+ve)
* kind ❌ (+ve)
* arrogant ✅ (-ve)
* helpful ❌ (+ve)

Pattern:

```
-ve -------- and -------- -ve
```

---

# Case 2: Opposite Signals → Opposite Direction (+ve → -ve or -ve → +ve)

Opposite words include:

* but
* however
* although
* yet
* despite
* though

These mean:

> "The second idea is **different or surprising**."

---

### Example 1

> Rahul is intelligent but ______.

"Intelligent" is **positive (+ve)**.

"But" tells us to expect an opposite idea.

Options:

* hardworking
* lazy ✅
* brilliant
* talented

Pattern:

```
+ve -------- but -------- -ve
```

---

### Example 2

> Although he is poor, he is ______.

"Poor" is generally **negative (-ve)**.

"Although" tells us to expect the opposite.

Options:

* generous ✅ (+ve)
* greedy
* selfish
* dishonest

Pattern:

```
-ve -------- although -------- +ve
```

---

# Visual Trick

### Support Words

```
Positive ---- and ---- Positive

Happy -------- and -------- Excited

Honest ------- also ------- Kind

Brave -------- moreover ---- Strong
```

or

```
Negative ---- and ---- Negative

Lazy -------- and -------- Careless

Cruel ------- also ------- Selfish
```

---

### Opposite Words

```
Positive ---- but ---- Negative

Rich -------- but -------- Miserly

Smart ------- however ---- Lazy
```

or

```
Negative ---- although ---- Positive

Poor -------- although ---- Honest

Weak -------- yet -------- Determined
```

---

# Don't Memorize +ve/-ve Blindly!

The **meaning of the sentence** is always more important.

Look at this example:

> The food was spicy and ______.

Is "delicious" positive? Yes.

Is "hot" positive? Not exactly.

Both **spicy** and **hot** describe similar qualities, so the second word should **support the first idea**, even if it isn't simply "positive."

So don't think only in terms of "good" and "bad." Think:

* Is the second idea **continuing** the first?
* Or is it **contrasting** with the first?

---

## A Better Way to Think

Instead of:

> **+ve and +ve**

Think:

> **Same idea → Same direction**

Instead of:

> **+ve but -ve**

Think:

> **Opposite idea → Opposite direction**

This works even when the words aren't clearly positive or negative.

---

### Quick Practice

Tell me whether the blank should be the **same direction** or the **opposite direction** (don't solve the blank yet):

1. He is honest and ______.
2. She was tired but ______.
3. Although the movie was long, it was ______.
4. The room was dirty and ______.

Try answering with just **"same"** or **"opposite"** for each (1–4). This is exactly the skill used in sentence completion.
