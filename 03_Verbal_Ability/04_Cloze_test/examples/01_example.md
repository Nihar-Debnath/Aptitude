This is exactly the kind of question TCS likes to ask. The mistake most students make is trying to solve **blank by blank**. **Don't do that.**

Instead, use this **5-step method**.

---

# Step 1: Read the entire paragraph first

> Today most businessmen are very worried. To begin with, they are not used to competition, in the past they sold whatever **(1)** produced at whatever prices they chose. But **(2)** increasing competition, customers began to **(3)** and choose. Imports suddenly became **(4)** available and that too at cheaper **(5)**.

Ask yourself:

> **"What is this paragraph talking about?"**

Answer:

* Earlier, businesses had **no competition**.
* They could sell anything.
* Later, competition increased.
* Customers got choices.
* Imported goods became available at lower prices.

Now solving becomes much easier.

---

# Blank (1)

Sentence:

> they sold whatever **(1)** produced

Options:

* it
* he
* they
* we

Read it aloud.

> they sold whatever **they** produced ✅

Meaning:

Businessmen sold whatever **they themselves** produced.

Other options:

❌ whatever it produced
→ What is "it"?

❌ whatever he produced
→ Singular, but subject is businessmen.

❌ whatever we produced
→ Doesn't fit.

✅ Answer = **they**

---

# Blank (2)

Sentence:

> But **(2)** increasing competition...

Options:

* with
* by
* after
* from

Read naturally.

> But **with increasing competition**

This is a very common English phrase.

Examples:

* With age...
* With time...
* With experience...
* With increasing demand...
* With increasing competition... ✅

Other options:

> by increasing competition

means competition itself increased something.

Wrong meaning.

> after increasing competition

sounds awkward.

> from increasing competition

incorrect.

✅ Answer = **with**

---

# Blank (3)

Sentence

> customers began to **(3)** and choose.

Options

* buy
* take
* pick
* want

Here you need **collocation**.

There is a famous English phrase:

> **pick and choose**

Meaning:

Choose carefully from many options.

Examples:

* Don't pick and choose your responsibilities.
* Customers can pick and choose products.

This is an idiom.

Other options:

buy and choose ❌

take and choose ❌

want and choose ❌

Only

✅ pick and choose

---

# Blank (4)

Sentence

> Imports suddenly became **(4)** available

Options

* hardly
* easily
* frequently
* conveniently

Read each one.

hardly available

means almost unavailable.

But paragraph says imports increased.

Wrong.

frequently available

Sounds strange.

Things aren't "frequently available."

conveniently available

Possible but unnatural.

easily available

Very common phrase.

Examples:

* Water is easily available.
* Books are easily available.

✅ Answer = easily

---

# Blank (5)

Sentence

> at cheaper **(5)**

Options

* costs
* returns
* dividends
* prices

This one is easiest.

Products are sold at

* cheaper prices ✅

Not

* cheaper costs
* cheaper returns
* cheaper dividends

# Final Answers

| Blank | Answer     | Why                                          |
| ----- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | **they**   | businessmen produced the goods               |
| 2     | **with**   | common phrase: "with increasing competition" |
| 3     | **pick**   | idiom: "pick and choose"                     |
| 4     | **easily** | common collocation: "easily available"       |
| 5     | **prices** | goods are sold at cheaper prices             |

Final paragraph:

> Today most businessmen are very worried. To begin with, they are not used to competition. In the past they sold whatever **they** produced at whatever prices they chose. But **with** increasing competition, customers began to **pick** and choose. Imports suddenly became **easily** available and that too at cheaper **prices**.

# The TCS Cloze Test Secret (the order to solve)

Don't solve from Blank 1 → Blank 5 mechanically.

Instead, look for the easiest clues first:

1. **Collocations (words that naturally go together)**

   * pick and choose
   * easily available
   * cheaper prices

2. **Grammar**

   * they produced

3. **Prepositions**

   * with increasing competition

This means you could solve the blanks in this order:

**3 → 4 → 5 → 2 → 1**

because the strongest clues come first.

## A simple checklist for every Cloze Test

Whenever you see a blank, ask these questions in order:

1. **Is this a fixed phrase?**
   Example: *pick and choose*, *take a decision*, *pay attention*.

2. **Does it make grammatical sense?**
   Check pronouns, tense, articles, and subject-verb agreement.

3. **Does it fit the meaning of the paragraph?**
   Read the sentence before and after the blank.

4. **Read the completed sentence aloud.**
   If it sounds natural in English, it's usually the correct choice.

This approach works remarkably well for TCS because many of their Cloze Test questions rely on **common collocations and natural English expressions** rather than obscure vocabulary.

That's a fantastic question. This is one of those subtle English distinctions that TCS loves to test.

The words **cost** and **price** are related, but **they are not the same thing**.

| Cost                                 | Price                                |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Money spent to make or buy something | Money customers pay to buy something |
| Seller's perspective                 | Customer's perspective               |

### Example

Suppose a company manufactures a pen.

* Cost to make the pen = ₹10
* Price at which it sells the pen = ₹15

So,

* **Cost = ₹10** (company spends this)
* **Price = ₹15** (customer pays this)

---

### Now read the sentence carefully

> Imports suddenly became easily available and that too at cheaper ______.

Who is buying the imports?

**Customers.**

Customers don't care about the manufacturer's **cost**. They care about the **price** they have to pay.

So:

> Imports became available at cheaper **prices**. ✅

---

### Why "cheaper costs" sounds wrong

Imagine I say:

> This mobile phone is available at cheaper costs.

Native English speakers would find this unnatural because **the phone is sold at a price**, not at a cost.

Instead, they would say:

* This mobile phone is available at a cheaper **price**. ✅
* These mobile phones are available at cheaper **prices**. ✅

---

### When do we use "cost"?

When talking about the **expense incurred** by the seller, manufacturer, or buyer.

Examples:

* The **cost of production** has increased.
* Labour **costs** are rising.
* Transportation **costs** are very high.
* The company reduced manufacturing **costs**.

Here, **cost** means **money spent**.

---

### When do we use "price"?

When talking about **selling or buying**.

Examples:

* The **price** of petrol has increased.
* The shop offers lower **prices**.
* The product is sold at a reasonable **price**.
* Customers compare **prices** before buying.

---

## A simple trick for exams

Whenever you see:

* **buy**
* **sell**
* **customer**
* **market**
* **shop**
* **available at...**

👉 Think **PRICE**.

Whenever you see:

* **manufacturing**
* **production**
* **expense**
* **investment**
* **company spends**

👉 Think **COST**.

---

### Compare these two sentences

❌ The company sold the laptop at a lower **cost**.

✅ The company sold the laptop at a lower **price**.

---

✅ The **cost** of manufacturing the laptop was reduced.

Both sentences are correct because they refer to different perspectives.

This is why the cloze test expects **prices** and not **costs**. The paragraph is discussing **what customers pay**, not **what manufacturers spend**.

This distinction between **cost (expense)** and **price (selling amount)** is a very common TCS verbal trap. If you learn to identify **whose perspective the sentence is talking about**—the seller's expense or the buyer's payment—you'll get these questions right much more consistently.
