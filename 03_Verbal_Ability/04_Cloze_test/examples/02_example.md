You're thinking in the right direction by trying to fill the paragraph using the meaning rather than randomly choosing words. Let's solve it the way an interviewer or a good English teacher would.

## Step 1: Understand the theme

Read the first two lines:

> As a rule of thumb, a manned mission costs from fifty to a hundred times more than a comparable unmanned mission.

This tells us:

* **Manned missions are expensive.**
* **Unmanned missions are cheaper.**

Next:

> Thus, for scientific exploration alone...

So the author is about to conclude:

> "If the only goal is science, then..."

---

## Blank (1)

> Thus, for scientific exploration alone, _____ missions...

Options:

* Manned
* unmanned
* space
* lunar

Ask yourself:

If unmanned missions are much cheaper, which missions would scientists prefer?

✅ **unmanned**

Sentence becomes:

> Thus, for scientific exploration alone, **unmanned** missions...

---

## Blank (2)

> ...unmanned missions, employing machine intelligence, are _____.

Options:

* liked
* wanted
* used
* preferred

Which sounds natural?

* are liked ❌
* are wanted ❌
* are used (possible, but not the intended conclusion)
* are preferred ✅

Because the previous sentence is comparing manned vs. unmanned missions.

Correct:

> unmanned missions... are **preferred**.

---

## Blank (3)

> However, there may well be _____ other than scientific...

Options:

* Reasons
* causes
* clues
* objects

Which phrase is common?

> reasons other than...

✅ This is a fixed expression.

Examples:

* reasons other than money
* reasons other than science

So

✅ **Reasons**

---

## Blank (4)

> for exploring _____.

Options:

* Moon
* sun
* space
* mission

Read ahead:

> social, economic, political, cultural...

These are reasons for exploring...

Not the Moon.

Not the Sun.

Not a mission.

They are reasons for exploring **space**.

✅ **space**

---

## Blank (5)

Sentence:

> social, economic, political, cultural or _____

Options:

* Casual
* historic
* historical
* emotional

Notice the pattern.

All the words are adjectives describing **types of reasons**:

* social
* economic
* political
* cultural
* ?

Which one fits?

* casual ❌ (doesn't fit)
* historic ❌ ("historic" means famous or important in history)
* historical ✅ (related to history)
* emotional (possible in isolation, but doesn't fit the list as naturally)

The list is describing categories of reasons:

* social
* economic
* political
* cultural
* **historical**

✅ **historical**

# Final Answers

| Blank | Correct Answer | Why                                      |
| ----- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| (1)   | **unmanned**   | Cheaper for scientific exploration       |
| (2)   | **preferred**  | Natural conclusion after comparing costs |
| (3)   | **Reasons**    | Fixed phrase: "reasons other than..."    |
| (4)   | **space**      | The topic is space exploration           |
| (5)   | **historical** | Fits the list of adjective categories    |

## Final Paragraph

> As a rule of thumb, a manned mission costs from fifty to a hundred times more than a comparable unmanned mission. Thus, for scientific exploration alone, **unmanned** missions, employing machine intelligence, are **preferred**. However, there may well be **reasons** other than scientific for exploring **space**: social, economic, political, cultural or **historical**.

## Where your answers went wrong

### ❌ Blank (1): Manned

The author just spent a sentence explaining that **manned missions are much more expensive**. It would be contradictory to conclude that manned missions are better for scientific exploration alone.

---

### ❌ Blank (2): Liked

The sentence is making a **recommendation**, not describing people's feelings.

* "are liked" → people like them.
* "are preferred" → they are the better choice.

Only **preferred** matches the context.

### A useful TCS/Cognizant trick

When you see a passage with words like **Thus**, **Therefore**, or **However**, pay extra attention:

* **Thus / Therefore** → expect a logical conclusion from the previous sentence.
* **However / But** → expect a contrast or exception.

In this question:

* "Thus" helped us choose **unmanned** and **preferred**.
* "However" signaled a shift: although unmanned missions are scientifically preferred, there are **other reasons** to send humans into **space**. This kind of logical flow is often more important than vocabulary alone in cloze tests.
