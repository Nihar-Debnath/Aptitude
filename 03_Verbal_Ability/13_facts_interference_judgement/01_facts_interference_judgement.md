Yes! **Facts, Inference, and Judgment** is a common type of **Verbal Ability / Reading Comprehension question**. It looks confusing at first, but there’s a very simple way to separate them.

## 1. FACT

A **fact** is something that is **directly stated or clearly given in the passage**.

👉 You don't need to assume anything.

**Example:**

> "Rohit has been working at TCS for three years. He works in the software development department."

Question: **Which company does Rohit work for?**

Answer: **TCS** → **FACT** ✅

Because the passage directly says it.

### 🔑 Trick:

**FACT = "The passage says it."**

---

## 2. INFERENCE

An **inference** is something that is **not directly stated**, but you can **logically conclude it from the information given**.

**Example:**

> "Rohit has been working at TCS for three years. He works in the software development department."

Question:

**Is Rohit an experienced employee?**

Answer: **Yes** → **INFERENCE** ✅

The passage doesn't literally say:

> "Rohit is experienced."

But because he has worked there for **three years**, we can reasonably conclude that he has experience.

### 🔑 Trick:

**INFERENCE = "The passage doesn't say it directly, but I can logically derive it."**

---

# 3. JUDGMENT

A **judgment** is an **opinion, evaluation, or personal conclusion** about something.

It usually contains words like:

* good / bad
* better / worse
* beautiful / ugly
* useful / useless
* successful / unsuccessful
* intelligent / foolish
* should / should not
* fair / unfair

**Example:**

> "Rohit has been working at TCS for three years."

Question:

**Rohit is a highly successful software engineer.**

Answer: **JUDGMENT** ❌

Why?

The passage only tells us that he has worked for three years. It doesn't give enough information to judge whether he is **highly successful**.

---

# 🔥 The easiest way to remember

Think of it as **3 levels**:

### 🟢 FACT

**SEE IT**

> Passage: "Riya scored 90 marks."

Fact:
→ Riya scored 90 marks.

Directly stated.

---

### 🟡 INFERENCE

**FIGURE IT OUT**

> Passage: "Riya scored 90 marks."

Inference:
→ Riya probably performed well in the examination.

It's not directly stated, but it's a reasonable conclusion.

---

### 🔴 JUDGMENT

**WHAT DO YOU THINK ABOUT IT?**

> Passage: "Riya scored 90 marks."

Judgment:
→ Riya is the **best student in her class.**

That's an evaluation, and we don't have enough information to establish it.

---

# 🧠 TCS Exam Trick

When you see an option, ask yourself:

**1. Is it explicitly written?**
→ **FACT**

**2. Is it not written, but can I logically conclude it?**
→ **INFERENCE**

**3. Does it express an opinion/evaluation or require personal interpretation?**
→ **JUDGMENT**

### One-line formula:

> **FACT = Given**
> **INFERENCE = Derived**
> **JUDGMENT = Evaluated**

That's basically the entire concept. Once you train yourself to ask **"Where did this information come from?"**, these questions become much easier.
