Exactly! 🔥 **That is a very good way to solve One Word Substitution** — instead of blindly memorizing, you can **break the unknown word into parts (roots/prefixes/suffixes)** and guess its meaning.

Your example is:

### **Pantheism**

Break it:

* **Pan** = all / everything
* **Theos / Theo** = God
* **-ism** = belief / doctrine / system

So:

**Pan + theo + ism**
= **belief in God/everything as God**

👉 **Pantheism = the belief that God is present in everything / the universe itself is divine.**

### 🧠 How to use it in an exam

Suppose you see an unknown word:

**Polyglot**

You may not remember it.

Break it:

**Poly** = many
**Glot** = language/tongue

→ **many languages**

So **Polyglot = a person who speaks many languages.** ✅

Another:

**Omnipotent**

**Omni** = all
**Potent** = powerful

→ **all-powerful**

So:

**Omnipotent = having unlimited power.** ✅

Another:

**Aquaphobia**

**Aqua** = water
**Phobia** = fear

→ **fear of water**

So:

**Aquaphobia = fear of water.** ✅

### ⚠️ One important correction

Your method is **excellent**, but don't assume every word can be perfectly decoded this way. Some words have **Greek/Latin roots**, and the meaning of a root can change slightly depending on the word.

So for your verbal ability preparation, the best strategy is:

**Unknown word → break into roots → identify root meanings → combine them → eliminate impossible options → choose the closest meaning.**

That's MUCH better than trying to memorize every word separately.
