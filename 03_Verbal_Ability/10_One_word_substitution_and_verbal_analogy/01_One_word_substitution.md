### One Word Substitution in Verbal Ability

**One Word Substitution** means **replacing a long phrase or group of words with a single word that has the same meaning.**

In exams like **TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Infosys**, you may be given a phrase and asked to find the **one word** that represents it.

### Simple example

**“A person who cannot read or write”**
→ **Illiterate**

Instead of saying:

> He is a person who cannot read or write.

We can simply say:

> He is **illiterate**.

### 🔥 The important thing for exams

Don't think of it as simply **“learning difficult words.”**

You need to learn the connection:

> **Phrase → Meaning → Exact word**

For example:

**“One who looks at the bright side of things”**
→ Meaning: a positive person
→ **Optimist**

**“One who looks at the dark/negative side of things”**
→ Meaning: a negative person
→ **Pessimist**

So, **One Word Substitution = vocabulary + understanding of meaning.**

For your verbal-ability preparation, this is usually a **memory-based vocabulary topic**, so learning the most common 100–200 substitutions is much more useful than trying to memorize thousands of random words.
