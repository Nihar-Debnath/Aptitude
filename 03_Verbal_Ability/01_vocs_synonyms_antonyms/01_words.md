**Rapacious** *(adjective)* means **extremely greedy**, especially for **money, power, or resources**. It describes a person, company, or even an animal that wants to take as much as possible.

### Simple Meaning

* **Rapacious = Very greedy + always wanting more**

### Pronunciation

**ruh-PAY-shus** /rəˈpeɪʃəs/

### Examples

#### 1. Person

> The **rapacious** businessman exploited his workers to make more profit.

**Meaning:** The businessman was extremely greedy for money.

#### 2. Company

> Many people criticized the company's **rapacious** pricing strategy.

**Meaning:** The company charged excessively because it wanted more profit.

#### 3. Animal

> Sharks are often described as **rapacious** predators.

**Meaning:** They hunt aggressively and consume prey eagerly.

#### 4. Power

> The dictator had a **rapacious** desire for power.

**Meaning:** He wanted more and more power without limits.

---

## Common Collocations

* **rapacious appetite** (an extreme desire)
* **rapacious greed**
* **rapacious predator**
* **rapacious corporation**
* **rapacious investor**

---

## Synonyms

* Greedy
* Avaricious
* Covetous
* Grasping
* Voracious *(for eating or consuming; sometimes used figuratively)*

---

## Antonyms

* Generous
* Selfless
* Charitable
* Unselfish

---

## Easy Trick to Remember

Think of **rapacious = "grab everything"**.

Imagine someone saying:

> **"Mine! Mine! Mine!"**

That person is **rapacious**.

---

## Mini Quiz

Which sentence uses **rapacious** correctly?

A. She is rapacious because she always donates to charity.
B. The rapacious landlord kept raising the rent to earn more money.
C. The weather was rapacious today.

✅ **Answer:** **B** — because it describes extreme greed.

---

### One sentence you can use in an interview:

> **"A rapacious person is someone who is extremely greedy for wealth, power, or resources and is never satisfied with what they have."**

**Avaricious** *(adjective)* means **extremely greedy for money or wealth**.

Unlike **rapacious**, which can refer to greed for **money, power, land, or resources**, **avaricious** is used mainly for **money**.

### Pronunciation

**av-uh-RISH-us** /ˌæv.əˈrɪʃ.əs/

### Simple Meaning

> **Avaricious = Extremely greedy for money.**

### Examples

1. **The avaricious banker cared more about profits than people.**

   * He was extremely greedy for money.

2. **His avaricious nature ruined many friendships.**

   * His love of money damaged his relationships.

3. **The avaricious landlord kept increasing the rent every year.**

   * The landlord wanted more and more money.

---

## Difference: Rapacious vs. Avaricious

| Word           | Main Meaning     | Focus                                          |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Avaricious** | Extremely greedy | **Money and wealth**                           |
| **Rapacious**  | Extremely greedy | **Money, power, land, resources, or anything** |

### Examples

* **The avaricious millionaire** refused to donate even a small amount.
* **The rapacious dictator** wanted more land and power.
* **The rapacious corporation** exploited natural resources for profit.

---

## Synonyms

* Greedy
* Miserly *(focused on not spending money)*
* Covetous
* Money-hungry

---

## Memory Trick

Notice that **avaricious** contains **"avarice."**

* **Avarice** *(noun)* = greed for money.
* **Avaricious** *(adjective)* = having greed for money.

So you can remember:

> **Avarice → Money greed → Avaricious person.**

### Quick Quiz

Which sentence is correct?

A. She is avaricious because she spends all her money on charity.
B. The avaricious businessman cheated customers to become richer.
C. The sky looked avaricious today.

✅ **Answer:** **B**. It correctly describes someone who is extremely greedy for wealth.

**Covetous** *(adjective)* means **having a strong desire to possess something that belongs to someone else**, especially money, property, or possessions.

It emphasizes **wanting what another person has**, not just being generally greedy.

### Pronunciation

**KUV-uh-tus** /ˈkʌv.ɪ.təs/

### Simple Meaning

> **Covetous = Wanting someone else's possessions.**

### Examples

1. **He gave a covetous glance at his friend's new car.**

   * He wished the car were his.

2. **The prince was covetous of the neighboring kingdom's wealth.**

   * He wanted to own that wealth.

3. **Don't be covetous of other people's success.**

   * Don't envy and desire what others have.

---

## Difference: Covetous vs. Avaricious vs. Rapacious

| Word           | Meaning                         | Focus                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Covetous**   | Desiring what belongs to others | Another person's possessions  |
| **Avaricious** | Extremely greedy for money      | Wealth and money              |
| **Rapacious**  | Aggressively greedy             | Money, power, land, resources |

### Examples

* **Covetous:** *He coveted his neighbor's house.*
* **Avaricious:** *He kept accumulating money and never spent it.*
* **Rapacious:** *The empire was rapacious, conquering more and more land.*

---

## Synonyms

* Envious
* Jealous *(not always the same, but sometimes related)*
* Desirous
* Grasping
* Greedy

---

## Memory Trick

Think of the verb **covet**.

* **Covet** = to strongly desire something that belongs to someone else.
* **Covetous** = having that desire.

Example:

> Your friend buys a new gaming laptop, and you keep thinking, *"I wish that were mine."*
> That's a **covetous** feeling.

---

### Quick Comparison

* **Greedy** → Wants more in general.
* **Avaricious** → Wants more **money**.
* **Covetous** → Wants **someone else's** possessions.
* **Rapacious** → Aggressively grabs **money, power, land, or resources**.

### Quiz

Which sentence uses **covetous** correctly?

A. She was covetous of her colleague's promotion. ✅
B. The weather was covetous today. ❌
C. The cat was covetous because it slept all day. ❌

**Answer:** **A** — it expresses a desire for something someone else has.

## **Proclivity** *(noun)*

**Meaning:** **A natural tendency or strong inclination to do something (usually habitual).**

**Pronunciation:** *pro-KLIV-i-tee* /prəˈklɪv.ə.ti/

### Simple Meaning

> **A natural habit or tendency.**

### Examples

* **He has a proclivity for solving difficult problems.**
* **She has a proclivity to arrive late.**

### Synonyms

* Tendency
* Inclination
* Bent
* Predisposition

### Antonyms

* Aversion
* Disinclination
* Reluctance

### Memory Trick

**Proclivity = "Pro" + "leaning"** → Your mind naturally **leans toward** something.

> ⚠️ It can be **positive or negative**.
>
> * Positive: *a proclivity for learning.*
> * Negative: *a proclivity for lying.*


These are **synonyms** and **antonyms** of **proclivity**.

## Synonyms

### 1. **Tendency**

A general habit or pattern of behavior.

> **He has a tendency to overthink.**

### 2. **Inclination**

A natural preference or desire to do something.

> **She has an inclination toward music.**

### 3. **Bent**

A natural talent or strong preference. *(A bit formal and less common.)*

> **He has a bent for mathematics.**

### 4. **Predisposition**

A natural or inborn tendency, often present before experience.

> **She has a predisposition to anxiety.**
> **He has a predisposition for leadership.**

---

## Antonyms

### 1. **Aversion**

A strong dislike of something.

> **He has an aversion to public speaking.**

### 2. **Disinclination**

A lack of willingness or desire.

> **She showed a disinclination to participate.**

### 3. **Reluctance**

Hesitation or unwillingness.

> **He accepted the offer with reluctance.**

---

## More Examples of **Proclivity**

* **She has a proclivity for reading before bed.**
* **He has a proclivity to help strangers.**
* **Children often have a proclivity for asking questions.**
* **The company has a proclivity for taking risks.**
* **He has a proclivity for spending money impulsively.** *(negative)*
* **She has a proclivity for learning new languages.** *(positive)*
* **The dog has a proclivity to chase squirrels.**
* **Some people have a proclivity toward perfectionism.**

### Memory Trick

**Proclivity = Your brain naturally "leans toward" doing something repeatedly.**

For example:

* ❤️ Love coding → **Proclivity for coding**
* 📚 Always reading → **Proclivity for reading**
* 😴 Sleeps late every day → **Proclivity for staying up late**
* 💸 Shops without thinking → **Proclivity for impulsive spending**

## **Vacillate** *(verb)*

**Meaning:** **To keep changing your mind between two or more choices; to be unable to decide.**

**Pronunciation:** *VAS-uh-layt* /ˈvæs.ɪ.leɪt/

### Simple Meaning

> **To hesitate or waver because you can't make a decision.**

### How it's Used

**Pattern:**

* **Vacillate between A and B**
* **Vacillate over/about something**

### Examples ✅

* **I vacillated between Computer Science and BCA before choosing BCA.**
* **She vacillated over whether to accept the job offer.**
* **The government vacillated on the new policy.**
* **Don't vacillate—make a decision.**

### How it's NOT Used ❌

❌ **He vacillated the book.** *(Wrong: vacillate doesn't mean "move" or "change" an object.)*

❌ **The weather vacillated my mood.** *(Wrong: it's usually the **person or decision** that vacillates.)*

✅ **My mood vacillated throughout the day.**

### Synonyms

* Waver
* Hesitate
* Dither
* Be indecisive
* Fluctuate *(only in some contexts)*

### Antonyms

* Decide
* Resolve
* Commit
* Be firm
* Be decisive

### Memory Trick

Think:

> **Vacillate = "Vacation between two choices."** 🛣️

You keep going **back and forth** instead of choosing one path.

### Difference

* **Vacillate** → Changing your **decision or opinion**.
* **Hesitate** → Pausing before acting or speaking.
* **Fluctuate** → Numbers, prices, temperatures, etc., go up and down.

**Examples:**

* **She vacillated between two careers.** ✅
* **He hesitated before answering.** ✅
* **Stock prices fluctuated all week.** ✅

* Minor
