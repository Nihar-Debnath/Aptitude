**Harness** can be used as both a **noun** and a **verb**.

1. **As a noun:** A set of straps and fittings used to control or support something.

   * Example: *The horse wore a harness to pull the cart.*
   * Example: *The worker wore a safety harness while climbing.*

2. **As a verb:** To **control, use, or make effective use of** something, especially a resource or power.

   * Example: *We need to harness solar energy to reduce electricity costs.*
   * Example: *She learned to harness her emotions during stressful situations.*

**Simple meaning:**
**Harness = to use something effectively for a purpose.**

A **Compulsory Education Programme** is a government policy or initiative that **requires all children within a certain age group to attend school**. Parents or guardians are legally responsible for ensuring their children receive this education.

### Simple meaning

**Compulsory = Mandatory (you must do it)**
**Education Programme = A plan or system for providing education**

So, **Compulsory Education Programme = A mandatory education system that children are legally required to participate in.**

### Example

Suppose a country has a compulsory education programme for children aged **6–14 years**. This means:

* Every child between 6 and 14 must attend school.
* The government provides schools and teachers.
* Parents are expected by law to send their children to school.

### In India

India's compulsory education programme is backed by the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009**. It guarantees:

* **Free education** for children aged **6–14 years** in eligible schools.
* **Compulsory education**, meaning the government must ensure children in this age group have access to schooling, and parents are expected to enroll them.

### One-line definition (good for exams)

> **A compulsory education programme is a government initiative that makes school education mandatory for children of a specified age group.**

An **Adult Education Programme** is a program designed to **teach adults who missed formal schooling or want to continue learning later in life**.

### Simple meaning

**Adult Education Programme = Educational courses for adults to improve their knowledge and skills.**

These programmes may include:

* **Basic literacy** (reading and writing)
* **Numeracy** (basic mathematics)
* **Digital literacy** (using computers and smartphones)
* **Vocational skills** (tailoring, carpentry, computer skills, etc.)
* **Financial literacy**
* **Health and life skills**

### Example

A 40-year-old person who never attended school joins evening classes to learn to read, write, and do basic arithmetic. This is an example of an **adult education programme**.

### In India

Adult education initiatives have included programs such as:

* **National Literacy Mission (NLM)** (launched in 1988)
* **New India Literacy Programme (ULLAS)**, which focuses on adult literacy and lifelong learning.

### One-line definition (good for exams)

> **An adult education programme is an educational initiative that provides learning opportunities to adults to improve literacy, knowledge, and practical skills.**

**Prevailing** means **existing, most common, or dominant at a particular time or place**.

### Simple meaning

* **Prevailing = Existing now / Most common / Widely accepted / Dominant**

### Examples

1. **The prevailing opinion** is that online learning is effective.
   → The **most common opinion** is that online learning is effective.

2. **The prevailing weather conditions** made travel difficult.
   → The **current weather conditions** made travel difficult.

3. We must adapt to the **prevailing market trends**.
   → We must adapt to the **current or dominant market trends**.

### Synonyms

* Current
* Existing
* Dominant
* Common
* Widespread
* Predominant

### Antonyms

* Uncommon
* Rare
* Unusual
* Obsolete

### Easy trick to remember

Think of **prevailing = "winning over others."**
The idea, condition, or trend that **wins** and becomes the **most common or dominant** is the **prevailing** one.

### One-line definition (exam-friendly)

> **Prevailing means existing or being the most common, accepted, or dominant at a particular time or place.**

**Analogical** is an adjective that means **based on or using an analogy**—that is, explaining or understanding one thing by comparing it to another similar thing.

### Simple meaning

**Analogical = Based on comparison or similarity.**

### Examples

1. **The teacher gave an analogical explanation.**
   → The teacher explained the topic by **comparing it to something familiar**.

2. **Analogical reasoning** helps solve new problems by comparing them with similar problems solved before.

### Example of an analogy

> **Bird : Nest :: Bee : Hive**

Just as a **bird lives in a nest**, a **bee lives in a hive**.

This type of comparison is called **analogical reasoning**.

### In aptitude and reasoning

**Analogical reasoning** means finding the relationship between one pair of words, numbers, or figures and applying the same relationship to another pair.

Example:

> **Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?**

Answer: **School**

Reason:

* A **doctor** works in a **hospital**.
* A **teacher** works in a **school**.

### Synonyms

* Comparative
* Similarity-based
* Relational

### One-line definition (exam-friendly)

> **Analogical means based on a comparison between two things that are similar in some way.**

**Physiological** is an adjective that relates to **the normal functions and processes of a living body**.

### Simple meaning

**Physiological = Related to how the body works.**

### Examples

1. **Breathing is a physiological process.**
   → Breathing is a **normal body function**.

2. **Hunger is a physiological need.**
   → Hunger is a **basic need of the body**.

3. Exercise causes several **physiological changes**, such as an increased heart rate and faster breathing.

### Physiological vs. Psychological

| Physiological                   | Psychological              |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Related to the **body**         | Related to the **mind**    |
| Heartbeat, digestion, breathing | Thoughts, emotions, memory |
| Physical processes              | Mental processes           |

**Examples:**

* **Physiological:** Sweating during exercise.
* **Psychological:** Feeling nervous before an exam.

### Synonyms

* Bodily
* Physical (in the context of body functions)
* Biological

### One-line definition (exam-friendly)

> **Physiological means relating to the normal functions and processes of a living organism, especially the human body.**

**Deductive** means **using general rules or facts to reach a specific conclusion**.

### Simple meaning

**Deductive = General → Specific**

You start with a **general statement (rule)** and apply it to a **specific case**.

### Example 1

* **All humans are mortal.** (General rule)
* **Rahul is a human.** (Specific fact)
* **Therefore, Rahul is mortal.** (Conclusion)

This is **deductive reasoning** because the conclusion follows logically from the premises.

### Example 2

* All birds have wings.
* A sparrow is a bird.
* Therefore, a sparrow has wings.

### Deductive vs. Inductive

| Deductive                                              | Inductive                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| General → Specific                                     | Specific → General                     |
| Starts with a rule                                     | Starts with observations               |
| Conclusion is logically certain (if premises are true) | Conclusion is probable, not guaranteed |

**Deductive:**

* All squares have four sides.
* This shape is a square.
* Therefore, it has four sides.

**Inductive:**

* I have seen 100 crows, and all were black.
* Therefore, **all crows are black.** (This is a likely conclusion, but not guaranteed.)

### In aptitude and logical reasoning

Deductive reasoning is used in:

* Statements and Conclusions
* Syllogisms
* Statements and Assumptions
* Statements and Arguments
* Cause and Effect

### Easy trick to remember

* **Deductive = Downward** (General → Specific)
* **Inductive = Upward** (Specific → General)

### One-line definition (exam-friendly)

> **Deductive means reasoning from a general rule or principle to reach a specific, logically valid conclusion.**

A **sound argument** is an argument that is **both logically valid and based on true premises (facts or accepted truths).**

### Formula

> **Sound Argument = Valid Logic + True Premises**

If either the logic is invalid or the premises are false, the argument is **not sound**.

### Example of a sound argument

* **Premise 1:** All mammals are warm-blooded.
* **Premise 2:** A whale is a mammal.
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, a whale is warm-blooded.

✅ The reasoning is valid, and the premises are true. So this is a **sound argument**.

---

### Example of an unsound argument (false premise)

* **Premise 1:** All birds can fly.
* **Premise 2:** A penguin is a bird.
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, a penguin can fly.

❌ The logic is valid, but the first premise is false because not all birds can fly. So the argument is **unsound**.

---

### Example of an unsound argument (invalid logic)

* **Premise 1:** All doctors are educated.
* **Premise 2:** Priya is educated.
* **Conclusion:** Therefore, Priya is a doctor.

❌ The premises may be true, but the conclusion does not logically follow. Many educated people are not doctors.

---

## In aptitude (Statement & Argument)

The phrase **"sound argument"** has a slightly different practical meaning.

A sound argument is one that:

* ✅ Is **logical**
* ✅ Is **relevant to the statement**
* ✅ Is **practical and realistic**
* ✅ Is based on **facts or common sense**
* ❌ Is not emotional or based on personal beliefs
* ❌ Does not make unrealistic assumptions

### Example

**Statement:** Should plastic bags be banned?

**Argument I:** Yes, because plastic bags pollute the environment.

* ✅ Strong and sound.

**Argument II:** No, because plastic bags come in many colors.

* ❌ Weak and unsound. The color of the bags has nothing to do with whether they should be banned.

---

### Easy way to remember

Whenever you see an argument in reasoning, ask yourself:

1. **Is it relevant to the statement?**
2. **Is it logical?**
3. **Is it based on facts or common sense rather than emotions?**
4. **Is it practical?**

If the answer to all four is **yes**, it's usually a **sound (strong) argument**.

### One-line definition (exam-friendly)

> **A sound argument is a logically valid argument that is supported by true, relevant, and practical reasons.**
