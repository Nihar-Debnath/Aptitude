Yes — **this is the tricky part of the question.** You're thinking:

> "He is 18 years old, but condition says 19 or above. So he fails."

But look carefully at the wording:

> **Anirudh Thakur was 18 years of age on 15th February 2009.**

It does **NOT** say he is 18 years old on June 30, 2010.

The rule says:

> **Be of 19 years of age or above as on June 30, 2010.**

So we have to calculate his age **on June 30, 2010**.

### Let's calculate it

He was **18 on 15 February 2009**.

From:

**15 Feb 2009 → 15 Feb 2010** = **1 year**

So he became:

**19 years old on 15 February 2010.**

Then:

**15 Feb 2010 → 30 June 2010**

That's another ~4 months.

Therefore, on **30 June 2010**, he is:

> **19 years and 4+ months old** ✅

So he **satisfies condition 2**.

He satisfies **all 4 conditions**.

### Therefore:

**YES, he should be admitted. ✅**

The key trick to remember:

> **Whenever a question says "age as on [specific date]", calculate the person's age on THAT date — don't simply use the age mentioned in the case.**

Here, **18 years was his age on February 15, 2009**, not on June 30, 2010.

**Normal case:**

> Conditions 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 → **ADMIT**

> Conditions 1 + 2 + 4 ✅

The **85% condition doesn't suddenly become a requirement for everyone.** That's the trick.
