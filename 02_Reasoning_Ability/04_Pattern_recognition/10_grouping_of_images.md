Excellent! The next topic is **Grouping of Images**, also known as **Image Grouping**, **Figure Grouping**, or **Grouping of Figures**.

This is a common **non-verbal reasoning** topic in placement exams like **TCS, Cognizant, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini, Accenture**, and many government exams.

---

# What is Grouping of Images?

You are given several figures.

Your task is to **group together the figures that have a common property**.

In simple words,

> **Find the figures that belong to the same family because they follow the same rule.**

Example

```text
○   △   □   ●
```

If the rule is:

"Closed figures"

Then

```text
○
△
□
```

belong together.

---

# Types of Grouping Questions

There are mainly two types.

## Type 1

Choose **which figures belong together.**

Example

```text
1. ○

2. □

3. △

4. →
```

Which three belong together?

Answer

```text
○
□
△
```

because all are geometric shapes.

---

## Type 2

Find the odd group.

Example

```text
A = ○ □ △

B = ☆ ◇ □

C = → ← ↑

D = ○ △ □
```

Three groups follow one rule.

One group is different.

---

# How to Solve

The biggest mistake beginners make is looking at the **whole figure**.

Instead, compare one property at a time.

Ask yourself

* Same shape?
* Same direction?
* Same number of sides?
* Same shading?
* Same rotation?
* Same number of objects?

# Common Tricks Used in Exams

## Trick 1

Rotation

Students think

```text
△
```

and

```text
▽
```

are different.

Actually,

they are the same shape, only rotated.

---

## Trick 2

Mirror Image

Rotation and mirror image are **not the same**.

Example

```text
L
```

and

```text
⅃
```

are mirror images.

Not rotations.

---

## Trick 3

Count Lines

Example

```text
★

☆
```

One may have 5 lines.

The other may have 10.

Always count.

---

## Trick 4

Shading

Half-shaded and fully shaded are different.

---

## Trick 5

Hidden Difference

Sometimes only one tiny line changes.

Always inspect carefully.

# Quick Solving Checklist

Whenever you see a Grouping of Images question, check these properties in order:

1. ✅ Shape (circle, square, triangle, etc.)
2. ✅ Open or closed figure
3. ✅ Number of sides, corners, or lines
4. ✅ Curved vs straight lines
5. ✅ Rotation
6. ✅ Mirror image
7. ✅ Shading (none, half, full)
8. ✅ Size (small or large)
9. ✅ Position of dots or symbols
10. ✅ Symmetry (vertical, horizontal, rotational)

# Grouping of Images vs Classification

Many students think these are the same, but there is a subtle difference.

| Classification                             | Grouping of Images                                       |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Usually find one odd figure                | Usually identify figures that belong together            |
| Can use numbers, words, letters, or images | Uses only figures/images                                 |
| Tests logical categories                   | Tests visual similarities and transformations            |
| Example: Dog, Cat, Cow, Car                | Example: Four shapes with different rotations or shading |

### Final Strategy

Don't try to recognize the entire figure instantly. Instead, **break every figure into its visual features**:

* Shape
* Number of lines
* Number of corners
* Direction
* Shading
* Symmetry
* Rotation

Then compare these features one by one. This systematic approach is much faster and more reliable than relying on intuition, especially in placement exams.
