Excellent! The next topic is **Figure Matrix**, also known as **Matrix Reasoning** or **Figure Series Matrix**.

This is one of the most important **non-verbal reasoning** topics in placement exams like **TCS, Cognizant, Infosys, Capgemini, Accenture, Wipro**, and many government exams.

---

# What is a Figure Matrix?

A **Figure Matrix** consists of figures arranged in a grid, usually:

```text
□   □   □
□   □   □
□   □   ?
```

One figure is missing.

Your task is:

> **Find the pattern among the rows and columns and choose the correct missing figure.**

Unlike an Embedded Image, here you're **not searching for a hidden figure**. Instead, you're discovering the **rule** that transforms one figure into another.

---

# Basic Idea

Think of it like a number series.

Example:

```text
2   4   6
8  10  12
14 16  ?
```

You would identify the numerical pattern and find **18**.

In a Figure Matrix, instead of numbers, you have **shapes**.

---

# Types of Figure Matrix Patterns

Most questions are based on one or more of these patterns.

---

# Type 1: Rotation

One figure rotates by a fixed angle.

Example

```text
→    ↓    ←
↑    →    ↓
?    ↑    →
```

Observe Row 1:

```text
→
↓
←
```

Each figure rotates **90° clockwise**.

Row 2 follows the same rule.

So Row 3 becomes

```text
←
↑
→
```

Answer

```text
←
```

# Matrix Pattern by Columns

Sometimes the rule is vertical.

Example

```text

○

△
```

Each column follows the same transformation.

# Mixed Pattern

Sometimes both directions matter.

Example

```text
→     ↓     ←

+     ×     *

□     ○     ?
```

The third row depends on the first two rows.

These are the hardest questions.

# Common Tricks Used in Exams

## Trick 1

Rotation + Addition

The figure rotates **and** gains one dot.

Students usually notice only one of the changes.

---



---

## Trick 3

Row Rule Different from Column Rule

Example

```text
Row → Rotation

Column → Shading
```

You must satisfy **both**.

## Trick 4

Counting Objects

Example


# Step-by-Step Method

Whenever you solve a Figure Matrix question, check the following in order.

---

## Step 1

Look for **rotation**.

* 90°
* 180°
* 270°

---

## Step 2

Check whether anything is **added**.

* New line
* Dot
* Shape
* Arrow

---

## Step 3

Check whether anything is **removed**.

---

## Step 4

Look for **movement**.

Objects may move:

* Left
* Right
* Up
* Down
* Clockwise

---

## Step 5

Check **size**.

Large → Small

Small → Large

---

## Step 6

Check **shading**.

White

↓

Half

↓

Black

---

## Step 7

Count.

Ask yourself:

* Number of sides?
* Number of dots?
* Number of lines?
* Number of arrows?

---

## Step 8

Finally, check **both rows and columns**.

Many students only check rows and miss the vertical rule.

# Common Mistakes

### ❌ Looking only at rows.

Always check columns too.

---

### ❌ Ignoring two simultaneous patterns.

The figure may rotate **and** gain a dot.

---

### ❌ Forgetting movement.

Sometimes nothing changes except the position of a small symbol.

---

### ❌ Ignoring the number of elements.

Always count:

* Dots
* Lines
* Shapes
* Corners
* Arrows

# Quick Solving Checklist

Whenever you see a Figure Matrix, check in this order:

1. ✅ Rotation (90°, 180°, 270°)
2. ✅ Reflection (mirror image)
3. ✅ Addition of lines, dots, or shapes
4. ✅ Deletion of elements
5. ✅ Movement (left, right, up, down, clockwise)
6. ✅ Shading changes
7. ✅ Size changes
8. ✅ Count objects (dots, lines, sides, corners)
9. ✅ Shape transformation (triangle → square → pentagon)
10. ✅ Verify the pattern across **both rows and columns**

## Figure Matrix vs Figure Series

Many beginners confuse these two topics.

| Figure Series                          | Figure Matrix                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Figures are arranged in a single line  | Figures are arranged in a grid (usually 2×2 or 3×3) |
| Find the next figure in the sequence   | Find the missing figure in the grid                 |
| Pattern is usually horizontal          | Pattern may be horizontal, vertical, or both        |
| One transformation at a time is common | Multiple transformations can occur simultaneously   |

Once you get comfortable checking rotation, movement, addition/deletion, and row/column relationships in a fixed order, Figure Matrix questions become much faster to solve.
