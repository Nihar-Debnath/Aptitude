# Image Analysis (Figure Analysis)

It is also known as:

* **Figure Analysis**
* **Visual Analysis**
* **Figure Interpretation**
* **Image Reasoning**

This topic tests your ability to **observe, analyze, and compare visual information** rather than perform calculations.

---

# What is Image Analysis?

In Image Analysis, you are given one or more figures, and you must analyze them to answer a question.

Unlike other topics:

* ❌ There is no formula.
* ❌ There is no fixed pattern.
* ✅ It tests your observation and logical thinking.

Example:

```text
       □
      / \
     ○  △
```

Question:

> Which figure has the maximum number of triangles?

You simply analyze the figure carefully.

# Common Tricks Used in Exams

## Trick 1

Students count only the smallest figures.

Wrong.

Always count

* Small
* Medium
* Large

---

## Trick 2

Ignoring overlapping regions.

Example

```text
○
□
```

Overlap creates additional regions.

---

## Trick 3

Missing hidden figures.

Some triangles are formed only after combining smaller ones.

---

## Trick 4

Square vs Rectangle

Remember

Every square is also a rectangle.

This is a favorite TCS trick.

---

## Trick 5

Rotated Shapes

A square rotated

45°

is still a square.

Many students call it a diamond.

# Step-by-Step Method

Whenever you solve an Image Analysis question:

---

### Step 1

Identify the question.

What do they ask?

* Count?
* Compare?
* Rotate?
* Hidden figure?

---

### Step 2

Break the image into smaller parts.

Never observe the whole figure at once.

---

### Step 3

Count systematically.

Example

Triangles

```text

↓

↓

```

### Step 4

Check

* Rotation
* Symmetry
* Overlap
* Regions

### Step 5

Verify once.

Many mistakes happen due to double counting.

# Quick Solving Checklist

Whenever you see an Image Analysis question:

1. ✅ Read the question first.
2. ✅ Identify what must be analyzed (count, compare, rotate, etc.).
3. ✅ Break the figure into smaller parts.
4. ✅ Count from smallest to largest.
5. ✅ Check overlaps and enclosed regions.
6. ✅ Consider symmetry and rotation.
7. ✅ Recheck your answer to avoid double-counting.

# Difference Between Similar Topics

| Topic                  | Main Objective                                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Image Analysis**     | Analyze a complete figure to answer questions about it.   |
| **Embedded Images**    | Find a hidden figure inside another figure.               |
| **Pattern Completion** | Fill in the missing part of a figure.                     |
| **Shape Construction** | Combine separate pieces to make a complete figure.        |
| **Figure Matrix**      | Find the missing figure using row/column patterns.        |
| **Dot Situation**      | Match the relative position of dots in different figures. |

# Master Strategy for Every Image Analysis Question

Whenever you see a figure, ask these questions in order:

1. **What shapes are present?**

   * Triangles?
   * Squares?
   * Circles?
   * Rectangles?

2. **What needs to be analyzed?**

   * Count?
   * Compare?
   * Hidden figure?
   * Symmetry?
   * Rotation?

3. **Break the figure into smaller sections** instead of looking at the whole image.

4. **Count systematically**:

   * Small shapes
   * Medium shapes
   * Large combined shapes

5. **Check for common traps**:

   * Overlapping regions
   * Rotated shapes
   * Squares that are also rectangles
   * Double-counting
