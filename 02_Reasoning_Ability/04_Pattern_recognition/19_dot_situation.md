Excellent! The next topic is **Dot Situation**, also known as **Dot Situation Test**, **Dot Placement**, or **Dot Pattern Reasoning**.

This topic appears in **TCS, Cognizant, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini, Accenture**, SSC, Banking, and many other aptitude exams.

It is one of the easiest non-verbal reasoning topics once you know what to observe.

---

# What is Dot Situation?

In Dot Situation questions, you are given:

* A figure with one or more **dots (•)** placed inside, outside, or on the boundary.
* Four answer figures.

Your task is:

> **Choose the figure in which the dot occupies the same relative position as in the question figure.**

Notice that **the shape may be rotated or mirrored**, but the **relative position of the dot** must remain the same.

---

# Objective

You are given

```text
Question Figure
      ↓
Where is the dot?
      ↓
Choose the correct option
```

The focus is **not on the shape**, but on the **position of the dot**.

---

# Basic Rule

Always ask:

> **Where exactly is the dot?**

Not

> "How does the figure look?"

# Types of Dot Situation Questions

---

# Type 1: Dot Inside a Shape

Example

```text
○

•
```

The dot lies completely inside the circle.

Choose the option where it is still inside.

---

# Type 2: Dot Outside a Shape

Example

```text
•
○
```

The dot remains outside after any rotation.

---

# Type 3: Dot on the Boundary

Example

```text
•
○
```

The dot touches the circle.

This is different from being inside or outside.

---

# Type 4: Dot Between Two Shapes

Example

```text
○ • □
```

The dot lies

* Outside the circle
* Outside the square
* Between both figures

Choose the option with the same relationship.

---

# Type 5: Dot Inside One Shape but Outside Another

Example

```text
   □
  ○•
```

Suppose

* The circle is inside the square.
* The dot is inside the square.
* The dot is outside the circle.

The correct option must preserve **both relationships**.

---

# Type 6: Multiple Enclosed Regions

Example

```text
 _______
|   ○   |
|  •    |
|_______|
```

Ask:

* Inside the rectangle?
* Inside the circle?
* Outside both?

# The Most Important Rule

Never describe the position using directions like

```text
Top

Bottom

Left

Right
```

These change after rotation.

Instead, describe it using relationships.

Example

Instead of saying

```text
Dot is on the left.
```

Say

```text
Dot is

Inside the triangle

Outside the circle

Between two lines
```

Relationships never change.

---

# Example

Question

```text
   △
  •
```

Correct description

```text
Dot is inside the triangle.
```

Not

```text
Dot is near the left.
```

---

# Rotation is Allowed

Suppose

```text
△

•
```

The triangle rotates.

The dot must rotate with it.

The relationship remains

```text
Inside the triangle.
```

---

# Mirror Image

Some exams allow mirror images.

Others do not.

Read the question carefully.

If nothing is mentioned,

assume only **rotation**.

# Common Tricks Used in Exams

---

## Trick 1

Students focus on the shape.

Wrong.

Focus on the dot.

---

## Trick 2

The figure rotates.

The dot rotates too.

---

## Trick 3

Multiple shapes.

Example

```text
Circle

Square

Dot
```

Students only check one shape.

Instead check

* Inside circle?
* Outside square?

Both must match.

---

## Trick 4

Boundary Position

Being

```text
Inside
```

and

```text
Touching
```

are different.

---

## Trick 5

Hidden Region

The dot may lie

Inside

↓

Outside

↓

Between

three different regions.

Check every region carefully.

# Step-by-Step Method

Whenever you solve a Dot Situation question:

### Step 1

Ignore the overall appearance of the figure.

Look only at the dot.

---

### Step 2

Describe its position.

Example

```text
Inside the square

Outside the circle

Between two lines
```

---

### Step 3

Rotate the figure mentally if needed.

---

### Step 4

Compare every option.

---

### Step 5

Choose the option with exactly the same relationships.

---

# Example

Question

```text
Square

Circle inside

Dot inside circle
```

Correct option

Must have

```text
Dot

Inside circle

Inside square
```

even if everything is rotated.

❌ Confusing "inside" with "touching."

# Quick Solving Checklist

Whenever you see a Dot Situation question:

1. ✅ Ignore the overall shape at first.
2. ✅ Identify every region around the dot.
3. ✅ Ask:

   * Inside which shape?
   * Outside which shape?
   * On the boundary of which shape?
4. ✅ Consider rotation if needed.
5. ✅ Compare relationships, **not directions**.
6. ✅ Reject any option where even one relationship changes.

---

# Dot Situation vs Pattern Completion vs Embedded Images

| Topic                  | What You Observe                                      |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dot Situation**      | The relative position of a dot inside/outside shapes. |
| **Pattern Completion** | The missing piece of a design or figure.              |
| **Embedded Images**    | A hidden figure inside a larger figure.               |

---

# Final Strategy (Golden Rule)

Whenever you solve a Dot Situation question, imagine you're describing the dot to someone who **can't see the figure**.

Instead of saying:

❌ "The dot is at the top-left."

Say:

✅ "The dot is inside the triangle, outside the circle, and not touching any boundary."

These **relative relationships stay the same** even if the figure is rotated, which is why this method works so well in placement exams.
