# What is a Series?

A **series** is simply a sequence of numbers, letters, or symbols arranged according to a hidden rule.

Your job is to:

> **Find the hidden rule and predict the missing or next term.**

Example:

```text
2, 4, 6, 8, ?
```

Hidden rule:

```text
+2
```

Answer:

```text
10
```

---

# Types of Series Patterns

There are many types, but almost every placement question falls into one (or a combination) of these categories.

## 1. Arithmetic Pattern (Addition/Subtraction)

The simplest type.

### Constant Addition

Example

```text
5, 8, 11, 14, ?
```

Observe

```text
5 → 8 = +3
8 → 11 = +3
11 → 14 = +3
```

Rule

```text
Add 3 every time.
```

Answer

```text
17
```

---

### Constant Subtraction

Example

```text
50, 45, 40, 35, ?
```

Rule

```text
−5
```

Answer

```text
30
```

# 4. Alternate Pattern

This is one of the **most common** in placement exams.

Instead of one rule, there are **two separate rules**.

Example

```text
2, 5, 4, 10, 6, 15, ?
```

Separate odd and even positions.

Odd positions

```text
2, 4, 6
```

Pattern

```text
+2
```

Next odd number

```text
8
```

Even positions

```text
5, 10, 15
```

Pattern

```text
+5
```

The answer is **8** because the missing term is in an odd position.

---

Another example

```text
3, 9, 4, 16, 5, 25, ?
```

Odd positions

```text
3, 4, 5
```

Pattern

```text
+1
```

Next

```text
6
```

Even positions

```text
9, 16, 25
```

Pattern

```text
3², 4², 5²
```

# 8. Fibonacci Pattern

One of the most famous series.

Each number equals the sum of the previous two.

Example


# How to Approach Any Number Series

Whenever you see a series, don't guess. Follow this order:

### Step 1: Check the differences.

Example

```text
7, 11, 15, 19
```

Differences

```text
4, 4, 4
```

If the difference is constant, it's an arithmetic series.

---

### Step 2: If the differences are not constant, check the second differences.

Example

```text
2, 5, 9, 14, 20
```

First differences

```text
3, 4, 5, 6
```

Second differences

```text
1, 1, 1
```

A constant second difference usually means the gap is increasing in a regular way.

---

### Step 3: Check multiplication or division.

Example

```text
5, 10, 20, 40
```

Every term is multiplied by 2.

---

### Step 4: Check for alternating patterns.

Split the series into odd and even positions.

---

### Step 5: Look for special sequences.

Ask yourself:

* Are these squares?
* Cubes?
* Prime numbers?
* Fibonacci numbers?
* Powers of 2 or 3?
* Factorials?

---

### Step 6: Look for combinations.

Sometimes the rule is something like:

* ×2, then +3
* +2, then ×3
* ×2 +1
* Add consecutive numbers (+1, +2, +3, +4...)

---

# Priority Order for Placement Exams

When solving number series in TCS, Cognizant, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini, and Accenture, check in this order:

1. ✅ Addition or subtraction
2. ✅ Multiplication or division
3. ✅ Differences and second differences
4. ✅ Alternate (odd/even) pattern
5. ✅ Squares and cubes
6. ✅ Prime numbers
7. ✅ Fibonacci
8. ✅ Mixed operations
9. ✅ Position-based patterns
10. ✅ Powers and factorials

If you build the habit of checking in this order, you'll identify the correct pattern much faster during the exam.
