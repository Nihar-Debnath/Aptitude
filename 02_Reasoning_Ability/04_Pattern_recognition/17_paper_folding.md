# What is Paper Folding?

In Paper Folding questions,

You are shown:

1. A flat sheet of paper.
2. The paper is folded one or more times.
3. You must determine **how the folded paper will look** or **how the paper unfolds**.

Unlike **Paper Cutting**, **no holes are punched**.

---

# Difference Between Paper Folding & Paper Cutting

| Paper Folding                        | Paper Cutting                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Only folding is involved             | Folding + holes/cuts                     |
| Predict the folded or unfolded shape | Predict the hole pattern after unfolding |
| Focus on fold direction              | Focus on fold direction + hole symmetry  |

---

# Basic Concept

Imagine a square paper.

Original

```text
+---------+
|         |
|         |
|         |
+---------+
```

Fold vertically.

```text
+----+
|    |
|    |
+----+
```

Fold again horizontally.

```text
+--+
|  |
+--+
```

Your job is to imagine the paper after these folds or after unfolding.

---

# Golden Rule

> **Every fold creates a line of symmetry.**

When you unfold the paper, everything on one side of the fold is reflected onto the other side.

---

# Types of Paper Folding

---

# Type 1: Vertical Fold

The paper folds from

Left → Right

or

Right → Left.

Example

Original

```text
+---------+
|         |
|         |
+---------+
```

Fold

```text
+----+
|    |
|    |
+----+
```

The left half lies exactly over the right half (or vice versa).

---

# Type 2: Horizontal Fold

Fold

Top → Bottom

or

Bottom → Top.

Example

```text
+---------+
|         |
|         |
+---------+
```

becomes

```text
---------
```

Now the upper and lower halves overlap.

---

# Type 3: Diagonal Fold

Paper folds along a diagonal.

Example

```text
+------+
|\     |
| \    |
|  \   |
+------+
```

One triangular half folds over the other.

These questions require more visualization.

---

# Type 4: Multiple Folding

Example

Vertical

↓

Horizontal

↓

Diagonal

The final folded shape becomes much smaller.

The examiner asks you to identify the correct folded or unfolded figure.

# Understanding Layers

Every fold doubles the number of layers.

| Number of Folds | Layers |
| --------------- | -----: |
| 0               |      1 |
| 1               |      2 |
| 2               |      4 |
| 3               |      8 |
| 4               |     16 |

Although Paper Folding questions don't usually ask about layers directly, this helps you visualize the paper.

# Fold Direction

This is the most important thing.

Suppose the paper folds

Left → Right.

The left side moves.

The right side stays fixed.

Students often imagine the wrong side moving.

Always identify **which side is being folded**.

---

# Sequence Matters

Example

Fold

Vertical

↓

Horizontal

This is **not** the same as

Horizontal

↓

Vertical.

The order of folds changes the final folded shape.

# Common Tricks Used in Exams

## Trick 1

Wrong Fold Direction

Suppose

Left folds over Right.

Many students imagine Right folding over Left.

---

## Trick 2

Wrong Order

The last fold is always the **first fold to open**.

Think backwards.

Example

```text
Fold A

↓

Fold B

↓

Fold C
```

To unfold

```text
Open C

↓

Open B

↓

Open A
```

---

## Trick 3

Diagonal Fold

Students often imagine a vertical reflection instead of a diagonal reflection.

---

## Trick 4

Ignoring Symmetry

Every fold creates symmetry.

# Step-by-Step Method

Whenever you solve a Paper Folding question:

### Step 1

Count the number of folds.

---

### Step 2

Identify the fold directions.

* Vertical
* Horizontal
* Diagonal

---

### Step 3

Follow the folds **in order**.

Don't skip any fold.

---

### Step 4

If you need to unfold,

reverse the order.

Last Fold

↓

First Open

---

### Step 5

Imagine symmetry across each fold line.

# Placement Exam Tips

Most Paper Folding questions can be solved by following these three golden rules:

### Rule 1: Identify the Fold Direction

* Vertical
* Horizontal
* Diagonal

### Rule 2: Remember the Fold Order

If the paper was folded:

```text
Fold 1
↓
Fold 2
↓
Fold 3
```

Then it must be unfolded as:

```text
Open Fold 3
↓
Open Fold 2
↓
Open Fold 1
```

### Rule 3: Think Symmetrically

Every fold creates a **mirror line**. When unfolding, whatever exists on one side of the fold is reflected to the other side.

---

## Paper Folding vs Paper Cutting (Most Important Difference)

A quick way to remember the distinction:

* **Paper Folding:** "Where does the paper go?"
* **Paper Cutting:** "Where do the holes go?"

If the question is about the **shape of the folded/unfolded paper**, it's Paper Folding. If it's about **holes or cut-outs after unfolding**, it's Paper Cutting. This simple distinction helps avoid one of the most common mistakes in non-verbal reasoning.
