Bro, **Data Sufficiency** sounds way more complicated than it actually is. 😂

The whole topic is basically:

> **"Do I have ENOUGH information to answer the question?"**

You are **NOT necessarily asked to find the answer**.

You're asked whether the given statements provide **sufficient information** to find the answer.

---

## 🔥 Simple example

### Question:

**What is the age of Rahul?**

**Statement I:** Rahul is 5 years older than Amit.

**Statement II:** Amit is 20 years old.

Now check them separately.

### Statement I alone ❌

> Rahul = Amit + 5

But we don't know Amit's age.

So Rahul's age **cannot be determined**.

❌ Not sufficient.

### Statement II alone ❌

> Amit = 20

But we don't know Rahul's relationship to Amit from this statement.

❌ Not sufficient.

### Both together ✅

From Statement I:

> Rahul = Amit + 5

From Statement II:

> Amit = 20

Therefore:

> Rahul = 25

So **both statements together are sufficient.**

---

# 🧠 What you're actually doing

Every Data Sufficiency question usually looks like:

> **Question:** Is X greater than Y?
>
> **Statement I:** ...
>
> **Statement II:** ...

And you test:

```text
Statement I alone → Enough?
Statement II alone → Enough?
Both together → Enough?
```

That's it.

---

# 🚨 VERY IMPORTANT

**"Sufficient" does NOT mean you need to calculate the answer.**

Suppose:

> Is Rahul older than Amit?

Statement I:

> Rahul is 5 years older than Amit.

You don't need to know either person's actual age.

You already know:

> Rahul > Amit

Therefore Statement I is **sufficient**.

---

# 🔥 The 4 common answer choices

Most questions give options like:

**A.** Statement I alone is sufficient.

**B.** Statement II alone is sufficient.

**C.** Both statements together are sufficient.

**D.** Even both statements together are not sufficient.

Sometimes the exact wording/options differ, but the logic is the same.

---

## Let's do one TCS-style example

### Question:

**What is the value of x?**

**I.** x + y = 10

**II.** y = 4

### Check Statement I:

x + y = 10

We don't know y.

So:

❌ **Not sufficient**

### Check Statement II:

y = 4

We still don't know x.

❌ **Not sufficient**

### Combine:

x + y = 10

y = 4

Therefore:

x + 4 = 10

x = 6

✅ **Both together are sufficient.**

---

# 🧠 The biggest trick

When solving Data Sufficiency, **DO NOT automatically combine the statements.**

Always follow:

### Step 1

Try **Statement I alone**.

### Step 2

Try **Statement II alone**.

### Step 3

If neither works, **combine I + II**.

### Step 4

Decide whether the information is enough.

---

## Think of it like this

Imagine I'm asking:

> **"How old is this person?"**

And I give you two clues:

📝 **Clue 1:** He is older than his brother.

📝 **Clue 2:** His brother is 20.

Clue 1 alone → ❌

Clue 2 alone → ❌

Both → maybe **still insufficient** if I haven't told you *how much older* he is.

That's another important possibility.

So:

> **Enough information ≠ necessarily a unique calculation.**

You need to be able to determine **one definite answer**.
