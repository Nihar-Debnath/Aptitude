For **Category 2: Date and Month same, Year different**:

### Your method

**1. Find year difference**

\[
2007-2001=6\text{ years}
\]

**2. Check leap years BETWEEN the two dates**

Here:

\[
2001\rightarrow2007
\]

The leap year is:

\[
\boxed{2004}
\]

So there is **1 leap year**.

**3. Every year normally gives 1 odd day**

6 years:

\[
6\times1=6
\]

But the leap year gives **one extra odd day**.

So:

\[
6+1=\boxed{7}
\]

\[
7\rightarrow0\text{ effective odd days}
\]

**4. Future → add to given weekday**

Given:

\[
5\text{ Jan 2001}=\text{Monday}
\]

0 odd days means **no change**.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{5\text{ Jan 2007}=\text{Monday}}
\]

### ✅ Answer: Monday

---

## ⚠️ The important rule

When the **date and month are exactly the same**, like:

\[
5 Jan 2001\rightarrow5 Jan 2007
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{\text{Year difference} + \text{extra odd day for each leap year crossed}}
\]

And yes:

> **Past → Future = add**

> **Future → Past = subtract**

### But remember:

Don't count a leap year just because it is somewhere in the numerical range. Count whether that leap year's **extra February day is actually crossed**.

For this example, **2004 February 29 is crossed**, so we add the extra 1.

Your Category 2 understanding is now:

\[
\boxed{\text{Year difference → leap-year extra → reduce by 7 → move weekday}}
\]
