# What is the Odd Days Table?

Now we move to **months**.

Every month has a certain number of days.

Instead of calculating the remainder every time, we make a small table.

### Normal year:

| Month     | Days | Odd days |
| --------- | ---: | -------: |
| January   |   31 |        3 |
| February  |   28 |        0 |
| March     |   31 |        3 |
| April     |   30 |        2 |
| May       |   31 |        3 |
| June      |   30 |        2 |
| July      |   31 |        3 |
| August    |   31 |        3 |
| September |   30 |        2 |
| October   |   31 |        3 |
| November  |   30 |        2 |
| December  |   31 |        3 |

Why do we get these numbers?

### 31-day month

\[
31=28+3
\]

28 = 4 complete weeks.

So:

\[
\boxed{31\rightarrow3\text{ odd days}}
\]

### 30-day month

\[
30=28+2
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{30\rightarrow2}
\]

### February normal

\[
28=7\times4
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{28\rightarrow0}
\]

### February leap

\[
29=28+1
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{29\rightarrow1}
\]

That's literally all the table means.

---

# Why do we need this table?

Because calendar questions often ask things like:

> **What day was 15 August 2025?**

Instead of counting every day from January 1, we can use the odd days of the months.

For example, before August:

| Month | Odd days |
| ----- | -------: |
| Jan   |        3 |
| Feb   |        0 |
| Mar   |        3 |
| Apr   |        2 |
| May   |        3 |
| Jun   |        2 |
| Jul   |        3 |

Then we also consider the 15 days of August.

We add the odd days and reduce them by 7 whenever necessary.

That's how calendar problems become manageable.
