# Type 1: General Questions / Puzzle Type Questions ⭐⭐⭐ (Most Common)

In these questions, you are given **2–5 statements** about family members, and you have to find the relationship.

### Example 1

> A is the brother of B.
> B is the daughter of C.
> C is the wife of D.

Find the relation of **A to D**.

### Step 1: Draw the family tree

```text
        D (♂)
         │
      C (♀)
         │
     ┌───┴───┐
     A (♂)  B (♀)
```

* B is daughter of C.
* A is brother of B.
* Therefore, A is also C's child.
* D is C's husband.

So A is **D's son**.

✅ **Answer:** Son

---

### Example 2

> P is the father of Q.
>
> Q is the sister of R.
>
> R is the mother of S.

```text
      P
      │
  ┌───┴───┐
  Q      R
          │
          S
```

Question:

How is **P related to S?**

P → Father of R

R → Mother of S

So P is **Grandfather**.

---

### What is tested?

* Parent
* Brother/Sister
* Uncle/Aunt
* Cousins
* Grandparents
* Son/Daughter

# Type 2: Coded Blood Relation ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Instead of writing "father" or "brother", the question uses **symbols or codes**.

Example:

Suppose

```
+
means Father

-
means Sister

×
means Mother

÷
means Brother
```

Question:

```
A + B - C
```

Translate one symbol at a time.

```
A + B

A is father of B.

B - C

B is sister of C.
```

Draw it.

```text
      A
      │
   ┌──┴──┐
   B     C
```

Question:

How is A related to C?

Answer:

Father

---

### Another Example

```
+
= Husband

-
= Daughter

×

= Brother
```

Question

```
P + Q - R
```

Read it carefully.

P is husband of Q.

Q is daughter of R.

So P is **R's son-in-law**.

Notice that this is **not a blood relation**, but exam questions may still ask for it.

---

### Shortcut

Always solve **left to right**.

Replace each symbol with its meaning before thinking about the answer.

# Type 3: Indicating Blood Relation (Pointing Questions) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

These are called **Pointing Questions** because someone points at another person and introduces them.

Example:

> Pointing to a man, Ravi said,
>
> "He is the father of my mother's only son."

Let's decode.

"My mother's only son"

↓

Ravi himself

"The father of Ravi"

↓

Ravi's father

Answer:

Father

---

### Example 2

> Pointing to a woman, Aman says,
>
> "She is the daughter of my grandmother."

Who is she?

Grandmother

↓

Her daughter

↓

Aman's mother or aunt

Since it doesn't specify which daughter,

✅ **Answer: Mother or Aunt (cannot be determined exactly).**

---

### Example 3

> Pointing to a girl, Rahul says,
>
> "She is the daughter of the only son of my father."

"My father"

↓

Only son

↓

Rahul

↓

Rahul's daughter

Answer:

Daughter

---

# How to Solve Indicating Questions

Whenever you see words like:

* Pointing to...
* Looking at...
* Introducing...
* Showing a photograph...
* Referring to...

Use this method:

1. Start from **"my"** (the speaker).
2. Decode the sentence one relationship at a time.
3. Draw a tiny family tree if needed.
4. Reach the final person.

# Comparison of All 3 Types

| Type                      | What is Given?             | What You Do                                        |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| General/Puzzle            | Family statements          | Draw a family tree and find the relation           |
| Coded Blood Relation      | Symbols (+, -, ×, ÷, etc.) | Replace symbols with meanings, then solve          |
| Indicating Blood Relation | A person points to someone | Decode the sentence from the speaker's perspective |

Based on previous aptitude patterns, the priority is:

🥈 **Indicating Blood Relation** – Very common
🥉 **Coded Blood Relation** – Less common, but easy once you understand the symbols

### Tip for Blood Relation Questions

No matter the type, the same strategy works:

* **Mark the gender** whenever it's given (♂/♀).
* **Draw a simple family tree** instead of keeping everything in your head.
* **Don't assume gender** if it's not mentioned.
* Solve **one statement at a time** from left to right.

Once you get comfortable drawing small family trees, even the longest blood relation questions become much easier to solve.
