Yep! This is a **statement sufficiency** question. The trick is to check **each statement separately**, not combine them immediately.

We know:

> **D is the brother of B.**

So:

```text
D ─── brother ─── B
```

We need to know:

> **How is B related to C?**

---

### Statement I

> **The son of D is the grandson of C.**

Visualize:

```text
       C
       │
      ?
       │
      D
       │
    D's son
```

If D's son is C's grandson, then **D is C's child**.

So:

```text
       C
       │
       D
       │
    D's son
```

And since D is B's brother:

```text
       C
      / \
     D   B
```

Therefore **B is C's child**.

But wait — we don't know whether B is **son or daughter**.

So we **cannot determine the exact relationship**.

❌ **Statement I alone is NOT sufficient.**

---

### Statement II

> **B is the sister of D.**

We already know:

```text
D = brother of B
```

Statement II tells us B is **female**.

But it tells us **nothing about C**.

```text
D ─── brother/sister ─── B

C = ???
```

❌ **Statement II alone is NOT sufficient.**

---

### Now combine I + II

From I:

```text
       C
      / \
     D   B
```

From II:

**B is the sister of D.**

Therefore B is:

👉 **C's daughter**

### ✅ Answer: **D — Both statements I and II are necessary.**

### 🧠 Super-simple rule

For statement sufficiency:

```text
Check I alone → Can I get exact answer? ❌
Check II alone → Can I get exact answer? ❌
Together → Can I get exact answer? ✅

Therefore → BOTH
```

**Answer = D**.
