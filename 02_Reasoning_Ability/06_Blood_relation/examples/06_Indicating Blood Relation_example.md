Excellent! This is another **Indicating Blood Relation** question.

This one is trickier because it starts from **"my wife's grandfather's only son"** instead of **"his mother..."**.

The trick is to **move from left to right** and draw after every phrase.

---

# Question

> Pointing towards the lady, Raj said:
>
> **"She is the only daughter of my wife's grandfather's only son."**

Who is the lady related to Raj?

---

# Step 1

Start with **Raj**.

```text
Raj (♂)
   │
 Wife (♀)
```

---

# Step 2

> **My wife's grandfather**

Go to the wife's grandfather.

```text
        Grandfather
             │
          (Family)
             │
          Wife (♀)
             │
           Raj (♂)
```

---

# Step 3

> **My wife's grandfather's only son**

Ask yourself:

**Who is the only son of the wife's grandfather?**

That is **the wife's father**.

```text
      Grandfather
           │
     Only Son
           │
        Wife (♀)
           │
         Raj
```

So now replace **Only Son** with **Wife's Father**.

```text
      Grandfather
           │
    Wife's Father
           │
        Wife (♀)
           │
         Raj
```

---

# Step 4

> **She is the only daughter of my wife's grandfather's only son.**

Now ask:

Who is the **only daughter** of **Wife's Father**?

That's simply the **Wife**.

```text
      Grandfather
           │
    Wife's Father
           │
        Wife (♀)
           │
         Raj
```

The lady being pointed at is the **wife**.

---

# Final Answer

How is the lady related to Raj?

✅ **She is Raj's Wife.**

**Answer: C) Wife**

---

# Shortcut Method

Whenever you see a long sentence like:

> My wife's grandfather's only son's only daughter

Don't read it as one sentence.

Break it into blocks:

```text
My
↓
Wife
↓
Wife's Grandfather
↓
His Only Son
↓
His Only Daughter
```

Now solve each block one at a time.

---

## Memory Trick

For **Indicating Blood Relation**, always ask:

> **"Whose are we talking about right now?"**

For this question:

```text
My
↓
My Wife
↓
My Wife's Grandfather
↓
His Only Son
↓
His Only Daughter
```

Since the **only son** is the wife's father, his **only daughter** is the wife herself.

---

### A useful observation for exams

Questions often contain phrases like **"only son"** or **"only daughter"**. These are there to remove ambiguity. If there were multiple sons or daughters, the answer might not be unique. So whenever you see **"only"**, pay special attention—it usually lets you identify exactly one person without any doubt.
