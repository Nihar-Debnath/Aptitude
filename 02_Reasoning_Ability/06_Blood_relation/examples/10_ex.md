Yes — this is a **family/relation reasoning puzzle**, but the paragraph makes it look MUCH harder than it actually is. 😄

The first thing to understand is that **this paragraph is not the question itself**. It gives you the **rules of the society**. Questions 1–3 will then give you specific situations to solve using these rules.

Let's break it down very simply.

---

# 1. There are only TWO groups

```text
        SOCIETY
       /       \
     RED      BROWN
```

A person belongs to either **Red** or **Brown**.

### Rule 1:

> No marriage is permitted within a group.

That means:

```text
RED  ❌ RED

BROWN ❌ BROWN

RED  ❤️ BROWN
```

So **husband and wife must belong to different groups.**

---

# 2. What happens after marriage?

This is the MOST IMPORTANT rule:

> **Men become part of their wife's group.**

Suppose:

```text
Man = RED
Woman = BROWN
```

After marriage:

```text
Man → BROWN
Woman → BROWN
```

So the **husband changes his group**.

But:

> **Women remain in their own group.**

So the wife doesn't change.

---

# 3. What about children?

> **Children belong to the same group as their parents.**

This wording is important.

Since the mother stays in her group and the father changes to his wife's group, after marriage:

```text
        MOTHER
        BROWN
          │
          ├──────────┐
          │          │
       FATHER      CHILD
       BROWN       BROWN
```

So **husband, wife and children all end up in the wife's group.**

---

# 4. What happens if the man becomes divorced or widowed?

This rule is easy:

> Widowers and divorced males return to their **birth group**.

Example:

```text
Before marriage:

Man → RED

Woman → BROWN


After marriage:

Man → BROWN
Woman → BROWN


After divorce:

Man → RED  ← returns to birth group
```

So always remember:

**Man's current group can change, but his birth group never changes.**

---

# 5. Marriage cannot happen with your direct descendant

> Marriage to a direct descendant is forbidden.

Direct descendant means:

```text
You
 ↓
Child
 ↓
Grandchild
 ↓
Great-grandchild
```

You cannot marry any of them.

---

# 6. Now the confusing sentence

The question says:

> **A Brown female could have had:**
>
> i. a grandfather born Brown
> ii. a grandfather born Red
> iii. two grandfathers born Brown

This is basically asking:

### "Which of these family situations are POSSIBLE?"

And you have to use the rules above.

---

# 🧠 The MOST IMPORTANT trick

For these questions, **don't try to memorize the whole paragraph**.

Keep this tiny cheat sheet:

```text
              MARRIAGE
          RED ❤️ BROWN
               │
               ↓
      Husband joins wife's group
               │
               ↓
        Wife stays in her group
               │
               ↓
        Children = wife's group

Divorced/widowed man
        ↓
Returns to birth group
```

And:

```text
❌ Same-group marriage
❌ Multiple marriages at same time
❌ Marriage with direct descendant
```

---

### One more VERY important distinction

You'll see phrases like:

**"born Brown"** or **"born Red"**

Don't confuse this with their **current group**.

For example:

```text
Man born RED
      ↓
Marries Brown woman
      ↓
Current group = BROWN
      ↓
Gets divorced
      ↓
Returns to RED
```

So:

**Born group ≠ always current group.**

That's probably the biggest trick in this entire set.

### III. Two grandfathers born Brown ❌

This is where the trick is!

The female has **two grandfathers**:

```text
       Grandfather      Grandfather
            │                │
         Parent           Parent
              \            /
               Brown Female
```

For both grandfathers to be **born Brown**, each would have to marry a **woman who is NOT Brown**, because:

> ❌ Brown man cannot marry Brown woman.

But the two grandmothers must be **Brown**, because their daughters/children are Brown.

So:

```text
Brown Grandfather ❌ Brown Grandmother
       ↑                    ↑
   same group          must be Brown
       │                    │
       └──── CANNOT MARRY ──┘
```

Therefore **III is impossible**.

* **Red-born** → if he married a Brown woman → ✅
* **Both grandfathers Brown-born** → ❌ because both grandmothers must be Brown → same-group marriage.

# Now the tricky one: III

> **Two grandfathers born Brown**

This means:

```text
        Brown Grandfather       Brown Grandfather
                │                      │
                ↓                      ↓
             Parent                 Parent
                 \                    /
                  \                  /
                    Brown Woman
```

Let's look at the **first Brown-born grandfather**.

If he was born Brown, what does he need to marry?

He **cannot marry a Brown woman**.

So he must marry:

```text
Brown man ❤️ Red woman
```

But then their child would belong to the **Red mother's group**.

So their child would be:

```text
RED
```

But we need the parent of our Brown woman to be **Brown**.

❌ Doesn't work.

The same problem happens with the second grandfather.

Therefore:

```text
I  → possible ✅
II → possible ✅
III → impossible ❌
```

# ✅ Answer: **C — I and II only**

### The one thing I want you to remember

Don't think about all the rules at once.

Just remember:

```text
MAN + WOMAN
   ↓
MAN joins WOMAN'S group
   ↓
CHILD belongs to that group
```

So if you know the **child is Brown**, the **mother must be Brown**.

That's what lets us work backward through the family tree.
