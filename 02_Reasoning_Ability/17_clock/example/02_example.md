Yes. Let's solve **8:35** slowly, using the exact ideas you just learned.

The question is:

> **What is the angle between the hour hand and minute hand at 8:35?**

There are **2 things we need to find**:

1. Where is the **minute hand**?
2. Where is the **hour hand**?

---

## Step 1: Find the minute hand

At **35 minutes**, the minute hand is on **7**.

Remember:

> Each number on the clock = **30°**

So from 12 to 7:

\[
7\times30 = 210°
\]

Therefore:

**Minute hand = 210° from 12**

---

## Step 2: Find the hour hand

Here's the tricky part.

At exactly **8:00**, the hour hand is on 8.

So its position is:

\[
8\times30=240°
\]

But the time is **8:35**, not 8:00.

The hour hand has moved **35 minutes toward 9**.

We learned earlier:

> Hour hand moves **0.5° every minute**.

So in 35 minutes, it moves:

\[
35\times0.5=17.5°
\]

Therefore the hour hand's position is:

\[
240+17.5=257.5°
\]

---

## Step 3: Find the angle between them

Minute hand:

\[
210°
\]

Hour hand:

\[
257.5°
\]

Difference:

\[
257.5-210
\]

\[
=\boxed{47.5°}
\]

# 🎯 Answer = **47.5°**

---

### Why can't we simply do this?

You might think:

> "8 is 240°, and 35 minutes means minute hand is at 210°, so 240 − 210 = 30°."

❌ **Wrong.**

Because at **8:35**, the hour hand isn't sitting exactly on 8.

It's already moved **17.5° toward 9**.

That's the biggest trick in clock questions:

> **The hour hand keeps moving even when the minutes are passing.**

So remember this little method:

**8:35**

**Minute hand:**

\[
35\times6=210°
\]

**Hour hand:**

\[
8\times30 + 35\times0.5
=257.5°
\]

**Difference:**

\[
\boxed{47.5°}
\]

That's it.
