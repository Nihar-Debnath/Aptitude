The question is:

> **How many times will both hands of a clock overlap each other in a day?**

---

## Step 1: What does "overlap" mean?

Look at **12:00**.

Both hands are pointing at **12**:

```text
        12
        ↑
       / \
      /   \
     ↑     ↑
   hour   minute
```

They are **on top of each other**.

That's called **overlap**.

So:

**Overlap = both hands are at the exact same position.**

---

## Step 2: What happens after 12:00?

The minute hand moves **faster** than the hour hand.

So after 12:00:

```text
12:00

       ↑
       |
       |
```

Then the minute hand moves ahead:

```text
12:10

       ↑
       |
       ↗
```

The hands are separated.

But the minute hand keeps moving **faster and faster around the clock**.

Eventually, it catches the hour hand again.

Something like:

```text
        ↑
        ↑
        ↑
```

They meet again.

That is **another overlap**.

---

## Step 3: How many times do they meet in 12 hours?

Let's actually think about a clock.

**12:00 → overlap #1**

Then roughly:

**1:05 → overlap #2**

Then:

**2:11 → overlap #3**

Then:

**3:16 → overlap #4**

**4:22 → overlap #5**

**5:27 → overlap #6**

**6:33 → overlap #7**

Then:

**7:38 → overlap #8**

Then:

**8:44 → overlap #9**

**9:49 → overlap #10**

**10:55 → overlap #11**

**12:00 → they meet again.**

That final **12:00 is the beginning of the next 12-hour cycle**.

So in one 12-hour period, we count:

**11 overlaps.**

**24 hours = two 12-hour periods**

# 🎯 Answer = **22 times**

# Step 2 — Understand the speed of the hands

There are two hands:

### 🕐 Minute hand

It takes **60 minutes** to go around the entire clock.

One full circle = **360°**

So in 1 minute:

[
360 ÷ 60 = \boxed{6°}
]

Therefore:

> **Minute hand moves 6° every minute.**

---

### 🕐 Hour hand

It takes **12 hours** to go around the clock.

12 hours = 720 minutes.

So:

[
360 ÷ 720 = \boxed{0.5°}
]

Therefore:

> **Hour hand moves 0.5° every minute.**

---

## Now compare them

| Hand        | Movement per minute |
| ----------- | ------------------: |
| Minute hand |              **6°** |
| Hour hand   |            **0.5°** |

The minute hand is much faster.

So every minute, the minute hand gains:

[
6 - 0.5 = \boxed{5.5°}
]

on the hour hand.

---

# Let's actually see why they meet

At **12:00**, they're together.

Now imagine **1 minute** passes.

Minute hand moved:

**6°**

Hour hand moved:

**0.5°**

So the gap between them is:

[
6-0.5=5.5°
]

After 2 minutes:

[
5.5 × 2 = 11°
]

After 10 minutes:

[
5.5 × 10 = 55°
]

They're getting farther apart.

But remember: the minute hand keeps going around the clock.

Eventually it has gained a **full 360°** on the hour hand.

That's when it catches the hour hand again.

So:

[
5.5 × \text{time} = 360
]

Therefore:

[
\text{time} = \frac{360}{5.5}
]

[
=65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}
]

So they meet roughly every:

> **65 minutes 27 seconds**

That's why the next meeting after 12:00 is around **1:05**.

# Step 1: How long is 12 hours?

We need everything in **minutes**.

[
12\times60 = \boxed{720\text{ minutes}}
]

So we have a total time of **720 minutes**.

---

# Step 2: One overlap takes 65 5/11 minutes

We found that the hands meet again after:

[
65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes}
]

Don't round it to 67. Keep the exact value.

Convert it into a fraction:

[
65\frac{5}{11}
==============

# \frac{715+5}{11}

\frac{720}{11}
]

So:

[
\boxed{\text{Gap between overlaps}=\frac{720}{11}\text{ minutes}}
]

---

# Step 3: Now ask the obvious question

We have **720 minutes total**.

Each overlap is separated by:

[
\frac{720}{11}\text{ minutes}
]

So how many such gaps fit into 720 minutes?

We simply divide:

[
\frac{720}{720/11}
]

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal:

[
720\times\frac{11}{720}
]

The 720 cancels:

[
\boxed{11}
]

### THAT is where the 11 comes from!

---

# Think about it without clocks

Imagine I tell you:

> A train arrives every **10 minutes**.
> How many arrivals happen in **110 minutes**?

You do:

[
110÷10=11
]

Same exact idea.

Here:

> Clock hands meet every **65 5/11 minutes**.
> 12 hours = **720 minutes**.

Therefore:

[
720÷65\frac{5}{11}=11
]

So:

[
\boxed{11\text{ overlaps in 12 hours}}
]

---

# But there's a subtle thing about 12:00

You might say:

> "Wait! They overlap at 12:00, and then again at 1:05..., so shouldn't there be 12?"

This is where counting gets tricky.

Look at the cycle:

```text
12:00 ── 65.45 ──> 1:05 ──> 2:10 ──> ... ──> 10:54 ──> 12:00
```

There are **11 intervals** between the starting 12:00 and the next 12:00.

So there are **11 overlap events inside one complete 12-hour cycle**.

The final 12:00 is also the starting point of the **next cycle**, so we don't count it twice.

Therefore:

[
\boxed{11\text{ overlaps in 12 hours}}
]

---

# Step 4: Now 24 hours

A day has:

[
24\text{ hours}
]

That's two 12-hour cycles.

So:

[
11+11=\boxed{22}
]

# 🎯 Final answer

[
\boxed{22\text{ overlaps in one day}}
]
