Yep! This is a **clock problem**, and the word **“noon”** is important but actually very simple.

## ☀️ What is noon?

**Noon = 12:00 PM**

It is exactly the middle of the daytime.

Think of the 24-hour day:

```text
12:00 AM → midnight 🌙
     ↓
     6:00 AM → morning
     ↓
    12:00 PM → noon ☀️
     ↓
     6:00 PM → evening
     ↓
    12:00 AM → midnight
```

### Important terms

| Term          | Meaning                           |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Noon**      | 12:00 PM                          |
| **Midday**    | 12:00 PM                          |
| **Midnight**  | 12:00 AM                          |
| **Morning**   | Usually after midnight until noon |
| **Afternoon** | After 12 PM                       |
| **AM**        | Midnight → before noon            |
| **PM**        | Noon → before midnight            |

So:

**12:00 PM = noon**
**12:00 AM = midnight**

⚠️ This is a VERY common exam trap.

# Now solve your question

> **A clock is started at noon. By 10 minutes past 5, the hour hand has turned through how many degrees?**

The clock starts at:

**12:00**

We need to find how much the **hour hand** moves until:

**5:10**

---

## Step 1: How much does the hour hand move in 1 hour?

A complete clock is:

[
360^\circ
]

There are 12 hours.

Therefore:

[
\frac{360}{12}=30^\circ
]

So the hour hand moves:

[
\boxed{30^\circ \text{ per hour}}
]

---

## Step 2: From 12:00 to 5:00

That's **5 hours**.

Therefore:

[
5\times30=150^\circ
]

So at 5:00, the hour hand has moved:

[
150^\circ
]

---

## Step 3: What about the extra 10 minutes?

This is where the question gets interesting.

The hour hand **doesn't stay fixed at 5** during the 5:00–6:00 hour.

It keeps moving.

In 60 minutes, it moves:

[
30^\circ
]

Therefore in 10 minutes:

[
30\times\frac{10}{60}
]

[
=5^\circ
]

So the extra 10 minutes adds **5°**.

---

## Step 4: Total

[
150^\circ+5^\circ
]

[
\boxed{155^\circ}
]

### ✅ Answer: **155° (Option D)**

---

# 🔥 Clock formulas you should memorize for aptitude

These are VERY useful for TCS-style questions.

### Hour hand

The hour hand moves:

[
\boxed{30^\circ/hour}
]

or

[
\boxed{0.5^\circ/minute}
]

So the hour-hand angle from 12 is:

[
\boxed{30H+0.5M}
]

where:

* H = hour
* M = minutes

For **5:10**:

[
30(5)+0.5(10)
]

[
=150+5
]

[
=\boxed{155^\circ}
]

---

### Minute hand

The minute hand moves:

[
\boxed{6^\circ/minute}
]

because:

[
\frac{360}{60}=6^\circ
]

So at 10 minutes:

[
10\times6=60^\circ
]

---

# 🧠 The three things to memorize

| Hand            |        Movement |
| --------------- | --------------: |
| **Hour hand**   |    30° per hour |
| **Hour hand**   | 0.5° per minute |
| **Minute hand** |   6° per minute |

And therefore the **angle between the hands** is:

[
\boxed{|30H-5.5M|}
]

If the answer is greater than 180°, take:

[
\boxed{360^\circ-\text{angle}}
]

to get the smaller angle.

---

## One more important distinction

Don't confuse these three types of questions:

### 1. "How much has the hour hand turned?"

Just calculate the **hour hand's movement**.

Example:

**12:00 → 5:10**

Answer:

[
\boxed{155^\circ}
]

### 2. "How much has the minute hand turned?"

Calculate minute-hand movement.

### 3. "What is the angle between the hands?"

You need **both hands**.

At 5:10:

Hour hand:

[
155^\circ
]

Minute hand:

[
60^\circ
]

Difference:

[
155-60=\boxed{95^\circ}
]

So **the hour hand has turned 155°**, but **the angle between the hands is 95°**.

That's a distinction worth remembering for your aptitude exam.
