First, understand the words:

* **Profit** = money a business earns after costs.
* **Dividend** = money paid to shareholders from profit.
* **Bonus** = extra money given to employees, often from company profit.

So **Dividend and Bonus are both related to Profit**, but they are **different things**.

Therefore the best relationship is:

> **Profit = big group, with Dividend and Bonus as separate groups inside it.**

Look at the options:

* **A** → two separate things inside Profit ✅
* B → Profit overlaps with both ❌
* C → all three overlap ❌
* D → Dividend & Bonus overlap each other ❌

### ✅ Answer: **A**

Here, **Dividend and Bonus come from profit**, so **A**.

Yes bro — **your thinking is correct**, but there's one important distinction.

### The relationship is:

**Science** is the big category.

Inside Science, there are:

* Physics
* Chemistry
* Biology
* etc.

So:

\[
\boxed{\text{Physics} \subset \text{Science}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{Chemistry} \subset \text{Science}}
\]

But **Physics and Chemistry are separate subjects**. Physics is not a subset of Chemistry.

So we need a diagram like:

```text
        SCIENCE
   ┌─────────────┐
   │             │
   │  ○     ○    │
   │ Physics     │
   │ Chemistry   │
   │             │
   └─────────────┘
```

That means **two separate circles inside one bigger circle**.

### Look at the options:

**A** = two separate circles inside one big circle ✅

**B** = one circle inside another ❌
**C** = all three overlap ❌
**D** = Physics and Chemistry overlap each other ❌

# ✅ Answer: A

And about what you said:

> "there is physics used inside chemistry"

Yes! **Physics concepts are used in chemistry**, but that doesn't mean *Physics is a subset of Chemistry*.

For these reasoning questions, think about **category/definition**, not whether one subject uses concepts from another.

**Physics + Chemistry → both are branches of Science.**
