Perfect. **Data Sufficiency** is one of the easiest aptitude topics once you understand the concept. It is **not about solving the question**—it's about deciding **whether enough information is given to solve it.**

This topic is asked in **TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Accenture, Capgemini**, and many other placement exams.

---

# What is Data Sufficiency?

Suppose I ask:

> **What is Rahul's age?**

Now I give you two statements.

### Statement I

```text
Rahul is older than Mohan.
```

Can you find Rahul's age?

❌ No.

---

### Statement II

```text
Rahul is 25 years old.
```

Can you find the answer?

✅ Yes.

This is the basic idea of Data Sufficiency.

---

# The Standard Format

Every question has:

* One question
* Two statements

Like this:

```text
Question:
What is x?

Statement I:
x = 10

Statement II:
x > 5
```

You don't directly solve the value unless necessary.

You only decide whether the information is **sufficient**.

---

# The Five Standard Answer Choices

These are almost the same in every exam.

### Option A

Statement I alone is sufficient.

Statement II alone is not.

---

### Option B

Statement II alone is sufficient.

Statement I alone is not.

---

### Option C

Both statements together are sufficient.

Neither statement alone is sufficient.

---

### Option D

Either statement alone is sufficient.

---

### Option E

Even both statements together are not sufficient.

---

# The Golden Algorithm (Always Follow This)

Whenever you solve a Data Sufficiency question:

### Step 1

Read the question.

Don't solve anything yet.

---

### Step 2

Check Statement I **alone**.

Ignore Statement II completely.

Ask:

> Can I answer the question?

Yes → mark **I is sufficient**.

No → mark **I is not sufficient**.

---

### Step 3

Now forget Statement I.

Check Statement II **alone**.

Again ask:

> Can I answer?

---

### Step 4

If neither works individually,

Combine them.

---

That's it.

# Common Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1

Using both statements immediately.

Always check **I alone** first.

---

### ❌ Mistake 2

Trying to find the exact answer.

You only need to know whether the answer **can** be found.

---

### ❌ Mistake 3

Thinking one possible answer is enough.

Example:

```text
x > 5
```

Could be

```text
6

7

8

100
```

Not sufficient.

# Prerequisites

The good news is that **Data Sufficiency doesn't require a new formula or chapter**.

It uses topics you've already learned:

* Percentages
* Profit & Loss
* Ratio
* Time & Work
* Average
* Algebra
* Geometry
* Number System

The only new skill is learning to judge **whether the given information is enough**.
