This is actually the **perfect first Data Sufficiency question** because it teaches the **meaning of all five options**.

Don't focus on the math yet. Focus on **how to think**.

---

# First, what is the question asking?

The question is:

```text
Is X a prime number?
```

Notice something.

It is **NOT** asking:

```text
What is X?
```

It is asking a **Yes/No question**.

So your goal is simply:

> **Can I answer YES or NO with certainty?**

That's all.

---

# Next, what are Statement I and Statement II?

The exam gives you **two independent pieces of information**.

### Statement I

```text
X = 2
```

### Statement II

```text
X = 2
```

Think of them as two different people telling you something.

For example:

**Person A says**

```text
X = 2
```

**Person B says**

```text
X = 2
```

The exam wants to know:

> Is Person A's information enough?

or

> Is Person B's information enough?

or

> Do you need both?

---

# Now let's understand every option.

---

## Option A

> **Statement I alone is sufficient.**

Meaning:

Ignore Statement II completely.

Pretend it doesn't exist.

Use only Statement I.

Can you answer the question?

If YES

Choose A.

---

Example

Question

```text
Is X prime?
```

Statement I

```text
X = 5
```

Can you answer?

Yes.

5 is prime.

You don't need Statement II.

So Statement I alone is sufficient.

---

## Option B

Exactly the opposite.

Ignore Statement I.

Use only Statement II.

If Statement II alone answers the question,

Choose B.

---

## Option C

This means

Statement I alone

❌ Not enough

Statement II alone

❌ Not enough

But together

✅ Enough

---

Example

Question

```text
What is x?
```

Statement I

```text
x+y=10
```

Not enough.

Statement II

```text
y=4
```

Not enough.

Together

```text
x+4=10

x=6
```

Now you can answer.

So choose **C**.

---

## Option D

This is the one students confuse the most.

It means

Statement I

✅ Enough

Statement II

✅ Enough

Both individually can answer the question.

You don't need to combine them.

---

Example

Question

```text
Is X prime?
```

Statement I

```text
X=2
```

Enough.

Statement II

```text
X=3
```

Also enough.

Either one works.

So choose **D**.

---

## Option E

This means

Statement I

❌ Cannot answer

Statement II

❌ Cannot answer

Together

❌ Still cannot answer

---

Example

Question

```text
Find x
```

Statement I

```text
x>5
```

Statement II

```text
x<20
```

Together

```text
5<x<20
```

Still many values.

So not sufficient.

Answer

**E**

---

# Now let's solve THIS question.

Question

```text
Is X a prime number?
```

---

## Step 1

Ignore Statement II.

Look only at Statement I.

```text
X = 2
```

Can you answer?

Yes.

Is 2 prime?

Yes.

You know the answer.

So Statement I is sufficient.

---

## Step 2

Now forget Statement I completely.

Look only at Statement II.

```text
X=2
```

Can you answer?

Yes.

Again,

2 is prime.

So Statement II is also sufficient.

---

Now summarize.

| Statement | Sufficient? |
| --------- | ----------- |
| I         | ✅ Yes       |
| II        | ✅ Yes       |

Since **both individually are sufficient**, the answer is

> **Option D**

---

# Why does Option D exist?

Many beginners ask:

> "If both statements are sufficient, why not choose C?"

Because **C means you NEED both statements together**.

Let's compare.

### Option C

```text
Statement I

Not enough

Statement II

Not enough

Together

Enough
```

---

### Option D

```text
Statement I

Enough

Statement II

Enough
```

Notice the difference?

**Option D is actually stronger than Option C.**

---

# The Golden Flowchart (Use this for EVERY Data Sufficiency question)

```
Read the Question
        │
        ▼
Check Statement I ONLY
        │
        ├── Enough? → YES
        │               │
        ▼               ▼
Check Statement II ONLY  Enough?
        │               │
        ▼               ▼
      YES             NO
```

Now decide:

* **I = Yes, II = No → A**
* **I = No, II = Yes → B**
* **I = No, II = No, Together = Yes → C**
* **I = Yes, II = Yes → D**
* **I = No, II = No, Together = No → E**

---

### One final rule that will save you in every Data Sufficiency question:

**Never combine the statements until you've checked each one individually.**

That's the biggest mistake beginners make. If you always follow the order:

1. Statement I only
2. Statement II only
3. Both together (only if needed)

you'll solve almost every Data Sufficiency question correctly.
