Let's solve it using the **Data Sufficiency algorithm**.

---

## Question

```text
Is X a prime number?
```

Remember:

We are **NOT** finding the value of X.

We only need to answer:

> **Can I definitely say YES or NO?**

---

## Statement I

```text
X = 2
```

Forget Statement II completely.

Can you answer the question?

Yes.

Because

```text
2 is a prime number.
```

So Statement I alone is sufficient.

Current result:

```text
Statement I → ✅ Sufficient
```

---

## Statement II

Now forget Statement I completely.

Look only at Statement II.

```text
X = 4
```

Can you answer the question?

Yes.

Because

```text
4 is NOT a prime number.
```

Notice something important:

The answer to the question is **No**, but that doesn't matter.

The question was:

> **"Is X a prime number?"**

You can confidently answer **No**.

That means Statement II is also sufficient.

Current result:

```text
Statement II → ✅ Sufficient
```

---

## Final Decision

| Statement | Sufficient? |
| --------- | ----------- |
| I         | ✅ Yes       |
| II        | ✅ Yes       |

Since **both statements individually are sufficient**, the answer is

> **✅ Option D**
>
> **Either of the statements individually is sufficient.**

---

# One Important Concept

Many students make this mistake:

> "Statement I says Yes, Statement II says No, so they contradict each other."

**Wrong.**

In Data Sufficiency, **you never compare the statements.**

Think of them as **two separate universes**.

### Universe 1

Only Statement I exists.

```text
X = 2
```

Can you answer?

✅ Yes.

---

### Universe 2

Only Statement II exists.

```text
X = 4
```

Can you answer?

✅ Yes.

---

Since **each universe gives a definite answer**, the correct option is **D**.

---

## A Rule You'll Use in Every Question

The actual answer (**Yes** or **No**) is **not important**.

The only thing that matters is:

> **Can I answer the question with certainty?**

If the answer is **Yes**, then that statement is **sufficient**.

This is the mindset you should have for every Data Sufficiency question.
