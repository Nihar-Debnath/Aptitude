Yes 👍 These are the **4 major types of Direction questions** you should prepare for your exam.

And the good news: **they are not 4 completely different topics.** They are basically the same direction diagram used in different ways.

## 🧭 Direction Questions — Exam Classification

### 1. Direction-Based Questions

These ask only **which direction** someone is facing or located in.

Example:

> A person is facing North. He turns 90° clockwise. Which direction is he facing?

**N → E**

✅ Answer: **East**

You mainly need:

* N, S, E, W
* NE, NW, SE, SW
* 45°, 90°, 180°
* Clockwise / anti-clockwise
* Left / right turns

---

### 2. Distance-Based Questions

Here the important thing is **how far** someone is from the starting point.

Example:

> A man walks 3 m North and then 4 m East. How far is he from his starting point?

Draw:

```text
        ●
        |\
      3 | \ 5
        |  \
        |___\
        ●  4
```

Use Pythagoras:

\[
\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5
\]

✅ Answer: **5 m**

So this type mainly tests **distance**, although directions may be involved.

---

### 3. Direction + Distance Questions ⭐

This is a combination of the first two.

Example:

> A man walks 3 m North and then 4 m East. In which direction and how far is he from his starting point?

We already know:

Distance:

\[
\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5m
\]

Direction:

North + East = **North-East**

✅ Answer:

**5 m North-East**

So remember:

> **Direction + Distance = direction + Pythagoras**

---

### 4. Shadow-Based Questions 🌞

These are slightly different because you need to know the **sun's position**.

The most important basic rule:

> 🌅 **Sun rises in the East.**
> 🌇 **Sun sets in the West.**

So shadows are generally opposite the Sun.

For example, if the Sun is in the East:

```text
Sun
 ☀️
  \
   \ light
    \
     🧍
      \
       \
      Shadow
        ↓
       West
```

So:

**Sun East → Shadow West**

Similarly:

**Sun West → Shadow East**

These questions can then combine with **morning/evening** and sometimes north/south positioning.

---

# 🧠 What you should learn for the exam

I'd study these in this order:

### LEVEL 1 — Direction basics

* N, S, E, W
* NE, NW, SE, SW
* Opposite directions

### LEVEL 2 — Turns ⭐

* Right = clockwise
* Left = anti-clockwise
* 45°, 90°, 180°
* Multiple turns

### LEVEL 3 — Direction-based questions

* Final direction
* Relative position

### LEVEL 4 — Distance

* Straight-line distance
* Pythagoras
* Common triangles: **3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17**

### LEVEL 5 — Direction + Distance

* Find both final direction and shortest distance

### LEVEL 6 — Shadows

* Morning/evening
* Sun direction
* Shadow direction

### 🔥 One master diagram to keep beside you

```text
                         N
                         ↑
                    NW ↖   ↗ NE
                       \   /
                        \ /
                W ←───── ● ─────→ E
                        / \
                       /   \
                    SW ↙   ↘ SE
                         ↓
                         S
```

If you can confidently use this diagram, **most direction questions become a matter of tracing movement on it** rather than trying to memorize answers.

So yes — **these 4 types are enough as your main exam framework**, and we'll tackle them one by one rather than mixing everything together.
