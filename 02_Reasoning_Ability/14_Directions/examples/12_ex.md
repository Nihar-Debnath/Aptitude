Yes! 😄 **Your confusion is exactly about the meaning of “R is sitting to the left of A.”** Let's separate the statements.

There are **5 people sitting in a row**:

**A, P, R, X, S, Z**? Wait — that's actually **6 people**, not 5. Let's count:

A, P, R, X, S, Z = **6 people**.

Now the important statement is:

> **S and Z are in the center. A and P are at the ends. R is sitting to the left of A.**

### Step 1: Put A and P at the ends

With 6 positions:

```text
1    2    3    4    5    6
_    _    _    _    _    _
```

A and P must occupy positions **1 and 6**.

But we don't yet know which one is where.

### Step 2: Use "R is sitting to the left of A"

If A were at position 1:

```text
A    _    _    _    _    P
```

There is **nobody to the left of A**.

Therefore, A **cannot** be at position 1.

So:

```text
P    _    _    _    _    A
```

Now R can be to the left of A.

### Step 3: Put S and Z in the center

For 6 people, the two center positions are **3 and 4**.

So:

```text
P    _    S/Z    Z/S    _    A
```

R has to be somewhere to the left of A, so R occupies one of positions 2 or 5?

Wait — position 5 is also left of A, so we need more information.

But notice **R can be either position 2 or 5**, while X takes the other.

So there are two possible arrangements:

```text
P   R   S   Z   X   A
```

or

```text
P   X   S   Z   R   A
```

In **both cases**, the person immediately to the right of P is **either R or X**, so the question as written is missing a constraint if it expects one unique answer.

### 🚨 But there's an even bigger issue

The question says:

> "A, P, R, X, S and Z are sitting in a row. **S and Z are in the center. A and P are at the ends. R is sitting to the left of A. Who is to the right of P?**"

With these exact statements, **the answer cannot be uniquely determined**.

For example:

**Arrangement 1**

`P R S Z X A`

→ Right of P = **R**

**Arrangement 2**

`P X S Z R A`

→ Right of P = **X**

Both satisfy every given condition.

So if the options are:

* A
* X
* S
* Z

then **X is probably the intended answer only if there is some missing condition or typo in the question.**

And to your original question:

> **"If A and P are at ends, how can R sit to the left?"**

It **can**, because "left of A" doesn't mean R has to be at the end. If A is at the **right end**, there are several positions to A's left where R can sit.

For example:

```text
P   R   S   Z   X   A
←-----------------→
        R is left of A
```

So **R does NOT have to be at an end**. It just needs to be somewhere on A's left.
