# 🧭 Directions — Reasoning

## 1. First, understand the 4 main directions

Imagine yourself standing in the middle:

```text
              North
                ↑
                |
     West  ←----+----→  East
                |
                ↓
              South
```

So remember:

* **North (N)** = ↑
* **South (S)** = ↓
* **East (E)** = →
* **West (W)** = ←

### 🧠 Easiest memory trick

Think:

> **Sun rises in the East 🌅**

So:

**East = Right side →**

And therefore:

**West = Left side ←**

---

# 2. The 4 diagonal directions

Between the main directions, we have:

```text
             North
               ↑
          ↖         ↗
       NW               NE
          ↙         ↘
               ↓
             South
```

So:

* **NE** = North-East ↗
* **NW** = North-West ↖
* **SE** = South-East ↘
* **SW** = South-West ↙

The complete map:

```text
                 N
                 ↑
            NW   |   NE
              ↖  |  ↗
                 |
W  ←-------------+-------------→  E
                 |
              ↙  |  ↘
            SW   |   SE
                 ↓
                 S
```

**This diagram is the foundation of the entire chapter.**

---

# 3. The most important thing: DON'T trust the person's viewpoint

This is where many students get confused.

Suppose a question says:

> Ravi is facing North. He walks 5 m and then turns right.

Since Ravi is facing **North**:

```text
          N
          ↑
          |
          R
```

His **right** is East.

So:

```text
          N
          ↑
          |
          R --------→ E
```

Therefore, he walks **East**.

---

# 4. Right and Left depend on which direction you're facing

This table is worth memorizing:

| Facing      | Right   | Left    |
| ----------- | ------- | ------- |
| **North ↑** | East →  | West ←  |
| **South ↓** | West ←  | East →  |
| **East →**  | South ↓ | North ↑ |
| **West ←**  | North ↑ | South ↓ |

### 🔥 Memorize this one!

For example:

### Facing North

```text
       ↑ N
       |
W ←----🧍----→ E
       |
       ↓ S
```

Right = **East**

Left = **West**

---

### Facing South

Now you're looking downward:

```text
       ↑ N
       |
W ←----🧍----→ E
       |
       ↓ S
```

But your **right hand is now West**.

So:

Right = **West**

Left = **East**

That's why you can't simply say:

> "Right means East."

❌ Wrong.

It's:

> **Right depends on the direction you're facing.**

---

# 5. Turns

Now let's learn turns.

Suppose you're facing **North**.

### Turn right

North → East

```text
       N
       ↑
       |
       └────→ E
```

### Turn left

North → West

```text
       N
       ↑
       |
W ←────┘
```

### Turn back

North → South

```text
N ↑
  |
  ↓ S
```

So:

**Right = 90° clockwise**

**Left = 90° anticlockwise**

**Back = 180°**

---

# 6. A very useful turn cycle

Think of the directions as a circle:

```text
             N
             ↑
             |
       W ←---+---→ E
             |
             ↓
             S
```

Going **right / clockwise**:

> **N → E → S → W → N**

Going **left / anticlockwise**:

> **N → W → S → E → N**

🔥 This is extremely useful.

---

# 7. Let's solve a simple question

### Question:

> A man is facing North. He walks 10 m and then turns right. In which direction is he now moving?

Step 1:

He's facing:

**North ↑**

Step 2:

He turns **right**.

From our table:

**North → Right = East**

### Answer:

**East**

---

# 8. Now a slightly harder one

> A man is facing North. He walks 10 m, turns right and walks 5 m. Then he turns right again. Which direction is he facing now?

Let's go step by step.

Initially:

**North**

First right:

**North → East**

Second right:

**East → South**

Therefore:

### Answer = South ✅

---

# 9. Now the important "final position" questions

These are very common in exams.

Example:

> A man walks 5 m North, then 5 m East. In which direction is he from his starting point?

Draw it.

Start here:

```text
          N
          ↑
          |
          |
Start ●───→
          E
```

More clearly:

```text
        A
        ↑
        |
        |
Start ●──────→ B
```

He's now:

* North of starting point
* East of starting point

Therefore he is **North-East (NE)**.

### Answer: NE

---

# 10. Another example

> A person walks 5 m East and then 5 m South. Where is he relative to his starting point?

Start:

```text
Start ●────→
            |
            ↓
            ●
```

He's:

* East
* South

Therefore:

### **South-East (SE)**

---

# 🧠 The golden rule

Whenever the question asks:

> **"In which direction is A from B?"**

Don't look at the direction A is walking.

Instead ask:

> **Where is A located relative to B?**

For example:

```text
        A
        ●
        |
        |
        ● B
```

A is **North of B**.

Therefore:

**A is North of B.**

---

# 11. Opposite directions

Another thing you MUST know:

| Direction | Opposite |
| --------- | -------- |
| North     | South    |
| East      | West     |
| NE        | SW       |
| NW        | SE       |

So:

**N ↔ S**

**E ↔ W**

**NE ↔ SW**

**NW ↔ SE**

This becomes very useful in questions like:

> A is 10 m north of B. Where is B with respect to A?

If A is north of B:

```text
A ●
  |
  |
B ●
```

Then B is **South of A**.

---

# 12. One very important exam concept: Distance

Sometimes they'll give:

> A walks 3 m North and then 4 m East. How far is he from the starting point?

Don't add:

3 + 4 = 7 ❌

Because the movement forms a right triangle:

```text
        ●
        |\
      3 | \ ?
        |  \
        |   \
Start ●─┘────●
       4
```

We use **Pythagoras**:

[
Distance=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}
]

[
=\sqrt{9+16}
]

[
=\sqrt{25}
]

[
=5m
]

### Answer = **5 m**

🔥 So direction questions can sometimes combine with **Pythagoras**.
