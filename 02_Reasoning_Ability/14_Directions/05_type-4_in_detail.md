# 🌅 1. Sunrise = Sun is in the EAST

The Sun **appears to rise in the East**.

So in reasoning questions:

> **Morning / Sunrise → Sun = East**

Imagine:

```text
                  NORTH
                    ↑

                    |
                    |
WEST  ←─────────── YOU ───────────→ EAST
                                  ☀️
                                 SUN
```

The Sun is in front of you **if you are facing East**.

Therefore:

### If a question says:

> "It is sunrise and a person is facing the Sun."

Then:

**Sun = East**

Therefore:

**Person is facing East.** ✅

---

# 🌇 2. Sunset = Sun is in the WEST

The Sun appears to set in the West.

So:

> **Evening / Sunset → Sun = West**

```text
                    NORTH
                      ↑

                      |
                      |
☀️ SUN  ←────────── YOU ──────────→
 WEST
```

If someone is facing the setting Sun:

### They are facing West.

---

# ☀️ 3. Now the MOST IMPORTANT concept: shadow

A shadow forms on the **opposite side from the light source**.

The Sun is the light source.

Therefore:

> **Shadow is always opposite the Sun.**

This is the fundamental rule.

---

## 🌅 At sunrise

Sun = **East**

Therefore shadow = **West**

```text
SUN
☀️
EAST
  ↓ light
  ↓
 🧍
  ↓
WEST
Shadow
```

So:

### Sunrise:

**Sun → East**

**Shadow → West**

---

## 🌇 At sunset

Sun = **West**

Therefore shadow = **East**

```text
Shadow
  EAST
    ↓
   🧍
    ↑
    ↑ light
    ↑
   ☀️
  WEST
  SUN
```

So:

### Sunset:

**Sun → West**

**Shadow → East**

---

# 🧠 So memorize ONLY this

### Morning:

**Sun = East**

**Shadow = West**

### Evening:

**Sun = West**

**Shadow = East**

That's the basic sunrise/sunset concept.

---

# 4. Now why do reasoning questions mention "facing"?

Because they want to convert **North/East/West/South into the person's left/right/front/back**.

For example:

> It is morning. A man is facing the Sun. Which direction is he facing?

Morning → Sun is East.

Therefore:

**He is facing East.**

Easy.

---

Now:

> It is evening. A man is facing the Sun. Which direction is he facing?

Evening → Sun is West.

Therefore:

**He is facing West.**

---

# 5. What if they say "facing away from the Sun"?

Just take the **opposite direction**.

### Morning

Sun = East.

Facing away from Sun:

**West**

### Evening

Sun = West.

Facing away from Sun:

**East**

So:

| Situation             | Sun  | Facing Sun | Facing away |
| --------------------- | ---- | ---------- | ----------- |
| **Sunrise / Morning** | East | East       | West        |
| **Sunset / Evening**  | West | West       | East        |

---

# 6. Now understand shadow + person

Suppose:

> It is morning. A man's shadow is directly behind him. Which direction is he facing?

Morning:

**Sun = East**

Therefore:

**Shadow = West**

If the shadow is **behind him**, then West is behind him.

So he must be facing the opposite direction:

**East**.

### Answer: East ✅

Visual:

```text
        SUN
         ☀️
       EAST
         ↓
       🧍
         ↓
      SHADOW
       WEST
```

The person is facing toward the Sun → **East**.

---

# 7. Another common pattern

> It is evening. A man's shadow is in front of him. Which direction is he facing?

Evening:

Sun = West

Shadow = East.

If shadow is **in front of him**, then East is in front of him.

Therefore he is facing:

**East**.

Notice something interesting:

The Sun is behind him.

```text
SUN                  Shadow
WEST                   EAST
 ☀️  →  🧍  →  shadow
       ↑
     facing
     EAST
```

So:

**Evening + shadow in front → facing East.**

# 9. What happens throughout the day?

For basic reasoning:

### 🌅 Morning

Sun is in the **East**

↓

Shadow is toward **West**

---

### ☀️ Around noon

The Sun is highest in the sky.

Therefore:

> **Shadow is shortest around solar noon.**

Some aptitude material may simplify this as **"no shadow at 12 noon."**

Don't treat that as a universal scientific fact. The exact shadow depends on location and the Sun's position.

For exam questions, however, **follow the convention stated by the question**.

---

### 🌇 Evening

Sun moves toward the **West**

↓

Shadow moves toward the **East**

# 🧠 Now the exam-solving method

Whenever you see a shadow question, do this:

### Step 1:

Look for **morning/sunrise** or **evening/sunset**.

### Step 2:

Determine Sun's direction:

**Morning → East**

**Evening → West**

### Step 3:

If they ask for the shadow:

Take the **opposite**.

### Step 4:

If they mention **facing / left / right / front / behind**, then use the person's facing direction to translate it.

---

Morning → Sun = East.

### ✅ Answer: West

### Example 2

> **At sunset, Ravi is facing the Sun. Where is his shadow with respect to him?**

Sunset → Sun = West.

Ravi faces West.

Shadow = East.

Since West is in front of him, East is **behind** him.

### ✅ Answer: Behind him.

---

# 1. Now: Left and Right depend on the PERSON

This is the part you are asking about.

Suppose the shadow is in **West**.

Whether West is the person's **left or right** depends entirely on which way the person is facing.

Let's make the table first:

| Person is facing | West is on | East is on |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| **North**        | Left       | Right      |
| **South**        | Right      | Left       |
| **East**         | Behind     | Front      |
| **West**         | Front      | Behind     |

🔥 This table is extremely useful for shadow questions.
