No problem bro. Let's **throw away the previous explanation completely**. I made it more complicated than it needed to be.

Let's understand this picture **visually first**, then solve it.

## 1. What is happening in this picture?

You have two triangles:

* 🔵 Small blue triangle = original
* 🟢 Large green triangle = enlarged triangle

The green triangle is basically the **blue triangle made bigger**.

That's called **dilation**.

The question asks:

> **How many times bigger is the green triangle than the blue triangle?**

That number is the **scale factor**.

---

## 2. We need only ONE side

Look at the **bottom side** of the triangles.

Blue triangle:

\[
C \rightarrow B
\]

Green triangle:

\[
C' \rightarrow B'
\]

We can use their coordinates.

From the graph:

### Blue:

\[
C=(0,-2)
\]

\[
B=(3,1)
\]

### Green:

\[
C' =(-1,-4)
\]

\[
B'=(5,2)
\]

Now let's see how much longer the green side is.

---

## 3. Look at the horizontal movement

For the **blue** side (CB):

C is at x = 0.

B is at x = 3.

So horizontally:

\[
3-0=3
\]

Blue side moves **3 units horizontally**.

For the **green** side (C'B'):

C′ is at x = -1.

B′ is at x = 5.

So:

\[
5-(-1)=6
\]

Green side moves **6 units horizontally**.

---

## 4. Compare them

Blue:

\[
3
\]

Green:

\[
6
\]

Ask:

> **6 is how many times 3?**

\[
6\div3=2
\]

BOOM.

\[
\boxed{\text{Scale factor}=2}
\]

So answer:

# ✅ C. 2

---

### 🧠 The easiest way to think about it

Don't worry about the center of dilation for this question.

Just remember:

> **Scale factor = New size ÷ Old size**

Here:

\[
\frac{\text{Green horizontal length}}{\text{Blue horizontal length}}
=

\frac{6}{3}

=

\boxed{2}
\]

So the green triangle is **2 times as large**.

---

**Translation:**

**Dilation:**

> "How many TIMES did the shape become bigger/smaller?"

Here:

\[
3 \rightarrow 6
\]

So:

\[
6\div3=\boxed{2}
\]

That's the entire idea.
