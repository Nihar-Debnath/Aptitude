Yep! This is a **translation (coordinate shifting)** question. The easiest way is to compare **one original point with its corresponding image point**.

### Step 1: Pick point A

From the graph:

* Original **A = (2, -1)**
* Transformed **A′ = (-4, 1)**

Now calculate the movement:

**x-coordinate:**
\[
2 \rightarrow -4
\]

So:
\[
-4-2=-6
\]

**y-coordinate:**
\[
-1 \rightarrow 1
\]

So:
\[
1-(-1)=+2
\]

Therefore, every point moves:

\[
\boxed{(-6,+2)}
\]

### Step 2: Write the translation rule

If a point is:

\[
(x,y)
\]

then moving **6 left** means (x-6), and moving **2 up** means (y+2).

So:

\[
\boxed{(x-6,\ y+2)}
\]

### ✅ Answer: **B**

\[
\boxed{(x-6,\ y+2)}
\]

### 🚀 Quick TCS trick

For these questions, **don't calculate every point**.

Just:

**Original point → Image point**

* Right = (+x)
* Left = (-x)
* Up = (+y)
* Down = (-y)

Here:

**A → A′ = 6 left + 2 up**

Therefore immediately:

\[
\boxed{x-6,\ y+2}
\]

**Option B.**

YES bro — **this is exactly where the confusion happens.** 😭

Both are related, but we're doing **two different things**:

### 1. Finding the CHANGE

Original A:

\[
(2,-1)
\]

New A′:

\[
(-4,1)
\]

To find how much the x-coordinate changed, we do:

\[
\text{new} - \text{old}
\]

So:

\[
-4-2=-6
\]

That tells us:

\[
\boxed{x\text{ changed by }-6}
\]

And **-6 means 6 units LEFT**.

---

### 2. Turning that change into the translation rule

A point originally has:

\[
(x,y)
\]

If its x-coordinate **changes by -6**, we write:

\[
x+(-6)
\]

which is simply:

\[
\boxed{x-6}
\]

Similarly:

\[
1-(-1)=2
\]

So y changed by **+2**:

\[
y+2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{(x-6,y+2)}
\]

---

### Think of it like this

| What we're doing | Calculation | Meaning           |
| ---------------- | ----------- | ----------------- |
| Find x-change    | (-4-2=-6)   | x decreased by 6  |
| Find y-change    | (1-(-1)=+2) | y increased by 2  |
| Write rule       | (x-6,\ y+2) | Move 6 left, 2 up |

### 🔥 The important formula

Whenever you have **old point → new point**:

\[
\boxed{\text{Change}=\text{New}-\text{Old}}
\]

So here:

\[
x:\quad -4-2=-6
\]

\[
y:\quad 1-(-1)=+2
\]

Then put those changes into the rule:

\[
\boxed{(x-6,y+2)}
\]

So **you were right to notice `-4 - 2 = -6`**. That's actually the cleanest way to solve it.

Absolutely bro. Now that you know **translation = moving the whole shape**, let's solve **this exact question slowly**, without jumping to the formula.

### Step 1: Identify what the question is asking

It says:

> **"The Translation Rule for the Image will be..."**

There are two triangles:

* 🔵 **Blue triangle** = original
* 🟢 **Green triangle** = image after translation

We need to find:

> **How far did the blue triangle move to become the green triangle?**

---

### Step 2: Don't look at the whole triangle

We only need **ONE matching point**.

Let's use **A** and **A′**.

From the graph:

**A** is at:

\[
\boxed{(2,-1)}
\]

Why?

* 2 to the **right** of the Y-axis → (x=2)
* 1 **below** the X-axis → (y=-1)

So:

**A = (2, -1)**

---

### Step 3: Find A′

The corresponding green point **A′** is:

\[
\boxed{(-4,1)}
\]

Why?

* 4 to the **left** of Y-axis → (x=-4)
* 1 **above** X-axis → (y=1)

So:

**A′ = (-4,1)**

---

### Step 4: Literally imagine moving A → A′

We start here:

\[
A=(2,-1)
\]

and need to reach:

\[
A'=(-4,1)
\]

#### First: What happened horizontally?

\[
2 \rightarrow -4
\]

Imagine the number line:

```text
-4  -3  -2  -1   0   1   2
 ↑                       ↑
A′                       A
```

From **2 to -4** = **6 places LEFT**.

So:

\[
x\rightarrow x-6
\]

---

#### Second: What happened vertically?

\[
-1\rightarrow1
\]

That's **2 places UP**.

So:

\[
y\rightarrow y+2
\]

---

### Step 5: Put the two movements together

The triangle moved:

**6 LEFT + 2 UP**

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{(x-6,\ y+2)}
\]

So the answer is:

# ✅ **B. ((x-6,y+2))**

### 🔥 The exam trick

You **do NOT need to calculate all three points**.

For translation questions:

**1. Pick one point**
**2. Find its original coordinates**
**3. Find where that point went**
**4. Count the movement**

Here:

\[
(2,-1)\rightarrow(-4,1)
\]

means:

**6 left, 2 up**

→ **(x-6,\ y+2)**

That's the whole question.
