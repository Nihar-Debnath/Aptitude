## The Golden Rules of Statement & Conclusion

### Rule 1: Treat the statement as true.

No matter whether it is true or false in real life, **accept it as a fact** for the question.

For example:

**Statement:**

> All fish can fly.

In real life, this is false.

But in the exam, you must **accept it as true**.

You are **not allowed** to think:

> "Wait, fish can't fly."

---

### Rule 2: Do not judge the statement.

Your job is **not** to decide whether the statement is correct or incorrect.

❌ Wrong thinking:

> This statement is unrealistic.

✅ Correct thinking:

> The question says this is true, so I'll use it as my starting point.

---

### Rule 3: The conclusion must come only from the statement.

Ask yourself:

> **"Can I prove this conclusion using only the information given?"**

If yes → ✅ Conclusion follows.

If no → ❌ Conclusion does not follow.

---

## Example

**Statement:**

> All cats are birds.
>
> Tom is a cat.

**Conclusion:**

> Tom is a bird.

Even though the statement is absurd in real life, from the given statements:

```
All Cats → Birds

Tom → Cat

Therefore

Tom → Bird
```

✅ The conclusion follows.

---

## Another Example

**Statement:**

> Some students play football.

**Conclusion:**

> All students play football.

Can you prove that?

No.

The statement only says **some**, not **all**.

❌ Conclusion does not follow.

---

## A Better Way to Think

Don't ask:

> "Is the statement true?"

Ask:

> "If the statement were true, would the conclusion have to be true?"

That's the mindset examiners want.

## Your Summary (slightly improved)

I'd write it like this in your notes:

```md
# Statement & Conclusion

## Rules

1. Accept every statement as TRUE, whether it is true or false in real life.

2. Never use personal knowledge, common sense, or assumptions.

3. Do not judge the statement.

4. Derive the conclusion ONLY from the given statement.

5. A conclusion follows only if it MUST be true based on the statement.

6. If a conclusion is only possible, likely, or based on an assumption, it does NOT follow.
```

### One sentence to remember forever

> **"Accept the statement. Question the conclusion."**

That's the heart of Statement & Conclusion. The statement is your fixed starting point; the only thing you evaluate is whether the conclusion is **logically forced** by that statement.
