Yes! This is a **truth-teller / liar / alternating-truth** question. It looks complicated, but there is a very systematic way to solve it.

Let's make the three tribes simple:

* **Sachcha (S)** → always tells **TRUE**
* **Jhutha (J)** → always tells **FALSE**
* **Lota (L)** → statements **alternate TRUE/FALSE**
  (could be TRUE-FALSE or FALSE-TRUE)

We have three people:

**GOOD, BAD, UGLY**

And they are from **three different tribes**.

---

# Step 1: Look at GOOD

GOOD says:

> **1. UGLY is Sachcha.**
> **2. I am Lota.**

If GOOD were **Sachcha**, both statements would have to be TRUE.

That would mean:

* UGLY = Sachcha
* GOOD = Lota

But GOOD cannot be both Sachcha and Lota.

❌ Therefore **GOOD is NOT Sachcha**.

---

# Step 2: Could GOOD be Lota?

If GOOD is Lota, his two statements must have **one TRUE and one FALSE**.

GOOD says:

1. UGLY = Sachcha
2. GOOD = Lota

If GOOD = Lota, statement 2 is definitely **TRUE**.

Therefore statement 1 must be **FALSE**.

So:

> UGLY ≠ Sachcha.

Since the three people belong to different tribes and GOOD = Lota, the remaining tribes for BAD and UGLY would be Sachcha and Jhutha.

Since UGLY ≠ Sachcha:

**UGLY = Jhutha**

and therefore:

**BAD = Sachcha**

Now check BAD.

BAD says:

> **1. GOOD is Jhutha.**
> **2. I am Sachcha.**

BAD = Sachcha, so **both statements must be TRUE**.

But:

* GOOD = Lota → "GOOD is Jhutha" is **FALSE** ❌
* BAD = Sachcha → TRUE

So BAD would be saying:

**FALSE, TRUE**

But Sachcha must say:

**TRUE, TRUE**

❌ Contradiction.

Therefore:

### GOOD cannot be Lota.

---

# Step 3: So GOOD must be Jhutha

We've eliminated:

❌ GOOD = Sachcha
❌ GOOD = Lota

Therefore:

## GOOD = Jhutha ✅

Let's verify it.

GOOD says:

> UGLY is Sachcha.

But if GOOD is Jhutha, this must be FALSE.

So:

**UGLY ≠ Sachcha**

GOOD's second statement:

> I am Lota.

That's also FALSE because GOOD = Jhutha.

So GOOD gives:

**FALSE, FALSE**

Perfect for a Jhutha person. ✅

---

# Step 4: Find BAD and UGLY

The three people are from different tribes.

We already have:

**GOOD = Jhutha**

So BAD and UGLY must be:

* Sachcha
* Lota

We know from GOOD's first statement:

> "UGLY is Sachcha" is FALSE.

Therefore:

**UGLY ≠ Sachcha**

So:

### UGLY = Lota

And:

### BAD = Sachcha

---

# Step 5: Verify everything

### GOOD = Jhutha

GOOD:

> UGLY is Sachcha → **FALSE**
> I am Lota → **FALSE**

✅ Jhutha says FALSE, FALSE.

---

### BAD = Sachcha

BAD:

> GOOD is Jhutha → **TRUE**
> I am Sachcha → **TRUE**

✅ Sachcha says TRUE, TRUE.

---

### UGLY = Lota

UGLY:

> BAD is Jhutha → **FALSE**
> I am Lota → **TRUE**

So UGLY says:

**FALSE → TRUE**

That's alternating! ✅

Everything works.

---

# 🎯 Answer

## **GOOD belongs to the JHUTHA tribe.**

The complete solution is:

| Person   | Tribe       |
| -------- | ----------- |
| **GOOD** | **Jhutha**  |
| **BAD**  | **Sachcha** |
| **UGLY** | **Lota**    |

---

### 🧠 The easiest method for these questions

Whenever you see these questions, make this tiny table first:

| Tribe       | What must they say?            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Sachcha** | TRUE, TRUE                     |
| **Jhutha**  | FALSE, FALSE                   |
| **Lota**    | TRUE, FALSE **or** FALSE, TRUE |

Then **assume one person's tribe**, check whether their statements fit, and eliminate contradictions.

You don't need to mentally juggle all the statements at once. That's what makes these questions feel much harder than they actually are.
