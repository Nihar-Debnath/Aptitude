These words are **quantifiers**. In **Statement & Conclusion** questions, they tell you **how strong or broad a statement is**. They are extremely important because conclusions often become **too strong** or **too weak** based on these words.

The words in your image are:

> **All, Every, Each, None, No one, Everyone, Everybody, Always**

These are called **absolute words** because they leave **no exceptions**.

---

# Meaning of each word

| Word                     | Meaning                     | Example                      |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **All**                  | 100%                        | All students passed.         |
| **Every**                | Each one without exception  | Every child has a bag.       |
| **Each**                 | Every individual separately | Each student received a pen. |
| **None**                 | Not even one                | None of the books are new.   |
| **No one**               | Nobody                      | No one came to class.        |
| **Everyone / Everybody** | Every person                | Everyone was happy.          |
| **Always**               | Every time                  | He always studies at night.  |

Notice they all mean **100%**.

---

# Why are they important in Statement & Conclusion?

Because **a conclusion cannot become stronger than the statement.**

---

## Example 1

### Statement

> **All dogs are animals.**

### Conclusion

> Dogs are animals.

✅ Correct.

The statement directly says that **every dog is an animal**.

---

### Wrong Conclusion

> **All animals are dogs.**

❌ Wrong.

The statement never said that.

---

## Example 2

### Statement

> Some students are intelligent.

### Conclusion

> **All students are intelligent.**

❌ Wrong.

The word **All** makes the conclusion much stronger than the statement.

---

## Example 3

### Statement

> Every employee must wear an ID card.

### Conclusion

> Some employees wear ID cards.

✅ Correct.

If **every** employee wears one, then **some** certainly do.

---

## Example 4

### Statement

> Some birds can fly.

### Conclusion

> Every bird can fly.

❌ Wrong.

The word **Every** makes the conclusion too strong.

---

# Important Rule

When you see words like

* All
* Every
* Each
* Always
* None
* Never
* No one

🚨 **Be careful.**

These words require **100% proof** from the statement.

# Another Trick

Suppose the statement is

> Most students like cricket.

Conclusion:

> Everyone likes cricket.

❌ Wrong.

Because

**Most ≠ Everyone**

---

Suppose the statement is

> Everyone likes cricket.

Conclusion

> Some people like cricket.

✅ Correct.

If everyone likes it, then obviously some people do.

## Aptitude Exam Tip (TCS, Cognizant, Accenture)

Whenever you solve **Statement & Conclusion** questions:

1. Underline words like **All, Every, Each, None, Always, Never**.
2. Ask yourself:

   * Is the conclusion using the **same strength** as the statement?
   * Or is it becoming stronger?
3. If the conclusion is **stronger than the statement**, it is usually **invalid**.

These absolute words are among the biggest clues in logical reasoning and are tested frequently in placement exams.

# Meaning of each word

| Word               | Meaning                               | Strength  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | --------- |
| **Some**           | At least one, but not all necessarily | Weak      |
| **Many**           | A large number                        | Medium    |
| **Few**            | A small number                        | Weak      |
| **May**            | Possible                              | Very Weak |
| **Might**          | Could possibly happen                 | Very Weak |
| **Maybe / May be** | It is possible                        | Very Weak |

# 1. Some

**Meaning:**

At least **one or more**.

Example:

> Some students play football.

It could mean:

* 2 students
* 10 students
* 100 students

We don't know.

It **doesn't mean all**.

---

## Statement

> Some birds can swim.

### Conclusion

> All birds can swim.

❌ Wrong

---

### Conclusion

> At least one bird can swim.

✅ Correct

Because **some** means one or more.

---

# 2. Many

**Meaning**

A large number.

Example:

> Many people use WhatsApp.

This doesn't mean everyone.

---

Statement

> Many employees work overtime.

Conclusion

> All employees work overtime.

❌ Wrong

---

# 3. Few

**Meaning**

A small number.

Example

> Few students failed.

Maybe 3 students.

Maybe 5 students.

Not everyone.

---

Statement

> Few roads are damaged.

Conclusion

> All roads are damaged.

❌ Wrong

---

# 4. May

**Meaning**

Possible.

Not certain.

Example

> It may rain today.

It could rain.

It could also not rain.

---

Statement

> It may rain tomorrow.

Conclusion

> It will definitely rain tomorrow.

❌ Wrong

---

# 5. Might

Almost the same as **may**.

Example

> He might come.

Means

Possible.

Not sure.

---

# 6. Maybe

Example

> Maybe she is busy.

Means

There is a possibility.

Nothing is certain.

# One more important placement rule

Words like **may**, **might**, **can**, **could**, **possibly**, and **perhaps** indicate **possibility**, not certainty.

Example:

**Statement:**

> Regular exercise may improve health.

**Conclusion 1:**

> Regular exercise improves health.

❌ Invalid (the statement only says it *may* improve health).

**Conclusion 2:**

> Regular exercise has the potential to improve health.

✅ Valid.

**Statement:**

> All students are honest.

**Conclusion:**

> Some students are honest.

✅ Generally accepted as true in aptitude questions (assuming the group exists).

---

### Rule 3: Weak → Strong ❌

**Statement:**

> Some students are honest.

**Conclusion:**

> All students are honest.

❌ False

---

### Rule 4: Weak → Weak ✅ (if supported)

**Statement:**

> Some students are honest.

**Conclusion:**

> At least one student is honest.

✅ True

## Think of it like a glass of water.

**Statement:**

> The glass is completely full.

Then saying:

> The glass has some water.

✅ True.

But if the statement is:

> The glass has some water.

Can you conclude:

> The glass is completely full?

❌ No.

## The exam shortcut

When you see these words:

🔴 **Strong words**

* All
* Every
* Always
* Never
* None
* Everyone

Ask yourself:

> **"Did the statement really prove 100%?"**

If not, the conclusion is probably false.

---

🟢 **Weak words**

* Some
* Many
* Few
* May
* Might
* Can
* Could

Ask yourself:

> **"Is this weaker than or equal to what the statement says?"**

If yes, it may be valid.

---

### One thing to remember

Don't judge **only by the word**. The conclusion must also be **related to the statement**.

For example:

**Statement:**

> All cats are animals.

**Conclusion:**

> Some dogs are friendly.

❌ False—not because of the word "some," but because it has **nothing to do with the statement**.

---

### The golden rule for Statement & Conclusion

1. ✅ The conclusion must be **based only on the statement**.
2. ✅ It must **not be stronger than the statement**.
3. ✅ It must stay **within the scope of the statement**—don't add new information.

If you follow these three rules, you'll solve a large percentage of Statement & Conclusion questions correctly.
