# What is a Course of Action?

A **Course of Action** is the **best practical step** that should be taken to solve the problem mentioned in the statement.

Think of it like this:

```text
Statement (Problem)
          ↓
What should be done?
```

Unlike **Assumption**, where we look for a hidden belief, here we look for the **best solution**.

---

# Formula

```text
Problem
   ↓
Logical
Practical
Effective
Action
```

---

# Example 1

### Statement

> Road accidents are increasing rapidly.

### Course of Action

I. Strict traffic rules should be enforced.

II. Roads should be closed permanently.

---

### Check I

Does it solve the problem?

✅ Yes.

Practical?

✅ Yes.

---

### Check II

Close all roads?

❌ Not practical.

---

Answer:

✅ Only I follows.

# Rules

## Rule 1: Action must solve the problem.

If it doesn't solve the problem,

❌ Reject.

---

Example

Statement

> Pollution is increasing.

Action

> Plant more trees.

✅ Helps.

---

Action

> Paint all buildings.

❌ Doesn't solve pollution.

---

## Rule 2: Action must be practical.

Don't accept impossible or unrealistic actions.

---

Statement

> Crime has increased.

Action

> Shut down the entire city.

❌ Impossible.

---

Action

> Increase police patrols.

✅ Practical.

---

## Rule 3: Action should not be extreme.

Words like

* All
* Every
* Never
* Only
* Completely
* Ban everything

are usually wrong.

---

Example

Statement

> Students use mobile phones in class.

Action

> Ban all mobile phones forever.

❌ Too extreme.

---

## Rule 4: Action should directly relate to the statement.

If it's unrelated,

❌ Reject.

---

Statement

> Farmers are suffering due to drought.

Action

> Increase internet speed.

❌ No relation.

---

## Rule 5: Action should address the cause or the problem, not something random.

---

Statement

> Food prices have increased.

Action

> Improve food production.

✅ Related.

---

Action

> Build more airports.

❌ Unrelated.

---

# Shortcut Questions

Whenever you see a Course of Action, ask:

### 1.

Does it solve the problem?

---

### 2.

Is it practical?

---

### 3.

Is it legal and reasonable?

---

### 4.

Is it directly connected?

---

If all answers are YES,

✅ It follows.

❌ Too extreme.

---

### Trap 2

Too Expensive/Impossible

Statement

> Traffic has increased.

Action

> Build a flyover in every village immediately.

❌ Impractical.

---

### Trap 3

Unrelated

Statement

Action

> Increase bus fare.

❌ No connection.

---

### Trap 4

Future Promise

Statement

> Water shortage exists.

Action

❌ Doesn't solve the problem.

# Difference from Assumption

| Statement & Assumption      | Statement & Course of Action |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Hidden belief               | Solution                     |
| Exists before the statement | Happens after the statement  |
| "What is believed?"         | "What should be done?"       |

---

# Ultimate Cheat Sheet

| Check                       | If YES   |
| --------------------------- | -------- |
| Solves the problem?         | ✅ Accept |
| Practical and possible?     | ✅ Accept |
| Directly related?           | ✅ Accept |
| Too extreme?                | ❌ Reject |
| Impossible or emotional?    | ❌ Reject |
| Unrelated to the statement? | ❌ Reject |

# Memory Trick

Think of a doctor.

* **Statement:** "The patient has a fever."
* **Assumption:** The doctor believes the patient is ill.
* **Course of Action:** Give medicine, diagnose the cause, advise rest.

So:

```text
Problem
   ↓
Find the most logical and practical solution.
```

That's exactly how you should approach every **Statement & Course of Action** question.

## Words to watch out for

### 🚨 Usually Reject

* All
* Every
* Always
* Never
* Completely
* Immediately (if unrealistic)
* Permanently

### ✅ Usually Accept

* Should
* Ought to
* Steps should be taken
* Authorities should
* Awareness should be created
* Strict action should be taken (when appropriate)

These usually indicate a reasonable, actionable solution rather than an extreme reaction.
