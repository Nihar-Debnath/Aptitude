# TIP 1: Choose Practical and Relevant Action

> **The action should be practical, logical, and related to the problem. It should solve or at least reduce the problem.**

### Ask yourself:

✅ Can this actually be implemented?

✅ Is it directly related to the problem?

✅ Does it solve or reduce the problem?

### Example

**Statement:**

> Air pollution is increasing.

**Action 1:** Plant more trees.

✅ Practical

✅ Related

✅ Reduces pollution

**Follows.**

---

**Action 2:** Build more shopping malls.

❌ Not related.

**Does not follow.**

---

## Memory Trick

> **P + R + S**

* **P** = Practical
* **R** = Relevant
* **S** = Solves/Reduces the problem

---

# TIP 2: Action Should Not Create Another Problem

Sometimes an action solves one problem but creates a bigger one.

Such actions are rejected.

### Example

**Statement:**

> Road accidents are increasing.

**Action:**

> Put one police officer behind every vehicle.

Will it reduce accidents?

✅ Yes.

Can it actually be done?

❌ No.

It needs lakhs of police officers.

It creates another problem (lack of manpower, huge cost).

**Does not follow.**

---

Another example

**Statement:**

> Traffic jams are increasing.

**Action:**

> Ban all private vehicles.

It may reduce traffic.

But now millions of people cannot travel.

Creates a new problem.

❌ Reject.

---

## Memory Trick

> **One solution should not create ten new problems.**

---

# TIP 3: Avoid Unpredictable Outcomes

The action should have a **clear and reasonable effect**.

If success depends on guessing or luck, reject it.

### Example

**Statement:**

> Company Z is facing heavy losses.

**Action 1:**

> Give a huge discount.

Will customers definitely come?

🤔 Nobody knows.

Maybe yes.

Maybe no.

Outcome is uncertain.

❌ Reject.

---

**Action 2:**

> Study competitors and improve products.

This is a logical first step.

✅ Accept.

---

Another example

**Statement:**

> Sales are decreasing.

**Action:**

> Hire a famous actor.

Will sales definitely increase?

Unknown.

❌ Reject.

---

## Memory Trick

Ask:

> **Can I confidently say this action will help?**

If the answer is **"Maybe"**, reject it.

---

# TIP 4: Avoid Negative and Harsh Actions

Don't choose cruel, extreme, or harsh actions unless absolutely necessary.

### Example

**Statement:**

> Students are cheating.

**Action:**

> Expel every student.

Too harsh.

❌ Reject.

---

Better action

> Increase invigilation.

✅ Accept.

---

Another

**Statement:**

> Theft has increased.

**Action:**

> Hang every thief.

Too harsh.

❌ Reject.

---

Better

> Increase police patrolling.

✅ Accept.

---

## Memory Trick

Always prefer

> **Correct → Educate → Prevent**

instead of

> **Destroy → Punish → Ban**

unless the statement clearly demands strict action.

---

# TIP 5: Prefer Long-Term Actions

Good solutions solve the problem for a long time.

Temporary solutions are weaker.

### Example

**Statement:**

> Water shortage.

Action 1

> Supply water through tankers every day.

Works today.

Not permanent.

⚠️ Weak.

---

Action 2

> Build rainwater harvesting systems.

Permanent.

Long-term.

✅ Better.

---

Another

**Statement:**

> Students are weak in English.

Action

> Give answers in tomorrow's exam.

Temporary.

❌ Reject.

---

Better

> Conduct communication classes.

Long-term.

✅ Accept.

---

# Ultimate Flowchart

Whenever you see a Course of Action, ask these 5 questions in order:

```text
1. Is it Practical?
        ↓
2. Is it Relevant?
        ↓
3. Does it solve/reduce the problem?
        ↓
4. Does it avoid creating new problems?
        ↓
5. Is it a reasonable, long-term solution?
```

If the answer is **YES** to all five, the action **follows**.

---

# Final Cheat Sheet

| Tip             | Accept ✅                        | Reject ❌                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Practical       | Can actually be implemented     | Impossible or unrealistic   |
| Relevant        | Directly related to the problem | Unrelated                   |
| Solve Problem   | Solves or minimizes the issue   | Doesn't help                |
| No New Problems | Doesn't create bigger issues    | Creates more problems       |
| Predictable     | Likely to work                  | Depends on luck or guessing |
| Harshness       | Reasonable action               | Extreme punishment          |
| Long-term       | Permanent or lasting solution   | Temporary patch             |

## The **Golden Formula** for TCS

Whenever you read an action, mentally score it:

* ✅ Practical?
* ✅ Logical?
* ✅ Related to the statement?
* ✅ Doesn't create a bigger problem?
* ✅ Reasonable outcome (not based on luck)?
* ✅ Not unnecessarily harsh?
* ✅ Preferably long-term?

If you get **5–7 "Yes" answers**, the course of action usually **follows**. This simple checklist works very well for placement aptitude exams.
