# Q1

### Statement

A train derailed on a bridge and fell into a river, killing 65 people.

### Action I

> Railway Authorities should clarify the reason for the accident to the Government.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Practical
* ✔ Relevant
* ✔ Helps find the cause (long-term prevention)

---

### Action II

> Government should allocate funds to compensate the destruction caused.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Practical
* ✔ Helps victims/recovery
* ✔ Standard government action after disasters

---

### Action III

> Protection walls of the bridge should be made stronger to avoid such accidents.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Long-term solution
* ✔ Helps reduce future damage if derailments occur

---

### ✅ Answer: **D. All follow**


# but


You're noticing something very important. This is where many students get confused because **different books and teachers use slightly different standards**.

Let's examine it.

---

## Statement

> A train derailed and fell into a river.

### Action I

> Railway authorities should **clarify the reason** for the accident to the Government.

Your teacher says this **doesn't follow** because:

> **Finding or reporting the cause itself is not an action that solves the problem.**

He's making a distinction between:

* **Investigating** ❌
* **Taking corrective action** ✅

---

### Think of this example

**Statement:**

> Students are failing in mathematics.

### Action A

> Find out why students are failing.

This only gives information.

It doesn't improve results.

---

### Action B

> Arrange remedial classes.

This actually solves the problem.

---

Your teacher considers **Course of Action** to mean:

> **An action that directly reduces or removes the problem**, not just gathers information.

---

## Then why is III accepted?

### Action III

> Strengthen the protection walls.

This actually changes something physically.

It reduces damage if another derailment occurs.

So it's a **corrective and preventive action**.

---

## A very useful distinction

| Type of Action                 | Usually Accepted?              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Investigate the cause          | ⚠️ Depends on the exam/teacher |
| Report the cause               | ⚠️ Even weaker                 |
| Take preventive measures       | ✅ Yes                          |
| Improve infrastructure         | ✅ Yes                          |
| Give temporary relief          | ⚠️ Depends                     |
| Permanently reduce the problem | ✅ Best answer                  |

---

## Why did I initially accept I?

Because many reasoning books accept:

> "Investigate the cause"

as a valid first step before solving the problem.

For example:

* Factory explosion → Investigate the cause.
* Bridge collapse → Investigate the cause.
* Plane crash → Investigate the cause.

Many exams consider this a reasonable course of action.

---

## But notice the wording here

It does **not** say:

> "Investigate the cause."

It says:

> **"Clarify the reason to the Government."**

That is even weaker.

It is merely **communicating information**, not taking action.

So if your teacher rejected I for this reason, the logic is:

* ❌ Doesn't solve the accident problem.
* ❌ Doesn't prevent future accidents.
* ❌ It's only reporting.

That is a defensible interpretation.

---

# For TCS, here's the safest mindset

Ask:

> **"If this action is implemented, will the actual problem become smaller or disappear?"**

* Just reporting information → ❌ Usually weak.
* Giving compensation → ❌ Helps victims, not the problem.
* Strengthening safety measures → ✅ Direct solution.

So if your teacher's **answer is "Only III follows,"** it means he is using a **strict interpretation**:

> **Only actions that directly prevent or reduce the problem are accepted.**

This is actually a good mindset for TCS because it helps eliminate many tempting but indirect options.

# Q2

### Statement

Authorities are blaming street hawkers for traffic jams.

---

### Action I

> Street hawkers should not be allowed during peak hours.

❌ **Does NOT follow**

**Why?**

* It is **too harsh** (Tip 4).
* No evidence that banning them is necessary.
* It affects livelihoods and may create another problem (Tip 2).

---

### Action II

> Hawkers should be warned and asked not to create chaos.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Practical
* ✔ Relevant
* ✔ Least harsh
* ✔ Attempts to solve the problem

---

### ✅ Answer: **Only II follows**

# Q3

### Statement

Many people became critically ill after drinking spurious liquor.

---

### Action I

> Close all liquor shops until stocks are tested.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Immediate safety measure
* ✔ Prevents more victims
* ✔ Practical

---

### Action II

> Ask the shop owner to leave the town.

❌ **Does NOT follow**

**Why?**

* Doesn't solve the problem.
* Avoids punishment.
* Not a legal/government action.

---

### Action III

> Arrest the owner and try him for criminal negligence.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Legal action
* ✔ Practical
* ✔ Prevents similar crimes

---

### ✅ Answer: **Only I and III follow**

# Q4

Major part of the rabi crop is damaged due to heavy rains.

---

### Action I

> Government should grant relief to affected farmers.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Immediate help
* ✔ Practical

---

### Action II

> Government should provide free seeds and fertilizer for the next season.

✅ **Follows**

**Why?**

* ✔ Long-term solution
* ✔ Helps farmers recover

---

### Action III

> Government should waive all loans.

❌ **Does NOT follow**

**Why?**

* Too extreme.
* Loan waiver is not always necessary for every affected farmer.
* Creates financial burden (Tip 2).

### ✅ Answer: **Only I and II follow**

## One important observation

Notice a pattern in TCS questions:

* ✅ **Investigate the cause** → Usually follows.
* ✅ **Warn, educate, improve systems** → Usually follows.
* ✅ **Provide reasonable relief** → Usually follows.
* ❌ **Ban everything** → Usually rejected.
* ❌ **Extreme punishments or unnecessary harshness** → Usually rejected.
* ❌ **Actions that create new problems or are financially/administratively unrealistic** → Usually rejected.

If you keep these patterns in mind along with the 5 tips, you'll find most Course of Action questions much easier to solve.
