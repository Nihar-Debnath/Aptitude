# Question 1 (Absolute Word Trap)

### Statement

Should the government make coding compulsory in schools?

### Argument

> Every student will become a software engineer if coding is taught in school.

Think...

### Answer

❌ **Weak Argument**

**Why?**

The word **"Every"** is an **unsupported absolute claim**.

Even if coding is taught, not every student becomes a software engineer.

# Question 9 (May/Might Trap)

### Statement

Should electric vehicles be promoted?

### Argument

> They may reduce pollution.

Think carefully.

Many students think **"may"** makes it weak.

### Answer

✅ **Strong Argument**

Why?

The argument is **reasonable**.

The word **may** shows a realistic possibility, not an unsupported exaggeration.

Notice something important:

The **table is not saying "may" is always weak.**

If someone says

> Electric vehicles may reduce pollution.

That is scientifically reasonable.

But if someone says

> Electric vehicles may make everyone rich.

That would be weak.

# The Biggest Myth About This Table

Many YouTube teachers say:

> **May, Might = Weak**

This is **not always true**.

The real rule is:

### ❌ Weak

> He may become the richest person in the world because he bought a laptop.

(Random assumption)

---

### ✅ Strong

> Vaccination may reduce the spread of infectious diseases.

(Reasonable scientific possibility)

# The Biggest Trick Companies Use

They combine **one strong feature** with **one weak feature**.

Example:

> Every citizen should join the army because it will make everyone happy.

You might notice **"security of nation"** and choose Strong.

But actually:

* "Every citizen" ❌ (unsupported absolute)
* "Make everyone happy" ❌ (exaggeration)

Overall:

❌ **Weak Argument**

## Golden Rule (More Important Than the Table)

Before deciding, ask these **five questions**:

1. **Is it relevant to the statement?**
2. **Is it logical?**
3. **Is it practical?**
4. **Is it supported by facts/common sense/authority?**
5. **Does it avoid exaggeration, vague language, and unsupported assumptions?**

If you answer **Yes** to all five, it's usually a **Strong Argument**, even if it contains words like **may**.
