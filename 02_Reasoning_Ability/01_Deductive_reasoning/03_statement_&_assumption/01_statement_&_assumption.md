These are **shortcut rules** that many aptitude teachers use to solve **Statement & Assumption** questions quickly. They are useful, but **don't treat them as 100% universal laws**. They work in most exam questions, but the safest method is still understanding the logic behind assumptions.

Let's go through each one.

---

# Rule 1

> **Words like EVERY, EACH, NONE, ALL, ONLY usually make the assumption FALSE.**

### Why?

These are **extreme words**.

An assumption usually contains a **necessary hidden belief**, not an extreme claim.

### Example

**Statement:**

> Buy our toothpaste for healthy teeth.

**Assumption:**

> **Only** this toothpaste gives healthy teeth.

❌ False

The company never said that **only** their toothpaste works.

---

Another one

Statement

> Wear a helmet while riding.

Assumption

> **Every** accident can be prevented by helmets.

❌ False

Too extreme.

---

### Trick

Whenever you see

* Every
* All
* None
* Never
* Only
* Always

🚨 Be suspicious.

Most of the time, the assumption is false.

But don't reject it blindly—read the statement first.

---

# Rule 2

> **Any assumption which is not connected (stuck) to the statement is FALSE.**

This is one of the most important rules.

---

Example

Statement

> Drink milk daily.

Assumption A

> Milk is nutritious.

✅ Connected.

---

Assumption B

> Cows should be protected.

❌ Not connected.

The statement isn't about cows.

So reject it.

---

Think like this:

```
Statement
     │
     │
Connected?
     │
Yes → Check further

No → Reject
```

---

# Rule 3

> **Any assumption talking about the past or future is FALSE.**

### Why?

Remember:

An assumption is something believed **before making the statement**.

It should not talk about

* what happened earlier
* what will happen later

---

Example

Statement

> Join this coding course.

Assumption

> You **will definitely** get a job.

❌ Future

Reject.

---

Another

Statement

> Lock the door.

Assumption

> Someone **already stole** your laptop.

❌ Past

Reject.

---

Correct assumption

> Locking the door improves safety.

✅ Present hidden belief.

---

# Rule 4

> **Any assumption going against the statement is FALSE.**

Makes sense.

---

Statement

> Eat vegetables daily.

Assumption

> Vegetables are unhealthy.

❌ Opposite.

Reject.

---

Statement

> Wear a seat belt.

Assumption

> Seat belts increase danger.

❌ Opposite.

Reject.

---

# Rule 5

> **If statement is general and assumption is specific (or vice versa), assumption is FALSE.**

This confuses many students.

Let's understand.

---

### Case 1

Statement

> Students should exercise regularly.

This talks about **all students**.

---

Assumption

> Rahul does not exercise.

❌ Specific person.

The statement never mentioned Rahul.

Reject.

---

### Case 2

Statement

> Rahul should exercise regularly.

Specific person.

---

Assumption

> All students are unhealthy.

❌ Too general.

Reject.

---

The level should match.

General ↔ General

Specific ↔ Specific

---

# But remember...

This rule has exceptions.

Example

Statement

> Every employee must wear an ID card.

Assumption

> Employees work in the company.

This assumption is still valid even though one sentence is broad and the other is also broad in a different way.

So don't use Rule 5 blindly.

---

# Are these rules enough?

Not completely.

The safest approach is this **3-step test**:

### Step 1

Is the assumption connected to the statement?

If No → Reject.

---

### Step 2

Is it a hidden belief required for the statement?

If No → Reject.

---

### Step 3

If this assumption becomes false,

does the statement lose its meaning?

If Yes → It's an assumption.

---

# Cheat Sheet

| Rule                                    | Usually True?                     | Example                                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Extreme words (All, Every, Only, Never) | ❌ Usually false                   | "Only this medicine works."                              |
| Not related to statement                | ❌ False                           | Statement about books, assumption about cars             |
| Talks about past or future              | ❌ False                           | "You will definitely succeed."                           |
| Opposes the statement                   | ❌ False                           | Statement: Eat fruits. Assumption: Fruits are unhealthy. |
| General ↔ Specific mismatch             | ⚠️ Usually false, but check logic | Statement: Students..., Assumption: Rahul...             |

## One important warning for TCS/Cognizant exams

Many coaching institutes teach these as **quick elimination rules**, not absolute laws. If a question seems to break one of these rules, **trust the logic of assumptions over the shortcut**.

Always ask yourself:

> **"Did the speaker have to believe this before making the statement?"**

That question is more reliable than memorizing shortcuts.
