## Statement

> **"Do not lean out of the door of the bus."** (A warning in a school bus)

This is a **warning**.

Whenever you see words like:

* Do not
* Avoid
* Beware
* Don't
* Must not

the speaker is giving advice or warning because they believe **something**.

Now let's check each assumption.

---

# Assumption I

> **Leaning out of a running bus is dangerous.**

### Ask yourself:

If leaning out of the bus **wasn't dangerous**, would anyone give this warning?

No.

There would be no reason to say

> "Don't lean out."

So this hidden belief is necessary.

✅ **Assumption I is implicit (True).**

---

# Assumption II

> **Children do not pay any heed to such warnings.**

("Pay heed" means **pay attention**.)

Let's test it.

Does the warning mean children usually ignore warnings?

No.

The school is simply giving a safety instruction.

They might obey.
They might not.

Nothing in the statement tells us children ignore warnings.

This assumption adds extra information.

❌ **Assumption II is NOT implicit (False).**

---

# Correct Answer

✅ **(A) Only Assumption I is implicit.**

---

# Learn the logic visually

### Statement

```
Do not lean out of the bus.
```

Why would someone say this?

↓

```
Because leaning out is dangerous.
```

This is the hidden belief.

---

Now look at Assumption II.

```
Children don't listen to warnings.
```

Was this needed to give the warning?

No.

Even if children are very obedient,

the teacher would still say

> "Don't lean out."

So it is **not required**.

---

# Use the "False Test"

This is my favorite technique.

---

### Test Assumption I

Suppose it is false.

```
Leaning out is NOT dangerous.
```

Then the warning becomes meaningless.

Why warn people about something harmless?

So Assumption I **must be true**.

---

### Test Assumption II

Suppose it is false.

```
Children DO pay attention to warnings.
```

Can the teacher still say

> "Don't lean out of the bus"?

Absolutely yes.

The warning still makes perfect sense.

So II is **not an assumption**.

---

# Common Mistake

Many students think:

> "Since the teacher is warning them, children must not listen."

This is incorrect.

A warning does **not** imply that people ignore warnings.

For example:

> Wear your seatbelt.

Does that mean people never wear seatbelts?

❌ No.

It simply means wearing one is important.

---

# A shortcut for warnings

Whenever you see a warning, ask:

> **What danger or risk made the speaker give this warning?**

That danger is often the assumption.

Examples:

| Statement                  | Hidden Assumption         |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Don't touch the wire.      | The wire may cause harm.  |
| Wear a helmet.             | Helmets improve safety.   |
| Don't smoke.               | Smoking is harmful.       |
| Don't lean out of the bus. | Leaning out is dangerous. |

Notice how none of these require assumptions like:

* People are careless.
* People never listen.
* Everyone will get injured.

Those are extra ideas, not necessary hidden beliefs.

---

This is actually a **classic Statement & Assumption question**, and it demonstrates one of the most important principles:

> **An assumption is what is necessary for the statement to make sense—not an explanation of people's behavior.**

That's why **I is implicit** and **II is not**.
