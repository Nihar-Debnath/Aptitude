This is one of the most important concepts in logic. Once you understand it, Statement & Conclusion, Syllogism, and many reasoning topics become much easier.

---

# Deductive Reasoning

**Deductive reasoning** goes from **general → specific**.

You start with a general rule, then apply it to a specific case.

If the premises are true and the logic is valid, **the conclusion must be true**.

### Structure

```text
General Rule
      ↓
Specific Fact
      ↓
Certain Conclusion
```

### Example 1

```text
All humans are mortal.
Socrates is a human.
```

Therefore,

```text
Socrates is mortal.
```

This is deductive reasoning.

---

### Example 2

```text
Every employee has an ID card.
Riya is an employee.
```

Therefore,

```text
Riya has an ID card.
```

Again, the conclusion must be true.

---

### Visual

```text
Humans
┌──────────────────────┐
│                      │
│   Socrates           │
│                      │
└──────────────────────┘

All Humans → Mortal

Therefore

Socrates → Mortal
```

# Inductive Reasoning

**Inductive reasoning** goes from **specific → general**.

You observe several examples and make a general rule.

The conclusion is **likely true**, but **not guaranteed**.

### Structure

```text
Observation
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
General Rule
```

### Example 1

You observe:

```text
Crow 1 is black.
Crow 2 is black.
Crow 3 is black.
Crow 4 is black.
```

You conclude:

```text
All crows are black.
```

This is induction.

But what if tomorrow you find a white crow?

Your conclusion was reasonable, but not certain.

---

### Example 2

You notice:

```text
Every placement interview I attended asked aptitude questions.
```

You conclude:

```text
Every company asks aptitude.
```

That's an inductive conclusion.

It's probably true based on your experience, but another company might not ask aptitude.

---

# The Biggest Difference

| Deductive Reasoning                          | Inductive Reasoning                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| General → Specific                           | Specific → General                 |
| Conclusion is certain (if premises are true) | Conclusion is probable             |
| Based on rules                               | Based on observations              |
| Used in logic questions                      | Used in predictions and hypotheses |

---

# Example Comparison

### Deductive

```text
All software engineers know programming.

Rahul is a software engineer.
```

Conclusion:

```text
Rahul knows programming.
```

✅ Must be true.

---

### Inductive

```text
Rahul knows programming.
Anita knows programming.
Riya knows programming.
```

Conclusion:

```text
All software engineers know programming.
```

❌ Not certain.

Maybe they learned programming for another reason.

# Easy Way to Remember

Imagine an upside-down triangle.

## Deduction

Starts wide and becomes narrow.

```text
All mammals

       ↓

Dogs

       ↓

My dog
```

General → Specific

---

## Induction

Starts narrow and becomes wide.

```text
My dog

Neighbor's dog

Friend's dog

       ↓

All dogs
```

Specific → General

# Which one is used in Aptitude?

For **Statement & Conclusion**, **Syllogism**, and most logical reasoning questions in placement exams, you'll mainly use **deductive reasoning**.

Why?

Because the question gives you some statements and asks:

> "What conclusion must logically follow?"

You're **not** asked to create a new rule from observations—that would be inductive reasoning.

---

# One Last Example

Imagine a teacher says:

```text
All students who score above 90 get an A grade.

Nihar scored 95.
```

### Deductive reasoning

```text
Nihar gets an A grade.
```

✅ Guaranteed.

---

Now imagine:

```text
Rahul got 95 and received an A.
Sneha got 93 and received an A.
Riya got 97 and received an A.
```

You conclude:

```text
Anyone scoring above 90 gets an A.
```

That's **inductive reasoning**—it's a pattern you've observed, but you don't know for certain until you see the official grading rule.

## A simple memory trick

* **Deduction = Descending** from a general rule to a specific case.
* **Induction = Increasing** from specific observations to a general rule.

When solving placement reasoning questions, keep asking yourself:

> **"Does this conclusion have to be true based only on the given statements?"**

If yes, you're using **deductive reasoning**, which is exactly what Statement & Conclusion questions are testing.
