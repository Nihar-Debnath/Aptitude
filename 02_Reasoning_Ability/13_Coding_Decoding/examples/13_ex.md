Yes! This one looks confusing at first, but there is a **very simple pattern** once we line the letters up.

We have:

**FIGURE → GKUGSG**

Write them position by position:

| Original |  F |  I |  G |  U |  R |  E |
| -------- | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| Code     |  G |  K |  U |  G |  S |  G |

Notice:

### Step 1: First 2 letters

* F → G = **+1**
* I → K = **+2**

### Step 2: Middle 2 letters

They are simply **swapped**:

* G U → **U G**

### Step 3: Last 2 letters

Again:

* R → S = **+1**
* E → G = **+2**

So the rule is:

**For a 6-letter word:**

> **1st +1, 2nd +2, swap 3rd & 4th, 5th +1, 6th +2**

Let's verify with **TRIPOD**:

**T R I P O D**

* T + 1 → **U**
* R + 2 → **T**
* I and P → **swap → P I**
* O + 1 → **P**
* D + 2 → **F**

Therefore:

**TRIPOD → U T P I P F**

which exactly matches **UTPIPF**. ✅

### Now solve LARGER

LARGER has 6 letters:

**L A R G E R**

Apply the same rule:

* L + 1 → **M**
* A + 2 → **C**
* R G → **G R** (swap)
* E + 1 → **F**
* R + 2 → **T**

So:

## **LARGER → MCGRFT** ✅

The key thing to notice was the **middle two letters are swapped**, not shifted.
