# 🤝 Partnership — Start From ZERO

Imagine you and your friend open a small shop.

You put **₹10,000** into the business.

Your friend puts **₹20,000**.

After some time, the business makes **₹30,000 profit**.

Now the question is:

> Who should get more profit?

Obviously, your friend.

Why?

Because your friend put in **more money**.

---

## Step 1: Understand the basic idea

You:

**₹10,000**

Friend:

**₹20,000**

Let's compare them:

\[
10,000:20,000
\]

Remove the zeros:

\[
1:2
\]

So your investment is:

**1 part**

Your friend's investment is:

**2 parts**

Therefore, the profit should also be divided:

\[
\boxed{1:2}
\]

---

# Now divide the ₹30,000 profit

The ratio is:

\[
1:2
\]

Total parts:

\[
1+2=3
\]

So divide ₹30,000 into **3 equal parts**:

\[
30,000\div3=10,000
\]

Therefore:

### You

\[
1\times10,000=\boxed{₹10,000}
\]

### Friend

\[
2\times10,000=\boxed{₹20,000}
\]

That's it.

### So the first rule is:

> **More investment → more profit.**

---

# 🟢 Now let's introduce TIME

This is the part that probably confused you earlier.

Suppose:

You invest **₹10,000 for 12 months**.

Your friend invests **₹10,000 for only 6 months**.

Who should get more profit?

Both put in the **same amount**.

But...

You kept your ₹10,000 in the business for **12 months**.

Your friend kept ₹10,000 for only **6 months**.

So you contributed for **twice as long**.

Therefore:

\[
12:6
\]

Simplify:

\[
\boxed{2:1}
\]

So you get **2 parts** of the profit.

Your friend gets **1 part**.

---

# 🧠 Think about it like this

Imagine the business is using your money.

You give it:

**₹10,000 × 12 months**

Your friend gives it:

**₹10,000 × 6 months**

You helped the business for longer.

So:

> **Money matters AND time matters.**

That's why we eventually use:

\[
\boxed{\text{Investment × Time}}
\]

But **don't memorize that yet**.

Just understand:

> **More money = more profit.**
> **More time = more profit.**

# 🤝 Partnership — Numerical Ability

## 1. What is Partnership?

When **two or more people invest money in a business and share the profit**, it is called a **partnership**.

For example:

* A invests ₹10,000
* B invests ₹20,000
* Business makes ₹30,000 profit

The question is: **How should they divide the ₹30,000?**

Since B invested twice as much as A, B should get twice as much profit.

So:

**A : B = 10,000 : 20,000 = 1 : 2**

Profit:

* A → ₹10,000
* B → ₹20,000

Total = ₹30,000

---

# ⭐ The MOST IMPORTANT FORMULA

For partnership:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Share} \propto \text{Investment} \times \text{Time}}
\]

Or simply:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio} = \text{Investment} \times \text{Time}}
\]

This is the **main concept** you need to remember.

### If everyone invests for the same time:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio} = \text{Investment Ratio}}
\]

### If investment AND time are different:

\[
\boxed{P_1:P_2 = I_1T_1:I_2T_2}
\]

Where:

* (P) = profit
* (I) = investment
* (T) = time

---

# 2. Why does TIME matter?

Imagine:

### A

Invests ₹10,000 for **12 months**

### B

Invests ₹10,000 for **6 months**

Both invested the same amount.

But A's money was used for twice as long.

Therefore:

\[
A:B = 10000\times12 : 10000\times6
\]

Cancel ₹10,000:

\[
=12:6
\]

\[
=\boxed{2:1}
\]

So A gets **twice the profit** of B.

---

# 3. The easiest way to solve Partnership questions

Whenever you see a partnership question, do this:

### Step 1 — Write Investment

### Step 2 — Write Time

### Step 3 — Multiply Investment × Time

### Step 4 — Make the ratio

### Step 5 — Divide the total profit according to that ratio

---

## Example 1 — Same time

A and B invest ₹20,000 and ₹30,000 respectively for one year.

Total profit = ₹25,000.

Find their individual profits.

### Step 1: Investment ratio

\[
A:B=20000:30000
\]

Cancel:

\[
A:B=2:3
\]

So profit ratio is:

\[
\boxed{2:3}
\]

Total parts:

\[
2+3=5
\]

One part:

\[
25000/5=5000
\]

Therefore:

**A's profit**

\[
2\times5000=\boxed{₹10,000}
\]

**B's profit**

\[
3\times5000=\boxed{₹15,000}
\]

Check:

₹10,000 + ₹15,000 = ₹25,000 ✅

---

# 4. Different investment + different time

This is where Partnership becomes slightly more interesting.

### Example 2

A invests ₹10,000 for 12 months.

B invests ₹20,000 for 6 months.

Find their profit ratio.

Don't compare just:

\[
10000:20000
\]

because their time is different.

Instead:

\[
A:B=(10000\times12):(20000\times6)
\]

Calculate:

\[
120000:120000
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{A:B=1:1}
\]

Even though B invested **twice the money**, A kept his money invested **twice as long**.

So they get **equal profit**.

---

# 🧠 The concept you should remember

Think of it like this:

> **Money × Time = Contribution to business**

So:

**₹10,000 for 12 months**

is equivalent to

**₹20,000 for 6 months**

because:

\[
10000\times12=20000\times6
\]

Therefore, both contributed equally.

---

# 5. There are mainly 2 types of partners

You'll commonly see:

### 🟢 Simple Partnership

Everyone invests for the **same amount of time**.

Then:

\[
\boxed{Profit\ Ratio=Investment\ Ratio}
\]

### 🟡 Compound Partnership

Investment periods are **different**.

Then:

\[
\boxed{Profit\ Ratio=Investment\times Time}
\]

Don't let the word **compound** confuse you here. It doesn't mean compound interest.

---

# 🔥 One shortcut you'll use A LOT

Suppose:

A invests ₹5,000 for 12 months.

B invests ₹8,000 for 6 months.

Instead of doing the full multiplication:

\[
5000\times12 : 8000\times6
\]

You can cancel first:

\[
5\times12 : 8\times6
\]

\[
60:48
\]

\[
\boxed{5:4}
\]

That's much faster in an exam.

## Your Partnership Cheat Sheet

| Situation                            | Formula                    |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Same investment, same time           | Equal profit               |
| Different investment, same time      | Profit ∝ Investment        |
| Same investment, different time      | Profit ∝ Time              |
| Different investment, different time | Profit ∝ Investment × Time |

### Golden rule:

\[
\boxed{\textbf{PROFIT RATIO = CAPITAL × TIME}}
\]

That's the **one formula** I want you to master first.
