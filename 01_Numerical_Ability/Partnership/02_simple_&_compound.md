Absolutely. Let's slow it down and make **Simple Partnership vs Compound Partnership** crystal clear. The word **"compound"** is probably what is making this confusing.

The key is: **we are NOT talking about compound interest here.**

# 🤝 Partnership

Partnership simply means:

> **Two or more people put money into a business and share the profit.**

The amount of profit each person gets depends on **two things**:

1. 💰 How much money they put in
2. ⏰ How long their money stays in the business

---

# 1️⃣ SIMPLE PARTNERSHIP

In a **simple partnership**, all partners keep their money invested for the **same amount of time**.

So **time doesn't need to be considered**.

### Example

A invests ₹10,000 for **1 year**.

B invests ₹20,000 for **1 year**.

Both are in the business for exactly the same time.

So we only compare the money:

[
A:B=10,000:20,000
]

Cancel the zeros:

[
\boxed{1:2}
]

Therefore:

> A gets 1 part of the profit.
> B gets 2 parts of the profit.

### That's simple partnership.

---

## 🧠 Why don't we care about time?

Because both stayed for the **same amount of time**.

Imagine the business operates for 12 months:

| Partner |   Money |      Time |
| ------- | ------: | --------: |
| A       | ₹10,000 | 12 months |
| B       | ₹20,000 | 12 months |

Both are there for 12 months.

So the only difference is:

**A → ₹10,000**

**B → ₹20,000**

Therefore B deserves twice the profit.

### Simple Partnership:

[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment Ratio}}
]

---

# 2️⃣ COMPOUND PARTNERSHIP

Now things change.

In **compound partnership**, the partners DON'T necessarily invest for the same amount of time.

So now we have to consider:

**Money + Time**

### Example

A invests ₹10,000 for **12 months**.

B invests ₹20,000 for **6 months**.

Look carefully.

A:

**₹10,000 for 12 months**

B:

**₹20,000 for 6 months**

Now we can't simply say:

[
10,000:20,000=1:2
]

because B wasn't in the business for the same amount of time.

---

# Let's understand it logically

Think of each ₹1 as contributing to the business every month.

### A

A gives:

₹10,000

for:

12 months

So A's contribution is:

[
10,000\times12=120,000
]

### B

B gives:

₹20,000

for:

6 months

So B's contribution is:

[
20,000\times6=120,000
]

Look!

Both contributed:

**120,000**

So their profit should be equal.

Therefore:

[
\boxed{A:B=1:1}
]

---

# 🔥 This is the BIG difference

## Simple Partnership

Everyone stays for the **same time**.

So:

> Just compare money.

[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment Ratio}}
]

---

## Compound Partnership

People stay for **different amounts of time**.

So:

> Compare money AND time.

[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment × Time}}
]

---

# Let's compare them side-by-side

### SIMPLE

A → ₹10,000 → 12 months

B → ₹20,000 → 12 months

Since time is same:

[
10,000:20,000
]

[
\boxed{1:2}
]

---

### COMPOUND

A → ₹10,000 → 12 months

B → ₹20,000 → 6 months

Now:

[
10,000\times12 : 20,000\times6
]

[
120,000:120,000
]

[
\boxed{1:1}
]

---

# 🚨 Very important

Don't think:

> "Simple partnership = easy question"

and

> "Compound partnership = difficult question."

That's **not what simple and compound mean**.

They refer to **whether the investment period is the same or different**.

### Remember:

**Same time → Simple Partnership**

**Different time → Compound Partnership**

---

# 🎯 Let's do another example

A invests ₹5,000 for 12 months.

B invests ₹10,000 for 12 months.

### What type?

Both are there for **12 months**.

So:

[
\boxed{\text{Simple Partnership}}
]

Profit ratio:

[
5,000:10,000
]

[
\boxed{1:2}
]

---

Now change just one thing:

A invests ₹5,000 for 12 months.

B invests ₹10,000 for **6 months**.

Now the time is different.

So:

[
\boxed{\text{Compound Partnership}}
]

Calculate:

[
5,000\times12 : 10,000\times6
]

[
60,000:60,000
]

[
\boxed{1:1}
]

---

# 🧠 The easiest memory trick

Whenever you see a partnership question, first ask:

### **"Is the time same?"**

**YES → Simple**

Just compare investment.

**NO → Compound**

Compare:

[
\boxed{\text{Investment × Time}}
]

That's the entire foundation.

---

### One more thing

In actual aptitude exams, you'll often see questions like:

> A and B start a business...

> A joins later...

> B leaves after 4 months...

> A invests ₹20,000 and B invests ₹30,000...

These are usually **compound partnership** questions because the time periods differ.

And they're actually very mechanical once you learn the **Investment × Time** method.
