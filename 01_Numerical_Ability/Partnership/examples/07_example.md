This is a **very important type** of partnership question because here we know the **profit ratio** and one person's investment, and we have to find the **other person's investment**.

Let's do it slowly.

## Given

* A starts with **₹3,500**
* B joins **after 5 months**
* Business runs for **1 year = 12 months**
* Profit ratio after 1 year:

\[
A:B=2:3
\]

* Find B's investment.

---

# Step 1: Find how long each person's money was in the business

### A

A started from the beginning.

So A's money was invested for:

\[
\boxed{12\text{ months}}
\]

### B

B joined after 5 months.

The business lasted 12 months.

So B's money was invested for:

\[
12-5=\boxed{7\text{ months}}
\]

This is the first important step.

---

# Step 2: Use our main rule

We know:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

So:

### A's contribution

\[
3500\times12
\]

\[
=\boxed{42000}
\]

### B's contribution

We don't know B's investment.

Let's call it **x**.

B's contribution:

\[
x\times7=7x
\]

---

# Step 3: Use the profit ratio

The question says:

\[
A:B=2:3
\]

But remember, profit ratio = contribution ratio.

So:

\[
42000:7x=2:3
\]

Now we need to find (x).

---

# Step 4: Cross multiply

We have:

\[
\frac{42000}{7x}=\frac{2}{3}
\]

Cross multiply:

\[
42000\times3=7x\times2
\]

\[
126000=14x
\]

Therefore:

\[
x=\frac{126000}{14}
\]

\[
\boxed{x=9000}
\]

# ✅ Answer

\[
\boxed{\text{B's investment = ₹9,000}}
\]

So the correct option is:

### **D. ₹9,000** ✅

---

# 🧠 Understand the logic, not just the formula

Think of it this way:

### A

₹3,500 × 12 months

\[
=42,000
\]

### B

₹9,000 × 7 months

\[
=63,000
\]

So their contributions are:

\[
42,000:63,000
\]

Divide by 21,000:

\[
\boxed{2:3}
\]

Exactly the profit ratio given in the question. ✅

---

## 🔥 Your shortcut for this type

When the question says:

> **A's investment is given + A/B join at different times + profit ratio is given + find B's investment**

Do:

**1. Find their investment periods**

**2. Calculate known person's Investment × Time**

**3. Assume unknown investment = (x)**

**4. Set up the profit ratio**

\[
\boxed{I_1T_1:I_2T_2=P_1:P_2}
\]

**5. Solve for (x)**

Here:

\[
3500\times12 : x\times7=2:3
\]

and immediately:

\[
\boxed{x=9000}
\]

This one is **Compound Partnership**. ✅

Why?

Because **A and B are invested for different amounts of time**.

* A → ₹3,500 for **12 months**
* B → unknown amount for **7 months**

Since the **time is different**, we use the compound partnership method:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

### Remember this rule:

| Situation              | Type                     | What to use       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Same time for everyone | **Simple Partnership**   | Investment ratio  |
| Different time         | **Compound Partnership** | Investment × Time |

So this question:

\[
\boxed{\text{Compound Partnership}}
\]

And that's why we did:

\[
3500\times12 : x\times7 = 2:3
\]

One small correction to keep your notes clean: **"compound partnership" here is just the aptitude term for different investment periods; it has nothing to do with compound interest.**
