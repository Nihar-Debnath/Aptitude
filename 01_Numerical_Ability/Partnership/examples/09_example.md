Absolutely. This is the same **Compound Partnership** pattern we've just learned: **investment × time**. Let's do it step by step.

## Given

* A invests ₹20,000
* B invests ₹15,000
* C joins after 6 months with ₹20,000
* Total business duration = **2 years**
* Total profit = **₹25,000**
* Find **B's share**

---

## Step 1: Find everyone's investment time

Business runs for:

\[
2\text{ years}=24\text{ months}
\]

### A

A starts at the beginning:

\[
\boxed{24\text{ months}}
\]

### B

B also starts at the beginning:

\[
\boxed{24\text{ months}}
\]

### C

C joins after 6 months:

\[
24-6=18
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{18\text{ months}}
\]

---

## Step 2: Investment × Time

Remember:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

### A

\[
20000\times24=480000
\]

### B

\[
15000\times24=360000
\]

### C

\[
20000\times18=360000
\]

Therefore:

\[
A:B:C=480000:360000:360000
\]

Remove the zeros:

\[
48:36:36
\]

Divide by 12:

\[
\boxed{4:3:3}
\]

So B's profit share is **3 parts** out of:

\[
4+3+3=10
\]

---

## Step 3: Find B's actual profit

Total profit:

\[
₹25,000
\]

B gets:

\[
\frac{3}{10}\times25000
\]

\[
=3\times2500
\]

\[
=\boxed{₹7,500}
\]

# ✅ Final Answer

\[
\boxed{\text{B's share = ₹7,500}}
\]

**Answer: A. ₹7,500** ✅

---

### 🧠 Notice something interesting

B and C have **exactly the same contribution**:

B:

\[
15000\times24=360000
\]

C:

\[
20000\times18=360000
\]

So they get **equal profit**.

That's why the final ratio is:

\[
\boxed{4:3:3}
\]

This is a great example of why we cannot simply compare investments. C invested more than B, but C stayed for less time.
