Yep — this one is the **reverse** of what we just did.

Previously, we knew the **investment + time** and found the **profit ratio**.

Here, they give us the **profit ratio** and the **time**, and ask us to find the **investment ratio**.

---

# Given

Profit ratio:

\[
\boxed{5:7:8}
\]

Time:

* A → 14 months
* B → 8 months
* C → 7 months

We need:

\[
\boxed{\text{Investment ratio}}
\]

---

# Step 1: Remember the main rule

We learned:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Investment}=\frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{Time}}}
\]

So here we have to **divide the profit ratio by the time**.

---

# Step 2: Do it for each partner

### A

Profit = 5

Time = 14

So:

\[
A\text{'s investment}=\frac{5}{14}
\]

### B

Profit = 7

Time = 8

\[
B\text{'s investment}=\frac{7}{8}
\]

### C

Profit = 8

Time = 7

\[
C\text{'s investment}=\frac{8}{7}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{Investment ratio}
=

\frac5{14}:\frac78:\frac87
\]

This looks ugly, so let's simplify it.

---

# Step 3: Remove the fractions

We have:

\[
\frac5{14}:\frac78:\frac87
\]

The denominators are:

\[
14,\ 8,\ 7
\]

We can multiply **all three** by 56.

Why 56?

Because 56 is divisible by 14, 8 and 7.

So:

\[
\frac5{14}\times56=20
\]

\[
\frac78\times56=49
\]

\[
\frac87\times56=64
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{20:49:64}
\]

# ✅ Final Answer

\[
\boxed{20:49:64}
\]

So the correct option is:

### **B. 20 : 49 : 64** ✅

---

# 🧠 But here's the important concept

There are **two directions** you need to know.

### When investment and time are given:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

Example:

\[
10,000\times6
\]

---

### When profit and time are given:

We reverse it:

\[
\boxed{\text{Investment Ratio}=\frac{\text{Profit Ratio}}{\text{Time}}}
\]

That's exactly what happened here:

\[
5:7:8
\]

divided by

\[
14:8:7
\]

giving:

\[
\boxed{20:49:64}
\]

### 🔥 Easy way to remember

**Going FORWARD:**

**Investment × Time → Profit**

**Going BACKWARD:**

**Profit ÷ Time → Investment**

Ahh, you're asking **how I found 56**. Good question — you shouldn't just magically pick 56. 😄

We had:

\[
\frac5{14}:\frac78:\frac87
\]

The denominators are:

\[
14,\ 8,\ 7
\]

We need a number that **all three denominators can divide exactly**.

That's called the **LCM (Least Common Multiple)**.

### Find LCM of 14, 8, 7

Let's list multiples:

**14:**

\[
14,\ 28,\ 42,\ \boxed{56},\ 70...
\]

**8:**

\[
8,\ 16,\ 24,\ 32,\ 40,\ 48,\ \boxed{56}...
\]

**7:**

\[
7,\ 14,\ 21,\ 28,\ 35,\ 42,\ 49,\ \boxed{56}...
\]

The **first number appearing in all three lists is 56**.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{LCM(14,8,7)=56}
\]

That's why we multiply the whole ratio by 56:

\[
\frac5{14}\times56=20
\]

\[
\frac78\times56=49
\]

\[
\frac87\times56=64
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{20:49:64}
\]

### 🔥 For your aptitude preparation

Whenever you get something like:

\[
\frac{a}{x}:\frac{b}{y}:\frac{c}{z}
\]

**Find the LCM of the denominators** (x,y,z), then multiply every term by that LCM.

Here:

\[
14,8,7 \rightarrow \boxed{56}
\]

That's the trick.
