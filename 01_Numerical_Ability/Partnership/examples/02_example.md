Yes — this is a **compound partnership** question, but there is one extra step: **the partners join at different times**.

Let's go slowly. This type is very important for exams.

## Given

* **Aman** starts with ₹70,000
* **Rakhi** joins **after 6 months** with ₹1,05,000
* **Sagar** joins **another 6 months later** with ₹1,40,000
* Business runs for **3 years**
* Find profit ratio:

\[
Aman:Rakhi:Sagar
\]

---

# Step 1: The most important thing — find how long each person's money was invested

The business runs for **3 years**.

Convert 3 years to months:

\[
3\times12=36\text{ months}
\]

So the business runs for **36 months**.

Now look at when everyone joins.

### Aman

Aman starts at the beginning.

So his money stays for the **entire 36 months**.

\[
\boxed{36\text{ months}}
\]

---

### Rakhi

Rakhi joins **after 6 months**.

So she doesn't get 36 months.

She gets:

\[
36-6=30\text{ months}
\]

\[
\boxed{30\text{ months}}
\]

---

### Sagar

Sagar joins **another 6 months later**.

Rakhi joined at month 6.

Sagar joins at:

\[
6+6=12\text{ months}
\]

So Sagar's money stays for:

\[
36-12=24\text{ months}
\]

\[
\boxed{24\text{ months}}
\]

---

# Step 2: Make our table

| Partner | Investment |      Time |
| ------- | ---------: | --------: |
| Aman    |    ₹70,000 | 36 months |
| Rakhi   |  ₹1,05,000 | 30 months |
| Sagar   |  ₹1,40,000 | 24 months |

Now we use:

\[
\boxed{\text{Profit Ratio}=\text{Investment}\times\text{Time}}
\]

---

# Step 3: Calculate Investment × Time

### Aman

\[
70,000\times36
\]

\[
=2,520,000
\]

### Rakhi

\[
1,05,000\times30
\]

\[
=3,150,000
\]

### Sagar

\[
1,40,000\times24
\]

\[
=3,360,000
\]

So:

\[
Aman:Rakhi:Sagar
\]

\[
=2,520,000:3,150,000:3,360,000
\]

---

# Step 4: Simplify the ratio

We can remove the common zeros:

\[
252:315:336
\]

Now divide by **21**:

\[
252\div21=12
\]

\[
315\div21=15
\]

\[
336\div21=16
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{12:15:16}
\]

# 🎯 Final Answer

\[
\boxed{\text{Aman : Rakhi : Sagar = 12 : 15 : 16}}
\]

---

# 🧠 But understand WHY we did this

This is the important part.

Don't just memorize the calculation.

Think:

### Aman

₹70,000 stays for **36 months**

### Rakhi

₹1,05,000 stays for **30 months**

### Sagar

₹1,40,000 stays for **24 months**

So we're basically asking:

> **How much money × how long did each person contribute to the business?**

That's why:

\[
\boxed{Investment\times Time}
\]

determines their profit share.

---

## 🔥 The shortcut for "joins later" questions

Whenever you see:

> A starts business
> B joins after 6 months
> C joins after another 6 months
> Business runs for 3 years

Do this first:

**Total business time = 36 months**

Then:

**A → 36 months**

**B → 36 − 6 = 30 months**

**C → 36 − 12 = 24 months**

Then:

\[
\boxed{Investment\times Time}
\]

for each person.

### So your exam process becomes:

**1. Convert everything to months**

↓

**2. Find each person's actual investment time**

↓

**3. Investment × Time**

↓

**4. Make the ratio**

That's it. ❤️

😂 Yeah, I **jumped straight to 21 without explaining where it came from.** That's my mistake.

We had:

\[
252:315:336
\]

We need to simplify this ratio. That means we need to find a number that **divides all three numbers**.

### Let's find it properly.

Look at:

* 252
* 315
* 336

We can divide all three by **3**:

\[
252\div3=84
\]

\[
315\div3=105
\]

\[
336\div3=112
\]

So:

\[
252:315:336 = 84:105:112
\]

Again, all three are divisible by **7**:

\[
84\div7=12
\]

\[
105\div7=15
\]

\[
112\div7=16
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{12:15:16}
\]

### Where did 21 come from?

Because:

\[
3\times7=21
\]

So dividing directly by 21:

\[
252\div21=12
\]

\[
315\div21=15
\]

\[
336\div21=16
\]

But **you absolutely don't need to magically know 21**.

For your level right now, I recommend doing:

\[
252:315:336
\]

**÷3 first**

\[
84:105:112
\]

**÷7**

\[
\boxed{12:15:16}
\]

That's much easier to understand.

> **The rule:** To simplify a ratio, find a number that divides **every number** in the ratio. You can divide step-by-step; you don't have to find the biggest number immediately.
