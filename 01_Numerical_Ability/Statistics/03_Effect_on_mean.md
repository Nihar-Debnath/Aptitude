Actually, **"effect on mean" is not a separate chapter or formula.** It's just a **shortcut** that comes from how the mean works.

Let's derive it together instead of memorizing it.

---

## Start with the definition of Mean

Suppose we have 5 numbers:

```text
2, 4, 6, 8, 10
```

Mean =

```text
(2+4+6+8+10)/5

=30/5

=6
```

---

## Now add 3 to every number

The new numbers become

```text
5, 7, 9, 11, 13
```

Let's calculate the mean again.

```text
(5+7+9+11+13)/5

=45/5

=9
```

Notice:

```text
Old Mean = 6

New Mean = 9
```

The mean also increased by **3**.

---

## Let's see why mathematically

Original numbers:

```text
a, b, c, d
```

Mean is

```text
(a+b+c+d)/4
```

Now add **5** to every number.

New numbers:

```text
a+5
b+5
c+5
d+5
```

New Mean:

```text
[(a+5)+(b+5)+(c+5)+(d+5)]/4
```

Open the brackets.

```text
(a+b+c+d+20)/4
```

Split the fraction.

```text
(a+b+c+d)/4 + 20/4
```

```text
Old Mean + 5
```

🎉 That's where the rule comes from.

It isn't magic.

It comes directly from the mean formula.

---

## Same for subtraction

Suppose you subtract 10 from every value.

New Mean

```text
[(a−10)+(b−10)+(c−10)+(d−10)]/4
```

```text
(a+b+c+d−40)/4
```

```text
(a+b+c+d)/4 − 40/4
```

```text
Old Mean − 10
```

---

## Same for multiplication

Original:

```text
Mean=(a+b+c)/3
```

Multiply every value by 2.

New Mean

```text
(2a+2b+2c)/3
```

Take out 2.

```text
2(a+b+c)/3
```

```text
2 × Old Mean
```

---

## Same for division

Divide every value by 4.

```text
(a/4+b/4+c/4)/3
```

Take out 1/4.

```text
(1/4)(a+b+c)/3
```

```text
Old Mean / 4
```

---

# So the "Effect on Mean" table is **not a formula to memorize**.

It is simply a consequence of the definition of mean.

| If every value is... | Then the mean...   |
| -------------------- | ------------------ |
| + k                  | increases by k     |
| − k                  | decreases by k     |
| × k                  | is multiplied by k |
| ÷ k                  | is divided by k    |

Once you understand **why** this happens, you don't have to memorize the table—it becomes obvious.

I think you'll enjoy the next topic too, because there are similar shortcuts for **effect on median** and **effect on standard deviation**, and they follow the same logic.
