# 1. What is a Sequence?

A **sequence** is simply a list of numbers arranged in a particular order according to some rule.

For example:

**2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ...**

There is a pattern:

> Add 2 each time.

So the next number is:

**12**

Another example:

**3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ...**

Pattern:

> Multiply by 2.

Next:

**96**

That's basically what a sequence question asks you to discover.

---

# 2. What is a Series?

In aptitude terminology, you'll often hear **sequence and series** together.

A sequence is the **ordered list of terms**:

> 2, 4, 6, 8, 10

A series technically refers to the **sum** of those terms:

> 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10

But in placement exams, people commonly use "number series" to mean questions where you have to identify the pattern.

So don't get stuck on the terminology.

For TCS, think:

> **Sequence/Series = Find the pattern.**

# 4. The MOST IMPORTANT skill

When you see a series, **don't randomly stare at the numbers.**

Follow a fixed pattern-checking order.

Suppose you get:

> 4, 9, 14, 19, 24, ?

First check:

### Step 1: Difference

9 − 4 = 5
14 − 9 = 5
19 − 14 = 5
24 − 19 = 5

Constant difference.

Therefore:

24 + 5 = **29**

---

If difference doesn't work, check:

### Step 2: Multiplication/division

Example:

> 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ?

×2 each time.

Answer:

**96**

---

If that doesn't work:

### Step 3: Squares/cubes

Example:

> 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?

These are:

1², 2², 3², 4², 5²

Next:

6² = **36**

---

Then:

### Step 4: Alternating patterns

Example:

> 2, 5, 4, 10, 6, 15, ?

Look at odd and even positions separately.

Odd positions:

2, 4, 6, ?

Even positions:

5, 10, 15

So next odd term:

**8**

This is a VERY important technique.

# 26. Arithmetic Progression — AP

Now we come to the actual **Progressions** part.

AP = **Arithmetic Progression**

An AP is a sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

Example:

> 5, 8, 11, 14, 17...

Difference:

**+3**

So this is an AP.

The common difference is represented by:

**d**

Here:

**d = 3**

---

# 27. AP formula: nth term

The most important formula:

\[
a_n = a + (n-1)d
\]

Where:

* **a** = first term
* **d** = common difference
* **n** = position
* **aₙ** = nth term

Example:

> Find the 10th term of 3, 7, 11, 15...

Here:

a = 3

d = 4

n = 10

Therefore:

\[
a_{10}=3+(10-1)4
\]

\[
=3+36
\]

\[
=\boxed{39}
\]

---

# 28. AP Sum

Sometimes they ask:

> Find the sum of the first n terms.

Formula:

\[
S_n=\frac n2[2a+(n-1)d]
\]

Or:

\[
S_n=\frac n2(a+l)
\]

where **l = last term**.

Example:

Find the sum:

> 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10

Here:

n = 5

a = 2

l = 10

Therefore:

\[
S_5=\frac52(2+10)
\]

\[
=30
\]

Answer:

**30**

---

# 29. Geometric Progression — GP

GP is another important progression.

In GP, every term is multiplied/divided by the same number.

Example:

> 2, 6, 18, 54, 162...

Each term ×3.

So:

**common ratio r = 3**

---

# 30. GP nth term

Formula:

\[
a_n=ar^{n-1}
\]

Example:

> Find the 5th term of 2, 6, 18, 54...

a = 2

r = 3

n = 5

\[
a_5=2(3)^{4}
\]

\[
=2(81)
\]

\[
=\boxed{162}
\]

---

# 31. GP Sum

Formula:

\[
S_n=a\frac{r^n-1}{r-1}
\]

when (r>1).

Example:

> 2 + 6 + 18 + 54

Here:

a = 2

r = 3

n = 4

\[
S_4=2\frac{3^4-1}{3-1}
\]

\[
=2\frac{81-1}{2}
\]

\[
=80
\]

---

---

### GP

# 33. Harmonic Progression — HP

For **TCS Ignite**, don't spend much time here.

HP means the **reciprocals** form an AP.

Example:

> 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4...

Take reciprocals:

> 1, 2, 3, 4...

That's an AP.

So the original sequence is HP.

**Priority: very low for your TCS preparation.**


## Harmonic Progression (HP) — Formula Sheet

If

\[
a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots
\]

are in HP, then their reciprocals

\[
\frac1{a_1},\frac1{a_2},\frac1{a_3},\ldots
\]

are in AP.

### 1. General HP nth term

If the reciprocals form an AP with:

* first term = (a)
* common difference = (d)

then:

\[
\boxed{H_n=\frac{1}{a+(n-1)d}}
\]

That's the **nth term of HP**.

# 34. Special series you SHOULD know

These are useful because they can appear as hidden patterns.

### Sum of first n natural numbers

\[
1+2+3+\cdots+n
\]

Formula:

\[
\frac{n(n+1)}2
\]

Example:

1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 10

\[
=\frac{10(11)}2
\]

= **55**

---

### Sum of squares

\[
1^2+2^2+3^2+\cdots+n^2
\]

Formula:

\[
\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6
\]

---

### Sum of cubes

\[
1^3+2^3+3^3+\cdots+n^3
\]

Formula:

\[
\left[\frac{n(n+1)}2\right]^2
\]

These are useful, but **not your first priority**.

# 35. The most important TCS trick: Difference Table

Whenever you see a weird sequence, immediately calculate differences.

Example:

> 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, ?

Difference:

5, 7, 9, 11

Differences aren't constant.

So look at the **second difference**:

7 − 5 = 2

9 − 7 = 2

11 − 9 = 2

Therefore next difference = 13.

36 + 13 = **49**

First differences:

7

11

15

19

These increase by 4.

Next:

23

Therefore:

57 + 23 = **80**

Answer:

**80**

# 37. How to attack ANY TCS series question

This is the part I want you to memorize.

When you see:

> 7, 14, 29, 60, ?

Don't panic.

Run this checklist:

### CHECK 1 — Difference

14 − 7 = 7

29 − 14 = 15

60 − 29 = 31

Not constant.

---

### CHECK 2 — Ratio

14 ÷ 7 = 2

29 ÷ 14 ≈ 2.07

60 ÷ 29 ≈ 2.07

Not obvious.

---

### CHECK 3 — Multiplication + addition

7 × 2 + 0 = 14

14 × 2 + 1 = 29

29 × 2 + 2 = 60

So:

60 × 2 + 3

= **123**

That's the pattern.
