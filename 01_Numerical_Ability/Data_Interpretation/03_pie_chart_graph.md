A **Pie Chart** in **Data Interpretation (DI)** is a **circle divided into slices**, where each slice represents a **part of the whole**.

Think of it like a **pizza** 🍕:

* The whole pizza = **100%** (or **360°**)
* Each slice = one category's share.

Here's a simple example:

![](./Monthly%20expenses.png)

The total expense is **₹50,000**.

From the chart:

* Rent = ₹20,000
* Food = ₹10,000
* Transport = ₹5,000
* Entertainment = ₹5,000
* Savings = ₹10,000

---

## Two ways pie charts are given in DI

### 1. Percentages (most common)

| Category | Percentage |
| -------- | ---------: |
| A        |        40% |
| B        |        25% |
| C        |        20% |
| D        |        15% |

If the total is **₹2,000**, then:

* A = 40% of 2000 = **₹800**
* B = **₹500**
* C = **₹400**
* D = **₹300**

---

### 2. Angles

Sometimes the chart gives angles instead of percentages.

Remember these important formulas:

* Total circle = **360°**
* Total percentage = **100%**

So,

[
\text{Value}=\frac{\text{Angle}}{360}\times\text{Total}
]

or

[
\text{Value}=\frac{\text{Percentage}}{100}\times\text{Total}
]

---

## Common angle values to memorize

| Angle | Percentage |
| ----: | ---------: |
|   36° |        10% |
|   72° |        20% |
|   90° |        25% |
|  108° |        30% |
|  120° |       33⅓% |
|  144° |        40% |
|  180° |        50% |
|  216° |        60% |
|  270° |        75% |
|  360° |       100% |

These values appear very frequently in placement exams.

---

## Types of questions asked

Suppose the total number of students is **600**.

The pie chart shows:

* Engineering = 40%
* Commerce = 30%
* Science = 20%
* Arts = 10%

Questions may be:

1. **How many Engineering students are there?**

   * 40% of 600 = **240**

2. **How many more Engineering students than Arts students?**

   * 240 − 60 = **180**

3. **What is the ratio of Commerce to Science?**

   * 30 : 20 = **3 : 2**

4. **What percentage is not in Engineering?**

   * 100% − 40% = **60%**

---

## Shortcut tips

* Always note the **total value** first.
* If percentages are given, convert them directly into values.
* If angles are given, convert the angle into a percentage or use the angle formula.
* Memorize common angle-to-percentage conversions to avoid repeated calculations.

---

### Placement exam tip

For companies like **TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Accenture, and Capgemini**, pie chart questions are usually straightforward. The challenge is not the chart itself but doing **percentage, ratio, and average calculations quickly**.

After you learn **Table DI** and **Pie Chart DI**, the next easiest topic is **Bar Graph DI**, followed by **Line Graph DI** and finally **Mixed DI** (two graphs combined).
