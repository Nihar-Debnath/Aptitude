A **Bar Graph** in **Data Interpretation (DI)** uses **rectangular bars** to compare values of different categories.

The **taller the bar, the larger the value**.

For example, suppose a company sold the following number of laptops:

![](./Laptop%20sales%20by%20company.png)

From this graph:

* Company A sold **120** laptops.
* Company B sold **160** laptops.
* Company C sold **90** laptops.
* Company D sold **200** laptops.

---

# Types of questions asked

### 1. Highest value

**Question:** Which company sold the most laptops?

**Answer:** Company D (200)

---

### 2. Lowest value

**Question:** Which company sold the fewest laptops?

**Answer:** Company C (90)

---

### 3. Difference

**Question:** By how many laptops did Company D outsell Company B?

[
200-160=40
]

**Answer:** **40**

---

### 4. Total

**Question:** Total laptops sold by all companies?

[
120+160+90+200=570
]

**Answer:** **570**

---

### 5. Average

**Question:** Average sales?

[
\frac{570}{4}=142.5
]

---

### 6. Percentage

**Question:** What percentage of the total sales came from Company A?

[
\frac{120}{570}\times100\approx21.05%
]

---

# Types of bar graphs in DI

### 1. Simple Bar Graph ✅

One bar for each category.

Example:

* Sales of Company A
* Sales of Company B
* Sales of Company C

---

### 2. Double (Grouped) Bar Graph

Two bars for each category.

Example:

| Company | 2024 |   2025 |
| ------- | ---: | -----: |
| A       |  ███ |  █████ |
| B       | ████ | ██████ |

Questions compare **2024 vs 2025**.

---

### 3. Multiple Bar Graph

Three or more bars for each category.

Example:

* Sales of Product A
* Product B
* Product C

for every year.

---

### 4. Stacked Bar Graph (less common)

A single bar is divided into parts, showing the contribution of each category to the total.

---

# Shortcut tips

### 1. Read the scale first

Always check the y-axis.

For example:

* 1 unit = 10
* 1 unit = 1000
* 1 unit = ₹50 lakh

Many students lose marks because they forget the scale.

---

### 2. Read the question first

If the question asks only about Company C, don't waste time reading all the bars.

---

### 3. Estimate when possible

If the options are:

* 395
* 420
* 610
* 850

You usually don't need exact calculations.

---

# Bar Graph vs Table

| Table DI                     | Bar Graph DI                          |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Values are written directly  | Values are represented by bar heights |
| Easier to read exact numbers | Easier to compare categories visually |
| More calculations            | More comparisons                      |

---

# Placement exam tip

In companies like **TCS, Cognizant, Accenture, Wipro, Capgemini**, the most common questions on bar graphs are:

* Highest/lowest value
* Difference
* Total
* Average
* Percentage
* Ratio
* Increase or decrease (if two years are shown)

These questions mainly test your calculation speed rather than advanced mathematics.

---

## DI roadmap (easy → difficult)

1. ✅ Table DI
2. ✅ Pie Chart DI
3. ✅ Bar Graph DI
4. ⏳ Line Graph DI
5. ⏳ Mixed DI (Bar + Pie, Table + Line, etc.)
6. ⏳ Caselet DI (data given in paragraphs)

You're now through the first three and can solve a large portion of placement-company DI questions. The next topic to learn is **Line Graph DI**, which introduces trends over time.
