# Data Interpretation (DI)

This is one of the **highest-scoring** topics in placement exams because it doesn't require advanced mathematics. It mainly tests:

* Basic arithmetic
* Speed
* Accuracy
* Observation

Think of DI like this:

> **The examiner gives you data instead of numbers, and your job is to interpret (understand) that data to answer questions.**

---

# What is Data Interpretation?

Instead of asking

> "What is 25% of 400?"

the question gives you a table or graph like this:

| Company | Sales (₹ lakh) |
| ------- | -------------: |
| A       |            400 |
| B       |            500 |
| C       |            300 |

Then asks:

* What is 25% of Company A's sales?
* Which company had the highest sales?
* What is the average sales?
* By what percentage are Company B's sales greater than Company C's?

So **DI = Data + Arithmetic**.

---

# What skills do you need?

You don't learn many new formulas.

You mainly use:

* Percentages ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
* Ratio & Proportion ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* Average ⭐⭐⭐⭐
* Profit & Loss (sometimes)
* Simple calculations
* Comparison

That's why we study Arithmetic before DI.

---

# Types of Data Interpretation

There are mainly **6 types**.

---

## 1. Table DI ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The most common type.

Example

| Year | Sales |
| ---- | ----: |
| 2022 |   400 |
| 2023 |   450 |
| 2024 |   600 |

Questions:

* Highest sales?
* Average sales?
* Percentage increase?
* Difference?

---

## 2. Bar Graph ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Example

```text
Sales

700 |      █
600 |      █
500 | █    █
400 | █ █  █
300 | █ █  █
      A B C
```

Questions are similar to table DI.

---

## 3. Pie Chart ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Represents percentages of a whole.

Example

```text
Marketing → 40%

HR → 20%

IT → 30%

Finance →10%
```

Questions:

* Which department spends the most?
* If total budget is ₹10 lakh, how much for IT?

---

## 4. Line Graph ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Shows change over time.

Example

```text
Sales

700
600        ●

500    ●

400 ●

    2022 2023 2024
```

Questions:

* Growth?
* Trend?
* Highest increase?

---

## 5. Mixed Graph ⭐⭐⭐

Combination of

* Table + Pie
* Bar + Line
* Pie + Table

Common in tougher exams.

---

## 6. Caselet DI ⭐⭐⭐

No graph.

Just a paragraph.

Example

> A company has 500 employees...

Then the data is hidden inside the paragraph.

# What questions are asked?

Suppose

| Product | Sales |
| ------- | ----: |
| A       |   200 |
| B       |   300 |
| C       |   500 |

Possible questions

### Percentage

What percentage of total sales is Product A?

---

### Average

Average sales?

---

### Ratio

Sales of A : C?

---

### Difference

How much more than B?

---

### Profit

If profit is 20% of sales...

---

### Growth

Sales increased from 200 to 300.

Find percentage increase.

---

Notice something?

Every question is actually an **Arithmetic** question.

The graph only gives you the numbers.

---

# Important Formulas

### Percentage

[
\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100
]

---

### Average

[
\frac{\text{Sum}}{\text{Number}}
]

---

### Ratio

[
A:B
]

---

### Percentage Increase

[
\frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original}}\times100
]

# Difficulty

There are two types.

---

## Easy DI (TCS, Cognizant)

Mostly

* Tables
* Bar graphs
* Pie charts

Simple calculations.

---

## Moderate DI (Accenture, Capgemini)

May combine

* Percentages
* Ratios
* Average
* Profit

in one question.

---

# Tricks to Solve Faster

### Trick 1

Read the questions first.

Don't study the whole graph.

Only collect the data needed.

---

### Trick 2

Circle important numbers.

Ignore unnecessary data.

---

### Trick 3

Avoid calculators.

Improve

* Tables
* Squares
* Percentages
* Fractions

---

### Trick 4

Estimate whenever possible.

Many options can be eliminated.

# Prerequisites

Before learning DI, you should know:

✅ Number System (basic calculations)

✅ Percentages ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

✅ Ratio & Proportion ⭐⭐⭐⭐

✅ Average ⭐⭐⭐⭐

✅ Basic arithmetic operations

Without these, DI becomes slow because you'll spend more time calculating than interpreting the data.

---

# Study Order for DI

I recommend learning it in this order:

1. **Table DI** (easiest)
2. **Bar Graph**
3. **Pie Chart**
4. **Line Graph**
5. **Mixed Graph**
6. **Caselet DI**

This progression goes from the simplest presentation of data to the most complex.

---

## A common misconception

Many students think **Data Interpretation is a separate math topic**. It isn't.

Think of it like this:

```text
DI = Data Reading + Arithmetic
```

The **graph or table** gives you the information.

Your **Arithmetic skills** (percentages, averages, ratios, etc.) help you answer the questions.

So if your Arithmetic is strong, DI becomes one of the easiest and fastest-scoring sections in placement aptitude. Since you'll be preparing for TCS and Cognizant-style exams, we'll practice DI by focusing on **how to quickly identify the required data first, then apply the right arithmetic concept**, rather than doing unnecessary calculations.
