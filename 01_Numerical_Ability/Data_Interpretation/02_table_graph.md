**Table Graph** is one of the most common types of **Data Interpretation (DI)** questions in placement exams like **TCS, Cognizant, Accenture, Wipro, Capgemini**, banking exams, SSC, CAT, etc.

A **table graph** is simply a **table containing numerical data**. Your job is to **read the data correctly** and answer questions based on it.

### Example

| Company | 2023 Sales | 2024 Sales |
| ------- | ---------: | ---------: |
| A       |        120 |        150 |
| B       |        100 |        140 |
| C       |        160 |        180 |
| D       |        140 |        130 |

Now the examiner may ask:

1. Which company had the highest sales in 2024?

   * **Answer:** Company C (180)

2. By how much did Company A's sales increase?

   * (150 - 120 = 30)

3. Which company had the highest percentage increase?

Let's calculate quickly:

* A: (\frac{30}{120}\times100 = 25%)
* B: (\frac{40}{100}\times100 = 40%)
* C: (\frac{20}{160}\times100 = 12.5%)
* D: Decrease

**Answer:** Company B

---

## Skills needed for Table DI

You don't need advanced maths. You mainly need:

* Addition
* Subtraction
* Multiplication
* Division
* Percentage
* Ratio
* Average
* Profit & Loss (sometimes)

---

## Types of questions asked

Suppose this table is given:

| Student | Maths | English | Science |
| ------- | ----: | ------: | ------: |
| A       |    70 |      80 |      90 |
| B       |    90 |      75 |      85 |
| C       |    85 |      95 |      80 |

Questions can be:

* Total marks of Student B
* Average marks of Student C
* Difference between A and B in Maths
* Highest scorer in English
* Percentage scored by A (out of 300)
* Ratio of Maths to Science marks of C

---

## Shortcut tips

### 1. Read the question first

Don't study the entire table. First see what the question asks.

### 2. Highlight only needed values

If the question asks about Company B, ignore A, C, and D.

### 3. Estimate when possible

If the options are very different, don't waste time on exact calculations.

Example:

* 398 × 24
* 401 × 24

Difference is obvious, so rough calculation is enough.

### 4. Use option elimination

Sometimes calculating every option is unnecessary. Check the most likely options first.

## Different DI types you should know

1. **Table DI** ✅
2. **Bar Graph**
3. **Line Graph**
4. **Pie Chart**
5. **Caselet DI** (paragraph with data)
6. **Mixed Graphs** (table + pie chart, bar + line graph, etc.)

---

Since you're preparing for placement exams, I recommend learning them in this order:

1. **Table DI** (easiest)
2. **Bar Graph DI**
3. **Pie Chart DI**
4. **Line Graph DI**
5. **Mixed DI**
6. **Caselet DI** (most time-consuming)

This order builds the calculation and interpretation skills step by step.
