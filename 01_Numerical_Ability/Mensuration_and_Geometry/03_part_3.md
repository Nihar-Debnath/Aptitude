# What is Mensuration?

Mensuration is the branch of mathematics that deals with the measurement of
geometrical figures.

It mainly involves finding:

- Area
- Perimeter
- Volume
- Curved Surface Area (CSA)
- Total Surface Area (TSA)

---

# Difference Between 2D and 3D Shapes

## 2D Shapes

2D (Two-Dimensional) shapes have:

- Length
- Breadth (Width)

They have **Area** and **Perimeter** but **no Volume**.

Examples:

- Square
- Rectangle
- Triangle
- Circle
- Parallelogram
- Rhombus
- Trapezium

---

## 3D Shapes

3D (Three-Dimensional) shapes have:

- Length
- Breadth
- Height

They have:

- Volume
- Surface Area

Examples:

- Cube
- Cuboid
- Cylinder
- Cone
- Sphere
- Hemisphere

# LSA, CSA and TSA

Students often confuse these terms.

## LSA (Lateral Surface Area)

Area of the side faces only.

Examples:

- Cube
- Cuboid
- Prism

Top and bottom are **not included**.

---

## CSA (Curved Surface Area)

Area of only the curved part.

Examples:

- Cylinder
- Cone
- Hemisphere

The circular base is **not included**.

---

## TSA (Total Surface Area)

Includes every exposed surface.

Examples:

- Cube → 6 faces
- Cylinder → Curved surface + 2 circles
- Cone → Curved surface + Base
- Hemisphere → Curved surface + Base

# Common Placement Tricks

---

## Trick 1

If the side of a square doubles,

```
Area becomes 4 times.
```

Example

```
5²

=25
```

```
10²

=100
```

---

## Trick 2

If the radius doubles,

```
Area becomes 4 times.
```

Because

```
π(2r)²

=4πr²
```

---

## Trick 3

If every dimension doubles,

```
Volume becomes 8 times.
```

Because

```
2×2×2

=8
```

---

## Trick 4

If every dimension triples,

```
Volume becomes 27 times.
```

Because

```
3³

=27
```

---

## Trick 5

Always remember

```
Diameter

=

2 Radius
```

## Trick 6

For right triangles,

Always check the common Pythagorean triplets first.

```

5 12 13

8 15 17

7 24 25

20 21 29

9 40 41

12 35 37
```

This avoids unnecessary calculations.

# Final Tips for Placement Exams

1. Memorize all formulas before solving questions.
2. Learn the common Pythagorean triplets.
3. Use **π = 22/7** unless the question specifies **3.14**.
4. Always write units (`cm²`, `m²`, `cm³`, etc.).
5. Draw a rough figure whenever possible.
6. Check whether the question asks for **Area**, **Perimeter**, **Volume**, **CSA**, **LSA**, or **TSA** before applying a formula.
7. Practice enough questions to recognize which formula fits immediately instead of deriving it during the exam.
