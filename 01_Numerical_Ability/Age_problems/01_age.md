# There is nothign on notes


Yes. Age problems look like they have many formulas, but honestly, **almost all of them come from one basic idea**:

> **Age changes by the same number of years for everyone.**

So if you understand how to represent age, you can solve almost any aptitude age question.

# 🎂 1. The basic representation

Suppose a person's **present age = (x)**.

Then:

### After (n) years

\[
\boxed{x+n}
\]

### (n) years ago

\[
\boxed{x-n}
\]

That's the foundation of age problems.

---

# 2. Two people's ages

Suppose:

* A's present age = (x)
* B's present age = (y)

Then:

### After (n) years

\[
\boxed{A=x+n}
\]

\[
\boxed{B=y+n}
\]

### (n) years ago

\[
\boxed{A=x-n}
\]

\[
\boxed{B=y-n}
\]

---

# ⭐ 3. The most important concept: Age difference NEVER changes

Suppose:

A = 30 years
B = 20 years

Difference:

\[
30-20=10
\]

After 5 years:

A = 35
B = 25

Difference:

\[
35-25=10
\]

10 years ago:

A = 20
B = 10

Difference:

\[
20-10=10
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Age difference remains constant}}
\]

This is probably the **most important rule in age problems**.

---

# 4. Finding someone's age from a ratio ⭐⭐⭐

Suppose:

> A and B are currently in the ratio (3:2).

Don't immediately think their ages are 3 and 2.

Instead write:

\[
A=3x
\]

\[
B=2x
\]

The (x) is the common multiplier.

---

### Example

A and B have ages in ratio (3:2).

Their age difference is 10 years.

Therefore:

\[
3x-2x=10
\]

\[
x=10
\]

So:

\[
A=30
\]

\[
B=20
\]

---

# 5. Ratio changes after some years ⭐⭐⭐

This is extremely common.

Example:

> A and B are currently in the ratio (3:2). After 10 years, their ratio will be (4:3). Find their present ages.

Start with:

\[
A=3x
\]

\[
B=2x
\]

After 10 years:

\[
A=3x+10
\]

\[
B=2x+10
\]

We're told:

\[
\frac{3x+10}{2x+10}=\frac43
\]

Cross multiply:

\[
3(3x+10)=4(2x+10)
\]

\[
9x+30=8x+40
\]

\[
x=10
\]

Therefore:

\[
A=30
\]

\[
B=20
\]

---

# 6. "n years ago" ratio

Exactly the same idea.

If current ages are:

\[
A=3x
\]

\[
B=2x
\]

Then 5 years ago:

\[
A=3x-5
\]

\[
B=2x-5
\]

If their ratio was given as (2:1):

\[
\frac{3x-5}{2x-5}=2
\]

Then solve for (x).

---

# 7. Parent-child age problems

These are usually very easy once you use the **age difference**.

Example:

> A father is 30 years older than his son. After 5 years, the father will be twice the son's age. Find their present ages.

Let son's age:

\[
x
\]

Father:

\[
x+30
\]

After 5 years:

Son:

\[
x+5
\]

Father:

\[
x+35
\]

Condition:

\[
x+35=2(x+5)
\]

Solve:

\[
x+35=2x+10
\]

\[
x=25
\]

So:

Son = 25
Father = 55

---

# 8. "Father was X times son's age"

Suppose:

> Father is currently 3 times son's age.

Son:

\[
x
\]

Father:

\[
3x
\]

If their age difference is 30:

\[
3x-x=30
\]

\[
2x=30
\]

\[
x=15
\]

Therefore:

Son = 15
Father = 45

---

# 9. A VERY useful shortcut for "times age"

Suppose:

Father is currently (k) times son's age.

And their age difference is (D).

Then:

\[
\boxed{\text{Son's age}=\frac{D}{k-1}}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{\text{Father's age}=\frac{kD}{k-1}}
\]

### Example

Father is 4 times son's age.

Difference = 30.

Son:

\[
\frac{30}{4-1}=10
\]

Father:

\[
4(10)=40
\]

---

# 10. Average age

If there are (n) people:

\[
\boxed{\text{Average age}=
\\frac{\text{Sum of ages}}{n}}
]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sum of ages}=\text{Average}\times n}
\]

This becomes useful when people enter/leave a group.

---

# 11. Someone joins a group

Suppose 5 people have average age 20.

Total age:

\[
5\times20=100
\]

A new person aged 30 joins.

New total:

\[
100+30=130
\]

Number of people:

\[
6
\]

New average:

\[
\frac{130}{6}=21.67
\]

---

# 12. Someone leaves a group

Same concept.

Suppose 6 people have average age 25.

Total:

\[
6\times25=150
\]

A person aged 30 leaves.

Remaining:

\[
150-30=120
\]

People remaining:

\[
5
\]

Average:

\[
120/5=24
\]

---

# 🔥 Your Age Problem Cheat Sheet

These are the things I'd memorize:

| Situation                   | Formula                            |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Present age                 | (x)                                |
| After (n) years             | (x+n)                              |
| (n) years ago               | (x-n)                              |
| Age ratio (a:b)             | (ax,\ bx)                          |
| Age difference              | Constant                           |
| Average age                 | (\frac{\text{sum}}{\text{number}}) |
| Sum of ages                 | Average × number                   |
| Father (k) times son        | (kx,\ x)                           |
| Difference (D), ratio (k:1) | Son (=\frac{D}{k-1})               |

---

# 🧠 The ONE method I want you to use

Whenever you see an age question, don't search your memory for some fancy formula.

Do this:

### Step 1 — Choose an unknown

\[
\boxed{\text{Age}=x}
\]

### Step 2 — Express other ages using (x)

If ratio (3:2):

\[
3x,\ 2x
\]

If difference 10:

\[
x,\ x+10
\]

### Step 3 — Move through time

After 5 years:

\[
x+5
\]

5 years ago:

\[
x-5
\]

### Step 4 — Apply the condition

Ratio:

\[
\frac{A}{B}=\frac{3}{2}
\]

Times:

\[
A=3B
\]

Difference:

\[
A-B=10
\]

Average:

\[
\frac{A+B}{2}=\text{given average}
\]

### Step 5 — Solve.

That's it.

**Age problems aren't really about memorizing formulas. They're mostly about translating English into equations.** Once you get comfortable with (x+n), (x-n), ratios, and the constant age difference, you'll be able to handle most aptitude age questions.
