Absolutely. This is a **very standard age-problem pattern**, and there's a nice shortcut once you see it.

### Given

* Present age of father + son = **90**
* **15 years ago**, father's age was **3 times** son's age.
* Find their present ages.

---

## Step 1: Let their present ages be variables

Let:

\[
\text{Father's age}=F
\]

\[
\text{Son's age}=S
\]

We know:

\[
F+S=90
\]

That's our first equation.

---

## Step 2: Go 15 years into the past

This is where you need to be careful.

If someone's **present age is (x)**:

> 15 years ago → (x-15)

Therefore:

Father's age 15 years ago:

\[
F-15
\]

Son's age 15 years ago:

\[
S-15
\]

The question says:

> Father's age was **thrice** the son's age.

"Thrice" = **3 times**.

Therefore:

\[
F-15=3(S-15)
\]

That's our second equation.

---

## Step 3: Solve it

We know:

\[
F+S=90
\]

So:

\[
F=90-S
\]

Put that into the second equation:

\[
(90-S)-15=3(S-15)
\]

Simplify:

\[
75-S=3S-45
\]

Move things around:

\[
75+45=3S+S
\]

\[
120=4S
\]

\[
S=30
\]

So the son's present age is:

\[
\boxed{30}
\]

And since their total age is 90:

\[
F=90-30
\]

\[
\boxed{60}
\]

# 🎯 Answer

**Father = 60 years**

**Son = 30 years**

---

## Let's verify it

Present:

> Father = 60
> Son = 30

Sum:

\[
60+30=90
\]

Correct.

15 years ago:

> Father = 45
> Son = 15

Was father thrice son's age?

\[
45=3\times15
\]

YES. ✅

---

# 🧠 The important part for you

The hardest part isn't the algebra. It's translating the English correctly.

Whenever you see:

> **15 years ago**

immediately write:

\[
\boxed{\text{Current age}-15}
\]

Whenever you see:

> **15 years later**

write:

\[
\boxed{\text{Current age}+15}
\]

And:

> **Twice** → (2\times)

> **Thrice** → (3\times)

> **Four times** → (4\times)

So this question literally becomes:

\[
\boxed{F+S=90}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{F-15=3(S-15)}
\]

Once you can translate those two sentences into those two equations, **the question is basically solved.**
