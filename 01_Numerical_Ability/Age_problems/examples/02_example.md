Yep — this is almost the **same type as the previous question**, except now the condition is **10 years later** instead of 15 years ago.

Let's do it slowly.

### Given

* Present sum of father + son = **60**
* After 10 years, father's age will be **3 times** son's age.

---

## Step 1: Define present ages

Let:

\[
\text{Father's age}=F
\]

\[
\text{Son's age}=S
\]

We know:

\[
\boxed{F+S=60}
\]

---

## Step 2: Move 10 years into the future

This is the important part.

If someone's current age is (x):

> 10 years later → (x+10)

So:

Father after 10 years:

\[
F+10
\]

Son after 10 years:

\[
S+10
\]

The question says:

> Father's age will be **thrice** the son's age.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{F+10=3(S+10)}
\]

---

## Step 3: Use the first equation

We have:

\[
F+S=60
\]

Therefore:

\[
F=60-S
\]

Put this into:

\[
F+10=3(S+10)
\]

We get:

\[
(60-S)+10=3(S+10)
\]

\[
70-S=3S+30
\]

Move things:

\[
70-30=3S+S
\]

\[
40=4S
\]

\[
S=10
\]

So the son's present age is:

\[
\boxed{10}
\]

And:

\[
F=60-10
\]

\[
\boxed{50}
\]

# 🎯 Answer

**Father = 50 years**

**Son = 10 years**

---

### Verify it

Present:

> Father = 50
> Son = 10

Sum:

\[
50+10=60
\]

After 10 years:

> Father = 60
> Son = 20

Is father 3 times son's age?

\[
60=3(20)
\]

✅ Correct.

---

## Notice the pattern between the two questions

This is **VERY important for your TCS preparation**:

### "15 years ago"

Subtract:

\[
\boxed{F-15=3(S-15)}
\]

### "10 years later"

Add:

\[
\boxed{F+10=3(S+10)}
\]

That's basically the entire trick behind these questions.

**Past → subtract**

**Future → add**

**Twice → ×2**

**Thrice → ×3**

Once you translate the sentence correctly, the rest is just basic algebra.
