Ahh yes — **this is NOT really a train-crossing problem.** 🚨

This is a different type of **Speed, Distance & Time** problem:

# ⏸️ Type: Speed with Stoppage / Average Speed with Stoppage

The key phrase is:

> **54 km/hr without stoppage and 45 km/hr with stoppage**

Here, **45 km/hr is the average speed for the entire journey**, including the time when the train is stopped.

---

## Let's understand the difference

### Without stoppage

The train is continuously moving at:

\[
54\text{ km/h}
\]

So in 1 hour, it would travel:

\[
54\text{ km}
\]

### With stoppage

The train sometimes stops.

Therefore, during a total 1-hour period, it doesn't actually move for the entire hour.

Its **average speed** becomes:

\[
45\text{ km/h}
\]

So in 1 actual hour, it covers:

\[
45\text{ km}
\]

---

# Solve the question

> Speed without stoppage = 54 km/h
> Speed with stoppage = 45 km/h
> Find how much time in an hour the train stops.

### Step 1 — Distance travelled in 1 hour

Because the average speed with stoppage is 45 km/h:

\[
D=45\times1
\]

\[
D=45km
\]

So the train actually covers **45 km in 1 hour**.

---

### Step 2 — Find how long it was actually moving

When moving, its speed is 54 km/h.

So:

\[
Time=\frac{Distance}{Speed}
\]

\[
=\frac{45}{54}
\]

\[
=\frac56\text{ hour}
\]

Convert to minutes:

\[
\frac56\times60=50\text{ minutes}
\]

So the train was **moving for 50 minutes**.

---

### Step 3 — Find stopping time

Total time:

\[
60\text{ minutes}
\]

Moving time:

\[
50\text{ minutes}
\]

Therefore:

\[
60-50=\boxed{10\text{ minutes}}
\]

# ✅ Answer: 10 minutes

---

# 🧠 The shortcut you should remember

For these questions:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stopping time} = \text{Total time} - \text{Moving time}}
\]

And:

\[
\boxed{\text{Moving time}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{actual speed}}}
\]

But there's an even faster formula.

If:

* Speed without stoppage = (S)
* Average speed with stoppage = (A)
* Total time = (T)

Then:

\[
\boxed{\text{Moving time}=\frac{A}{S}\times T}
\]

So stopping time:

\[
\boxed{\text{Stopping time}=T\left(1-\frac{A}{S}\right)}
\]

For this question:

\[
=60\left(1-\frac{45}{54}\right)
\]

\[
=60\left(1-\frac56\right)
\]

\[
=60\times\frac16
\]

\[
\boxed{10\text{ minutes}}
\]

---

## 🔥 Recognize this question type

Whenever you see wording like:

> **"Speed without stoppage is X km/h and speed with stoppage is Y km/h. Find the stopping time..."**

Immediately think:

### **Average Speed + Stoppage Problem**

And **don't use train length, platform, relative speed, etc.**

It's simply:

**Average speed → total distance → actual moving time → stopped time.**
