Yes! This one is **much easier** than the previous question. And **NO factorial is needed here.** 🎯

Let's do it from the meaning of the question first.

## 1. What do we have?

The pot contains:

* ⚪ White = 2
* ⚫ Black = 6
* 🩶 Grey = 4
* 🟢 Green = 8

So total balls:

[
2+6+4+8=\boxed{20}
]

---

## 2. What is the question asking?

It says:

> What is the probability of picking a ball that is **black OR green**?

We want either:

```text
⚫ Black
   OR
🟢 Green
```

Number of black balls:

[
6
]

Number of green balls:

[
8
]

So favorable balls:

[
6+8=\boxed{14}
]

---

## 3. Now use the basic probability formula

Remember:

[
\boxed{\text{Probability}=
\frac{\text{favorable outcomes}}
{\text{total outcomes}}}
]

Here:

```text
Favorable = 14
Total = 20
```

Therefore:

[
P(\text{black or green})
========================

\frac{14}{20}
]

Simplify by 2:

[
=\boxed{\frac7{10}}
]

# ✅ Answer: **7/10 — Option B**

---

# Now your important question: "Do we need factorial?"

### ❌ NO.

And this is VERY important.

Look at the previous question:

> 5 girls and 2 boys are **sitting in a row**.

There, we had to **arrange people**.

That's why we needed:

[
5!,\quad 7!,\quad 2!,\quad {}^6C_2
]

We were **counting arrangements**.

---

But this question says:

> Pick **ONE ball** randomly.

We're not arranging anything.

We're simply counting balls:

```text
White   = 2
Black   = 6
Grey    = 4
Green   = 8
```

That's it.

So:

[
\boxed{\text{No factorial}}
]

---

# 🧠 Your exam rule

Whenever you see a probability question, **don't automatically start using factorial.**

First ask:

### "What am I doing?"

If you're simply **picking one thing**:

> Count favorable things / count total things.

Like this question:

\[
\frac{6+8}{2+6+4+8}
=

\frac{14}{20}
=
\boxed{\frac7{10}}
\]

### If you're arranging people/things:

Then factorial/permutation/combination **might** be needed.

---

### Compare the two questions:

**Q1:**

> 5 girls + 2 boys sitting in a row

You're **ARRANGING** → factorial/combination appeared.

**Q2:**

> Pick 1 ball from a pot

You're just **PICKING** → simple counting.

So for this one, your brain should immediately go:

> **"No arrangement. No factorial. Just favorable ÷ total."** 🔥
