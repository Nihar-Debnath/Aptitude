😂 Yeah, this looks completely different from the shirt questions. But don't worry — **this is actually much easier once you understand one calendar fact.**

Let's start from absolute zero.

## Step 1: How many days are in a normal year?

A **non-leap year** has:

[
\boxed{365\text{ days}}
]

Now divide 365 by 7 because there are **7 days in a week**.

[
365 = 7\times52+1
]

So a normal year contains:

[
\boxed{52\text{ complete weeks}+1\text{ extra day}}
]

---

## Step 2: What does 52 complete weeks mean?

Every week has exactly **one Thursday**.

So in 52 complete weeks, we definitely have:

[
\boxed{52\text{ Thursdays}}
]

But there is **1 extra day**.

That extra day could be:

* Monday
* Tuesday
* Wednesday
* **Thursday**
* Friday
* Saturday
* Sunday

There are **7 possibilities** for that extra day.

---

## Step 3: When will we get 53 Thursdays?

We already have 52 Thursdays.

To get **53 Thursdays**, the extra day must be **Thursday**.

So:

[
P(\text{53 Thursdays})
======================

\frac{\text{favourable possibilities}}{\text{total possibilities}}
]

Favourable possibilities:

[
1
]

because only **Thursday** works.

Total possibilities:

[
7
]

Therefore:

[
\boxed{\frac17}
]

### ✅ Answer: **B. (1/7)**

---

# But wait — why isn't it (53/365)?

This is probably your next question.

You might think:

> "There are 365 days, so shouldn't probability be something like (53/365)?"

**No.**

We're not randomly picking a day from the year.

We're asking:

> **Which day of the week is the extra 365th day?**

There are only **7 possible cases**:

```text
Extra day
   ↓
Monday
Tuesday
Wednesday
Thursday  ← gives 53 Thursdays
Friday
Saturday
Sunday
```

Only **1 out of these 7** gives us 53 Thursdays.

Therefore:

[
\boxed{1/7}
]

---

## 🧠 The important concept

Whenever you see:

> **"How many X's can occur in a year?"**

first remember:

### Non-leap year

[
365=52\times7+1
]

Therefore:

**Every day occurs 52 times**, and **one day occurs 53 times**.

Which day gets the 53rd occurrence depends on what day the year starts/ends on.

So:

> Probability of **53 Thursdays** in a non-leap year

[
\boxed{\frac17}
]

---

### Tiny memory trick

**Normal year → 52 weeks + 1 day**

**52 weeks → 52 of every day**

**Extra 1 day → one day gets 53**

**7 possible days → probability = (1/7)**

That's all this question is testing.
