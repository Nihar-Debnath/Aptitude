Yep! This is a classic **Time & Work** question. And the trick is actually very simple once you understand what `1/8`, `1/10`, etc. mean.

Let's go **from absolute basics**.

### Step 1: Understand what "A can finish in 8 hours" means

If A alone takes **8 hours** to finish the entire work:

> A's work in 1 hour = **1/8**

Similarly:

* B takes 10 hours → B does **1/10** work per hour
* C takes 12 hours → C does **1/12** work per hour

So their **1-hour work rates** are:

\[
A=\frac18,\quad B=\frac1{10},\quad C=\frac1{12}
\]

---

### Step 2: A, B and C work together for 2 hours

Together, their rate is:

\[
\frac18+\frac1{10}+\frac1{12}
\]

Find the LCM of 8, 10 and 12:

\[
LCM=120
\]

So:

\[
\frac{15}{120}+\frac{12}{120}+\frac{10}{120}
=\frac{37}{120}
\]

That's their work in **1 hour**.

Therefore in **2 hours**:

\[
2\times\frac{37}{120}
=\frac{74}{120}
=\frac{37}{60}
\]

So they completed:

\[
\boxed{\frac{37}{60}}
\]

of the work.

---

### Step 3: Find the remaining work

The entire work is:

\[
1
\]

They already completed:

\[
\frac{37}{60}
\]

Therefore remaining:

\[
1-\frac{37}{60}
\]

\[
=\frac{60-37}{60}
\]

\[
=\boxed{\frac{23}{60}}
\]

So after A leaves, **B and C have to complete (23/60) of the work.**

---

### Step 4: B and C work together

B's rate:

\[
\frac1{10}
\]

C's rate:

\[
\frac1{12}
\]

Together:

\[
\frac1{10}+\frac1{12}
\]

LCM = 60:

\[
\frac6{60}+\frac5{60}
=\frac{11}{60}
\]

So B + C complete:

\[
\frac{11}{60}
\]

of the work **per hour**.

We need them to complete:

\[
\frac{23}{60}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{Time}=
\frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Rate}}
\]

\[
=\frac{23/60}{11/60}
\]

The 60 cancels:

\[
=\frac{23}{11}
\]

\[
=2+\frac1{11}
\]

### 🎯 Answer:

\[
\boxed{2+\frac1{11}\text{ hours}}
\]

So **Option A**.

---

## 🔥 The formula you should remember

Whenever you see:

> A completes work in X days/hours

immediately convert it to:

\[
\boxed{\text{1-day/hour work}=\frac1X}
\]

And if multiple people work together:

\[
\boxed{\text{Combined rate}=\text{A's rate}+\text{B's rate}+\text{C's rate}}
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{\text{Time}=\frac{\text{Remaining Work}}{\text{Combined Rate}}}
\]

### For THIS question, visualize it like this:

**Entire work = 1**

→ A+B+C work for 2 hours
→ **37/60 completed**

→ A leaves
→ **23/60 remains**

→ B+C work at **11/60 per hour**

→ Time = `(23/60) ÷ (11/60)`

→ **23/11 = 2 + 1/11 hours**

That's the entire question. Once you understand **"rate = work done in 1 hour"**, these Time & Work questions become MUCH less scary. 🔥
