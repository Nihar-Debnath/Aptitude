This is a classic **Unitary Method** question (also called **Unit Price** or **Cost per Unit**).

The idea is simple:

> **When comparing two products with different quantities, always compare the cost of 1 unit (1 gram, 1 kg, 1 litre, etc.).**

Let's solve it step by step.

---

# Given

### Packet A

```text
100 g → ₹72
```

### Packet B

```text
150 g → ₹108
```

We have to find which gives more peanuts for the same money.

---

# Method 1 (Unitary Method) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Find the cost of **1 gram**.

---

### Packet A

100 g costs ₹72.

So,

```text
1 g = 72/100
```

```text
= ₹0.72
```

---

### Packet B

150 g costs ₹108.

```text
1 g = 108/150
```

Divide numerator and denominator by 6.

```text
108/150

=

18/25
```

Now divide

```text
18÷25

=0.72
```

So

```text
1 g = ₹0.72
```

---

## Compare

| Packet | Cost of 1 gram |
| ------ | -------------- |
| A      | ₹0.72          |
| B      | ₹0.72          |

Exactly the same.

✅ **Answer = D) Both have same value**

---

# Method 2 (Percentage Thinking)

Notice something.

Packet B has

```text
150 g
```

Packet A has

```text
100 g
```

Packet B has

```text
50% more quantity
```

because

```text
100 →150

Increase =50%
```

Now look at price.

```text
72→108
```

Increase

```text
36
```

Percentage increase

```text
36/72 ×100

=50%
```

Quantity increased by **50%**.

Price also increased by **50%**.

Therefore,

👉 Value remains the same.

No calculation needed.

# Which method should you use in placements?

### If numbers are easy

Use **Unitary Method**.

### If percentage relation is obvious

Use **Percentage Thinking**.

### If fractions look messy

# TCS Shortcut

Whenever you see

```text
100 g → ₹72

150 g → ₹108
```

Don't calculate decimals immediately.

Ask yourself:

> **Has the quantity increased by the same percentage as the price?**

Here,

```text
100 →150

+50%
```

```text
72→108

+50%
```

Same increase.

👉 Same value.

Answer in **5 seconds**.

---

## General Rule

Whenever you're comparing two products:

* If **price increases more than quantity**, the second product is **worse**.
* If **quantity increases more than price**, the second product is **better**.
* If **both increase by the same percentage**, they offer the **same value**.

This idea appears frequently in arithmetic questions related to **unitary method, percentages, and profit & loss**, so it's worth remembering.
