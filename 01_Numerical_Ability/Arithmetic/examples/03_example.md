Excellent observation! This method is actually **better than the one I showed** for TCS.

The reason I didn't use it initially is because I was teaching the **basic chain/unitary method** first. Your teacher is using an **algebraic substitution method**, which is more elegant and often faster once you're comfortable with equations.

Let's understand it.

---

# Step 1: Assign variables

Instead of writing "cost of one cow," define variables.

```text
Cost of 1 Cow = C
Cost of 1 Sheep = S
Cost of 1 Goat = G
Cost of 1 Elephant = E
```

Now convert every English sentence into an equation.

---

## Statement 1

> 6 cows cost the same as 9 sheep.

This becomes

\[
6C = 9S
\]

---

## Statement 2

> 27 sheep cost the same as 30 goats.

This becomes

\[
27S = 30G
\]

---

## Statement 3

> 50 goats cost the same as 3 elephants.

This becomes

\[
50G = 3E
\]

---

## Statement 4

> 2 elephants cost ₹7200.

This becomes

\[
2E = 7200
\]

Therefore,

\[
E = 3600
\]

---

# Step 2: Start from the known value

We know

\[
E=3600
\]

Now instead of finding **1 goat**, the teacher cleverly keeps everything in terms of **E**.

From

\[
50G=3E
\]

Divide by 50.

\[
G=\frac{3E}{50}
\]

This is exactly what your teacher wrote.

---

# Step 3: Find 30 goats

Multiply both sides by 30.

\[
30G=\frac{3E}{50}\times30
\]

Simplify.

\[
30G=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

This is the boxed equation.

---

# Step 4: Use the second equation

We already know

\[
27S=30G
\]

But

\[
30G=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

Substitute.

\[
27S=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

Now sheep are also expressed in terms of elephants.

---

# Step 5: Use the first equation

We have

\[
6C=9S
\]

The teacher notices something.

He already has

\[
27S=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

So he makes the sheep count the same.

Multiply

\[
6C=9S
\]

by 3.

\[
18C=27S
\]

Now substitute.

\[
18C=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

---

# Step 6: Solve

Divide by 18.

\[
C=\frac{9E}{90}
\]

Simplify.

\[
C=\frac{E}{10}
\]

Since

\[
E=3600
\]

\[
C=\frac{3600}{10}=360
\]

✅ Answer = **₹360**

---

# Why did your teacher multiply by 3?

This is the key idea.

He had

\[
27S=\frac{9E}{5}
\]

But his first equation was

\[
6C=9S
\]

The number of sheep doesn't match.

So he multiplied the first equation by 3.

\[
6C=9S
\]

↓

\[
18C=27S
\]

Now both equations have **27S**, so they can be connected.

This is called **making like terms** before substitution.
