# 🔢 Divisibility Rules

### First: What does "divisible" mean?

If a number can be divided by another number **with no remainder**, it is divisible by that number.

For example:

**24 ÷ 6 = 4** → no remainder → ✅ 24 is divisible by 6.

**25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1** → ❌ 25 is not divisible by 6.

---

# ⭐ The important divisibility rules

For aptitude exams, focus mainly on **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11**.

| Divisor | Divisibility Rule                                                | Example                     |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **2**   | Last digit is even: 0,2,4,6,8                                    | 348 ✅                       |
| **3**   | Sum of digits is divisible by 3                                  | 123 → 1+2+3=6 ✅             |
| **4**   | Last **2 digits** divisible by 4                                 | 1316 → 16 ÷ 4 ✅             |
| **5**   | Last digit is 0 or 5                                             | 235 ✅                       |
| **6**   | Divisible by **2 AND 3**                                         | 246 → even + digit sum 12 ✅ |
| **8**   | Last **3 digits** divisible by 8                                 | 5136 → 136 ÷ 8 = 17 ✅       |
| **9**   | Sum of digits divisible by 9                                     | 729 → 18 → ✅                |
| **10**  | Last digit is 0                                                  | 450 ✅                       |
| **11**  | Difference between alternating digit sums is 0 or multiple of 11 | 121 → (1+1)-2 = 0 ✅         |

---

## 🧠 Let's understand each one properly

### 1️⃣ Divisibility by 2

Very easy.

Look at the **last digit**.

If it is:

**0, 2, 4, 6, 8 → divisible by 2**

Examples:

**348** → last digit 8 → ✅

**527** → last digit 7 → ❌

You don't care about the other digits.

---

### 2️⃣ Divisibility by 3

Add **all the digits**.

If the sum is divisible by 3, the original number is divisible by 3.

Example:

**729**

7 + 2 + 9 = **18**

18 is divisible by 3.

Therefore:

**729 is divisible by 3.** ✅

Another:

**572**

5 + 7 + 2 = **14**

14 isn't divisible by 3.

Therefore:

**572 is NOT divisible by 3.** ❌

---

### 3️⃣ Divisibility by 4

This is important.

👉 Look ONLY at the **last 2 digits**.

Example:

**1,316**

Ignore **13**.

Look at:

**16**

16 ÷ 4 = 4

Therefore **1316 is divisible by 4.** ✅

Why can we ignore the other digits?

Because **100, 1000, 10000... are themselves divisible by 4.**

So only the last 2 digits matter.

---

### 4️⃣ Divisibility by 5

Look at the last digit.

It must be:

**0 or 5**

Examples:

250 → ✅
735 → ✅
421 → ❌

Easy marks. 😎

---

### 5️⃣ Divisibility by 6 ⭐

This one is slightly different.

**6 = 2 × 3**

Therefore a number must satisfy **BOTH**:

> Divisible by 2 **AND** divisible by 3.

Example:

**246**

Check 2:

Last digit = 6 → even → ✅

Check 3:

2 + 4 + 6 = 12 → divisible by 3 → ✅

Therefore:

**246 is divisible by 6.**

But consider:

**234**

Divisible by 2? ✅

2 + 3 + 4 = 9 → divisible by 3? ✅

Therefore divisible by 6. ✅

---

### 6️⃣ Divisibility by 8 ⭐

Look ONLY at the **last 3 digits**.

Example:

**5,136**

Look at:

**136**

136 ÷ 8 = 17

Therefore:

**5136 is divisible by 8.** ✅

Another:

**7,324**

Look at **324**.

324 ÷ 8 = 40.5

So ❌ not divisible by 8.

---

### 7️⃣ Divisibility by 9

Exactly like 3.

Add all digits.

But now the sum must be divisible by **9**.

Example:

**4,536**

4 + 5 + 3 + 6 = **18**

18 is divisible by 9.

Therefore:

**4536 is divisible by 9.** ✅

---

### 8️⃣ Divisibility by 10

Last digit must be:

**0**

Examples:

120 → ✅
450 → ✅
7800 → ✅
785 → ❌

---

# 🔥 9️⃣ Divisibility by 11

This looks scary initially, but it's actually quite easy.

Take the digits **alternately**.

Example:

### 121

Write:

**1 2 1**

Take alternate positions:

(1 + 1) − 2

= **0**

0 is divisible by 11.

Therefore **121 is divisible by 11.** ✅

---

Another example:

### 1331

Alternate digits:

**1 + 3 = 4**

Other side:

**3 + 1 = 4**

Difference:

4 − 4 = **0**

Therefore 1331 is divisible by 11. ✅

---

### Example: 1234

First group:

1 + 3 = **4**

Second group:

2 + 4 = **6**

Difference:

6 − 4 = **2**

2 isn't divisible by 11.

Therefore:

**1234 is NOT divisible by 11.** ❌

---

# 🧠 The rules you should memorize

Don't try to memorize 20 different rules right now.

For your aptitude preparation, lock these into your brain:

> **2 → Last digit even**

> **3 → Sum of digits ÷ 3**

> **4 → Last 2 digits ÷ 4**

> **5 → Last digit 0 or 5**

> **6 → Divisible by BOTH 2 and 3**

> **8 → Last 3 digits ÷ 8**

> **9 → Sum of digits ÷ 9**

> **10 → Last digit 0**

> **11 → Alternating sum difference ÷ 11**

These are the **core rules**. Once these are solid, we can move to the more interesting aptitude questions where they ask things like:

**"What digit should replace x so that 45x72 is divisible by 9?"**

or

**"Find the smallest number that must be added to 7354 to make it divisible by 8."**

Those are where divisibility rules become really useful. 🔥
