### The basic rule

> **AND → Multiply**
> **OR → Add**

But there's one important thing: it means **how the choices are combined**, not simply that whenever you see the English word "and" you blindly multiply.

---

### 🟢 AND → Multiply

If you need to do **both** things:

> Choose **4 men AND 2 women**

You have:

\[
{}^6C_4
\]

ways to choose men, and for **each** of those choices, you have:

\[
{}^5C_2
\]

ways to choose women.

Therefore:

\[
{}^6C_4\times{}^5C_2
\]

\[
=15\times10
\]

\[
=\boxed{150}
\]

Think:

> **This choice + that choice → multiply**

---

### 🔵 OR → Add

If you have **different alternatives**, add them.

For example:

> A student can choose **3 programming courses OR 4 design courses**.

If they choose only one course, then:

\[
3+4=\boxed{7}
\]

Because you're choosing **one option from one group OR one option from another group**.

---

### Real-life way to remember

Imagine a restaurant:

**AND:**

You choose:

* 3 burgers **AND**
* 4 drinks

Every burger can be paired with every drink:

\[
3\times4=12
\]

**OR:**

You choose:

* 3 burgers **OR**
* 4 pizzas

You choose only one item:

\[
3+4=7
\]

---

### 🔥 P&C shortcut

| Situation        | Operation    |
| ---------------- | ------------ |
| Do **A AND B**   | **Multiply** |
| Do **A OR B**    | **Add**      |
| Select only      | Combination  |
| Select + arrange | Permutation  |

So for the committee question:

> Choose 4 men **AND** 2 women

\[
\boxed{{}^6C_4\times{}^5C_2}
\]

And for a question like:

> Choose either 3 boys **OR** 2 girls

\[
\boxed{{}^3C_1+{}^2C_1}
\]

This **AND/OR rule** will become extremely useful once we start doing the slightly harder P&C questions.
