### What is Weighted Average?

A **weighted average** is an average where **some values have more importance because they occur in larger quantities**.

Think of it like this:

> **Normal average:** everyone gets equal importance.
> **Weighted average:** bigger quantities get more importance.

### Simple example

Suppose:

* 1 kg rice costs ₹40
* 3 kg rice costs ₹60

Can we just do:

\[
\frac{40+60}{2}=₹50
\]

❌ **No!**

Why? Because you have **3 kg of ₹60 rice** but only **1 kg of ₹40 rice**.

The ₹60 rice has more weight.

So calculate:

\[
\text{Weighted Average}
=

\frac{(40\times1)+(60\times3)}{1+3}
\]

\[
=\frac{40+180}{4}
\]

\[
=\boxed{₹55/kg}
\]

So the mixture's actual average price is **₹55/kg**, not ₹50.


### The formula you need

If there are different values with different quantities:

\[
\boxed{\text{Weighted Average}=
\frac{\text{Value}_1\times\text{Quantity}_1+\text{Value}_2\times\text{Quantity}_2+\cdots}
{\text{Total Quantity}}}
\]

### Now connect it to Mixture

Suppose:

**2 kg @ ₹40 + 3 kg @ ₹60**

Then:

\[
\frac{(40\times2)+(60\times3)}{2+3}
\]

\[
=\frac{80+180}{5}
\]

\[
=\boxed{₹52/kg}
\]

So **₹52 is the weighted average price of the mixture.**

And this is exactly where **Alligation** comes in:
**Alligation is basically a shortcut for finding the ratio of quantities needed to produce a particular weighted average.**

So remember this chain:

**Mixture → Weighted Average → Alligation**

Once you understand weighted average, Alligation becomes MUCH easier.
