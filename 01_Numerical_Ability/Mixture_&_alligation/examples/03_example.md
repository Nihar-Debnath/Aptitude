Yep — this one is slightly different from the previous question because **one quantity is already given**. Let's go very slowly.

### First, translate the question

We have:

* **First oil:** ₹4/kg
* **Second oil:** ₹5/kg
* **Mixture:** ₹4.6/kg
* **First oil quantity:** 40 kg
* **Second oil quantity:** ? → let's call it **x kg**

So:

```text
First oil                 Second oil
₹4/kg                     ₹5/kg
40 kg                      x kg
      \                    /
       \                  /
        Mixture = ₹4.6/kg
```

### Step 1: Why is ₹4.6 the average?

Because the question says:

> "produce a mixture having the rate of Rs. 4.6 per kg"

That means **1 kg of the final mixture costs ₹4.6**.

So ₹4.6 is the **weighted average price**.

---

## Step 2: Use the weighted average formula

Remember:

\[
\text{Average}=\frac{\text{Total Cost}}{\text{Total Quantity}}
\]

### First oil

₹4/kg × 40 kg:

\[
4\times40=160
\]

So first oil costs **₹160**.

### Second oil

₹5/kg × x kg:

\[
5x
\]

So total cost of both oils:

\[
160+5x
\]

Total quantity:

\[
40+x
\]

Therefore:

\[
\frac{160+5x}{40+x}=4.6
\]

That's the whole setup.

---

## Step 3: Solve it

\[
\frac{160+5x}{40+x}=4.6
\]

Multiply both sides:

\[
160+5x=4.6(40+x)
\]

\[
160+5x=184+4.6x
\]

Move (4.6x):

\[
5x-4.6x=184-160
\]

\[
0.4x=24
\]

\[
x=\frac{24}{0.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{x=60}
\]

### ✅ Answer: **60 kg — Option D**

---

## But there's an even easier way using Alligation

Since you now know the logic:

```text
       ₹4                 ₹5
         \                 /
          \               /
             ₹4.6
            /    \
       5-4.6    4.6-4
          0.4       0.6
```

So:

\[
\text{First : Second}=0.4:0.6
\]

\[
=2:3
\]

We already know **First oil = 40 kg**.

So:

\[
2\text{ parts}=40
\]

Therefore:

\[
1\text{ part}=20
\]

Second oil = 3 parts:

\[
3\times20=\boxed{60\text{ kg}}
\]

### 🔥 Notice the connection

The **weighted-average method** gave:

\[
\boxed{\frac{160+5x}{40+x}=4.6}
\]

The **Alligation method** directly gave:

\[
\boxed{40:x=2:3}
\]

Both give **60 kg**.

For your aptitude exam, if you see **one quantity already given**, Alligation is usually much faster. But understanding the weighted-average equation is important because it tells you **WHY the method works**.
