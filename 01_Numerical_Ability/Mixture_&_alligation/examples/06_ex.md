Exactly — **but with one important condition**. 👍

If the alligation gives:

\[
\boxed{1:1}
\]

it means:

> **The two ingredients must be taken in equal quantities.**

So if the question gives you **20 L of one ingredient**, then the other ingredient must also be:

\[
\boxed{20L}
\]

### Example

15% solution + 5% solution → 10% solution

Alligation:

\[
15% \quad\quad 5%
\]

Target = 10%

Differences:

\[
15-10=5
\]

\[
10-5=5
\]

So:

\[
5:5=\boxed{1:1}
\]

Therefore:

**20 L of 15% solution** → need **20 L of 5% solution**.

---

### ⚠️ But don't think:

> "Whenever I get 1:1, the answer is always the given number."

Instead think:

> **1:1 means equal quantities.**

So whatever quantity is known, the other quantity must be **the same**.

For example:

* Given 20 L → other = **20 L**
* Given 50 kg → other = **50 kg**
* Given 8 L → other = **8 L**

That's the key. 🔥

And if you get:

\[
\boxed{2:1}
\]

it means one ingredient needs **twice as much** as the other — you don't simply copy the given number.
