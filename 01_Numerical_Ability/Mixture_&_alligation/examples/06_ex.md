Exactly! **Here it becomes 1 : 1.** 🎯

Let's see why.

We have:

* First solution = **15% sugar**
* Second solution = **5% sugar**
* Desired mixture = **10% sugar**

Put them in alligation:

```text
        15%
          \
           \  10 - 5 = 5
            \
            10%
            /
           /  15 - 10 = 5
          /
         5%
```

So the differences are:

[
15-10=5
]

[
10-5=5
]

They're equal.

Therefore:

[
\boxed{15%:5%=5:5=1:1}
]

### Now the important part

The question says:

> Add how many litres of the **second solution** to **20 litres of the first solution**?

Our ratio is:

[
\text{First solution : Second solution}=1:1
]

So if the first solution is **20 litres**, the second solution must also be:

[
\boxed{20\text{ litres}}
]

### Why 1:1 makes sense without even doing Alligation

Look at the concentrations:

```text
5% -------- 10% -------- 15%
       5%           5%
```

10% is **exactly halfway** between 5% and 15%.

So we need **equal amounts** of both.

For example:

**20 L of 15% + 20 L of 5%**

The average concentration is:

[
\frac{15+5}{2}=10%
]

✅ Therefore, **20 litres of the second solution**.

And notice how this connects perfectly with what we just learned:

> **Equal distance from the target → equal quantity.**
