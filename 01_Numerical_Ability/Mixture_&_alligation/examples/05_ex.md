Yep bro — this is the **same Alligation / Mixture topic** we were just doing. The only difference is that here they're asking for the **actual litres**, not just the ratio.

Let's go very slowly.

## 1. First understand what the question is saying

We have two acid solutions:

* **84% acid**
* **78% acid**

We mix them to get:

* **80% acid**
* Total quantity = **24 litres**

And the question asks:

> How many litres of the **84% solution** are needed?

So:

```text
84% solution  +  78% solution
              ↓
          80% solution
          Total = 24 L
```

---

## 2. Use Alligation

Put the percentages like this:

```text
        84%
          \ 
           \
            80%
           /
          /
        78%
```

Now calculate the differences:

### For 84%:

[
84-80=4
]

### For 78%:

[
80-78=2
]

Remember our previous discussion:

**The differences go to the opposite ingredient.**

So:

```text
        84%  ← gets 2
          \
           \
           80%
           /
          /
        78%  ← gets 4
```

Therefore:

[
\boxed{84% : 78% = 2:4}
]

Simplify:

[
\boxed{1:2}
]

---

## 3. Now use the total 24 litres

The ratio is:

[
1:2
]

That means there are:

[
1+2=3 \text{ total parts}
]

And those 3 parts together equal **24 litres**.

Therefore:

[
1\text{ part}=\frac{24}{3}=8\text{ litres}
]

The 84% solution represents **1 part**.

So:

[
\boxed{8\text{ litres}}
]

### ✅ Answer: **D) 8**

---

### 🔥 The pattern you should learn

For these questions:

**Step 1:** Identify the two concentrations.

**Step 2:** Identify the final concentration.

**Step 3:** Alligation → get the ratio.

**Step 4:** Use the total quantity to convert the ratio into litres/kg/etc.

For this question:

[
84% \quad\boxed{2}\quad :\quad\boxed{4}\quad 78%
]

[
=1:2
]

Total = 24 L

[
\frac{24}{1+2}\times1
=\boxed{8L}
]

So this is a **Mixture & Alligation question**, specifically an **Alligation + Total Quantity** type.




YES — this is the exact part you should understand. And honestly, **don't memorize "opposite direction" yet**. Let's understand *why*.

We have:

* 84% acid
* 78% acid
* Want 80%

Think about the distances:

```text
78% -------- 80% -------- 84%
       2%          4%
```

Now imagine you're mixing the two.

### Suppose we take equal amounts

Say:

**1 litre of 84% + 1 litre of 78%**

The average will be:

[
\frac{84+78}{2}=81%
]

But we want **80%**.

So we need to bring the average **down**.

How?

👉 We need **more of the 78% solution**.

So the amount of 78% must be **larger** than the amount of 84%.

---

### Now look at the distances

80% is:

* **4% away from 84%**
* **2% away from 78%**

```text
              4%
        ←──────────→
84% ---------------- 80% ---- 78%
                         ←──→
                           2%
```

The 84% solution is **farther away** from 80%.

Therefore, we need **less 84%**.

The 78% solution is **closer** to 80%.

Therefore, we need **more 78%**.

So:

[
\boxed{84% : 78% = 2:4}
]

or

[
\boxed{1:2}
]

---

## But WHY does the opposite happen mathematically?

Here's the simplest way to see it.

Suppose:

* (x) litres of 84%
* (y) litres of 78%

We want 80%.

The amount **above 80%** contributed by 84% is:

[
84-80=4
]

So every litre of 84% gives us **4% extra**.

The amount **below 80%** from 78% is:

[
80-78=2
]

Every litre of 78% gives us **2% shortage**.

For the final mixture to become exactly 80%:

> **Extra from 84% = Shortage from 78%**

Therefore:

[
4x=2y
]

Divide by 2:

[
2x=y
]

So:

[
x:y=1:2
]

That's where the **opposite** comes from.

### 🔥 Think of it as balancing

```text
84% gives +4
78% gives -2

       +4 × LESS amount
                =
       -2 × MORE amount
```

Because 84% is **farther from 80%**, we need **less of it**.

Because 78% is **closer to 80%**, we need **more of it**.

That's why:

[
\boxed{84%:78%=2:4=1:2}
]

So the "opposite direction" isn't some random alligation rule. **It's because the bigger difference needs a smaller quantity to balance the smaller difference.**
