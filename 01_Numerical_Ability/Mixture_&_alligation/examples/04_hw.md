You are remembering the alligation rule correctly. The ONLY thing we need to understand is **which number represents which quantity**.

### Look at the actual situation

We have:

```text
EXPENSIVE                 CHEAP
₹7.20                     ₹5.70
   \                       /
    \                     /
     \                   /
          ₹6.30
         MIXTURE
```

Now ask:

**If I use more ₹7.20 rice, will the mixture become more expensive or cheaper?**

More expensive rice → mixture moves **toward ₹7.20**.

But our mixture is ₹6.30, which is:

* ₹0.90 away from ₹7.20
* ₹0.60 away from ₹5.70

Look:

```text
₹5.70 -------- ₹6.30 -------- ₹7.20
       0.60          0.90
```

Now here's the key:

### Imagine putting 1 kg of each rice

Average:

\[
\frac{7.20+5.70}{2}=6.45
\]

But we need **₹6.30**, which is closer to **₹5.70**.

Therefore, we need **MORE cheap rice**.

So naturally:

```text
Expensive : Cheap
     LESS : MORE
```

Therefore it must be:

\[
\boxed{2:3}
\]

---

### Now your calculation

You did:

\[
7.20-6.30 : 6.30-5.70
\]

\[
=0.90:0.60
\]

\[
=3:2
\]

**The calculation itself is 100% correct.**

But those numbers mean:

```text
0.90 → quantity of CHEAP rice
0.60 → quantity of EXPENSIVE rice
```

So you have to write:

\[
\boxed{\text{Expensive : Cheap}=0.60:0.90}
\]

not

\[
0.90:0.60
\]

---

### 🧠 One sentence to remember

**In alligation, each difference goes to the OPPOSITE ingredient.**

```text
        ₹7.20
           \ 
            \  0.60  ← goes to ₹7.20
             \
             ₹6.30
             /
            /  0.90  ← goes to ₹5.70
           /
        ₹5.70
```

So:

\[
\boxed{₹7.20:₹5.70=0.60:0.90=2:3}
\]

Your **0.90 : 0.60 = 3 : 2** is not mathematically wrong — you just assigned the two differences to the **wrong ingredients**.
