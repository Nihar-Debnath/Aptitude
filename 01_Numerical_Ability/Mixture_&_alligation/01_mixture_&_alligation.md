In **Numerical Ability**, **Mixture & Alligation** is a topic where you deal with **mixing two or more things having different prices, concentrations, qualities, etc.**

The easiest way to understand it is:

### 1. Mixture

A **mixture** is simply the result of combining different quantities of substances.

For example:

* 2 kg rice at ₹40/kg
* 3 kg rice at ₹60/kg

After mixing:

**Total quantity = 2 + 3 = 5 kg**

**Total cost = 2×40 + 3×60 = ₹260**

So the mixture costs:

\[
\frac{260}{5}=\boxed{₹52/kg}
\]

This is called the **weighted average**.

---

### 2. Alligation

**Alligation is a shortcut** for finding the **ratio in which two things should be mixed** to get a desired average price/concentration.

For example:

> Rice costing ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg are mixed to get a mixture worth ₹50/kg. Find the ratio.

Use:

```text
        ₹40             ₹60
          \             /
           \           /
             ₹50
           /     \
       60-50     50-40
          10        10
```

So:

\[
\text{Ratio} = (60-50):(50-40)
\]

\[
=10:10
\]

\[
=\boxed{1:1}
\]

So **1 kg of ₹40 rice + 1 kg of ₹60 rice** gives ₹50/kg.

---

### The main Alligation formula

If:

* Cheaper value = **C**
* Dearer value = **D**
* Mean/mixture value = **M**

Then:

\[
\boxed{\text{Cheaper : Dearer}=(D-M):(M-C)}
\]

### Example

₹30/kg and ₹50/kg sugar are mixed to get ₹38/kg.

\[
30 \qquad 50
\]

\[
\quad 38
\]

Cross-difference:

\[
50-38=12
\]

\[
38-30=8
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{30:50=12:8=3:2}
\]

So you need **3 parts of ₹30 sugar and 2 parts of ₹50 sugar**.

---

### What kind of questions come in exams?

You'll usually see:

1. **Price mixtures** — two things with different prices.
2. **Concentration mixtures** — e.g. milk/water, alcohol/water.
3. **Finding the ratio** in which two substances should be mixed.
4. **Finding the mean price/concentration** of a mixture.
5. **Replacement questions** — remove some mixture and add another substance.
6. **Profit/loss + mixture** combined questions.

For your aptitude exams like **TCS/Cognizant**, the most important thing is to first understand **weighted average → alligation → replacement**, in that order.
