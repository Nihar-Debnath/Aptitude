Absolutely! 🔥 **Simple Interest (SI) and Compound Interest (CI)** is a very important topic for numerical ability, especially for exams like **TCS, Cognizant, banking exams, etc.**

Let's learn it from **zero**, step-by-step, and focus on the **short tricks used in aptitude questions**.

## 1. First understand the basic terms

Suppose you borrow/invest **₹10,000** at **10% interest per year**.

There are 4 terms you need:

| Term              | Meaning                      |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Principal (P)** | Original amount = ₹10,000    |
| **Rate (R)**      | Interest rate = 10% per year |
| **Time (T)**      | Time = number of years       |
| **Amount (A)**    | Principal + Interest         |

So:

**Interest = Amount − Principal**

---

# 2. Simple Interest

In **Simple Interest**, interest is calculated **only on the original principal**.


For SI, the formula you need is:

\[
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
\]

And:

\[
A=P+SI
\]

### Example

₹10,000 is invested at **10% SI for 3 years**.

\[
SI=\frac{10000\times10\times3}{100}
\]

\[
SI=3000
\]

Therefore:

\[
A=10000+3000=\boxed{₹13,000}
\]

### The important idea:

Every year, you get the **same interest**.

* Year 1 → ₹1,000
* Year 2 → ₹1,000
* Year 3 → ₹1,000

Total = ₹3,000.

---

# 3. Compound Interest

This is where things become interesting. 😎

In **Compound Interest**, the interest itself starts earning interest.

Suppose:

**P = ₹10,000**
**R = 10%**
**T = 3 years**

### Year 1

Interest:

\[
10%\text{ of }10000=1000
\]

New amount:

\[
10000+1000=11000
\]

### Year 2

Now interest is calculated on **₹11,000**, not ₹10,000.

\[
10%\text{ of }11000=1100
\]

New amount:

\[
11000+1100=12100
\]

### Year 3

Now:

\[
10%\text{ of }12100=1210
\]

New amount:

\[
12100+1210=13310
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{A=₹13,310}
\]

and

\[
CI=13310-10000=\boxed{₹3,310}
\]

---

# 4. The BIG difference

Remember this:

### Simple Interest

> Interest is always calculated on **P**.

### Compound Interest

> Interest is calculated on the **previous year's amount**.

That's the entire fundamental difference.

|                 |     SI |     CI |
| --------------- | -----: | -----: |
| Year 1 interest | ₹1,000 | ₹1,000 |
| Year 2 interest | ₹1,000 | ₹1,100 |
| Year 3 interest | ₹1,000 | ₹1,210 |
| Total interest  | ₹3,000 | ₹3,310 |

---

# 5. Compound Interest Formula

For CI:

\[
A=P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^T
\]

Then:

\[
CI=A-P
\]

So the standard process is:

**Step 1:** Find Amount

\[
A=P\left(1+\frac R{100}\right)^T
\]

**Step 2:** Find CI

\[
CI=A-P
\]
