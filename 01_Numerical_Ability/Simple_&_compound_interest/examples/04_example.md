### Question

> Find SI on ₹3200 at 5% p.a. from **4 April to 16 June**.

We need to find **how many days** are between the two dates.

---

## Step 1: Count the days

From **4 April → 16 June**.

Break it month-wise:

### April

April has 30 days.

From 4 April to 30 April:

[
30-4=\boxed{26\text{ days}}
]

### May

Whole May:

[
\boxed{31\text{ days}}
]

### June

Up to 16 June:

[
\boxed{16\text{ days}}
]

Therefore:

[
26+31+16=\boxed{73\text{ days}}
]

---

## Step 2: Use your FAST trick 🔥

Rate = **5% per year**

1 year = 365 days.

As you learned:

[
365\div5=73
]

Therefore:

[
\boxed{73\text{ days}=1%}
]

And our time is exactly **73 days**.

So the overall interest is:

[
\boxed{1%}
]

of ₹3200.

---

## Step 3: Find 1% of ₹3200

[
SI=1%\text{ of }3200
]

[
=\frac{1}{100}\times3200
]

[
\boxed{SI=₹32}
]

# ✅ Final Answer

[
\boxed{\text{Simple Interest}=₹32}
]

**4 April → 16 June**

[

### ⚠️ One thing to remember about date questions

For these questions, **don't include the starting date** when counting the duration.

So:

**4 April → 16 June = 73 days**, not 74.

That's why this question was designed so nicely: **73 days exactly corresponds to 1% at 5% p.a.** 😄

Because we're calculating the **elapsed time between two dates**, not counting how many calendar dates are written down.

Think of it like a timeline:

**4 April → 5 April**

How much time passed?

[
\boxed{1\text{ day}}
]

Not 2 days.

If you counted both dates, you'd say "4th and 5th = 2 days," but only **one full day passed**.

### Apply the same idea here

From **4 April → 16 June**:

#### April

Starting at 4 April, we move forward to 30 April:

[
30-4=26
]

#### May

[
31
]

#### June

We move from the start of June to 16 June:

[
16
]

Therefore:

[
26+31+16=73
]

So:

[
\boxed{4\text{ April}\rightarrow16\text{ June}=73\text{ days}}
]

### Another very simple way to see it

Imagine borrowing money on **4 April**.

At the moment you borrow it, **zero days of interest have passed**.

After:

* 1 day → 5 April
* 2 days → 6 April
* ...
* 73 days → 16 June

So interest is charged for **73 days**.

### ⚠️ But there's an important convention

In some financial contexts, the exact day-count convention can differ, and some aptitude questions may explicitly tell you to count both dates. **For standard TCS/aptitude SI date questions, use the elapsed-day method unless the question specifies otherwise.**
