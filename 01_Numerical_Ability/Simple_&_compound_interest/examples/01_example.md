Sure. Let's solve the **exact same question in both methods** so you can see that they give the same answer.

### Question

Find the **Amount and Simple Interest** on ₹6,000 at **6% per annum for 2 years**.

Given:

* (P = ₹6000)
* (R = 6%)
* (T = 2) years

---

## Method 1 — Normal SI formula

Formula:

\[
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
\]

Put the values:

\[
SI=\frac{6000\times6\times2}{100}
\]

\[
SI=60\times6\times2
\]

\[
\boxed{SI=₹720}
\]

Now:

\[
A=P+SI
\]

\[
A=6000+720
\]

\[
\boxed{A=₹6720}
\]

---

# Method 2 — The shortcut you just learned

Your teacher's trick is:

\[
\boxed{SI=(RT)%\text{ of }P}
\]

### Step 1: Find (RT)

\[
R\times T=6\times2=12
\]

So the **overall interest is 12%**.

### Step 2: Find 12% of Principal

\[
SI=12%\text{ of }6000
\]

\[
=\frac{12}{100}\times6000
\]

\[
\boxed{SI=₹720}
\]

### Step 3: Find Amount

\[
A=P+SI
\]

\[
A=6000+720
\]

\[
\boxed{A=₹6720}
\]

---

## 🔥 See the difference

**Normal method:**

\[
SI=\frac{6000\times6\times2}{100}
\]

**Shortcut method:**

\[
6\times2=12%
\]

Then:

\[
12%\text{ of }6000=720
\]

So the shortcut is basically:

> **First convert (R) and (T) into one overall percentage, then take that percentage of P.**

For simple interest, this is often **much faster in aptitude questions**.
