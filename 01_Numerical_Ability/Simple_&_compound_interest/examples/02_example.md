Yep — the only new thing here is that **time is given in months**, while the rate is **per annum (per year)**.

### Given

* (P = ₹6000)
* (R = 6%) per annum
* (T = 8) months

### Step 1: Convert months into years

Since:

\[
12\text{ months}=1\text{ year}
\]

Therefore:

\[
8\text{ months}=\frac{8}{12}=\frac23\text{ year}
\]

---

## Using your shortcut method

Remember:

\[
SI=(RT)%\text{ of }P
\]

Here:

\[
R=6%,\qquad T=\frac23
\]

So:

\[
RT=6\times\frac23=4
\]

Therefore, the **overall interest = 4%**.

Now find 4% of ₹6000:

\[
SI=4%\text{ of }6000
\]

\[
=\frac4{100}\times6000
\]

\[
\boxed{SI=₹240}
\]

### Amount

\[
A=P+SI
\]

\[
A=6000+240
\]

\[
\boxed{A=₹6240}
\]

### Final answer

\[
\boxed{\text{Simple Interest}=₹240}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{Amount}=₹6240}
\]

**The key trick:** whenever the rate is **per annum**, convert the time into **years** first.
So **8 months → (8/12) years**.

YES! 😄 There is an even easier way for **month-based SI questions**.

Instead of converting months into years, convert the **annual rate into monthly rate**.

### Given:

* (P = ₹6000)
* (R = 6%) per annum
* Time = 8 months

Since **6% is for 12 months**:

[
\text{1 month interest rate}=\frac{6}{12}=0.5%
]

So for **8 months**:

[
0.5%\times8=\boxed{4%}
]

Now simply:

[
SI=4%\text{ of }6000
]

[
=\frac4{100}\times6000
]

[
\boxed{SI=₹240}
]

And:

[
A=6000+240=\boxed{₹6240}
]

### 🔥 Your fastest method

For **SI when time is in months**:

[
\boxed{\text{Overall rate}=\frac{R\times\text{months}}{12}}
]

Here:

[
\frac{6\times8}{12}=4%
]

Then:

[
\boxed{SI=4%\text{ of }6000=₹240}
]

So you **don't really need to write (8/12) years** every time. Just do:

**6 × 8 ÷ 12 = 4% → 4% of 6000 = ₹240.**
