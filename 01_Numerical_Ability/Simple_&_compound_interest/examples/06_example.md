Absolutely. Let's do this **two ways** — the normal formula and the **2 : 1 compound-interest trick** you just learned.

## Given

\[
P=₹45,000
\]

\[
R=10%
\]

\[
T=2\text{ years}
\]

We need **Compound Interest (CI)**.

---

# Method 1 — Normal Compound Interest Formula

Formula:

\[
A=P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^T
\]

Put the values:

\[
A=45000\left(1+\frac{10}{100}\right)^2
\]

\[
=45000(1.1)^2
\]

\[
=45000\times1.21
\]

\[
A=₹54,450
\]

Now:

\[
CI=A-P
\]

\[
CI=54450-45000
\]

\[
\boxed{CI=₹9,450}
\]

---

# Method 2 — Your EASY 2 : 1 Trick 🔥

For **2 years**, the pattern is:

\[
\boxed{2:1}
\]

### First part

10% of ₹45,000:

\[
10%\text{ of }45000=4500
\]

The first coefficient is **2**:

\[
4500\times2=9000
\]

### Second part

Now take 10% of ₹4,500:

\[
10%\text{ of }4500=450
\]

The second coefficient is **1**:

\[
450\times1=450
\]

Now add:

\[
CI=9000+450
\]

\[
\boxed{CI=₹9,450}
\]

---

## 🔥 See the trick visually

For **2 years → 2 : 1**

\[
\boxed{4500\times2+450\times1}
\]

\[
=9000+450
\]

\[
\boxed{₹9,450}
\]

Both methods give the same answer.

### Remember:

| Years   | CI pattern        |
| ------- | ----------------- |
| 2 years | **2 : 1**         |
| 3 years | **3 : 3 : 1**     |
| 4 years | **4 : 6 : 4 : 1** |

So when you see **2 years at 10%**, this shortcut can be **much faster** than calculating the entire amount.
