### For Compound Interest

If the question says:

> **10% p.a., compounded half-yearly**

THEN it is telling you the **compounding frequency**.

So:

* Half-yearly → rate ÷ 2, periods × 2
* Quarterly → rate ÷ 4, periods × 4
* Monthly → rate ÷ 12, periods × 12

For example:

> 10% CI for 2 years, compounded half-yearly

\[
R=10\div2=5%
\]

\[
T=2\times2=4\text{ periods}
\]

---

### For Simple Interest

There is **no compounding frequency**.

If the question says:

> 10% SI for 6 months

you simply convert the time:

\[
T=\frac{6}{12}=\frac12\text{ year}
\]

Then use:

\[
SI=\frac{P\times R\times T}{100}
\]

Similarly:

> 73 days

\[
T=\frac{73}{365}\text{ year}
\]

---

### 🔥 So remember this

**CI + "compounded ___"**

→ Change **rate and number of periods**.

**SI + months/days**

→ Just **convert the time into years**.

One more thing: **CI can also have months/days without saying "compounded monthly."** In that case, don't automatically do `÷12 ×12`. The wording has to actually specify the compounding frequency.
