This is **not a standard Profit & Loss formula question**.

It belongs to the category:

> **Profit & Loss → Successive Transactions / Average Profit**

Some books also call it:

* **Average Annual Profit**
* **Multiple Transactions**
* **Profit over Multiple Years**
* **Successive Profit Problems**

---

## How to Identify This Type

Whenever you see words like:

* "buys again"
* "next year"
* "again sells"
* "first transaction"
* "second transaction"
* "average profit"
* "average annual profit"

you should think:

> **Solve each transaction separately, then take the average.**

---

## Structure of This Question

### Year 1

Bought for

```text
₹800
```

Sold at

```text
10% Profit
```

Profit

\[
800\times10%=80
\]

---

### Year 2

Now the bicycle price increases by

```text
20%
```

New Cost Price

\[
800\times1.2=960
\]

Sold at

```text
30% Profit
```

Profit

\[
960\times30%=288
\]

---

### Average Annual Profit

There are **2 years**.

Total profit

\[
80+288=368
\]

Average annual profit

\[
\frac{368}{2}=184
\]

✅ **Answer = C. 184**

## For Placement Exams

This type is **less common** than topics like Marked Price, Discount, or False Weight, but it does appear in **TCS, Infosys, and some campus placement papers**.

The key idea is:

* Don't try to find an "average percentage."
* Calculate the **actual profit in each transaction**, add them, and then divide by the number of years (or transactions, depending on what the question asks).

So whenever you see **"average annual profit"**, think:

\[
\boxed{\text{Average Annual Profit}=\frac{\text{Total Profit of All Years}}{\text{Number of Years}}}
\]

This is exactly what the examiner is testing in this question.
