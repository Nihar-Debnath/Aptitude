False Weight Problems (also called **Dishonest Shopkeeper Problems**) are one of the most common advanced Profit & Loss concepts in placement exams.

The trick is that **the shopkeeper cheats by using incorrect weights or measures**. Even if he sells at the cost price, he still earns a profit because he gives **less quantity than he claims**.

---

# What is a False Weight Problem?

Normally,

```text
Customer pays for 1 kg

↓

Customer receives 1 kg
```

But in a false weight problem,

```text
Customer pays for 1 kg

↓

Customer receives only 900 g
```

The customer thinks they bought 1 kg, but actually gets less.

The shopkeeper keeps the remaining 100 g as hidden profit.

---

# Why is it a Profit & Loss Question?

Suppose the shopkeeper buys

```text
1 kg rice = ₹100
```

He tells the customer:

```text
1 kg rice = ₹100
```

But he actually gives only

```text
900 g
```

### Actual cost to the shopkeeper

900 g costs

[
100\times\frac{900}{1000}
=90
]

### Money received

₹100

### Profit

[
100-90=10
]

Profit = ₹10

---

# Visual Representation

```text
Customer thinks

Pays ₹100

↓

Gets 1 kg

Reality

Pays ₹100

↓

Gets only 900 g
```

The shopkeeper saves

```text
100 g
```

which becomes extra profit.

---

# Formula

If

Actual weight = A

False weight = F

then

[
\boxed{
Profit%=
\frac{A-F}{F}\times100
}
]

Notice that the denominator is the **false weight (what is actually given)**.

---

## Example 1

A dishonest shopkeeper uses a **900 g weight instead of 1 kg** while selling goods at the cost price.

Find his profit%.

### Step 1

Actual weight

1000 g

False weight

900 g

### Step 2

Difference

100 g

### Step 3

Profit %

[
\frac{100}{900}\times100
]

[
=\frac19\times100
]

[
=11.11%
]

**Answer = 11⅑% Profit**

---

## Example 2

He uses an **800 g weight instead of 1 kg**.

Profit %

[
\frac{200}{800}\times100
]

[
=\frac14\times100
]

[
25%
]

---

## Example 3

He gives only **750 g instead of 1 kg**.

Profit %

[
\frac{250}{750}\times100
]

[
=\frac13\times100
]

[
33.33%
]

# When Both Discount and False Weight Are Used

These are the toughest questions.

Example:

```text
Cost Price = ₹100/kg

Marked Price = ₹120/kg

Discount = 10%

Uses a 900 g weight.
```

### Step 1

Find the selling price after discount.

[
SP
==

# 120\times0.9

108
]

The customer pays ₹108 for what they think is **1 kg**.

### Step 2

But the shopkeeper gives only 900 g.

Cost of 900 g

[
100\times\frac{900}{1000}
=90
]

### Step 3

Profit

[
108-90=18
]

Profit %

[
\frac{18}{90}\times100
=20%
]

Even after giving a discount, the shopkeeper still earns **20% profit** because of the false weight.

# Types of False Weight Questions

### Type 1

Selling at **Cost Price** but using false weight.

➡️ Use the false-weight formula directly.

---

### Type 2

Selling at a **Profit** and also using false weight.

Example:

```text
20% Profit

and

900 g instead of 1 kg
```

Here, profit comes from **both**:

* Higher selling price
* Less quantity given

---

### Type 3

Giving a **Discount** but using false weight.

Example:

```text
10% Discount

900 g instead of 1 kg
```

The apparent discount may still result in an overall profit because of the reduced quantity.

---

### Type 4

Using both **Marked Price + Discount + False Weight**.

These are the most advanced placement questions and combine all the concepts you've learned:

* Cost Price (CP)
* Marked Price (MP/MRP)
* Discount
* Selling Price (SP)
* False Weight

---

## Exam Tip

Whenever you see words like:

* "dishonest shopkeeper"
* "uses a false weight"
* "uses a 900 g weight instead of 1 kg"
* "short weight"
* "less quantity"
* "faulty weighing machine"

immediately think:

> **The shopkeeper is earning extra profit by giving less quantity than the customer paid for.**

This is one of the favorite advanced question types in TCS, Cognizant, Wipro, Accenture, Capgemini, and similar placement exams because it combines percentage, ratio, and profit & loss concepts into a single problem.
