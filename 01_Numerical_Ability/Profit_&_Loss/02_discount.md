# 21. Marked Price (MP)

## What is Marked Price?

The price **printed on the product** before any discount is offered.

It is also called

* Marked Price (MP)
* List Price (LP)
* Catalogue Price
* Sticker Price

Sometimes people say **MRP**, but remember:

> **MRP (Maximum Retail Price)** is the maximum legal price that can be charged in countries like India.
>
> In aptitude questions, unless specified otherwise, **MP and MRP are generally treated as the same value.**

---

### Example

A shirt has

```text
MRP = ₹1000
```

The shopkeeper offers

```text
20% Discount
```

Customer pays

```text
₹800
```

Here

* MP (MRP) = ₹1000
* Discount = ₹200
* SP = ₹800

---

# 22. Discount

A discount is the reduction given on the **Marked Price**.

Formula

[
\boxed{
Discount = MP - SP
}
]

---

Example

MP = ₹1500

SP = ₹1200

Discount

[
1500-1200=300
]

Discount = ₹300

---

# 23. Discount Percentage

Unlike profit and loss,

Discount % is always calculated on **Marked Price**, NOT Cost Price.

Formula

[
\boxed{
Discount%=
\frac{Discount}{MP}\times100
}
]

or

[
\boxed{
Discount%=
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
}
]

---

Example

MP = 2000

SP = 1600

Discount

400

[
\frac{400}{2000}\times100
=20%
]

---

# 24. Selling Price after Discount

Formula

[
\boxed{
SP=
MP\left(1-\frac{Discount%}{100}\right)
}
]

---

Example

MP = ₹500

Discount = 20%

[
SP
==

500\times0.8
=400
]

---

# 25. Finding Marked Price

Formula

[
\boxed{
MP=
\frac{SP\times100}
{100-Discount%}
}
]

---

Example

SP = ₹720

Discount = 10%

[
MP
==

\frac{720\times100}{90}
=800
]

# 27. Complete Flow of a Product

A product normally goes through these stages:

```text
Manufacturer

↓

Cost Price (CP)

↓

Shopkeeper adds profit

↓

Marked Price (MP)

↓

Discount is offered

↓

Selling Price (SP)

↓

Customer buys
```

Notice:

* **Profit/Loss** compares **CP** and **SP**.
* **Discount** compares **MP** and **SP**.

| Concept    | Formula Base |
| ---------- | ------------ |
| Profit %   | Cost Price   |
| Loss %     | Cost Price   |
| Discount % | Marked Price |

This is one of the most common areas where students make mistakes.

# 31. Complete Formula Sheet

## Profit

[
Profit=SP-CP
]

## Loss

[
Loss=CP-SP
]

## Profit %

[
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
]

## Loss %

[
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
]

## Discount

[
Discount=MP-SP
]

## Discount %

[
\frac{MP-SP}{MP}\times100
]

## Selling Price after Profit

[
SP=CP\left(1+\frac{Profit}{100}\right)
]

## Selling Price after Loss

[
SP=CP\left(1-\frac{Loss}{100}\right)
]

## Selling Price after Discount

[
SP=MP\left(1-\frac{Discount}{100}\right)
]

## Cost Price

[
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100+Profit%}
]

[
CP=\frac{SP\times100}{100-Loss%}
]

## Marked Price

[
MP=\frac{SP\times100}{100-Discount%}
]

# 32. Exam Strategy

When solving any Profit & Loss question, first identify **which two prices are being compared**:

| Comparison  | Formula                                                                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CP ↔ SP** | Profit or Loss                                                                |
| **MP ↔ SP** | Discount                                                                      |
| **CP ↔ MP** | Markup (how much the shopkeeper increased the price before giving a discount) |

A useful way to remember the flow is:

```text
CP
   │
   │ (+ Markup)
   ▼
MP (or MRP)
   │
   │ (- Discount)
   ▼
SP
```

Then:

* Compare **CP and SP** → Profit/Loss.
* Compare **MP and SP** → Discount.
* Compare **CP and MP** → Markup.
