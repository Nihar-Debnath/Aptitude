😂 This one is actually a **TRICK question**, not an algebra-identity question.

Let's read it carefully.

> Calculate the product
> \[
> (x-a)(x-b)(x-c)\ldots(x-z)
> \]
> where the factors continue from **a through z**, including the factor **((x-x))**.

---

## Step 1: Understand what the dots mean

They are saying:

\[
(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)(x-d)\ldots(x-z)
\]

So there is a factor for **every letter**:

\[
a,b,c,d,e,\ldots,x,y,z
\]

That means one of the factors is:

\[
\boxed{(x-x)}
\]

---

## Step 2: Calculate that factor

\[
x-x=0
\]

So the entire expression becomes:

\[
(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)\cdots\boxed{(x-x)}\cdots(x-z)
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{Product}=\text{something}\times0\times\text{something}
\]

And we know:

\[
\boxed{\text{Anything}\times0=0}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{0}
\]

### ✅ Answer: **A) 0**

---

# 🧠 The trick you should recognize

This isn't asking you to actually multiply **26 factors**.

Whenever you see a huge multiplication expression, **look for a zero factor first**.

For example:

\[
17\times25\times89\times0\times734\times92
\]

You don't calculate anything.

Immediately:

\[
\boxed{0}
\]

Same idea here.

---

### One important distinction

This question is **not really an "algebraic identity" question** like:

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

It's more of an **algebraic expression / simplification / logical observation** question.

And honestly, for your aptitude prep, this is exactly the kind of question where **smart observation beats calculation**. 🔥
