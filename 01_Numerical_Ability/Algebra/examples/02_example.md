Yes — **this is a different and very important type of Algebra question**.

This is called:

# 🟢 Word Problems → Forming a Quadratic Equation

The question is **not asking you to solve the quadratic equation**.

It's asking:

> **"What is the quadratic equation?"**

So our job is to convert the rectangle information into an equation.

---

## Step 1: Identify what we don't know

The rectangle has:

* Length = ?
* Breadth = ?

Let the **breadth = (x)**.

That's usually the easiest choice because the question tells us the length **in terms of breadth**.

---

## Step 2: Translate the length statement

The question says:

> Length is **4 times the breadth plus 4 cm**.

Breadth:

\[
x
\]

4 times breadth:

\[
4x
\]

Plus 4:

\[
4x+4
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Length}=4x+4}
\]

---

## Step 3: Use the area formula

For a rectangle:

\[
\boxed{Area=Length\times Breadth}
\]

The area is given as:

\[
154
\]

So:

\[
(4x+4)(x)=154
\]

---

## Step 4: Expand

\[
4x^2+4x=154
\]

Move everything to one side:

\[
4x^2+4x-154=0
\]

We can divide everything by 2:

\[
\boxed{2x^2+2x-77=0}
\]

### ✅ That's the quadratic equation.

---

# 🧠 Why is it called "quadratic"?

Look at the equation:

\[
2x^2+2x-77=0
\]

The highest power of (x) is:

\[
x^2
\]

That's why it's a **quadratic equation**.

The general form is:

\[
\boxed{ax^2+bx+c=0}
\]

Here:

\[
a=2,\quad b=2,\quad c=-77
\]

---

# 🔥 The pattern you should recognize

This type of question usually follows:

### ① Choose the unknown

Usually:

\[
\text{Breadth}=x
\]

### ② Express the other quantity using (x)

For example:

\[
\text{Length}=4x+4
\]

### ③ Use the given formula

Rectangle:

\[
Area=L\times B
\]

### ④ Substitute

\[
(4x+4)x=154
\]

### ⑤ Rearrange into:

\[
\boxed{ax^2+bx+c=0}
\]

That's the entire process.

---

# Let's see why it becomes quadratic

This is the key insight.

You have:

\[
Length=4x+4
\]

and:

\[
Breadth=x
\]

Area is:

\[
Length\times Breadth
\]

So:

\[
(4x+4)\times x
\]

When you multiply:

\[
4x\times x=4x^2
\]

**BOOM → (x^2)**

That's why a quadratic equation appears.

---

# 🚨 This is a separate Algebra question type

So far you've encountered:

### Type 1 — Simple equations

\[
2x+5=25
\]

### Type 2 — Algebraic identities

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

### Type 3 — Algebraic expression tricks

Like:

\[
(x-a)(x-b)\cdots(x-x)\cdots(x-z)=0
\]

### 🟢 Type 4 — Word problems forming quadratic equations

Like this rectangle problem:

\[
\boxed{2x^2+2x-77=0}
\]

And this category can include:

* rectangle length/breadth
* consecutive numbers
* product of two numbers
* age problems
* area problems
* speed/distance problems
* number problems

where the unknown ends up having an (x^2) term.

---

## One important thing for your exam

Don't confuse these two questions:

### "What is the quadratic equation?"

You **only form the equation**.

For this question:

\[
\boxed{2x^2+2x-77=0}
\]

### "Find the dimensions"

Then you would have to **solve** the quadratic equation and find (x), then calculate length.

So this question is testing your ability to **convert a word problem into a quadratic equation**, not necessarily your quadratic-solving ability.
