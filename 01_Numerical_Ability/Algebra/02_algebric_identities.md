Yes! This is a **very important Algebra topic for aptitude**, and the good news is that you don't need to memorize a huge list. For TCS-style Numerical Ability, a few identities cover most questions.

The key is to understand **why the identities work**, not just memorize them.

---

# 1. What is an algebraic identity?

An **identity** is an equation that is true for **every possible value** of the variables.

For example:

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

Whether (a=2,b=3), or (a=10,b=7), or anything else, it always works.

That's why it's called an **identity**.

---

# 2. Identity #1 — Square of a sum

The most important one:


\[
\boxed{(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2}
\]

### Don't memorize it blindly.

Let's expand it.

\[
(a+b)^2
\]

means:

\[
(a+b)(a+b)
\]

Now multiply everything:

\[
a\times a=a^2
\]

\[
a\times b=ab
\]

\[
b\times a=ab
\]

\[
b\times b=b^2
\]

So:

\[
a^2+ab+ab+b^2
\]

Since:

\[
ab+ab=2ab
\]

we get:

\[
\boxed{a^2+2ab+b^2}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2}
\]

---

# 3. How to use it in questions

Suppose:

\[
(103)^2
\]

You could multiply:

\[
103\times103
\]

But algebraic identity gives us a shortcut.

Write:

\[
103=100+3
\]

Therefore:

\[
103^2=(100+3)^2
\]

Use:

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

Here:

\[
a=100,\quad b=3
\]

Therefore:

\[
100^2+2(100)(3)+3^2
\]

\[
=10000+600+9
\]

\[
\boxed{10609}
\]

🔥 Much faster.

---

# 4. Another example

Find:

\[
52^2
\]

Write:

\[
52=50+2
\]

Therefore:

\[
52^2=(50+2)^2
\]

\[
=50^2+2(50)(2)+2^2
\]

\[
=2500+200+4
\]

\[
\boxed{2704}
\]

---

# 5. Identity #2 — Square of a difference

The second important one:


\[
\boxed{(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2}
\]

Notice something important:

### Plus version:

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+\boxed{2ab}+b^2
\]

### Minus version:

\[
(a-b)^2=a^2-\boxed{2ab}+b^2
\]

Only the **middle term changes sign**.

The final (b^2) is still positive.

---

# 6. Example: (97^2)

Instead of doing:

\[
97\times97
\]

write:

\[
97=100-3
\]

Therefore:

\[
97^2=(100-3)^2
\]

Using:

\[
(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
\]

we get:

\[
100^2-2(100)(3)+3^2
\]

\[
=10000-600+9
\]

\[
\boxed{9409}
\]

This is **extremely useful for aptitude** because numbers close to 10, 100, 1000 etc. become easy.

---

# 7. Example: (48^2)

Write:

\[
48=50-2
\]

Therefore:

\[
48^2=(50-2)^2
\]

\[
=50^2-2(50)(2)+2^2
\]

\[
=2500-200+4
\]

\[
\boxed{2304}
\]

---

# 8. Identity #3 — Difference of squares

This one is VERY important:

\[
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
\]

Notice:

### Square minus square

becomes:

### Difference × Sum

\[
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
\]

---

# 9. Why does it work?

Take:

\[
(a-b)(a+b)
\]

Multiply:

\[
a(a+b)-b(a+b)
\]

\[
=a^2+ab-ab-b^2
\]

The middle terms cancel:

\[
+ab-ab=0
\]

So:

\[
=a^2-b^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{(a-b)(a+b)=a^2-b^2}
\]

---

# 10. Example: (101^2-99^2)

If you calculate both separately:

\[
101^2-99^2
\]

that's unnecessary.

Notice:

\[
101^2-99^2
\]

is:

\[
a^2-b^2
\]

So:

\[
=(101-99)(101+99)
\]

\[
=2\times200
\]

\[
\boxed{400}
\]

🔥 That's the power of identities.

---

# 11. Another example

Find:

\[
52^2-48^2
\]

Use:

\[
a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)
\]

Therefore:

\[
=(52-48)(52+48)
\]

\[
=4\times100
\]

\[
\boxed{400}
\]

No need to calculate (52^2) or (48^2).

---

# 12. The 3 identities you MUST know

For your aptitude exam, put these at the top of your notes:

### Identity 1

\[
\boxed{(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2}
\]

### Identity 2

\[
\boxed{(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2}
\]

### Identity 3

\[
\boxed{a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)}
\]

These three are the **core**.

---

# 13. But there's another extremely useful form

Sometimes questions give you:

\[
a^2+b^2
\]

We know:

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab}
\]

Similarly:

\[
\boxed{a^2+b^2=(a-b)^2+2ab}
\]

These aren't new identities to memorize separately. They're just rearrangements of the first two.

---

# 14. Very common aptitude question

> If (x+y=10) and (xy=21), find (x^2+y^2).

This is EXACTLY where identities become useful.

We know:

\[
(x+y)^2=x^2+2xy+y^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
x^2+y^2=(x+y)^2-2xy
\]

Given:

\[
x+y=10
\]

and:

\[
xy=21
\]

Substitute:

\[
x^2+y^2=10^2-2(21)
\]

\[
=100-42
\]

\[
\boxed{58}
\]

Notice what happened:

We **never needed to find (x) or (y)**.

That's a major aptitude trick.

---

# 15. Another important question

> If (x-y=8) and (xy=15), find (x^2+y^2).

Use:

\[
(x-y)^2=x^2-2xy+y^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
x^2+y^2=(x-y)^2+2xy
\]

Substitute:

\[
=8^2+2(15)
\]

\[
=64+30
\]

\[
\boxed{94}
\]

---

# 16. Finding (x^2+y^2) — remember this pattern

If the question gives:

### (x+y) and (xy)

Use:

\[
\boxed{x^2+y^2=(x+y)^2-2xy}
\]

---

### (x-y) and (xy)

Use:

\[
\boxed{x^2+y^2=(x-y)^2+2xy}
\]

This type is **very likely to appear in aptitude**.

---

# 17. Finding (x^2-y^2)

Suppose:

\[
x+y=15
\]

and:

\[
x-y=5
\]

Find:

\[
x^2-y^2
\]

Don't find (x) and (y).

Use:

\[
\boxed{x^2-y^2=(x+y)(x-y)}
\]

Therefore:

\[
=15\times5
\]

\[
\boxed{75}
\]

Super easy.

---

# 18. Another common type

> If (x+y=12) and (x-y=4), find (x) and (y).

We have:

\[
x+y=12
\]

\[
x-y=4
\]

Add both equations:

\[
2x=16
\]

Therefore:

\[
x=8
\]

Now:

\[
8+y=12
\]

\[
y=4
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{x=8,\ y=4}
\]

---

# 19. What about cubes?

There are also identities involving cubes. These are worth learning **after** the three square identities.

### Sum of cubes

\[
\boxed{a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)}
\]

### Difference of cubes

\[
\boxed{a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)}
\]

For example:

\[
8^3+2^3
\]

Instead of calculating:

\[
512+8=520
\]

we can use:

\[
8^3+2^3
\]

\[
=(8+2)(8^2-8\times2+2^2)
\]

\[
=10(64-16+4)
\]

\[
=10\times52
\]

\[
\boxed{520}
\]

You don't need to obsess over cubes yet. **Master the first three first.**

---

# 🧠 How to recognize which identity to use

This is probably the most important part.

When you see:

### Something like

\[
(50+3)^2
\]

Think:

\[
\boxed{(a+b)^2}
\]

---

### Something like

\[
(50-3)^2
\]

Think:

\[
\boxed{(a-b)^2}
\]

---

### Something like

\[
100^2-98^2
\]

Think:

\[
\boxed{a^2-b^2}
\]

---

### Something like

\[
x+y,\ xy
\]

and they ask for:

\[
x^2+y^2
\]

Think:

\[
\boxed{(x+y)^2=x^2+2xy+y^2}
\]

---

### Something like

\[
x-y,\ xy
\]

and they ask for:

\[
x^2+y^2
\]

Think:

\[
\boxed{(x-y)^2=x^2-2xy+y^2}
\]
