# 📊 Average — Numerical Ability

### 1. What is Average?

Average simply means:

> **If we distribute the total equally among all items, how much does each item get?**

For example:

Numbers: **10, 20, 30**

Total = 10 + 20 + 30 = **60**

There are 3 numbers.

So average = 60 ÷ 3 = **20**

### ⭐ The main formula

\[
\boxed{\text{Average}=\frac{\text{Sum of all values}}{\text{Number of values}}}
\]

And the two rearrangements are extremely important:

\[
\boxed{\text{Sum}=\text{Average}\times\text{Number of values}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{Number of values}=\frac{\text{Sum}}{\text{Average}}}
\]

---

# 2. The most important trick

Suppose the average of **5 numbers is 20**.

Don't calculate everything individually.

Immediately:

\[
\text{Sum}=20\times5=100
\]

So whenever you see:

> **Average + Number of items**

Think:

### **TOTAL = Average × Number**

This is the foundation of almost every average question.

---

# 3. Adding a new number

Suppose:

> Average of 5 numbers is 20. A new number 30 is added. Find the new average.

### Step 1: Find old total

\[
20\times5=100
\]

### Step 2: Add new number

\[
100+30=130
\]

### Step 3: Number of items becomes 6

\[
\text{New Average}=\frac{130}{6}
\]

\[
\boxed{21.67}
\]

---

# 4. Removing a number

Suppose:

> Average of 6 numbers is 25. One number, 30, is removed. Find the new average.

Old total:

\[
25\times6=150
\]

Remove 30:

\[
150-30=120
\]

Numbers remaining:

\[
6-1=5
\]

New average:

\[
\frac{120}{5}=\boxed{24}
\]

---

# 5. Adding multiple numbers

Suppose:

> Average of 10 students is 40. Two students with marks 50 and 60 join the group. Find the new average.

Old total:

\[
40\times10=400
\]

New total:

\[
400+50+60=510
\]

New number:

\[
10+2=12
\]

New average:

\[
\frac{510}{12}=\boxed{42.5}
\]

---

# 6. The shortcut you should learn

There is a beautiful shortcut when **one value is added**.

Suppose:

* Old average = 20
* New number = 30
* Number of old items = 5

The new number is **10 above the old average**.

So the total increases by 10.

New average:

\[
20+\frac{10}{6}
\]

\[
=21.67
\]

Notice the denominator is **new number of items = 6**.

### General idea:

If a new value is **x above the old average**, the average increases by:

\[
\frac{x}{\text{new number of items}}
\]

Similarly, if it is below the average, the average decreases.

---

# 7. Very important concept: Average is not always one of the numbers

Example:

\[
10,\ 20,\ 30,\ 40
\]

Average:

\[
\frac{100}{4}=25
\]

There is no 25 in the list.

That's completely fine.

\boxed{\text{Total}=\text{Average}\times\text{Number of items}}
\]

Once this becomes automatic, average questions get MUCH easier. 💪
