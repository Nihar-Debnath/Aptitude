Ahhh, **this is exactly the confusing part**. You're thinking:

> "There are 5 tyres, so why did we multiply by 4 instead of 5?"

Because **only 4 tyres are being used at any one time**.

Let's visualize it:

### 🚗 At the beginning

```text
       🚗
   🛞  🛞
   🛞  🛞     ← 4 tyres on the road

   🛞          ← 1 spare tyre
```

The car travels **40,000 km**.

During that journey, at every moment, there are only **4 tyres touching the road**.

So the amount of tyre usage is:

[
4 \times 40,000 = 160,000
]

---

### But then what about the 5th tyre?

That's where **"used equally"** comes in.

Suppose we rotate them:

```text
First period:
A B C D = working
E = spare

Second period:
A B C E = working
D = spare

Third period:
A B C D = working
E = spare
```

Eventually, **all 5 tyres take turns being used**.

So the **160,000 km of total work** gets divided equally between 5 tyres:

[
160,000 \div 5 = 32,000
]

Therefore:

**Each tyre gets 32,000 km of use.**

---

### 🔥 The distinction you need to remember

**5 tyres exist**, but **only 4 tyres are working at a time**.

That's why:

[
\boxed{4 \times 40,000 \div 5 = 32,000}
]

If you did **5 × 40,000**, you'd be saying that **all 5 tyres were simultaneously carrying the car for the entire 40,000 km**, which isn't true because **one tyre is always the spare**.
