>A car travels from point A to B at 20 kmph and travels back from B to A at 60 kmph. Find the average speed of the car.

This is a **very common Time, Speed & Distance trap**. The answer is **30 km/h**, not 40.

### Given

A → B:

\[
20\text{ km/h}
\]

B → A:

\[
60\text{ km/h}
\]

The distance from A to B and B to A is the **same**.

---

## The important rule

When **equal distances** are travelled at two different speeds:

\[
\boxed{\text{Average Speed}=\frac{2uv}{u+v}}
\]

where (u) and (v) are the two speeds.

So:

\[
\text{Average Speed}
=

\frac{2(20)(60)}{20+60}
\]

\[
=\frac{2400}{80}
\]

\[
=\boxed{30\text{ km/h}}
\]

---

## 🚨 Why NOT 40 km/h?

You might think:

\[
\frac{20+60}{2}=40
\]

But that's the **average of the speeds**, not the average speed of the journey.

The car spends **more time travelling at 20 km/h** and less time travelling at 60 km/h.

### Easy way to visualize it

Assume A → B is **60 km**.

At 20 km/h:

\[
t_1=\frac{60}{20}=3\text{ hours}
\]

At 60 km/h:

\[
t_2=\frac{60}{60}=1\text{ hour}
\]

Total distance:

\[
60+60=120\text{ km}
\]

Total time:

\[
3+1=4\text{ hours}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{Average Speed}
=

\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}
\]

\[
=\frac{120}{4}
\]

\[
\boxed{30\text{ km/h}}
\]

### 🔥 TCS shortcut

Whenever you see:

> **Same distance + two speeds**

Immediately use:

\[
\boxed{\frac{2uv}{u+v}}
\]

For this question:

\[
\boxed{\frac{2(20)(60)}{20+60}=30\text{ km/h}}
\]

**Never simply average the two speeds unless the time spent at each speed is equal.**
