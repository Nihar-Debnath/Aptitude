>The average of 20 numbers is zero. Of them, at the most, how many may be greater than zero?


This is a **classic aptitude trick**. 😄

### Given

Average of 20 numbers = 0.

So:

[
\frac{\text{sum of 20 numbers}}{20}=0
]

Therefore:

[
\boxed{\text{sum}=0}
]

Now the question asks:

> **At most, how many can be greater than zero?**

---

### Think about it

Can **all 20** numbers be greater than zero?

No.

If all 20 were positive:

[
(+)+(+)+(+)+\cdots+(+)>0
]

Their sum couldn't be zero.

So **at least one number must be negative or zero**.

Therefore, at most:

[
20-1=\boxed{19}
]

### Can 19 actually be positive?

YES!

For example:

[
1,1,1,\ldots,1,-19
]

There are:

* **19 positive numbers**
* **1 negative number**

Sum:

[
19(1)-19=0
]

Therefore average:

[
0/20=0
]

So 19 is achievable.

# 🎯 Answer: (\boxed{19})

### 🧠 The shortcut

Whenever you see:

> **Average = 0**

immediately think:

[
\boxed{\text{Total sum}=0}
]

Then:

* Maximum positive numbers = **n − 1**
* Maximum negative numbers = **n − 1**
* Because you need at least one number of the opposite sign (unless zeros are involved).

For 20 numbers:

[
\boxed{19}
]

This is **not really an average-calculation question**. It's testing whether you understand what **average zero means about the total sum**.
