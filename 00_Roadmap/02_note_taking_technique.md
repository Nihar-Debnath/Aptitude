Since I know you're preparing for **TCS Ignite, Cognizant, Capgemini, Wipro, Accenture**, and you already keep **Reasoning** and **Verbal** notes in files, I'd actually **not** recommend taking traditional math notes.

Math is different from English and Reasoning.

**For Verbal**

* Rules
* Grammar
* Vocabulary
* Exceptions

➡️ Notes are extremely useful.

**For Numeric Ability**

* Formulas
* Short tricks
* Patterns
* Mistakes

➡️ Huge notebooks are usually a waste of time because you'll learn by solving questions.

## Example: Percentage.md

```markdown
# Percentage

## Formula

Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Increase %

(New - Old) / Old ×100

Decrease %

(Old - New)/Old ×100

## Fraction to %

1/2 = 50%
1/3 = 33.33%
1/4 = 25%
1/5 = 20%
1/8 = 12.5%

## Percentage to Fraction

50% = 1/2
25% = 1/4
20% = 1/5

## Important Tricks

Increase by 20%
×1.20

Decrease by 20%
×0.80

## Mistakes I Made

- Forgot original value in denominator.
- Mixed increase and decrease formulas.
```

Notice what's **not** there:

* No solved examples.
* No long explanations.
* No theory copied from YouTube.

Only what you'll need to revise.

# Keep one master Formula Sheet

```markdown
# Formula Sheet

## Average

Sum / Number of values

-------------

## Speed

Distance / Time

-------------

## Time

Distance / Speed

-------------

## Profit %

Profit / Cost Price ×100

-------------

## SI

P×R×T /100

-------------

## CI

P(1+r/100)^n
```

Before every mock test, revise only this file.

---

# Keep a Mistake Book (Most Important)

This is where most people improve.

```markdown
# Mistakes

## Percentage

❌ Forgot denominator.

-----------------------------------

## Average

❌ Forgot to divide by number of observations.

-----------------------------------

## Time & Work

❌ Added days instead of work efficiencies.

-----------------------------------

## Profit Loss

❌ Used Selling Price instead of Cost Price.
```

Every time you make the same mistake twice, write it here.

# What NOT to write

❌ Entire chapter from a book

❌ Every solved example

❌ Every question

❌ Every shortcut from YouTube

If you can Google it in 10 seconds, don't spend time writing it.

# My recommendation for your preparation

Because you're balancing English, DSA, and placement preparation, I'd keep numeric notes **extremely lean**:

* **One topic file** (formulas + 5–10 key tricks + common mistakes)
* **One master formula sheet** (all formulas in one place)
* **One calculation tricks sheet** (fractions, squares, cubes, percentages, etc.)
* **One mistake log** (updated after every practice session)

Then spend **80–90% of your numeric ability time solving problems** and only **10–20% revising notes**. In aptitude, improvement comes far more from practice than from maintaining detailed notes.
